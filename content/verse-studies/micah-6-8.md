@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Micah 6 is a lawsuit. The mountains are called as witnesses. The Lord asks His people what He has done to weary them. They answer with a bidding war: burnt offerings, yearling calves, thousands of rams, rivers of oil, even the firstborn. Then this verse cuts the auction down.

He has already shown you. The man does not need a new revelation. He needs to stop offering God a spectacle in place of obedience.

@word H4941 | justly
*Do* justly. Not feel justly. Not post justly. The next verses indict short measures, wicked scales, and violent rich men. *Mishpat* here has hands.

@word H2617 | mercy
The verb is *love* chesed. You do not merely perform kindness when it is convenient. You attach yourself to covenant loyalty the way a man attaches himself to what he actually loves.

@word H6800 | humbly
The walk is *with* your God, not ahead of Him with a résumé of offerings. The KJV margin is close: humble thyself to walk.

The three are not a menu. Justice without chesed becomes a club. Chesed without justice becomes sentiment. Both without the humble walk become a man improving himself in God's general direction.

@section 2. Where It Sits

The chapter opens as covenant lawsuit, not as a poster for civic religion. Verses 6–7 are Israel trying to buy the Lord off with more religion. Verse 8 is the Lord's answer: I already told you. Verses 9–16 then name the actual charges — wicked treasure, short measure, deceitful weights, violence, lies, the statutes of Omri.

So the verse is not a soft alternative to doctrine. It is the doctrine applied against a people who still had altars and no honesty in the market. If you quote Micah 6:8 while leaving the scales crooked, you have joined the people the verse was written against.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The confession later names this verse as a proof that good works are only what God has commanded, not what men invent out of zeal. That is the right instinct. Micah 6:8 is not a license to replace God's word with "being a good person." It is God's word refusing the substitutes.

The lawsuit in the chapter already interprets the verse: the Lord is not asking for more calves. He is asking for the obedience He already showed.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 16 — Of Good Works | /lbcf/chapter-16.html
Good works are only such as God has commanded in his Holy Word, and not such as without the warrant thereof are devised by men out of blind zeal, or upon any pretense of good intentions.
@endquote

The confession cites Micah 6:8 at that sentence. That is the lock. The verse does not invent a new morality beside Scripture. It names the obedience Scripture already required, against the religious inventions of verses 6–7.

Paragraph 2 adds the other edge: these works are fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith — not the price of God's favor.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Stop offering God a bigger religious production than the obedience you already know.

Do justly where your hands actually land this week — the scale, the invoice, the word you said about a brother, the way you treat the man who cannot pay you back.

Love chesed. That means attach yourself to covenant loyalty at home first. A man who is fierce about public justice and thin with mercy toward his wife has not done this verse. He has picked a slogan.

Walk humbly *with* your God. Not ahead of Him with a cause. Not behind Him at a safe distance. Beside Him, low enough to be corrected.

If your firstborn rhetoric is louder than your honest measure, you are still in verses 6–7.

He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good. The showing is past tense. The man is not waiting on a new download. He is being judged for the substitutes he already prefers.

What doth the LORD require of thee? Not thousands of rams. Not rivers of oil. Not the firstborn. Those were Israel's bid in verses 6–7. Verse 8 cuts the auction down. The requirement is older than the spectacle.
