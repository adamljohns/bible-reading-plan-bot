Every verb in the verse has God as the subject. Stone does not decide to become flesh. That is the point, and it is the offense.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Count the *I will* before you preach the new heart as a project. I will give. I will put. I will take away. I will give. Four actions, one agent. The man in the verse is not consulting, preparing, or meeting God halfway with a softer attitude. He is the site of a replacement.

The last contrast is easy to mishear in English. *Heart of flesh* here is not Paul's later use of "flesh" as the old man at war with the Spirit. In Ezekiel, stone is dead weight in the chest. Flesh is living tissue -- the kind that can feel a cut, receive a word, move. God is not promising a more sentimental temperament. He is promising a heart that is no longer a rock.

@word H2319 | new
*Chadash* is new, fresh -- the word Jeremiah uses for the new covenant, and Isaiah for new heavens and a new earth. It is not "improved." It is not the old heart with a better habit stacked on it. When God makes something *chadash* in the prophets, He is doing what the old thing could not do. A new song is not a louder old song. A new heart is not a motivated stony one.

@word H3820 | heart
*Leb* is the inner man -- trust, memory, counsel, stubbornness -- the command post, not the mood. Proverbs told you not to lean on your own understanding in this same inner place. Ezekiel says the post itself is stone, and will have to be given again. That is why appeals to "follow your heart" die here. The problem is not that Israel failed to listen to the *leb.* The problem is what the *leb* was made of.

If this word is the seat of decision, then a stony heart is not a sad feeling. It is an inability. Stone does not choose. It sits.

@word H7307 | spirit
*Ruach* is spirit, wind, breath -- the invisible animating thing. Verse 26 gives a new *ruach* within. Verse 27 goes further: I will put *my* spirit within you, and cause you to walk in my statutes. The new spirit of 26 is the interior renovation; the Spirit of 27 is God's own, given as the power that makes walking actually happen. Do not collapse them, and do not separate them. The man is remade inside, and then indwelt. Neither is a self-help.

@word H68 | stony
*Even* is stone -- pebble to boulder, memorial, altar, judgment. Here it is an adjective on the heart: the inner man as rock. Calvin's question later is the right one. If a stone has any softness you can work with, then grace is a supplement. If the picture means what it says, no good is coming out of that heart until it is made another thing. The verse does not say God will chip the stone. He will *take it away.*

@word H1320 | flesh
*Basar* is flesh, body, living tissue, and by extension mortal humanity. In this verse it does two jobs. The stony heart is taken out of your *flesh* -- out of your living body, where it did not belong as a rock. And you are given a heart of *flesh* -- an organ that belongs in a body, able to be written on, as Jeremiah said of the new covenant, and as Paul will say of tablets that are hearts of flesh.

So "flesh" here is mercy. It is the opposite of ossified. Read Paul later and you will need a different contrast. Do not import that war back into Ezekiel and make the gift sound like a problem.

@section 2. Where It Sits

The verse is not a greeting card. It is the center of a name-speech. Verse 22: I do not this for your sakes, O house of Israel, but for my holy name's sake, which you have profaned among the heathen. The new heart is not a reward for spiritual interest. It is God repairing His name in a people who had dragged it through the nations.

Then the sequence, which the slogans always flatten. I will gather you. I will sprinkle clean water, and you shall be clean; from all your filthiness and from all your idols I will cleanse you. *Then* the new heart and new spirit, stone out, flesh in. Then My Spirit within, causing you to walk in the statutes. Then the land, and the covenant formula: you shall be my people, and I will be your God.

Cleansing, new heart, indwelling, obedience, belonging. The heart is not the first gift in the paragraph, and it is not the last. Men who want a new heart without having their idols named are asking for verse 26 without verse 25. Men who want a new heart without walking are asking for 26 without 27. The Lord GOD offers the whole chain, and He offers it for His name.

Jeremiah 31 sits next to this in the church's memory: I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their heart. Ezekiel supplies the surgery Jeremiah assumed. You cannot write on stone with any permanence that counts here. You replace the tablet.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading took this verse as the death of cooperative regeneration. The will is not a weak muscle that grace assists. It is a stone that grace replaces. Effectual calling, in the confession's language, *is* this verse: He takes away the heart of stone and gives a heart of flesh, renews the will, and draws to Christ, yet so that they come most freely, being made willing by His grace. The freedom is on the far side of the gift. It is not the door you open so the gift can come in.

Calvin pressed the similitude until it hurt. If you can make a stone tender and flexible, then say that the heart is merely aided. If the Spirit meant that no good can be extracted until the heart is altogether new, do not share with Him what He claims for Himself alone. That is the honest reading of *take away* and *give.* Anything milder is a different metaphor, and usually a different religion -- one in which the sinner remains the decisive worker, and God is the consultant.

They also kept verse 22 in view, because a new heart for your sake is a different gospel than a new heart for His name's sake. Pride can survive a makeover. It cannot survive being told that the deepest renovation in you was not about you.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 10 — Of Effectual Calling | /lbcf/chapter-10.html
Those whom God has predestined to life he is pleased, in his appointed and accepted time, effectually to call by his Word and Spirit out of that state of sin and death in which they are by nature, to grace and salvation by Jesus Christ. He enlightens their minds spiritually and savingly to understand the things of God; he takes away their heart of stone and gives them a heart of flesh; he renews their wills, and by his almighty power determines them to that which is good and effectually draws them to Jesus Christ — yet so that they come most freely, being made willing by his grace.
@endquote

The confession does not allude to Ezekiel. It *says* Ezekiel, and then explains the freedom that follows. Made willing. Not already willing, then made new. The stone does not volunteer. The flesh-heart comes, and then it comes to Christ.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 9 — Of Free Will | /lbcf/chapter-09.html
Man, by his fall into a state of sin, has wholly lost all ability of will to any spiritual good accompanying salvation; so as a natural man, being altogether averse from that good, and dead in sin, is not able by his own strength to convert himself, or to prepare himself for it.
@endquote

*Not able by his own strength to convert himself, or to prepare himself for it.* That is the stony heart in prose. If you can prepare yourself -- soften a little, get ready, meet God at the halfway mark -- you did not need verse 26. You needed a coach. The prophet says you needed a gift, and the confession believes the prophet.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Stop waiting to feel the stone chip. If you are in Christ, the decisive taking-away has already been done by the Spirit through the Word. You are not the surgeon. You are the evidence. Verse 27 is how you check: do you walk, however clumsily, in what He said? A new heart that never keeps a statute is not this promise. It is a story you are telling about your inner life.

If you are not in Christ, do not treat this verse as a project for the week. You cannot take out your own heart. Hear the word that calls, and cry to the God who gives. The same paragraph that refuses your cooperation offers you water, Spirit, and a name restored. That is not an insult to your will. It is the only hope a stone has.

Then tell the truth about remaining war. The confession will not let regeneration mean you now only will what is good. Flesh in Ezekiel is the living heart. The other "flesh" still fights. You will need verse 27 every day -- His Spirit within, causing you to walk -- not because the gift was fake, but because the walk is the point. A new heart is not a mood you protect. It is an interior that can finally obey.

And do not use this on other people as flattery or as contempt. You do not know who is stone and who is not by how nice they seem. God does this for His name, in His time, through His Word. Your job is closer to Ezekiel 33 than you may like: speak what He has said, and leave the surgery to the only one who can take a heart out and put another in.
