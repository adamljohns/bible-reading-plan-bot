The letter ends in a fight, and the strength named is not yours. Every piece you are told to put on is already His. The assignment is smaller than we want: stand.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

*Finally.* Everything before this has been gift and walk: dead, then made alive; one body; husbands, wives, children, servants. Then the field. Be strong in the Lord and in the power of His might. Put on the whole armor of God. The wrestling is not against flesh and blood. Take the armor again, that you may withstand in the evil day, and having done all, stand. Then the pieces. Then prayer, always, for all the saints.

Read it as one charge, not as a costume list. Strength first, and the strength is located: *in the Lord.* Armor second, and the armor is named: *of God.* Enemy third, so you stop swinging at people. Stance fourth: stand, withstand, stand. Then kit. Then the air the kit is worn in: prayer.

@word G1743
*Endynamoo* is to put power into someone, to strengthen. The command is passive in force even when it is an imperative: be made strong. *In the Lord* is the location, not a motto. A man trying to be strong *and then* get into the Lord has reversed the verse. The might in the next clause is *His.* You are not being told to locate a reserve in yourself. You are being told where to stand so that His power is the power on the field.

@word G3833
*Panoplia* is the full armor -- every piece a heavy-armed man wears, not a strap picked for taste. Paul says it twice, verses 11 and 13. The repetition is the rebuke to selective Christianity: truth without righteousness, gospel without faith, a sword with no helmet. Whole, or you are not dressed. And it is *of God* before it is a list. Isaiah 59 has the LORD putting on righteousness as a breastplate and a helmet of salvation. Paul is issuing kit that already belongs to the fighter who saved you.

@word G3180
*Methodeia* is a method, a way laid out in advance, then worked with craft. Wiles are not random weather. They are procedures that have worked before. The devil in this paragraph is a slanderer with a system. You do not need a new enemy every week. You need to stop being surprised by the approach you already know.

@word G4487
*Rhema* is a spoken word, a specific utterance, more concrete than *logos* in many uses. The sword of the Spirit *is* the word of God. It is the one offensive piece in the list, and it is not your cleverness. It is what the Spirit has already said. A man who will not take up Scripture has gone to this fight with a scabbard.

@word G4335
*Proseuche* is prayer directed to God, and verse 18 will not let the armor be put on in silence. Always, with all prayer and supplication, in the Spirit, watching, with perseverance, for all the saints. The armor is issued to a body. You pray it on, and you pray it for the man next to you. A private fighter who never asks for the rest of the line has misunderstood the *you.* In Greek it is plural the whole way down.

@h3 The objective is still standing
Four times: able to stand, withstand, having done all to stand, stand therefore. Not take the city. Not become impressive. Still be there when the evil day has done its worst. The measure of this paragraph is not the ground you took. It is that the position you were given is still held.

@section 2. Where It Sits

This is the last movement of Ephesians, and the order is pastoral. Three chapters of what God has done. Three chapters of walking it out -- unity, the old self, light, marriage, children, masters. Only then the devil. Paul will not start the Christian life with the enemy. He starts it with the inheritance. By the time the armor appears you already know whose you are. The fight is defense of a gift, not a hunt for an identity.

He is in chains as he writes. Verse 20: an ambassador in bonds. The soldier in the room was real, but the pieces are Isaiah's before they are Rome's. Truth, righteousness, gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the Word -- that is the gospel restated as kit, not a hobbyist's interest in military gear.

What follows the armor is not a war manual. It is a prayer request and greetings. The fight lands in speech, in the saints, in a man asking that utterance be given him. Spiritual warfare in this letter looks like a church that tells the truth, stays dressed, and prays.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed tradition read the armor as union with Christ applied to combat, not as a second system for advanced believers. You fight in what is His because you are in Him. That kept the passage from superstition on one side -- as if the Christian life were mainly confrontations with the dark -- and from moralism on the other, as if the list were virtues to cultivate until you were safe.

Calvin refused to let prayer float free of the rest of the kit.

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.20 | /institutes/b3-c20.html
For before urging believers to pray in the Spirit always, with vigilance and assiduity, he enjoins them to take "the shield of faith," "the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God."
@endquote

The order is Paul's, and Calvin marks it. Faith, salvation, the Word -- then prayer. Prayer without that kit is words poured into the air. Prayer with it is how a weak man stays in the fight: watchful, persistent, and not alone. *For all saints* is the confession that your private war is never private.

The tradition also insisted the enemy is real and not ultimate. Principalities and powers are not a metaphor for bad moods. They are also not a rival god. You stand. You do not negotiate, and you do not panic. The evil day is expected. The armor was issued because it would be.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures | /lbcf/chapter-01.html
The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.
@endquote

The sword in verse 17 is not a mood and it is not a private whisper. It is this rule. Sufficient -- so you are not waiting for a better weapon. Certain -- so the fiery darts do not get to redefine what is true. Infallible rule of *obedience* as well as knowledge: the Word is how you stand, not only how you think. A church that will not take up Scripture has already lost the only offensive piece it was given.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 95 | /catechism.html
That the Word may become effectual to salvation, we must attend to it with diligence, preparation, and prayer; receive it with faith and love, lay it up in our hearts, and practice it in our lives.
@endquote

Diligence, preparation, prayer. That is verses 17-18 in the catechism's voice: the sword taken up, and prayer as the way it is taken up. Laying it up in the heart is the shield's work too. Faith in this paragraph is not a feeling you try to inflate when the dart comes. It is trust already stored, used to quench what is on fire.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Put it on before the day, because that is how armor works. The Word before the phone. The truth about God and about your actual situation, named, before the slander arrives. Righteousness that is Christ's, then the next clean act. Gospel on the feet -- ready to move, not looking for a fight with flesh and blood in the office. Faith as the thing you hold up when the old accusation starts its run. Salvation on the head, so one bad hour cannot tell you that you are lost. Then pray, out loud if you have to, for the saints you can name.

Stop aiming at the wrong body. Verse 12 is there because you will want to wrestle your wife, your boss, your brother. They are not the principality. Treat people as the enemy and you have already believed a wile.

And take standing as the win. Some seasons the whole assignment is that you are still married, still sober, still in the Word, still in the church, still telling the truth at closing. That is not a consolation prize. That is the stated objective, in the evil day, in borrowed armor, with the rest of the line praying.
