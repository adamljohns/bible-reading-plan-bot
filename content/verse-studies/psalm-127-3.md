@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Psalm 127 is a Song of Ascents, of Solomon. Verses 1–2 have already killed the anxious builder: unless the LORD builds the house, they labor in vain who build it; unless the LORD keeps the city, the watchman stays awake in vain. Then verse 3 turns from the construction site to the nursery, and it will not let you treat those as two different lives.

*Behold.* Look. The thing you were tempted to postpone, or resent, or manage as an interruption of the real work, is the heritage.

@word H1121 | children
Hebrew *banim* can be sons in the strict sense; the psalm's next lines (arrows, quiver, speaking in the gate) are aimed at a man's household line, not at a unisex sentiment about "kids in general."

@word H5159 | heritage
Children are not a lifestyle accessory. They are a portion assigned. You do not invent a heritage. You receive it, or you despise it.

@word H6529 | fruit
The fruit of the womb is not a metaphor for your personal growth. It is a child.

@word H7939 | reward
Children are not a side project a man adds when the house is finished. They are the LORD's heritage, and the fruit of the womb is His pay. A man who treats sons as an interruption of his real work has misread the invoice.

Verses 4–5 keep the military picture: arrows in a warrior's hand; a quiver; speaking with enemies in the gate. Heritage here is not nursery softness only. It is a man being given the next generation as weapons and as witnesses.

@section 2. Where It Sits

The psalm is Solomon's, and it is aimed at the man who will build. House, city, watch, labor, sleep — then children. The argument is that anxious toil cannot secure what only the LORD gives, and that what He gives as heritage is not a distraction from the house. It is the point of the house.

Read this after Deuteronomy 6:6–7 and the order is the same. Words in the father. Words sharpened on the children. Children received as heritage, not as a brand. A man who builds a platform and treats the quiver as optional has not heard Solomon. He has heard the age.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The church's older instinct on this verse is not "have as many children as a contest." It is: do not call waste what God calls wages. The modern inversion — children as a cost center, delayed until the résumé is finished — is exactly the vanity verses 1–2 already named, now aimed at the womb.

Increase is not an embarrassment the confession forgot to update. Psalm 127:3 is that ordinance as gift. Heritage from the LORD. Reward. Not a lifestyle option parked beside the real vocation.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

If the Lord has given you sons, stop talking about them as if they interrupted the mission. They are the mission's next shaft.

Receive them as *nachalah* — a portion, not a hobby. Then do Deuteronomy 6:7 with them. A quiver you will not sharpen is not a blessing you are stewarding. It is a weapon you are leaving unstrung.

If you do not yet have children, do not despise the verse by turning it into a joke or a political badge. Ask whether your present labor is the vain kind — house without the Lord, city without the Keeper — and whether you are already training your heart to treat a future heritage as an inconvenience.

The test is not how loudly you praise family. The test is whether the fruit of the womb is, in your actual week, treated as His reward.
