Six verses, two scenes, one claim. The LORD is my shepherd. Everything after that is what a sheep says when the claim is true, including in the valley.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

The psalm is short enough to be treated as a motto. It is not. It moves. Verses 1–3 speak of the shepherd in the third person. Verse 4 turns and addresses Him. The valley is where *He* becomes *Thou*. Then a table in the presence of enemies, a cup that runs over, and a last sentence that will not stop at death: goodness and mercy shall pursue me, and I will dwell in the house of the LORD to length of days.

David does not begin with the valley. He begins with a name and an office. The LORD. My shepherd. Then a consequence that sounds like overclaim until you have walked with it: I shall not want.

@word H7462 | shepherd
*Ra'ah* is to shepherd, tend, feed, pasture, rule. Comprehensive care, not a job title on a wall. A shepherd in David's world walked ahead, fought, counted, and stayed. The verb is ordinary and total. When the New Testament writers put this psalm on Christ, they are not decorating. John 10:11, Hebrews 13:20, 1 Peter 2:25, Revelation 7:17 all take the office as real. The psalm's first verb is the doctrine of the rest.

@word H2637 | I shall not want
*Chaser* is to lack, to be without, to be deficient. The sentence is not "I shall have everything I like." It is: with this shepherd, the lack that would undo a sheep will not be the last word. Green pasture and still water in verse 2 are the first illustrations, not the whole inventory. The valley will test whether *chaser* still holds when the grass is gone.

@word H3372 | I will fear
*Yare* is to fear, be afraid, revere. The psalm does not say there is nothing fearful in the valley. It says *I will fear no evil* — *ra*, harm, the bad thing that has a face. The reason is not temperament. *For thou art with me.* The rod and the staff comfort because they are His, not because valleys are imaginary. Calvin will say the shudder is real. The prevailing thought is presence.

@word H2617 | mercy
*Chesed* is covenant love and loyalty — not a mood, a bond. "Goodness and mercy" are not two decorations trailing a successful life. They are the LORD's own character walking behind the sheep. Mercy here is not the cancellation of the valley. It is the refusal of the Shepherd to let the valley be the last scene.

@word H7291 | shall follow
*Radaph* is to pursue, chase, persecute. The word is used of enemies running a man down. David turns it. Goodness and *chesed* do the hunting. The sheep is not dragging blessings behind him like trophies. He is being followed by a loyalty that will not quit. That is a harder comfort than "nice things will happen." It means you can stop chasing what is already chasing you in the Shepherd's name.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Psalm 23 sits after Psalm 22. That is not trivia. Psalm 22 is the forsaken cry, the pierced hands, the divided garments. Psalm 23 is the shepherd psalm. The church has always heard them in that order: the suffering first, then the care. A man who only knows Psalm 23 has not yet read the book. A man who only knows Psalm 22 has not yet heard what the Shepherd does with the one who walked the valley.

The psalm is "of David." A shepherd who became a king is talking about being a sheep. That inversion is the point. The man who could command armies still locates his safety in being led. Verse 5's table in the presence of enemies is not a picnic. It is a king eating while hostility still has a seat in the room. Provision does not wait for the enemies to leave.

The structure is a day and a life. Lie down. Be led. Restored. Paths of righteousness for His name's sake — the paths are not first about the sheep's reputation. They are about the Shepherd's. Then the valley. Then the table. Then the house. The last line is length of days, the KJV margin says, and "for ever." The psalm will not let the house be only a temple visit. It is dwelling.

Read verse 3's *for his name's sake* against the whole. The leading is not a reward for being an easy sheep. It is the Shepherd keeping His own name. That is why the valley does not break the logic. If He leads for His name, He is still leading when the path goes down.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin will not let you pretend the valley is a metaphor you can skip. He quotes verse 4 as what a pious mind says when it rises, and then he admits the shudder.

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.2 | /institutes/b3-c02.html
The pious mind will always rise, and be able to say with David, "Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me"
@endquote

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.2 | /institutes/b3-c02.html
Doubtless it is a terrific thing to walk in the darkness of death, and it is impossible for believers, however great their strength may be, not to shudder at it; but since the prevailing thought is that God is present and providing for their safety, the feeling of security overcomes that of fear.
@endquote

That is the psalm's own ratio. The darkness is terrific. Strength does not cancel the shudder. Presence is the prevailing thought. Security is not the absence of the valley. It is what wins the argument inside it.

In the providence chapter he stacks this verse with the others the saints use when the world looks random: the LORD is on my side; though an host encamp, my heart shall not fear; though I walk through the valley. The confidence does not come from a quiet map. It comes from knowing God is at work everywhere, and that His work is their safety.

The Reformed reading also refused two cheap uses. First: that "I shall not want" is a prosperity slogan. The psalm includes a valley and enemies at the table. Second: that the Shepherd is a mascot for self-care. The rod comforts. Rods correct. A staff that never touches you is not this psalm.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 5 — Of Divine Providence | /lbcf/chapter-05.html
God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power and wisdom, does uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence
@endquote

*Direct* and *govern* are the shepherd verbs in confession English. The still waters and the valley are both under that sentence. A providence that only covers green pasture is not the confession's, and it is not Psalm 23.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 17 — Of the Perseverance of the Saints | /lbcf/chapter-17.html
Many storms and floods may rise and beat against them, but they shall never be torn from the foundation and rock to which faith has fastened them.
@endquote

That is verse 4 without the pastoral image. Storms rise. The fastening holds. The confession will even say the felt sense of God's love may be clouded for a time. The psalm's "I will fear no evil" is not a claim that the feeling never dips. It is a claim that the Shepherd does not.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 40 | /catechism.html
The souls of believers are at their death made perfect in holiness, and do immediately pass into glory; their bodies, being still united to Christ, do rest in their graves till the resurrection.
@endquote

*I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever* is that answer in the psalm's key. The house is not only a building in Jerusalem. It is the dwelling the catechism names: glory now for the soul, rest for the body, and the full enjoyment still ahead. The Shepherd's last word is not the valley. It is the house.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Stop quoting verse 1 as if it were a mood. Ask whether you are being led. A man who will not be led is not a sheep in this psalm. He is a stray using the vocabulary. The paths of righteousness are for His name. If your path is only for yours, you are not in verse 3 yet.

When the valley comes, do not wait to feel brave. Calvin said the shudder is impossible to skip. Name the presence. *Thou art with me.* The rod and the staff are not interior. They are the Shepherd's tools — correction and support. If you only want the staff, you have not accepted the office.

Eat the table in the presence of the thing that hates you. The psalm does not say the enemies leave before the meal. Some provision happens while the threat is still in the room. You are not required to pretend it isn't there. You are required not to let it sit at the head.

And stop chasing what verse 6 says is already pursuing you. *Radaph.* Goodness and *chesed* run. Your work is to stay a sheep — under the name, in the paths, eyes on the Shepherd who walked Psalm 22 before He spoke Psalm 23. The house is ahead. Length of days. The lack will not have the last word, because the Shepherd will not.
