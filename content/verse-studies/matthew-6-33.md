The verse everyone uses to baptize ambition. Read the paragraph it sits in and the ambition dies. Jesus is not adding a spiritual goal to a full life. He is telling you what to do instead of worrying about dinner.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

The sentence opens with a contrast, and the contrast is the whole point. *But.* Against what? Against the Gentiles' hunt for food, drink, and clothes in the verse before. They seek those things. You are told to seek something else, and to seek it first.

*First* is not a scheduling tip. It is a claim about what is allowed to govern the rest. If the kingdom is first, dinner is not competing with it. If dinner is first, the kingdom is a hobby you fit in when the pantry is settled -- which, for an anxious man, is never.

And then the promise, which is the part that gets cut loose and sold. *All these things shall be added.* Added to what? To a life already aimed. They are not the prize. They are the provision that comes along when a man has stopped treating provision as the prize.

@word G2212
*Zeteo* is not a glance. The lexicon's range -- to search for, to strive after, to desire -- is the range of a hunt, not a preference. Jesus uses it as a command, present and ongoing. You do not seek the kingdom once and then graduate to practical matters. The seeking is the life.

The Gentiles in the verse before are also seeking. Their seeking is not lazy. It is aimed at the wrong object. The difference this verse names is not effort. It is *what* you spend the effort on.

@word G932
*Basileia* is reign more than territory. Seek first God's *reign* -- His government over you, His rule arriving, the order of things He is putting in place. That is a larger object than a religious feeling and a smaller one than a political program. A man under a king has orders. Seeking the kingdom is looking for those orders and getting under them.

@word G1343
*Dikaiosyne* is the other half of the object, and it keeps the first half from becoming vague. You do not seek a kingdom in general. You seek *His* righteousness -- the rightness that belongs to God, not the reputation you can assemble. In this sermon the word has already done heavy work: the poor who hunger for it, the righteousness that must exceed the scribes, the warning not to do your righteousness in front of men.

So the seeking is not a hunt for a spiritual mood. It is a hunt for the life God calls right, and for the standing only He can give. Both belong to Him. Neither is a product you manufacture and then present.

@word G4369
*Prostithēmi* is to add, to place alongside, to increase. The grammar is passive: these things *shall be added*. You are not told to add them. Someone else does the adding. That is the rebuke to the man who treats the kingdom as a strategy for getting the things. The adding is God's work, and the things being added are the ones named in the paragraph -- food, drink, clothing -- not a blank license for whatever you had in mind.

@h3 The word nobody treats as a limit
*All these things.* In English it sounds like a vault. In the paragraph it is a pointer. Verse 31 listed the questions: what shall we eat, what shall we drink, wherewithal shall we be clothed. Verse 32 called them *all these things*. Verse 33 promises them again. The promise is as large as a Father's knowledge of what you need, and as small as a day's meals. It will not fund a life whose real god was comfort.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Matthew 6 has already taught you to pray. The Lord's Prayer sits in this same chapter, and its second petition is *Your kingdom come*. Verse 33 is that petition turned into an order of operations. You asked for the kingdom. Now you are told to seek it first, which is what a man does when he meant the prayer.

Immediately before: do not be anxious about your life, what you will eat or drink, or about your body, what you will wear. Birds. Lilies. Solomon in all his glory. Your Father knows. The Gentiles chase these things; you have a Father who already knows you need them.

So verse 33 is not a new subject. It is the positive command that belongs to the prohibition. *Do not be anxious* is not a mood you can order up. *Seek first* is the thing you can actually do with the hours the anxiety was going to eat.

Immediately after: therefore do not be anxious about tomorrow. The *therefore* means verse 33 is doing work for verse 34. If the kingdom is first and the Father adds what today requires, tomorrow is not your assignment. Today's trouble is enough.

The larger sermon matters too. Chapter 5 opened with the bankrupt being congratulated. Chapter 6 opens with a warning not to perform righteousness for an audience. Then prayer. Then fasting. Then treasure. Then the eye. Then two masters. Then this paragraph about anxiety. Seeking the kingdom first is what a man does when he has already been told he cannot serve God and money.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading of this verse refused two popular corruptions at once.

The first is the reading that turns the kingdom into a lever for earthly increase. If you seek God, He will add the house, the career, the margin. The paragraph will not carry that. The added things are the needs the Gentiles were chasing, and they are added to people who have stopped treating those needs as the main thing. A promise of provision is not a prosperity scheme.

The second is the reading that makes *His righteousness* into a project of self-improvement -- as if Jesus were saying, get your ethics in order and then the groceries will follow. The confession's account of righteousness runs the other way: it is accounted, not infused as the ground of standing; it is Christ's, then it becomes the shape of a life. Seeking *His* righteousness is not building a record. It is going after the rightness that is His to give and His to require.

Calvin put the seeking of the kingdom at the level of why a man exists at all.

@quote Calvin, Institutes II.1 | /institutes/b2-c01.html
It is impossible for us to think of our first original, or the end for which we were created, without being urged to meditate on immortality, and to seek the kingdom of God.
@endquote

That is not a technique for a calmer week. It is the end for which we were created. Anxiety about food is not just a psychological problem in this tradition. It is a man living as if the end for which he was made were calories.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 109 | /catechism.html
In the second petition, which is, Your kingdom come, we pray that Satan's kingdom may be destroyed, and that the kingdom of grace may be advanced, ourselves and others brought into it and kept in it, and that the kingdom of glory may be hastened.
@endquote

The catechism will not let *kingdom* stay as a mood or a metaphor. It has an enemy (Satan's kingdom), a present form (the kingdom of grace), a personal stake (ourselves and others brought into it and kept in it), and a future (the kingdom of glory). Seeking that first is not vague spirituality. It is taking sides, entering, staying, and wanting the thing finished.

*Kept in it* is the phrase for an anxious man. The prayer is not only that you find the kingdom. It is that you are held there while the ordinary pressures of verse 31 are still real. Seeking first and being kept are not two religions.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 11 — Of Justification | /lbcf/chapter-11.html
Those whom God effectually calls, he also freely justifies; not by infusing righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting and accepting their persons as righteous; not for anything wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone.
@endquote

*Not by infusing righteousness into them* is the confession landing on the same adjective Jesus used: *His.* The righteousness you are told to seek is not a quality you brew and then present. It is accounted. It is Christ's. The seeking in verse 33 is not the manufacturing of a record that will get the Father to add things. It is the life of a man who already knows whose righteousness counts, and who is now ordering Tuesday around that fact.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Do not use this verse to bless the life you already wanted. That is the common theft. A man keeps his real first things -- the job, the image, the account -- and then quotes verse 33 as if putting the kingdom on a list of goals were the same as seeking it first. First is not a slot on a list. It is what the other things get to be about.

So name your actual first. What gets the first hour. What gets the first fear. What you would not drop even if Jesus asked. If the honest answer is a paycheck, a reputation, or a child's achievement, the verse has found you, and it is not complimenting you.

Then keep the promise inside its paragraph. *All these things* means the Father knows about dinner. It does not mean every plan will be funded. A man can seek the kingdom first and still have a thin week. The birds are fed; they are not idle, and they do not eat like kings. The promise is that you are not a forgotten creature while you obey. It is not that obedience is a career strategy.

And start before you feel ready. The Gentiles seek after they are scared. The command is to seek first, which means the seeking is not the thing you do once the anxiety is managed. It is how the anxiety gets answered -- a different object for the same energy, today, while the pantry is whatever it is.
