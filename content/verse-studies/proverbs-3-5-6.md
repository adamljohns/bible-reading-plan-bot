Two verses, one couplet plus a promise. Trust, do not lean, acknowledge in all the ways — then He makes the paths straight. The last clause is not a crystal ball. It is government.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Everyone can finish the first line. Trust in the LORD with all your heart. It is on mugs and it is true. Solomon wrote more than a slogan. Hebrew poetry does its work by pairing. Verse 5 tells you what trusting displaces: lean not on your own understanding. Verse 6 tells you the field: in all your ways know Him. Then the promise: He shall make the paths straight.

The pair is not "do not think." Proverbs is a book that demands hard thinking and calls the man who will not think a fool. The prohibition is on *leaning*. There is a difference between using your mind and putting your weight on it. Verse 6 then refuses the private room. All the ways. Not the religious ones only.

@word H982 | Trust
*Batach* is the strong word for trust, and its picture is the point: to lean your full weight. You will put your weight somewhere. The only question is what is underneath you when it matters. The object is named. The LORD — the covenant name, not a generic posture toward the universe.

@word H8172 | lean
*Shaʿan* is to lean — to put your weight on something so that if it moves, you fall. It is not a glance. It is a rest. Verse 5 uses two lean-words on purpose. *Batach* toward the LORD. *Shaʿan* forbidden toward your *binah*. The verse is not contrasting trust with thought. It is contrasting two props.

@word H998 | understanding
*Binah* is discernment, the mind that distinguishes. Three verses earlier the book is telling you to seek it like silver. So the warning is not anti-intellectual. It is telling you that the faculty you are right to develop is the wrong floor to stand on. Solomon is not warning the fool off his stupidity. He is warning the wise man off his wisdom.

@word H3045 | acknowledge
*Yada* is to know, perceive, be acquainted with. "Acknowledge" in English can sound like a nod. The Hebrew is thicker. In all your ways *know* Him. Not mention Him. Know. The first commandment, the catechism will say, is to know and acknowledge God as the only true God and our God. Verse 6 is that knowledge taken into the roads, not left in the sanctuary.

@word H3474 | he shall direct
*Yashar* is to be or make straight, level, right. The KJV's *direct* and the WEB's *make straight* are the same verb. The promise is not preview. It is not "He will show you a film of next year." It is: He will straighten what you walk. Crooked made right. That is providence, not a weather report. The paths (*orach*) are the course of a life. He governs them. You still walk.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Proverbs 1–9 is not a list of proverbs. It is a father talking to a son at length before the short sayings start — a sustained argument that wisdom is a person to be loved and folly is a person to be avoided.

Chapter 3 opens with the father asking the son not to forget his teaching, and promising length of days and peace. Mercy and truth bound around the neck, written on the tablet of the heart. Then this pair. Then: be not wise in thine own eyes; fear the LORD and depart from evil. It will be health to thy navel. Honor the LORD with thy substance. Then the father's "my son" again.

So 3:5–6 is not a detached motto for decision week. It sits inside a chapter that also talks about money, discipline, and not despising the LORD's chastening. "All your ways" includes the storehouse and the wound. A man who trusts God with a career move and leans on himself with his money has not done verse 6.

The existing study on 3:5 alone already pressed the second clause of that verse. This page will not let you stop there. Verse 6 is the field test. All the ways. Know Him. Then the paths are straightened. Cut verse 6 off and you have a trust that never has to show up at work. Cut verse 5 off and you have a technique for guidance that never surrenders the prop.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin read Solomon on this very point when he treated providence. Human planning is not cancelled. Presuming to act as if you were not ruled is.

@quote Calvin, Institutes I.17 | /institutes/b1-c17.html
For while he derides the stupidity of those who presume to undertake anything without God, as if they were not ruled by his hand, he elsewhere thus expresses himself: "A man's heart deviseth his ways but the Lord directeth his steps"
@endquote

@quote Calvin, Institutes I.17 | /institutes/b1-c17.html
intimating, that the eternal decrees of God by no means prevent us from proceeding, under his will, to provide for ourselves, and arrange all our affairs
@endquote

That is 3:5–6 without the mug. Devise. Direct. Provide for yourselves under His will. The Reformed reading refused two ditches. One: that trust means you stop using *binah*. The book forbids that. Two: that arranging your affairs means you get to lean on the arrangement. Verse 5 forbids that. The directing in verse 6 is the LORD's, whether or not you saw the straightening coming.

The tradition also refused to turn "He shall direct thy paths" into a promise of inner impressions. The promise is government. You may still choose in the dark. You are told who is steering, not where the road photographs.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 5 — Of Divine Providence | /lbcf/chapter-05.html
God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power and wisdom, does uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence
@endquote

*Direct* is the confession's word and Solomon's promise. *From the greatest even to the least* is why *all your ways* is not exaggeration. A providence with gaps would leave you a room in which leaning on *binah* was still required. The confession will not grant the room.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 51 | /catechism.html
The first commandment requires us to know and acknowledge God to be the only true God and our God, and to worship and glorify him accordingly.
@endquote

*Know and acknowledge.* Verse 6 in catechism English. Not a guidance seminar. The first commandment taken into the ways. If He is not acknowledged as God in a way, that way is still a rival sanctuary.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 14 | /catechism.html
God's works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions.
@endquote

*All their actions.* The paths being straightened are not a third thing besides your actions. They include them. Trust does not remove you from the path. It locates the One who levels it.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Test yourself on the lean, not on the slogan. Where does the anxiety drop when a plan improves? That is the prop. Use *binah*. Proverbs would have you use it. Then notice whether you can sleep if the plan does not. If you cannot, you have found what you were *shaʿan*-ing on.

Take verse 6 into the rooms you would rather keep. All the ways: the job, the house, the browser, the speech, the money. Knowing Him there is not a plaque. It is treating those roads as His, which means some of them will have to change direction. Straightening can feel like loss before it feels like help.

And do not use this pair as a promise that you will know. It promises He will direct. The man trusting God is often the man who chooses with the best understanding he has, refuses to rest his weight on it, names the LORD in the way he is actually walking, and sleeps. Not because he has a preview. Because the paths are being made straight by someone who does not need one.

Competence is a real gift. It works most of the time, which is what makes it a comfortable lean. The verses are not asking you to become less capable. They are asking you to notice that you are standing on something that will hold you right up until the day it does not — and to put the weight on the Name that does not move.
