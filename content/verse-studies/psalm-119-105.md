A lamp is for the next step. A light is for the path. The word is not a floodlight for the whole map. It is enough for the feet you have to put down now.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Nun. The fourteenth letter of the Hebrew alphabet, and the fourteenth stanza of the longest chapter in the Bible. Eight verses, almost every one naming the word by one of the psalm's usual names. This one is the verse people print on lamps. Fair enough. Read what it actually promises.

Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path. Two images, not one. The lamp is at the feet — oil, wick, a small clay vessel, enough to see the next stone. The light is on the path — a *netivah*, a trail, not a highway. The KJV margin says *lamp: or, candle*. Neither word is a sun. You still walk in a world that is dark enough to need one.

@word H1697 | Thy word
*Dabar* is the spoken matter — word, command, thing. In this psalm it is one of the eight or so names for what God has said: law, testimonies, precepts, statutes, commandments, judgments, word. The lamp is not a mood. It is speech. A feeling in the dark is not this verse. The thing that lights the feet is what God has uttered.

@word H5216 | lamp
*Ner* is an oil lamp — small clay or metal, olive oil, a wick. Proverbs 6:23 says the commandment is a lamp and the law is light. 2 Peter 1:19 says you do well to take heed to the prophetic word as to a lamp shining in a dark place. The psalm is not promising stadium lighting. A *ner* is what a man carries when the night is real. It is portable. It has to be kept. Oil runs out if you will not tend it.

@word H7272 | feet
*Regel* is the foot, the organ of travel, and then the metaphor for a life's path. The lamp is not aimed at the horizon first. It is aimed at the feet. That is mercy. You are not required to see the next ten years. You are required to see the next step well enough not to break it. Men who demand a map of the decade before they will obey have not accepted *regel*.

@word H216 | light
*Or* is light in every ordinary sense — daylight, fire, and then the illumination of mind and way. Genesis 1 uses it. Here it is paired with the lamp so you will not think the word only prevents a stumble. It also shows a trail. Lamp for the stone. Light for the direction. Both from the same *dabar*.

@word H5410 | path
*Netivah* is a trail, a beaten track, a narrow track — not the broad *derek* of a main road. The word is lighting a footpath, not a boulevard. That matches the rest of the psalm. The young man of verse 9 is keeping his way. The hiding of verse 11 is storage against sin. This verse is the night-walk of the same man. The trail is there. It is not the crowd's road.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Psalm 119 is an acrostic: twenty-two stanzas, one for each letter, eight verses each. The form is the argument. A man ordering his whole alphabet around what God has said. Verse 105 opens *Nun*. The stanza that follows will speak of affliction, of the wicked laying snares, of a heart inclined to perform the statutes to the end. The lamp is not a study-desk verse only. It is given to a man who is already being hunted.

Verse 9 asked how a young man keeps his way pure. Verse 11 hid the word in the heart so he might not sin. Verse 105 is the same word taken outdoors. The heart-storage of 11 does not stay indoors. It becomes a lamp when the feet have to move. The three verses are one practice: heed, hide, walk.

The psalm will not let you treat the word as a charm in the pocket. A lamp you will not lift does not light the feet. A light you will not follow does not make a path. The Nun stanza ends with "I have inclined mine heart to perform thy statutes alway, even unto the end." The lamp is for performance, not for atmosphere.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin hears this verse as the Law's use for the regenerate: not what the Law can give by itself, but the advantage David celebrates when the word is already his.

@quote Calvin, Institutes II.7 | /institutes/b2-c07.html
Again, "Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path"
@endquote

@quote Calvin, Institutes II.7 | /institutes/b2-c07.html
The whole psalm abounds in passages to the same effect.
@endquote

He has just quoted Psalm 19 on the Law converting the soul and enlightening the eyes. Then this. The Reformed reading is not that Scripture is a night-light for feelings. It is that the same word that convicts is the word that conducts. The regenerate man does not graduate past needing a lamp. David, well along, still says *my feet*.

When Calvin comes to predestination he uses the image in reverse, which tells you how seriously he took it:

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.21 | /institutes/b3-c21.html
to want any other knowledge of predestination than the knowledge his word sets forth is no less foolish than to walk where there is no path, or to look for light in the dark
@endquote

Walk with no path. Look for light in the dark. That is 119:105 inverted and refused. Wanting a second light — a private map, a secret knowledge, a word God did not say — is walking unarmed in the night the psalm already lit.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures | /lbcf/chapter-01.html
The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.
@endquote

*Only* is the word that keeps you from buying a second lamp. Sufficient for obedience — that is the feet. The confession will not let you treat the word as a source of religious information that does not govern the next step.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures | /lbcf/chapter-01.html
All things in Scripture are not alike plain in themselves, nor alike clear to all; yet those things which are necessary to be known, believed, and observed for salvation, are so clearly set forth and opened in some place of Scripture or other, that not only the learned, but the unlearned, in a due use of ordinary means, may attain to a sufficient understanding of them.
@endquote

A *ner* is an ordinary means. The unlearned man with a lamp and a due use of it is the confession's picture, not a scholar with a sun. Clarity is not the promise that every verse is easy. It is the promise that what you must obey is not locked.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 95 | /catechism.html
That the Word may become effectual to salvation, we must attend to it with diligence, preparation, and prayer; receive it with faith and love, lay it up in our hearts, and practice it in our lives.
@endquote

Lay it up — verse 11. Practice it in our lives — verse 105. The catechism will not let the lamp stay on a shelf. Feet. Path. Practice.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Tend the oil. A lamp is a small thing that goes out. Reading that does not continue is a wick you will not trim. Pick the next step you actually have to take this week — a conversation, a refusal, a duty you have delayed — and put the word on those feet. Not the whole map. The feet.

Do not wait for a sun. The verse gave you a *ner*. Men stall their obedience until God explains the decade. The psalmist asked for the stone in front of the foot. If you can see that stone by what God has already said, you have enough light to move. Remaining still because the horizon is dark is not humility. It is a man who wanted a different verse.

The path is a *netivah*. It will feel narrow. The broad road does not need a lamp. This one does. Walk it. The Nun stanza's last line is still in force: incline the heart to perform, even unto the end. The lamp is for that performance. Lift it, or stop quoting it.
