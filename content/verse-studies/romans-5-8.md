Paul locates the proof of God's love at a specific point in time, and it is not the point you would choose.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

The verse is an argument, not an exclamation. Paul is proving something, and the structure is: God demonstrates His love, and here is the demonstration -- while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. The evidence is a historical event, and the force of the evidence is entirely in the timing.

He has just spent two verses on how rarely anyone dies for anyone. For a righteous man, scarcely. For a good man, perhaps someone would dare. Then the contrast: God's love went to a class of people no one dies for. Not the righteous, not the good -- sinners, and not reformed ones.

@word G2089
The smallest word in the verse and the one carrying it. *Eti* means still, yet, even now -- it marks a condition that is *ongoing at the moment being described*. Christ died while the sinning was still in progress. Not after it stopped, not in anticipation of it stopping. The lexicon notes that Romans 5 is this word's most famous home, and rightly: strip *eti* out and the verse becomes a reward for improvement.

@word G268
*Hamartolos* is the standing category, not an occasional lapse -- one *characterized* by sin. In the Gospels it is the label pinned on the people the respectable would not eat with. Paul chooses the ugliest available term for the recipients and puts it in the same sentence as the demonstration of love. The two are not in tension for him. They are the point.

@word G599
*Apothnesko* is the ordinary, complete word for dying. It is worth noticing that Paul's evidence is not a feeling, a promise, or a gesture. The demonstration is a death, an event with a date, and the verb carries no softening.

@word G4921
*Sunistemi* means to commend, to prove, to establish — to place things together so a conclusion follows. It is an evidence word, closer to entering an exhibit than to expressing affection. God is not confessing a feeling here; He is producing proof.

@word G26
*Agape* is love originating in the lover rather than in the worth of the beloved. That definition is the verse: the timing clause exists precisely to rule out the idea that something attractive in us called it forth.

@h3 The clause that does the work
*While we were yet sinners.* Every other religion and most of our instincts put a different word there -- *once* we were no longer sinners, or *because* we might stop being sinners. Paul says *while*. The death happened during the condition, not after its resolution, and not on condition of its resolution.

Which means the love in this verse cannot be undone by a return of the condition. If it was given while you were in that state, discovering yourself in that state again is not new information to the one who gave it.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Romans 5 opens the great turn in the letter. Chapters 1 through 3 built the indictment; chapter 4 established that Abraham was justified by faith, not works; and chapter 5 begins *therefore, being justified by faith, we have peace with God.*

The paragraph containing our verse is an argument from the greater to the lesser, and you have to see the whole shape or the verse loses half its power. Paul's logic: if God did the *harder* thing -- loving and dying for enemies -- then the easier thing is certain. Verse 9: much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. Verse 10: if when we were enemies we were reconciled by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life.

*Much more* is the refrain. Verse 8 is not offered as a warm thought. It is the premise in a proof of security, and the conclusion Paul draws from it is that a Christian's future is safer than his past ever was.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading of this verse has always emphasized that the love is prior -- prior to faith, prior to repentance, prior to any change in the object. That is not a technicality. It determines where a believer looks for assurance.

If God's love responds to your condition, assurance requires monitoring your condition, and the monitoring never ends well. If the love was demonstrated while the condition was at its worst, then assurance looks outward, at an event in history that is not moving. The tradition's insistence on grounding assurance in Christ's finished work rather than in the believer's experience is simply this verse, applied.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 11 — Of Justification | /lbcf/chapter-11.html
Those whom God effectually calls, he also freely justifies; not by infusing righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting and accepting their persons as righteous; not for anything wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone.
@endquote

*Not for anything wrought in them, or done by them* is the confession saying *while we were yet sinners* in legal language.

The phrase to sit with is *accepting their persons*. Not accepting their improvement, their trajectory, or their potential -- their persons, as they are, on someone else's account. That is what makes the timing in Paul's verse doctrinally load-bearing rather than merely touching. A love that arrived during the worst of it is a love that was never keyed to the improvement.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

This verse is aimed at a specific and very common form of unbelief: the suspicion that God's disposition toward you tracks your recent performance. That God was pleased last month when things were tidy and is presently disappointed. Almost every Christian operates this way at some level, and almost no one says it out loud, because said out loud it is obviously false.

Paul's answer is not to tell you to feel differently. It is to point at a date. The demonstration already happened, under conditions that were worse than the ones you are currently embarrassed about, and it is not being re-evaluated.

So use the verse the way Paul uses it -- as an argument, not a sentiment. When the accusation comes that you have exhausted His patience, the reply is not *I feel loved*. The reply is *He did it while I was still a sinner, so the sinning is not the disqualifier, and there was never a version of this that ran on my performance.*

And notice what that frees you to do. A man who is not trying to secure love can finally tell the truth about himself -- to God, to his wife, to another believer. Confession is only terrifying if the relationship is conditional. This verse says it never was.
