Paul does not tell a young pastor to demand respect. He tells him to make contempt harder to maintain, and he names the six places where that gets done.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Two clauses, and the second is how the first happens. Let no one despise your youth. *But* -- be an example to the believers, in word, in manner of life, in love, in spirit, in faith, in purity.

The *but* is the whole method. Paul does not say, announce your office more loudly. He does not say, wait until you are older. He says, live in such a way that youth is no longer a useful excuse for ignoring you. The congregation's contempt is not cured by a speech about honor. It is crowded out by a life.

*Conversation* in the King James is not talk. In 1611 it meant your walk, your habitual way of living. The list is not five virtues plus speech. It is speech and then the rest of the man.

@word G2706 | despise
*Kataphroneo* is to think down on, to treat as of little account, to disregard. The *kata* is downward. Paul is not asking Timothy to manage his feelings about being young. He is naming what older believers will be tempted to do with a younger man's office: discount it. The verb assumes the temptation is real. Ephesus had elders. Timothy was sent into that.

@word G3503 | youth
*Neotes* is youth, the stretch of life still being formed. It is not a child's word, and it is not a compliment. In the ancient world a man could be called young well into the years when we would stop using the word. Paul is not pretending Timothy is a boy. He is telling him that whatever his age is, it will be used against him unless the life closes the argument.

@word G391 | conversation
*Anastrophe* is the turned-about life, the habitual conduct, the pattern you could plot from a month of watching. It sits second in the list on purpose. Word without this is the thing Paul is always hunting in this letter -- teachers whose mouths outrun their weeks. The example is not first a sermon. It is a week.

@word G26 | charity
*Agape* is love as gift and duty, not as a mood. In a young minister the missing piece is often this one. Zeal is cheap at that age. Accuracy can be cheap. Love of the flock -- the slow, untheatrical kind that stays in the room with difficult people -- is what makes the other five believable. Without it, youth looks like ambition even when it is not.

@word G47 | purity
*Hagneia* is purity, chastity, moral cleanness, and it is not a footnote. Paul puts it last in the list the way you put a lock on the door. A young man's ministry will be despised for many false reasons. It will be *rightly* despised if this one is missing. The word is wider than the bed and it includes the bed. Timothy is to be an example *in* it, not merely adjacent to it.

@h3 The list is the office
Word. Walk. Love. Spirit. Faith. Purity. Some copies of the letter do not carry *in spirit*; both public-domain texts on this page include it. Nothing in the charge hangs on the extra slot. The point of the inventory is that the example is the whole man, not a specialty. A man can be brave in the pulpit and still be ignorable in the other five. Paul will not let Timothy pick the one he is good at.

@section 2. Where It Sits

This is not a youth-group verse. It is a field order to a man Paul left in Ephesus to stop false teachers. Chapter 1: charge some that they teach no other doctrine. Chapter 3: the qualifications of overseers, including *not a novice*. Then chapter 4: some will depart from the faith, attending to seducing spirits; Timothy is to be a good minister, nourished in the words of faith. Verse 11: these things command and teach. Then our verse. Then: until I come, attend to reading, to exhortation, to doctrine. Neglect not the gift. Take heed to yourself and to the doctrine.

So the youth problem is happening inside a command to teach. Timothy has to open his mouth in a church that has reasons to prefer older voices, some of those reasons honest. Paul's answer is not to make the office louder. It is: command and teach, and be the kind of man whose life does not give the scoffers free material.

Verse 16 is the end of the unit: take heed to yourself and to the doctrine; continue in them; you will save both yourself and those who hear you. The example of verse 12 is not public relations. It is how a young man's teaching becomes credible enough to save anyone.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed tradition read the pastor's life as part of the ministry, not as a private extra. Calvin will not let doctrine stay on the tongue.

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.6 | /institutes/b3-c06.html
Doctrine is not an affair of the tongue, but of the life; is not apprehended by the intellect and memory merely, like other branches of learning; but is received only when it possesses the whole soul, and finds its seat and habitation in the inmost recesses of the heart.
@endquote

That is Paul's list in one sentence. Word is not enough. The doctrine has to find a habitation. A young minister who thinks the congregation owes him a hearing because he has the right system has not yet learned what this verse is for.

Calvin also refused the cheap opposite: despising the office because the man is not impressive.

@quote Calvin, Institutes IV.3 | /institutes/b4-c03.html
Nor could the office be more highly eulogised than when he said, "He that heareth you heareth me; and he that despiseth you despiseth me."
@endquote

The congregation does not get to despise Timothy's youth as a way of despising Christ's sending. Timothy does not get to hide behind the sending as a way of skipping the example. Both sides of the verse are in force. Honor the office. Be the kind of man the office looks like.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

The confession puts a duty on the church that matches Paul's first clause: due respect for the men who watch for their souls.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 26 — Of the Church | /lbcf/chapter-26.html
The work of pastors being constantly to attend the service of Christ in his churches in the ministry of the Word and prayer, watching for their souls as they that must give an account to him, it is incumbent upon the churches to whom they minister not only to give them all due respect, but also to communicate to them of all their good things according to their ability.
@endquote

*Due respect* is what "let no one despise" looks like from the pew. Age is not listed as the condition. The work is: Word and prayer, watching, accounting. A church that withholds respect until the pastor's hair greys has added a qualification Paul did not write.

The catechism files the same duty under the fifth commandment, where honor is not only for parents.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 69 | /catechism.html
The fifth commandment requires the preserving the honor and performing the duties belonging to everyone in their various places and relations, as superiors, inferiors, or equals.
@endquote

Timothy is inferior in years and, in Paul's sending, a superior in office. Both are true at once. The congregation owes the honor of the relation. Timothy owes the life that does not make that honor look foolish. The verse holds both men to their side.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

If you are young and in any kind of charge -- a home, a crew, a church, a younger brother who watches you -- do not spend the week demanding not to be despised. That demand is itself easy to despise. Be the example in the six places, especially the ones that do not photograph. Word and purity will be tested. So will the slow ones: love, faith, the way you talk when you think the saints are not in earshot.

If you are older, this verse is as much yours as his. Contempt for youth is often laziness wearing experience. The question is not whether he is younger than you. It is whether he is an example in the things Paul listed. If he is, your discount is disobedience. If he is not, your job is still not to sneer. It is to be, yourself, the example you say you cannot find in him.

And nobody gets to use this verse as a personality program. *Be thou an example* is not "build a brand." The Greek behind *be* is closer to *become*. It is a life taking shape in public, in a real church, under a real charge, with reading and doctrine in the next verse so that the example has content. A magnetic young man with an empty teaching is not this text. Neither is a precise young man whose week contradicts him. Paul wants both, in one person, until he comes.
