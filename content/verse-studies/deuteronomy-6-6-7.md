Two verses, one order. The words land in the father first. Then they are sharpened on the children in the ordinary hours. Skip either half and you have a poster, not a house.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Moses has just given the Shema. Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one. You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, soul, and might. Then, before phylacteries, doorposts, or a son's question, he gives this pair.

Verse 6: these words shall be in your heart. Verse 7: you shall whet them on your children, and talk of them when you sit, walk, lie down, and rise. The grammar is not a suggestion. Shall be. Shall teach. Shall talk. The words are not a poster for the house. They are a resident of the man, then speech in the traffic of the day.

The KJV margin on *teach them diligently* is the whole second verse: Hebrew *whet, or, sharpen.* Moses does not say put a lesson on the calendar. He says take the commanded words of verse 6 and put an edge on your sons with them.

@word H1697 | these words
*Dabar* is speech that has become a matter — a word, a command, a thing. "These words" are not inspirational fragments. They are the commandments, statutes, and judgments of 6:1, concentrated in the confession of 6:4–5. What has to be in the heart, and then in the children's ears, is the whole claim: this God, this love, this exclusive allegiance.

@word H6680 | which I command
The words do not enter the heart as a hobby. They are commanded. Moses is not offering Israel a spirituality. He is placing them under a charge that has to be internalized, then spoken. A father who treats the Shema as optional will treat verse 7 as a youth-group subcontract.

@word H3824 | in thine heart
*Lebab* is the longer twin of *leb*. It still means heart — the inner man of love, memory, and will. Hebrew "heart" is not feelings as opposed to the head. It is the place decisions are actually made. So verse 6 is not "feel warmly about the Bible." It is: these words must get down into the part of you that chooses before you have time to perform. A father whose heart is empty cannot whet a blade he does not hold.

@word H8150 | teach them diligently
*Shanan* means to whet or sharpen, as a sword or arrow, and by extension to teach sharply and repeatedly. Related to *shen*, tooth. Teaching that is *shanan* is not a once-a-week talk. It is a blade taken to the stone again. A dull father produces dull sons. The verse will not let you blame the youth group for an unsharpened house.

@word H1696 | shalt talk
The verb *dabar* is to speak with weight — not chatter, not a slogan on the way out the door. The words already commanded in verse 6 become speech in verse 7. Sit. Walk. Lie down. Rise up. That is a whole day. A silent house is not a peaceful house. It is an empty whetstone. A house that only talks of the Lord at a set "devotion time" has scheduled God. Moses wants the words in the ordinary traffic.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Deuteronomy 6 is the chapter after the Ten Words. It is aimed at a people about to inherit houses they did not build, wells they did not dig, vineyards they did not plant (6:10–12). The danger is not ignorance in the wilderness. The danger is fullness in the land.

So the sequence is deliberate. One LORD (v. 4). Whole-self love (v. 5). Words in the heart (v. 6). Then — only then — diligent teaching of the children in the ordinary hours (v. 7). Bind them. Write them. And when the son asks what the testimonies mean, tell him the exodus story (vv. 20–25).

Start at verse 7 and you get a parenting technique. Keep verse 6 in front of it and you get a man who must first carry what he means to sharpen. The pair is the lock and the whetstone. Separate pages on this site already treat each verse. This page will not let you keep them apart. The commanded words first become a resident of the man, and only then become speech in the house.

The four hours close the loophole. Sit in the house — table, couch, indoor quiet. Walk by the way — commute, errand, the walk to the truck. Lie down — last words of the day. Rise up — first words of the morning. If the words are only present in one room of the day, they are a hobby. Verse 7 is how a father keeps the next generation from drowning in gift. He whets them while they are still in the ordinary hours, before prosperity makes the words optional.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed household instinct on this pair is the same as on Psalm 119:11: Scripture is not clergy property. It is the father's inward supply and then his daily speech. That is why the tradition built family worship instead of leaving the text to the pulpit.

Calvin hears Moses put the same charge on the dying day. The words go into the heart. Then they are commanded to the children. The hours in verse 7 are not extra credit. They are the life of the house.

@quote Calvin, Institutes II.7 | /institutes/b2-c07.html
When about to die, he thus addressed the people, "Set your hearts unto all the words which I testify among you this day, which ye shall command your children to observe to do, all the words of this law. For it is not a vain thing for you; because it is your life"
@endquote

Set your hearts. Then command your children. That is 6:6–7 in the last speech of the book. The witnesses who built family worship were refusing to let verse 7 become a Sunday-school subcontract, and refusing to let verse 6 remain a private warmth that never becomes talk.

The tradition also refused two corruptions. First: that a curriculum can replace a heart. A father who buys the plan and does not carry the words is decorating. Second: that a heart which never talks is obedience. Verse 6 without verse 7 is hoarding. The words were given to be sharpened on the next generation, not stored like a private treasure.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures | /lbcf/chapter-01.html
The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.
@endquote

*Obedience* is the clause that ties the confession to both verses. A rule that never gets into the heart cannot govern the house. A sufficient rule that never gets into a father's mouth cannot sharpen a son. You do not need a second secret word for your children if the commanded words are actually in you and actually talked when you sit and when you walk.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 22 — Of Religious Worship and the Sabbath Day | /lbcf/chapter-22.html
God is to be worshiped everywhere in spirit and in truth — as in private families daily, and in secret each one by himself, so more solemnly in the public assemblies, which are not carelessly nor wilfully to be neglected or forsaken, when God by his Word or providence calls thereunto.
@endquote

*Private families daily.* The confession heard verse 7. Family worship is not a Victorian extra. It is the ordinary place the commanded words become talk. Secret, family, assembly — three rooms, none optional. Verse 6 is the secret room. Verse 7 is the family room. Skip the assembly and you starve. Skip the family and you subcontract. Skip the heart and both rooms are theater.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 116 | /catechism.html
God requires parents to bring up their children in the instruction and discipline of the Lord: to teach them his Word diligently, to pray for them and with them, and to set before them a godly example — not trusting in their own labor, but in the grace of God to save.
@endquote

*Teach them his Word diligently* is *shanan* in catechism English. The rest of the answer keeps the father from turning the whetstone into a technique: pray with them, set the example, and do not trust your labor. Verse 6 is that last clause in advance. If the words are not in you, the labor is a show. If they are in you and you still will not talk, you are refusing the requirement.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Do not start with a family plan. Start with whether the words are in you.

Pick the Shema, or the verse you keep telling your sons they should know, and put it in your own mouth until it is in your heart. If you cannot say it without looking, you are not ready to *shanan* it. You are decorating. The test is not whether the house has Bibles. The test is whether, when you sit down and when you walk, the words that come out of you are the words that were commanded — or the words that happen to be in you instead.

Then name the last ordinary hour in which you talked those words to your sons. If you cannot name it, verse 7 has not started. Pick one stretch this week — table, truck, or bedtime — and whet one commanded text there every day. Not a lecture. An edge. Say it. Ask them what it means. Tell them the exodus when they ask why it matters.

If you cannot do verse 6, you will fake verse 7. If you can do verse 6 and still stay silent in the house, you are hoarding what was given to be sharpened on your children. A silent house is not the only failure. A talking house whose father does not carry the words is the same failure with more noise.

The danger named later in the chapter is fullness — houses you did not build, wells you did not dig. A full house with an empty heart is exactly the man this pair is aimed at. He can buy curriculum. He cannot buy a heart that already carries the words, and he cannot buy talk he will not do.

So keep them in that order. Shall be in thine heart. Then teach them diligently. Sit. Walk. Lie down. Rise. That is a whole day, not a scheduled slot. The Shema is not a slogan for the boys. It is a charge on you this day.
