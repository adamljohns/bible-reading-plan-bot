The verse on the gym wall is a prison verse about hunger and plenty. Strip the paragraph and it becomes a lie. Keep the paragraph and it becomes a man who can be empty without coming apart, because Christ is putting power into him.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

*I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.* Six words in English that have been asked to bless a thousand ambitions they were never written for.

The *all things* has already been defined in the two verses before it. I have learned to be content in whatever state. I know how to be abased and how to abound. I have been instructed to be full and to be hungry, to abound and to suffer need. *Then*: I can do all things. The all is the range he just named -- low and high, empty and full -- not a blank check for whatever you had in mind on a Friday.

And the power is not a technique. It is a Person, presently strengthening.

@word G2480
*Ischyo* is to be strong, to have force, to be able. It is ability, not wish. Paul is not saying he feels capable. He is saying he has strength for the thing in front of him. The present tense matches the next verb: the strengthening is current. This is not a memory of a strong season. It is Christ at work in a man who may, this week, be hungry.

@word G3956
*Pas* is all, every, the whole. It is a dangerous word in this verse because English hears unlimited. In the paragraph it is bounded by what Paul has just listed. All those states. All those lessons. The word still has weight -- nothing in the range is exempt; need does not get a pass, and plenty does not get a pass -- but it is not a slogan for omnipotence. A man quoting this at a goal God never assigned is using *pas* to cover a theft.

@word G1722
*En* is in, within, in the sphere of. The King James *through* and the WEB *through* are both trying to say how the ability arrives, and the Greek is locative before it is instrumental. You can do this *in* Christ, the one who strengthens. Not beside Him, using Him. In Him. The man still in his own name, hunting a power boost, is not in the verse.

@word G5547
*Christos* is the Anointed One. Paul does not say "through my mindset" or "through my training." He names the Messiah. The same Christ who emptied Himself in chapter 2 is the one putting strength into a prisoner in chapter 4. Union, again. The strength is not a detached force. It is His, shared with a man who belongs to Him.

@word G1743
*Endynamoo* is to put power into, to infuse with strength. Same family as *dynamis*. It is the verb of Philippians 4:13 and of Ephesians 6:10 -- be made strong in the Lord. Here it is a participle: the Christ *who is strengthening* me. Ongoing. If the strengthening stopped, the "I can" would stop. That is not a threat. It is the location of the boast.

@h3 The secret he learned
Verse 12 says he has been *instructed* -- initiated -- into the secret of plenty and of want. Contentment in this paragraph is not a temperament. It is a learned thing, and the teacher is named in verse 13. You do not stumble into this. You are schooled in it, often by the very abasement you wanted Christ to skip.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Philippians 4 has already given the peace that guards, the command to rejoice, the true, just, pure things to think on. Then a thank-you note. The church has sent a gift by Epaphroditus. Paul rejoices that they have revived their care. Then the refusal: not that I speak in respect of want. He is glad of the gift and will not be owned by the need.

So verse 13 is not a peak performance text. It is why he can receive money without groveling and go without it without collapsing. Verse 14: you did well to share my affliction. Verse 17: not that I seek the gift, but fruit to your account. Verse 18: I have all, I abound. Verse 19: my God shall supply every need of yours according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus.

The same Christ, the same supply. 4:13 is Paul's side of 4:19. He can do the hunger and the fullness because Christ strengthens him. They can face their needs because God supplies. Pull 4:13 out of that economy and you get a mascot. Leave it there and you get a church and an apostle, both depending, both giving, neither in charge of the tap.

He writes it in custody. The man who can do all things is not at liberty. That fact should have killed the gym-wall reading on contact.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed tradition heard this verse under the cross, not under triumph. Strength is what you invoke when you have already found you cannot support the weight.

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.8 | /institutes/b3-c08.html
Therefore, he visits us with disgrace, or poverty, or bereavement, or disease, or other afflictions. Feeling altogether unable to support them, we forthwith, in so far as regards ourselves, give way, and thus humbled learn to invoke his strength, which alone can enable us to bear up under a weight of affliction.
@endquote

*Alone* is the word. Paul's *I can* is not a discovery of inner resource. It is the end of that discovery. Affliction -- and plenty, which this paragraph also names -- schools a man to call on a strength that is not his. The holiest still need the lesson. Calvin will not let even David stay in the sentence *I shall never be moved.*

The tradition also refused the reading that makes contentment a denial of means. Paul receives the gift. He thanks them. God uses the Philippians. Strength in Christ is not a vow to need no one. It is the ability to need, and to not-need, without either one becoming your god.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 85 | /catechism.html
The tenth commandment requires full contentment with our own condition, with a right and charitable frame of spirit toward our neighbor, and all that is his.
@endquote

Philippians 4:11-13 is that answer in a man's life. Full contentment with our own condition -- abased or abounding. A right frame toward the neighbor: he will not envy, and he will not despise the church that finally had opportunity to give. The tenth commandment is not a small law. It is the law against the restlessness that makes 4:13 into a slogan for getting more.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 13 — Of Sanctification | /lbcf/chapter-13.html
Through the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ, the regenerate part does overcome.
@endquote

*Continual supply.* That is *strengtheneth* as a participle. Not a deposit you spend down. The confession will not let sanctification become a stored virtue. The same Christ, by His Spirit, keeps putting power in, and the man keeps standing -- in hunger, in plenty, in the thing he did not choose. Overcome, here, is not a trophy. It is not being emptied of Christ when the circumstances change.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Put the verse back on the right wall. It belongs over want, and over plenty, not over a goal list. If you are in need, the question is whether Christ is enough to keep you from sinning to fill the hole -- lying, quitting, raging, stealing the comfort you were not given. If you are in plenty, the question is whether Christ is enough to keep you from the stupider sins of fullness -- pride, numbness, the sense that you no longer have to ask.

Do not quote this at a calling God has not given. "I can do all things" is not "I can do that thing I want." Ability in Christ is for the assignment in front of you, including the humiliation you want to skip. Strength that will not be abased is not this strength.

And learn it the way he did. He says he learned. He says he was instructed. Contentment of this kind is not a switch. It is a schooling, usually in the state you would not have chosen, with a Christ who will not stop strengthening a man who stays in Him. Ask for that. Then do the next ordinary obedience in the state you are actually in -- hungry or full -- without waiting to feel like a verse on a wall.
