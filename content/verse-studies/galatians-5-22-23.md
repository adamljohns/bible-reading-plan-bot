Nine words, one fruit. Paul does not hand you a self-improvement list. He names what the Spirit grows in a man who has stopped trying to finish by the flesh.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

*But.* Against the works of the flesh just listed -- obvious, plural, damning. Then: the fruit of the Spirit *is* -- singular -- and a string of names. Love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance. Against such there is no law.

The grammar is doing doctrine. Works of the flesh are many and they scatter. Fruit is one crop with several flavors. You do not pick patience off a tree that has no love. You do not grow self-control as a private project while the rest of the cluster is absent. The Spirit produces a life, not a menu.

And it is *of the Spirit.* Not of grit, not of temperament, not of a better system. The contrast in the chapter is flesh and Spirit, not flesh and willpower.

@word G2590
*Karpos* is fruit on a plant, then the outcome of a life. Jesus used it for trees and for men: by their fruit you will know them. Paul puts it here so you will stop confusing production with manufacture. Fruit is grown. It is the issue of a root and a season. A man can fake a work. He cannot fake a harvest for long.

@word G26
*Agape* leads the list on purpose. The rest of the cluster grows in that soil. This is not romance and it is not preference. It is the love the cross commended, the love that can be commanded because it is a settled doing, not a mood. If the first name is missing, the other eight are being impersonated.

@word G3115
*Makrothumia* is long-temper: *makros* plus *thumos*. Not slow because you are weak. Slow because you have length. God is called this in Scripture. The Spirit grows in a man a share of that length -- the capacity to be wronged today without detonating, to stay in a hard marriage or a slow obedience without rewriting the story as permission to quit.

@word G1466
*Egkrateia* is in-strength: holding yourself inside a boundary. Temperance, self-control. It is the last name, and it is not a personality of tightness. It is power that has a governor. The flesh in this chapter is appetite without a bridle. The Spirit does not empty desire. He puts a hand on it. A man who cannot say no to himself is not yet walking in this fruit, whatever else he can quote.

@word G3551
*Nomos* is law. *Against such there is no law* is not antinomian swagger. It is the end of the argument that began in verse 18: if you are led by the Spirit you are not under the law. The law was never against love, joy, peace. It had to restrain the other list. Where the Spirit is producing this crop, the law is not your enemy because you are not doing the thing it condemns. You do not need a statute to keep a loving man from biting his neighbor. The bite was the other tree.

@h3 Why the list is not a scorecard
Read it as diagnosis, not as nine new laws. The flesh list in verses 19-21 is what a life looks like when the Spirit is not the root. This list is what a life looks like when He is. Measuring yourself against the nine as a project will grow pride or despair, which are not on the list. Looking at the nine to see whether the Spirit is actually at work -- that is what they are for.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Galatians has been a fight about whether Gentiles must take on the law to be finished. Chapter 5 opens: for freedom Christ set us free; do not go back under a yoke. Then the surprise: freedom is not fuel for the flesh. Through love, serve one another. The whole law is fulfilled in one word: love your neighbor.

Then the war: walk by the Spirit and you will not gratify the flesh. Flesh against Spirit, Spirit against flesh. If you are led by the Spirit you are not under the law. Then the two trees, obvious. Then our verses. Then: those who belong to Christ have crucified the flesh. If we live by the Spirit, let us also walk by the Spirit. Do not become conceited.

So 22-23 is not a poster for nice character. It is how free people actually live without returning to Sinai or to appetite. The fruit is the Spirit's answer to both errors in this letter: legalism that thinks the law can finish you, and license that thinks freedom means the flesh gets a vote.

Verse 21 is still in the room: those who *practice* the flesh list will not inherit the kingdom. Fruit is not optional decoration for people who like ethics. It is the public evidence of which tree you are.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed tradition read this cluster as sanctification, not as a natural harvest of a well-managed personality. Some men are even-tempered by birth. That is not *makrothumia* of the Spirit. The confession's interest is origin. If it is fruit, the tree is the Spirit; if you grew it to be seen, it was a work, and this chapter already told you about works.

Calvin would not let the Spirit's work erase the fact that the man still acts.

@quote Calvin, Institutes II.5 | /institutes/b2-c05.html
every thing good in the will is entirely derived from the influence of the Spirit, yet, because we have naturally an innate power of willing, we are not improperly said to do the things of which God claims for himself all the praise
@endquote

You really love. You really wait. You really hold yourself. And God gets the praise, because the goodness in the will came from Him. That is how fruit language works. The apple is on *your* branch. The life in the sap is not yours to boast of.

The tradition also heard *no law* as the law's true end, not its abolition. The law is not against such things because the law's target was the other list. Love is the fulfilling of the law, which this chapter already said. The Spirit does not produce lawless men. He produces men the law cannot charge.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 17 — Of the Perseverance of the Saints | /lbcf/chapter-17.html
He therefore continues to beget and nourish in them faith, repentance, love, joy, hope, and all the graces of the Spirit, unto immortality.
@endquote

*Beget and nourish.* Fruit language, in the chapter on perseverance. The graces are not a phase. He keeps growing them. Love and joy are named out loud, as in Paul's list. The same paragraph will admit that the felt sense of God's love may be clouded for a time. Fruit can be in season and still be weather-beaten. The confession will not let a dry month prove the tree was never planted.

@quote The Baptist Catechism, Q. 39 | /catechism.html
The benefits which in this life do accompany or flow from justification, adoption, and sanctification, are assurance of God's love, peace of conscience, joy in the Holy Spirit, increase of grace, and perseverance in it to the end.
@endquote

Love, peace, joy -- the first three names in Galatians, showing up as benefits that *flow from* justification, not as the price of it. Increase of grace is the rest of the cluster growing. Perseverance is the tree still standing. The catechism will not let you reverse the order. You are not fruit-bearing in order to be accepted. You are accepted, and this is what acceptance starts to look like in a body.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Do not turn this verse into a new Sinai with nicer vocabulary. You will not get more patient by staring at the word *patience.* Walk by the Spirit -- which in this letter means refuse the flesh's obvious deeds, stay in the Word, stay among the people you are tempted to bite, and ask for the Spirit you already have. Fruit grows where the root is not starved.

Then look at the first name and the last. Love, and self-control. Most men want peace and joy as a mood and will skip the two that cost. If your household cannot find the love, and you cannot say no to a screen, a bottle, a mouth, or a private sin, you do not need a more complicated reading. You need the war of verses 16-17, and the crucifixion of verse 24.

And take the singular seriously. Ask what *one* crop is showing, not whether you can check a box on a quiet day. A burst of kindness in public with bitterness at home is not this fruit. The Spirit grows a life. The test is the cluster, in the ordinary week, among the people who already know you.

Against such there is no law. If that is what is growing, you are not in a grey area. Keep walking. If it is not, the other list is still on the page, and it is not a metaphor.
