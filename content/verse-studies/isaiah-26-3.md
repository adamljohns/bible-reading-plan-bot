The Hebrew does not say "perfect peace." It says peace, peace. Then it names the only mind that gets that keeping, and the reason is not a technique.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

The verse is a promise about what God will do, not a method for what you will feel. *Thou wilt keep.* The subject is not the man. The man has a mind, and that mind is either stayed or it is not, but the keeping is God's work. If you reverse those, you will turn one of the quietest sentences in Isaiah into a self-help slogan, and it will fail the first week it is tested.

The King James margin is the honest gloss: *perfect: Heb. peace, peace.* Hebrew doubles the word when it wants the thing in full. Not a nicer peace. Peace said twice. The same doubled cry is what false prophets used when there was no peace at all, which is why this verse has to be read with its conditions still attached. Peace, peace -- *because he trusteth in thee.* Take the last clause off and you have the counterfeit.

@word H5341 | keep
*Natsar* is to watch or guard with the attention of a sentry -- not to store something on a shelf, but to keep it from harm. It is a watchman's verb. Isaiah has already given you the city in verse 1: salvation appointed for walls and bulwarks. This verse is what those walls do for the man inside them. God does not hand him a calm personality and walk away. He *keeps* him. The peace is as safe as the guard is awake.

That also means the promise is not "you will feel peaceful." A city can be kept while the man on the wall still hears the noise outside. The verb is about preservation, not about mood.

@word H7965 | peace
*Shalom* is wholeness -- complete well-being, not the mere stopping of noise. It comes from the idea of being complete. Which is why "perfect peace" is a translator's way of catching the doubled Hebrew: *shalom shalom.* The thing promised is not a quieter inner weather. It is a whole condition, kept by someone else.

Hold that against the rest of the chapter and the word stops being decorative. Verse 1 has a strong city. Verse 5 has a lofty city laid down to the dust. One city is kept. One is not. *Shalom* here belongs to the first. It is not a generic spiritual temperature. It is the condition of a people whose walls are salvation while another city is coming down.

@word H5564 | stayed
*Samak* is to lean, to rest weight, to lay a hand upon. The lexicon's ordinary picture is the hand laid on the sacrifice -- identification, transfer, the man putting his weight on what will stand for him. That is the verb under *stayed.* The mind in this verse is not concentrating hard. It is leaning.

Which is why later English that says "steadfast" is not wrong, but it can hide the physics. Steadfast can sound like a trait you have. *Samak* is a posture toward an object. The question is not how firm you feel. It is what you have put your weight on.

@word H982 | trusteth
*Batach* is the same trust-word as Proverbs 3:5: to lean your full weight on someone. Isaiah is about to say it again in the next verse, as a command: trust in the LORD forever. The because-clause in verse 3 is not a second, extra condition. It is the same lean, named as the reason the keeping happens. The mind stayed, and the man trusting, are not two different spiritual activities. They are one act described from the inside and from the fact.

The prophets spend a great deal of ink on *misplaced* batach -- in horses, in walls, in Egypt, in the city's own height. This chapter has just watched a lofty city go down. The trust that gets *shalom shalom* is not trust in the abstract. It is trust in the God who is about to be named the rock of ages.

@h3 The margin on "mind"
The King James also notes: *mind: or, thought, or, imagination.* The Hebrew word there is the inner framing of a man -- what he is forming and turning over -- not "mind" as a modern word for intellect as opposed to feeling. You do not need a lexicon page to take the margin seriously. What gets stayed is the thing you are *working out* in the dark: the plans, the rehearsals, the worst-case. That is what has to lean, or it will run.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Isaiah 24-27 is a long judgment that ends in songs. Chapter 24 levels the earth. Chapter 25 praises the God who swallows death. Chapter 26 is a song to be sung *in the land of Judah* -- not in a quiet room, in a land that has just watched cities fall.

Verse 1: we have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks. Verse 2: open the gates, that the righteous nation which keeps the truth may enter. Then our verse. Then verse 4, which is the verse's own application: trust in the LORD forever; for in the LORD JEHOVAH is everlasting strength -- the margin says *the rock of ages.* Then the other city: he brings down them that dwell on high; he lays it low, even to the dust.

So the peace is not the absence of upheaval. It is the condition of the man whose weight is on the God who is still a wall while walls are coming down. If you lift verse 3 out of that song and use it to promise a smooth week, you have quoted a line from a war-song and made it a lullaby.

The "in that day" of verse 1 also keeps the promise from shrinking to private technique. This is corporate before it is interior. A nation that keeps faith walks through opened gates; a mind stayed on God is kept in peace, peace. The two are the same salvation, seen at city-scale and at the scale of one man's thought-life. Neither works as a slogan detached from the God who saves.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading tied this verse to two doctrines people now prefer to keep apart: providence and faith. God *keeps.* The man *trusts.* If you make the peace the product of a mental habit, you have reversed the grammar. If you make the trust a feeling that must arrive before the keeping starts, you have made the sentry wait on the citizen. The tradition would not do either.

Calvin's line of argument in the Institutes is that a mind composed under God is not a mind that has arranged its circumstances. It is a mind that has resigned the course of its life to the Lord, and therefore does not have to call itself wretched when the accidents come. The peace is not the accident-rate dropping. It is the refusal to treat the accidents as the last word. That is much closer to Isaiah's song than the modern use of the verse as a breathing exercise.

They also refused to let "perfect peace" mean sinless calm or unbroken feeling. The confession will say in another place that the felt sense of God's light and love may be clouded for a time, and the man is still kept. Isaiah's own next lines are about waiting in the way of God's judgments, the soul's desire set on His name. Waiting is not the same as feeling settled. The stayed mind can be a waiting mind. What it cannot be is a mind that has put its weight back on the lofty city.

And they read the doubled *shalom* as fullness, not as intensity of emotion. Whole peace -- peace with God, and the well-being that follows from being kept -- not a mood you can measure on a given morning. Romans will later say that being justified by faith we have peace with God. Philippians will say the peace of God will *guard* the heart and the thoughts. Isaiah already had both halves: kept, and the thoughts stayed.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 14 — Of Saving Faith | /lbcf/chapter-14.html
By this faith a Christian believes to be true whatsoever is revealed in the Word, for the authority of God himself; and also apprehends an excellency therein above all other writings, and all things in the world; as it bears forth the glory of God in his attributes, the excellency of Christ in his nature and offices, and the power and fullness of the Holy Spirit in his workings and operations: and so is enabled to cast his soul upon the truth thus believed; and also acts differently upon that which each particular passage thereof contains; yielding obedience to the commands, trembling at the threats, and embracing the promises of God for this life, and that which is to come; but the principal acts of saving faith have immediate relation to Christ, accepting, receiving, and resting upon him alone, for justification, sanctification, and eternal life, by virtue of the covenant of grace.
@endquote

*Cast his soul upon* and *resting upon him alone* are this verse in confessional prose. *Samak* and *batach* are not a second kind of religion for anxious people. They are what saving faith does. The stayed mind is not a personality type. It is the soul put down on Christ, and left there.

The confession also keeps the order Isaiah keeps. Faith is enabled. Then it casts. The man does not first generate peace and then find he can trust. He is given a place to put his weight, and the keeping is God's.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 17 — Of the Perseverance of the Saints | /lbcf/chapter-17.html
Many storms and floods may rise and beat against them, but they shall never be torn from the foundation and rock to which faith has fastened them. The felt sense of God's light and love may, through unbelief and the temptations of Satan, be clouded and obscured from them for a time; yet he himself remains the same, and they shall surely be kept by the power of God unto salvation.
@endquote

Verse 4 is already waiting with the rock of ages. The confession and the prophet are using the same picture. Storms are granted. The felt sense may go dark. The keeping does not track the feeling. *Natsar* is a sentry, not a mood ring. If your peace has to remain conscious in order to count, you have not yet believed the verb.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Test the verse on a night when the thoughts will not stay. That is what the margin meant by imagination. The mind that needs this promise is not the mind already quiet. It is the mind that is framing outcomes at two in the morning -- replaying the conversation, building the worst case, leaning on a city that cannot hold. The command in the next verse is not "feel better." It is *trust ye in the LORD for ever.* Put the weight back. The peace is promised to that act, not to a temperament.

Then stop asking the promise to do what verse 5 is doing. Someone else's lofty city is being laid in the dust in this same song. Yours may be too. *Shalom shalom* is not a guarantee that the job holds, the diagnosis reverses, or the people who should have stayed do. It is a guarantee that the man whose mind is stayed on God is *kept* while those things happen. If you need the circumstances to quiet down before you will call this verse true, you are still trusting the lofty city.

Practice the lean in ordinary means, because that is how the confession says faith is increased: the Word, prayer, the table, the gathered people. A mind "stayed on thee" is not a mind emptied. It is a mind occupied with a particular God -- His name, His remembrance, as verse 8 says. If your attempt at peace requires you to stop thinking about Him and start managing your breathing as the main event, you have changed the object. The sentry is not your technique. The sentry is God.

And tell the truth about counterfeit peace. *Peace, peace* can be said over a dull conscience, a refused repentance, a refusal to walk through the open gates of verse 2. The righteous nation *keeps the truth* and then enters. A mind that will not be stayed on God because it is still negotiating with a sin is not a candidate for this keeping. It is a candidate for the other city. The verse is comfort. It is not cover.
