Four commands, no slogan. Watch. Stand. Act like men. Be strong. Verse 14 will add love so the first four do not become a brawl.

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Paul is closing the letter. Collections, travel plans, Timothy, Apollos — then this volley. It is not a new topic. It is what a chaotic church needs after fourteen chapters of gifts, factions, lawsuits, and a resurrection that some of them denied.

The KJV's *quit you like men* is the line the modern ear wants to sand off. Do not sand it. The Greek is not "be a generic adult." It is *andrizomai*.

@word G1127 | Watch
Watch is first because a man who is asleep cannot do the other three. This is a sentry word, not a vibe. A key eschatological word — the posture of disciples in light of Christ's return.

@word G4739 | stand fast
The object is named: *in the faith*. Not in your personality. Not in the party. In the faith this letter has just defended, including the resurrection in chapter 15.

@word G407 | quit you like men
WEB's *be courageous* is the fruit. KJV keeps the gendered verb Paul actually used. A church full of boys playing at gifts needed men. Old Testament background: Joshua's "be strong and courageous."

@word G2901 | be strong
Not gym talk. A command to take strength after three other commands have already named the post. A man who wants the last verb without the first three is asking for swagger, not strength.

Verse 14 follows immediately: "Let all that you do be done with love." The four commands are not a license to become a faction with better posture. They are how love stands up in a church that has been acting like children.

@section 2. Where It Sits

1 Corinthians 16 is logistics after theology. Chapter 15 has just made the resurrection non-negotiable. Chapter 16 collects money for saints, sketches Paul's route, warns that a great door is open and there are many adversaries (16:9), then tells them how to receive Timothy without contempt.

So 16:13 is not a random tattoo verse. It is the close-order drill for a church that has been immature in public. Watch — because adversaries are real. Stand in the faith — because some of them left the gospel for cleverness. Act like men — because they have been partisan and soft where they should have been steadfast. Be strong — because the work is not finished when the letter is.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed instinct is to keep the four together and to keep verse 14 attached. Courage without the faith is a Corinthian faction. Strength without love is the same faction with a louder voice. Watchfulness without standing is anxiety.

I am not hanging a verbatim Henry quote on this page this turn. The CCEL fetch for adjacent pages did not return a clean public-domain paragraph I could check word for word. Paul's own sequence is the witness: resurrection, then logistics, then this drill, then love.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 13 — Of Sanctification | /lbcf/chapter-13.html
They who are united to Christ, effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new spirit created in them, through the virtue of Christ's death and resurrection, are also farther sanctified, really and personally, through the same virtue, by his Word and Spirit dwelling in them; the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed, and the several lusts thereof are more and more weakened and mortified, and they more and more quickened and strengthened in all saving graces, to the practice of all true holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord.
@endquote

*Krataioo* belongs there. Strength is a sanctification command, not a personality upgrade. The faith you stand in is the faith once delivered, not a mood you work up before a fight.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Do the four in order this week. Do not start at "be strong."

Watch: name the actual threat — the hour, the feed, the conversation, the doctrine you have been too lazy to test. Stay awake there.

Stand fast in the faith: say the thing you know is true when the room wants a softer gospel. Not a new hill. The one 1 Corinthians 15 already named.

Quit you like men: stop outsourcing the hard sentence in your house. Timothy was not to be despised. Neither is the work under your roof.

Be strong: after the first three, take the next obedient action with your body. Strength that never becomes a deed is still swagger.

Then do verse 14, or you will only have built a louder version of Corinth.
