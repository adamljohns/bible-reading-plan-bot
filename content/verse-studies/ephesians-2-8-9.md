Verse 8 names the source. Verse 9 names the thing it kills. Read them as two sentences and you can still smuggle a little credit. Paul wrote them as one argument, and the last word is *boast.*

@section 1. The Text and Its Words

Verse 8: by grace, through faith, not of yourselves, the gift of God. Verse 9: not of works, so that no one would boast. The second sentence is not a footnote. It is why the first one had to be said that way.

*Saved* is already done. *Have been saved* -- a completed rescue with a standing result. Then the channels: grace as the cause, faith as the instrument. Then the locks: not of yourselves, it is the gift. Then the last lock, which is the one religious men still pick: not of works. Then the purpose clause, which is not a mood but a design: *lest any man should boast.*

If a man can still point at a contribution -- even a small one, even a spiritual one -- verse 9 has not yet done its work.

@word G5485
*Charis* is unmerited favor, and in Paul it is not a temperature. It is how God actually deals with the dead. Verse 5 already said it in parentheses: *by grace you have been saved.* Verses 8-9 pick that parenthesis up and nail it down. Grace here is not God meeting you halfway. It is God doing the whole distance for people who, three verses earlier, were corpses.

@word G4102
*Pistis* is trust, reliance, firm conviction -- the one verb in the sentence a man actually does. You believe. Paul knows that, which is why he will not let faith become the thing you contributed. *Through* faith, not because of faith as a merit. Faith is the open hand, not the payment. A hand that brags about being open has already closed.

@word G1435
*Doron* is a gift, and in ordinary Greek it is often a gift brought *to* a god. Paul turns the direction around. The word for what men carry to an altar is used for what God hands to men. The whole arrangement -- grace, faith, the being-saved -- is *the gift of God.* That is the clause verse 9 is protecting. If any piece of it originated in you, it was not this kind of gift.

@word G2041
*Ergon* is work, deed, what is done. Verse 9 does not say works are evil. It says they are not the root of this rescue. The next verse will require them as fruit. Pull verse 9 out of the paragraph and you get cheap grace. Pull verse 10 out and you get a man trying to finish what grace started. Paul wrote both, in that order, on purpose.

@word G2744
*Kauchaomai* is to boast, to glory, to take pride in. This is the target. The gospel, in these two verses, is engineered so that boasting dies. Not because boasting is tacky. Because it is a lie about where the rescue came from. A man who is standing because he was raised does not get to talk as if he climbed.

@h3 What "that not of yourselves" is doing
The pronoun is neuter; *grace* and *faith* are feminine. The usual and the safest reading is that Paul is pointing at the whole package, not picking one noun. Which is more severe, not less. If the entire salvation-by-grace-through-faith is not of yourselves, then the last remaining candidate for your contribution is inside the thing being denied. Verse 9 then names the most common candidate out loud: works.

@section 2. Where It Sits

Ephesians 2 does not begin with an invitation. It begins with a coroner's report. You were dead in trespasses and sins, walking according to this world, by nature children of wrath. Then verse 4: *But God, being rich in mercy.* He made us alive together with Christ, raised us, seated us. Verse 7 says why: so that in the ages to come He might show the exceeding riches of His grace.

Verses 8-9 are the explanation of that whole movement. *For* by grace you have been saved. The *for* reaches back to the mercy, the making-alive, the showing of riches. A dead man does not cooperate with his resurrection. That is why it cannot be of yourselves, and why it cannot be of works. Paul is not being austere. He is being consistent with verse 1.

Then verse 10, which is not a softening. *We are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand that we should walk in them.* Works are excluded as the cause and required as the path. The same God who forbids boasting in verse 9 has already prepared the next obedience. A man who uses 8-9 to idle has not read the paragraph. A man who uses 10 to climb back into credit has not read 8-9.

@section 3. What the Witnesses Saw

This is the passage the Reformation was willing to split the church over, and the concern was pastoral before it was institutional. If any part of the rescue rests on the believer's contribution, assurance becomes a weather report. You can never know if you did enough. Paul closes that door so a man can sleep.

Calvin heard verse 9 as the silencing of the flesh.

@quote Calvin, Institutes III.13 | /institutes/b3-c13.html
that they may bury in profound silence all arrogance of the flesh
@endquote

He has just quoted these very verses. The purpose of being saved this way is that God gets the name, and the believer's mouth shuts. That is not anti-joy. Boasting was never joy. It was a man trying to share the credit with the one who raised him.

The tradition also refused to let "not of works" become hostility to obedience. Faith is never alone in the person justified. It is alone as the instrument of standing. Verse 9 forbids the one; verse 10 requires the other. The confession learned to say both in the same breath.

@section 4. Confessional Anchor

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 11 — Of Justification | /lbcf/chapter-11.html
Those whom God effectually calls, he also freely justifies; not by infusing righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting and accepting their persons as righteous; not for anything wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ's sake alone; not by imputing faith itself, the act of believing, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their righteousness; but by imputing Christ's active obedience to the whole law, and passive obedience in his death for their whole and sole righteousness by faith, which faith they have not of themselves; it is the gift of God.
@endquote

The last clause is Paul, almost word for word. The confession will not even let *faith itself* be counted as the righteousness. That is verse 8 and verse 9 in one sentence: through faith, and not of the act of believing as a work, and not of any other evangelical obedience. *Gift of God* is not decoration. It is the mechanism.

@quote 1689 LBCF, ch. 16 — Of Good Works | /lbcf/chapter-16.html
We cannot by our best works merit pardon of sin or eternal life at the hand of God, by reason of the great disproportion that is between them and the glory to come, and the infinite distance that is between us and God, whom by them we can neither profit nor satisfy for the debt of our former sins; but when we have done all we can, we have done but our duty, and are unprofitable servants.
@endquote

That is verse 9 in confessional English. Best works. Not shabby ones. Even the good ones cannot buy the thing. *Unprofitable servants* is what a man says when boasting has died and work has not. You still labor. You do not invoice God for it.

@section 5. For the Man Reading This

Watch what happens to your standing after a bad month. If a poor stretch makes you feel less saved, you have moved the foundation from gift to output, and verse 8's tense is the correction: *have been saved.* Done. Still true. The same test runs the other way. If a strong stretch makes you feel more saved, verse 9 has found you. No one would boast -- including the man who is currently performing well.

There is a quieter version, and it catches serious men. Not boasting in deeds, but in *faith* -- a private sense that you saw it, you responded, you were the one who got it. Paul shut that door in verse 8 before he named works in verse 9. The gift includes the open hand.

Then get up and work, because verse 10 is still there. The man who knows the house was given to him is free to maintain it without sending the owner a bill. Boasting dies. The prepared works do not. That is the whole paragraph, and it will hold a life if you will let both verses speak at once.
