How Our Deep Studies Are Built

What goes on the page, where each fact comes from, and what we won’t print.

A deep study takes one verse and works through it in six parts: the text and its words, where the verse sits in its book, what the church’s teachers saw in it, what our confession says about it, what it asks of the man reading it, and where to go next. Each study runs past 1,200 words and is drafted with AI assistance, and a page that long can be wrong in ways you’d never catch by reading it, since an invented quotation reads as smoothly as a true one. So we built the studies on one rule and wrote a program to enforce it: every fact that can be checked comes from a source we host on this site, and whatever we can’t check stays out of quotation marks. The Bereans “searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so” (Acts 17:11, KJV), and we want you to be able to do the same with us.

Why the page prints the KJV and the WEB

Every study prints its verse twice, once in the King James Version and once in the World English Bible. Both are in the public domain, so we can quote as much of them as a study needs, and you can copy the page, print it for your men’s group or read it aloud without asking anyone’s permission. There is nothing on a study page that we can’t reproduce in full. The KJV is also the text our Strong’s numbers are tied to, which lets a study point from an English word straight to the Hebrew or Greek behind it, and the WEB sets the same verse beside it in today’s English.

Why the NKJV and the ESV aren’t printed

We read the NKJV, the ESV, the NASB, the CSB, the NET and the NIV when we work on a verse, and comparing them often shows what a Hebrew or Greek word is doing. Those translations are under copyright, so we read them for reference and we don’t print them in a study.

For reading in modern English, use our Bible reader, the Bible Translation Engine, which sets the English beside its Hebrew and Greek roots. The reader is also where our own translation is taking shape. The MBT, the MOOP Bible Translation, is an original rendering built from public-domain sources, the 1769 King James with its Strong’s numbers and the World English Bible. It’s a working manuscript that is being written chapter by chapter, and where a verse has been finished the reader gives the MBT as its main text, as it does at Proverbs 3:5–6. Every study links to its own verse and chapter in the reader under “Go Deeper.”

The word study

The first section of a study names the Hebrew or Greek words that carry the verse. We don’t pick those from memory. A script pulls every Strong’s number in the verse from the Strong’s-tagged King James text, and it looks each one up in our own Greek and Hebrew Lexicon for the original script, the transliteration, the part of speech and the definition. A word in a study is a word that is really in the verse, and its Strong’s number opens a lexicon page you can read for yourself. Some of our lexicon entries aren’t finished yet, and a study leaves those words out instead of guessing at them, because the checker rejects any page that cites an unfinished entry.

The confessional anchor

Every study has a section that ties the verse to what we confess, because a verse study that floats free of the church’s confession tends to end up wherever its writer already wanted to go. The anchor is the 1689 London Baptist Confession of Faith, with the Baptist Catechism and John Calvin’s Institutes of the Christian Religion beside it. All three are hosted on this site, and a study quotes them from the hosted text and links the chapter, so you can read the paragraph where it stands and judge whether we used it fairly.

The quotation rule

Every quotation in a study carries its attribution, and before a study can be published an automated gate checks each quotation word for word against a source file: the Scripture text, the confession, the catechism, the Institutes, or an edition of another writer that we have opened and read the sentence in. A study that fails the check doesn’t ship. When we can’t verify the wording of a church father, a Puritan or a commentator, we describe his argument, name the work, and leave the quotation marks off. The Genesis 1:1 study does that with Augustine. We don’t invent quotations, and the gate is there so that a draft can’t invent one for us.

The same gate checks that the verse printed on the page matches the KJV or the WEB, that every Strong’s number resolves to a finished lexicon entry, that all six sections are present, and that every link on the page goes somewhere.

Drafts and review

New studies are listed on the verse studies index with a yellow Draft study label, and the page itself carries a note at the bottom that says “Draft Study” and “Not yet reviewed by Adam.” A draft is open for you to read, but it is kept out of search engines until Adam has reviewed it. Once he approves a study the note comes off, and the index lists it as a “Deep study.”

Narration and follow-along

We are adding narration to the studies one at a time. Where a study has it, a player sits at the top of the page, and as the study is read aloud the words light up so you can follow along, with speed buttons if you’d like it faster. The voice is a computer voice that runs on our own machine, and Scripture is read in a second voice so that you can hear where the Bible is speaking and where we are.

If you find a mistake in a study, tell us, and we’ll check it against the source and fix it.
