Verse Study · Deuteronomy

Deuteronomy 32:35

The verse everyone quotes to stop a fight is first a sentence about God keeping a ledger. The foot slides on His clock, not yours.

Vengeance is mine, and recompense, at the time when their foot slides; for the day of their calamity is at hand. Their doom rushes at them.”— World English Bible (public domain)

To me belongeth vengeance, and recompence; their foot shall slide in due time: for the day of their calamity is at hand, and the things that shall come upon them make haste.— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The first clause is a claim of ownership. Vengeance is not a mood Israel is invited to share. It belongs to Someone. Recompense is paired with it, so the sentence is not only about anger. It is about a return that matches the deed.

Then the verse turns from the Owner to the timetable. Their foot shall slide in due time. The day of their calamity is at hand. The things that shall come upon them make haste. Three clocks, one Owner. The man who wants to settle the score today is arguing with the third clause.

The first Hebrew word names the thing that belongs to God. The rest of the vocabulary tells you when and how He pays.

  • שִׁלֵּם Shillem · “recompence” · Strong’s H8005

    The Hebrew noun shillem refers to a requital or recompense — the idea that a deed or action brings back a return, either reward or retribution. It is related to the verb shalam, to repay or make whole.

    Shillem is requital — a deed coming back as return, reward or retribution. It is kin to shalam, to repay or make whole. So the verse is not licensing a private vendetta. It is saying God will close the account. What was done will not stay unpaid. The New Testament cites this line twice for that reason: Romans 12:19 to stop your hand, Hebrews 10:30 to warn the apostate that the same God still pays.

  • מוֹט Mot · “shall slide” · Strong’s H4131

    The Hebrew verb mot (מוֹט) means to totter, shake, slip, or fall — describing instability and the failure of that which was meant to stand firm. It appears frequently in the Psalms in both negative declarations about the righteous (they will NOT totter) and in descriptions of the wicked's eventual fall.

    Mot is to totter, shake, slip, or fall — the failure of what was meant to stand. The Psalms love the negative: the righteous shall not be moved. Here the verb is positive and terrible. The foot that looked planted will go. The slide is not an accident on a wet stone. It is the moment the standing runs out.

  • עֵת 'Et · “in due time” · Strong’s H6256

    The Hebrew noun 'et denotes time as a specific moment or season — not mere chronological time (for which Hebrew uses other terms) but kairos-like appointed time: the right moment, the opportune hour, or a season characterized by particular events. Ecclesiastes 3 catalogues twenty-eight seasons of human experience using this word.

    'Et is not clock-time. It is appointed time — the right moment, the season God has named. Ecclesiastes uses it for every human season. Here it is the hour God has stored. The injured man wants now. The verse answers with when. Those are different words, and only one of them is holy.

  • אֵיד Eyd · “calamity” · Strong’s H343

    The Hebrew word eyd refers to disaster, calamity, or sudden ruin. It describes a day of misfortune, catastrophe, or divine judgment.

    Eyd is disaster, sudden ruin, a day of misfortune that does not reverse. Proverbs laughs at it when the fool who refused wisdom finally meets it. Job names it when the hedge comes down. Here it is a day, and it is theirs. Not a random crash. Their calamity. The verse will not let you treat judgment as weather.

  • חוּשׁ Chuwsh · “make haste” · Strong’s H2363

    The Hebrew chuwsh means to hasten or move with urgency. It appears in poetic contexts where the psalmist pleads with God to act quickly on his behalf.

    Chuwsh is to hasten, to move with urgency. The psalmist uses it when he begs God to hurry. Here the haste belongs to the doom, not to the prayer. From the ground it can look slow. The verse says the things coming are already making haste. Delay is not cancellation. It is the gap in which a man still has time to stop playing God.

2. Where It Sits

This is the Song of Moses, not a proverb for a quarrel. Moses is about to die. He calls heaven and earth to hear. The Rock’s work is perfect; all His ways are judgment. Then the song tells the story of a people found in a waste, kept as the apple of the eye, who grew fat and kicked. Jeshurun forsook the God who made him. They sacrificed to new gods their fathers never feared.

So verse 35 is not first a word to a Christian with a difficult neighbor. It is God speaking inside a covenant lawsuit. The treasures of verse 34 are stored wrath: Is not this laid up in store with me, and sealed up among my treasures? Verse 35 is the breaking of the seal. Verse 36 follows at once: the LORD shall judge His people and repent Himself for His servants when their power is gone. Judgment and compassion in the same breath. The song will not let you have one without the other.

The “their” in their foot is the enemy who will misread Israel’s collapse as his own high hand (32:27), and it is also the faithless generation the song has been indicting. Hebrews hears the second use. Paul hears the first. Both citations are in the verse. A study that only comforts the offended man has cut the song in half.

What comes after is the unmaking of false refuge. Where are their gods, the rock in whom they trusted? Let them rise and help you. Then the claim that sits under every clause of verse 35: I, even I, am he, and there is no god with me: I kill, and I make alive; I wound, and I heal; neither is there any that can deliver out of my hand. Vengeance belongs to Him because there is no other court.

Romans 12:19 takes the line into the church’s ordinary life. Do not avenge yourselves. Give place to wrath. It is written. Hebrews 10:30 takes the same line as a warning to those who go on sinning after receiving the knowledge of the truth. The verse is therefore both a bridle and a threat. Soften either edge and you have not read Deuteronomy 32.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin will not let the church turn the command against revenge into optional advice for the unusually spiritual. The Schoolmen had done that: made “love your enemies” a counsel for monks, not a law for Christians. He answers with this verse, and he answers in the present tense.

Is not the word of the Lord eternally true: “Vengeance is mine, I will repay?”

Calvin, Institutes II.8

Let them either erase these passages from the Law, or let them acknowledge the Lord as a Lawgiver, not falsely feign him to be merely a counsellor.

Calvin, Institutes II.8

That is the Reformed reading in two strokes. The word is eternally true. And it is law, not a suggestion for men who feel like being patient. The man who keeps a private ledger is not merely unwise. He is sitting in a seat that is already occupied.

The tradition also refused to confuse this with anarchism. The same God who forbids private revenge arms the magistrate with a sword. Civil punishment is not a man taking verse 35 into his own mouth. It is one of the means by which the Owner of vengeance governs a violent world. Mix those and you get either a church that never calls the sheriff or a man who baptizes his temper as justice.

Calvin’s other line in the same chapter is the practical one: you shall not avenge, nor bear any grudge. The song and Leviticus 19:18 are the same God speaking. Stored wrath in heaven is not a license for stored wrath in your chest.

4. Confessional Anchor

God has appointed a day in which he will judge the world in righteousness by Jesus Christ, to whom all power and judgment is given of the Father.

1689 LBCF, ch. 32 — Of the Last Judgment

That is Deuteronomy 32:35 stretched to the end of the world. Appointed day. Not your day. Righteousness, not your scorecard. The Father has given the judgment to the Son. The song’s “I, even I” is not cancelled in the New Testament. It is located: the tribunal of Christ.

As Christ would have us to be certainly persuaded that there shall be a day of judgment, both to deter all men from sin and for the greater consolation of the godly in their adversity, so will he have the day unknown to men, that they may shake off all carnal security and be always watchful

1689 LBCF, ch. 32 — Of the Last Judgment

Two uses, named. Deterrence, and consolation in adversity. Verse 35 does both. The man about to strike is deterred. The man who cannot get justice this week is consoled — not by the lie that nothing happened, but by the fact that the day is appointed and the foot will slide.

God, the supreme Lord and King of all the world, has ordained civil magistrates to be under him, over the people, for his own glory and the public good. To this end he has armed them with the power of the sword, for the defense and encouragement of those who do good, and for the punishment of evildoers.

1689 LBCF, ch. 24 — Of the Civil Magistrate

The confession will not let “vengeance is mine” mean “no sword on earth.” The sword is under Him. It is not yours to pick up in the driveway. If you are the magistrate, you are a servant of a wrath that is not your personal heat. If you are not, you give place.

Every sin deserves God’s wrath and curse, both in this life and that which is to come.

The Baptist Catechism, Q. 89

That is why the recompense is not optional and not late in the sense that matters. Sin earns. God pays. The catechism will not let you treat the other man’s wrong as a small thing God might overlook if you are nice enough to drop it. You drop your hand because He does not drop the account.

5. For the Man Reading This

The first obedience is with your mouth. Stop saying you will make it right. You will not. You cannot get the scale even, and if you could you would still be sitting in God’s chair. Name the theft, the slight, the betrayal. Then say the sentence: vengeance is His. Recompense is His. That is not passivity. It is jurisdiction.

The second obedience is with the clock. In due time will offend you. The foot has not slid yet. The man still sleeps well. The verse told you the doom is making haste while your eyes say it is not. Believe the verse. Do not help the slide. The appointed time is not a slot you get to fill.

The third obedience is the distinction the confession drew. Call the magistrate when a crime has been done. That is not revenge. That is using the sword God ordained. What you may not do is the extra: the text, the smear, the slow bleed at work, the grudge you rehearse on the drive. Leviticus already named that as bearing a grudge. Calvin was right. It is law.

And take the warning as well as the comfort. Hebrews did not quote this verse to help you sleep after a bad meeting. It quoted it to people in the church who were treating the blood of the covenant as a common thing. If you are the man whose foot should slide — if you have used power, or a mouth, or a hidden life, and you are still standing — the haste in the last clause is aimed at you. Today is still 'et in the other direction: time to come off the rock that will not hold.

The song ends with atonement for the land and the people (32:43). The same God who will not share vengeance also makes atonement. That is the only safe place to stand while you wait. Not your fairness. His.
