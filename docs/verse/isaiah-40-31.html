Verse Study · Isaiah

Isaiah 40:31

Everyone frames the eagles. The verse starts with a but, and the strength is promised to people who were already fainting.

But those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.— World English Bible (public domain)

But they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint. renew: Heb. change— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The sentence does not begin with flight. It begins with a contrast. Verse 30 has just said that even the youths faint and the young men utterly fall. But they that wait upon the LORD. The promise is not for the naturally vigorous. It is for the people who have already watched native strength fail, including the kind that usually does not.

The King James margin on renew is the other thing posters leave off: Heb. change. The verb is not “feel better.” It is closer to a change of clothes — the old strength taken off, another put on. If you come to this verse hoping to have your own power topped up, you have misread the warehouse. The faint are given a different strength, and they get it by waiting, not by trying harder at the same thing that already failed.

  • קָוָה qavah · “wait” · Strong’s H6960

    A Hebrew verb meaning to wait for, look eagerly for, hope, expect. The root carries the physical image of twisting or stretching a cord taut — like a rope under tension.

    Qavah means to wait for, to look eagerly for, to hope — and the root-picture is a cord pulled taut. Biblical waiting is not sitting in a chair until a mood arrives. It is tension toward an object. A rope does not slack itself and call that faith. It stays stretched to the thing it is tied to.

    That is why “wait upon the LORD” cannot be replaced with “stay positive” or with a vague spiritual patience. The object is named. You can wait in the wrong direction at full intensity and still be the youths in verse 30. The tautness is not the virtue. The LORD is.

  • חָלַף chalaph · “renew” · Strong’s H2498

    To pass through, pass on, change, or renew. This versatile verb describes both the passing away of what is temporary and the renewal of what endures.

    Chalaph is to pass on, to change, to renew. The lexicon is honest about the range: things wear out and pass through, and this same verb is the one Isaiah uses for the exchange of strength. The margin’s change is not a curiosity. It is the meaning. They shall change their strength. Weakness is not improved. It is replaced.

    Which is the only reading that survives verse 29. He gives power to the faint. He increases strength in people who have no might. There is nothing there to renovate. The transaction is gift, and the waiting is the open hand, not the work that earns the gift.

  • כֹּחַ koach · “strength” · Strong’s H3581

    Hebrew word for strength or power, about 130 times. Describes physical strength, capacity, or resources.

    Koach is strength as capacity — physical power, resources, the ability to do. Isaiah has already used it in this paragraph: God gives koach to the weary. Human koach is the thing verse 30 watches collapse in people who should have had plenty of it. Divine koach is the thing verse 29 says is given. Verse 31 is not a third kind. It is the second kind, received by waiting.

    Do not let the eagle image smuggle in a different definition. The strength here is not a personality. It is power for a people who had none.

  • נֶשֶׁר nesher · “eagles” · Strong’s H5404

    Nesher refers to the largest birds of prey — eagles and/or griffon vultures — and appears about 26 times in the OT. This majestic bird serves as one of Scripture's most powerful symbols of God's strength, care, and swiftness.

    Nesher is the great bird of prey — eagle, or the griffon vulture that rides the high air. Scripture uses it for speed, for height, and for the way God carried Israel: I bare you on eagles' wings. The bird is not working the way a sprinter works. It is using what is already under it. That is the point of putting nesher in a paragraph about people who have no might. The mounting up is not a display of native vigor. It is what happens when the exhausted are held in a strength that is not theirs.

    If you preach this as “you were made to soar,” you have complimented the young men who just utterly fell. The verse is not about your latent potential. It is about a borrowed sky.

  • יָגַע yaga' · “weary” · Strong’s H3021

    Yaga' (יָגַע) means to toil, labor strenuously, or become weary from exertion. It appears about 26 times in the Old Testament and conveys both physical exhaustion and the labor that leads to it.

    Yaga' is to toil until you are spent — the labor and the exhaustion together. Verse 28 has already used it of God, in the negative: the everlasting God, the LORD, the Creator of the ends of the earth, fainteth not, neither is weary. The same word-family runs through the paragraph on purpose. God does not yaga'. Youths do. Waiters will run and not yaga'. The contrast is the doctrine. Human toil bottoms out. God’s does not. The people tied to Him by qavah are not promised a lighter load so much as a strength that does not originate in the load-bearer.

2. Where It Sits

Isaiah 40 opens the long comfort that follows the judgment chapters. Comfort ye, comfort ye my people. The grass withers, the flower fades, the people are grass. Nations are a drop in a bucket. Jacob, in exile-hearing range, has a specific complaint, and verse 27 writes it out: my way is hid from the LORD, and my judgment is passed over from my God. That is the sentence verse 31 is answering. Not a poster. A protest.

The protest is: God has lost track of me. The answer is not “try soaring.” It is: have you not known? Have you not heard? The everlasting God does not faint. He gives power to the faint. Even youths fall. But they that wait.

So the eagles sit inside an argument about whether the Creator has overlooked a beaten people. If you lift verse 31 into a gym or a graduation, you can still say true sentences with it, and you will have moved it off the problem it was written to meet. The first readers were not looking for extra energy. They were wondering whether their case had been filed away.

Notice the order of the last three verbs: mount up, run, walk. Flight, then the race, then the ordinary going. The text does not rank them as better and worse days of the Christian life, though a man will have all three. It stacks promised continuance. They shall. They shall. They shall. The waiting people do not get one dramatic lift and then a return to verse 30. They get a life that can take height, pace, and the long road without the collapse that is otherwise certain.

Chapter 41 will open with the coastlands told to renew their strength and come to court. The phrase is still in the air. Strength in this stretch of Isaiah is a legal and creational claim before it is an athletic one. Who has power, and who is tired, and whose word stands.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading heard this as a text about God’s self-sufficiency before it was a text about human resilience. God fainteth not. That is the engine. The waiter’s new strength is derivative, the way heat in a room is derivative of a fire you did not light. If you start with the eagle, you will preach athletics. If you start with verse 28, you will preach God, and then the eagle makes sense as a picture of people who are being carried.

They also refused to turn waiting into idleness. Qavah as taut cord is closer to prayer, hearing, and the ordinary means than to resignation. Calvin’s larger argument about providence is that creatures do not keep themselves, and that leaning on the Word is how the godly acknowledge not only a Creator but a present Governor. Waiting on the LORD is that lean, held over time. It is not a refusal to run. Verse 31 still has them running. It is a refusal to run on the strength verse 30 already watched fail.

The tradition likewise would not let “renew” mean the recovery of your old self. Change of strength is conversion-shaped even when the verse is being spoken to people already named Jacob and Israel. You do not wait in order to become a more impressive version of the young man who fell. You wait in order to be given what you did not have. The faint are the correct applicants. The capable are the ones the paragraph goes out of its way to humble.

And they kept the verse in the complaint of verse 27. Hidden way, passed-over judgment: that is unbelief wearing the clothes of lament. The answer is a question — have you not heard? — which means the waiting is not empty time. It is staying under a word already spoken. People who will not be told that God does not faint cannot be told that they will not faint. The second promise rides on the first doctrine.

4. Confessional Anchor

The Lord our God is but one only living and true God; whose subsistence is in and of himself, infinite in being and perfection; whose essence cannot be comprehended by any but himself; a most pure spirit, invisible, without body, parts, or passions, who only has immortality, dwelling in the light which no man can approach unto; who is immutable, immense, eternal, incomprehensible, almighty, every way infinite, most holy, most wise, most free, most absolute, working all things according to the counsel of his own immutable and most righteous will for his own glory.

1689 LBCF, ch. 2 — Of God and of the Holy Trinity

Who only has immortality and almighty are verse 28 in the confession’s voice. He fainteth not, neither is weary. There is no searching of His understanding. The waiter is not plugging into a force. He is tied to this God. If God were a creature with parts and passions, the taut cord would be tied to another fainting thing, and verse 30 would still be the last word.

The grace of faith, whereby the elect are enabled to believe to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of baptism and the Lord’s Supper, prayer, and other means appointed of God, it is increased and strengthened.

1689 LBCF, ch. 14 — Of Saving Faith

Increased and strengthened is the church’s ordinary form of Isaiah 40:31. Waiting on the LORD is not a private sport. Faith is wrought and then fed by the means God appointed. The people who want eagle-wings without Word, table, prayer, and the rest are asking for chalaph without the warehouse God actually uses. The confession is not embarrassed by that ordinariness. Isaiah is not either. “Hast thou not heard?” comes before “they shall mount up.”

5. For the Man Reading This

Start where Jacob started, if that is where you are. My way is hid. That sentence feels like realism when you are tired. Isaiah treats it as a sentence that needs to be unlearned. The Creator of the ends of the earth has not misplaced your file. Waiting begins when you stop treating your exhaustion as evidence that God is tired too.

Then wait in the taut sense, not the limp one. Put the cord on a named God and keep it there: Scripture in the mouth, prayer that asks, a people who will not let you disappear. If your “waiting” is only delay — not doing the next duty until you feel mounted up — you have changed the verb. The verse still has running and walking in it. Strength is for going, not for spectating your own renewal.

Take the margin into the week you are actually in. Change of strength. You may not get your old pace back. You may get a different power for a different mile: less display, more continuance. Youths fall. Walkers who do not faint are the miracle the text is willing to end on. Do not despise the walk as if God only keeps His word when you are airborne.

And do not use this on other people as a scold. The faint are the ones He gives power to. If a man is spent, the next true sentence is verse 29, not a lecture about eagles. Tell him God is not weary. Then wait with him. The cord is easier to keep taut when another man is holding the same line.
