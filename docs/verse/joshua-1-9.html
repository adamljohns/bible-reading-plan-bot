Verse Study · Joshua

Joshua 1:9

Courage here is not a mood you work up. It is an order, issued because the Lord is already going with the man.

Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.”— World English Bible (public domain)

Have not I commanded thee? Be strong and of a good courage; be not afraid, neither be thou dismayed: for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The sentence starts with a question, and the question is not looking for information. Have not I commanded you? God is not asking Joshua how he feels about the river. He is reminding him that the order has already been given, three times in this chapter, and that fear is now disobedience.

The courage is not a personality. It is paired with strength, then fenced by two refusals — do not be afraid, do not be dismayed — and then grounded in a location: the LORD your God is with you wherever you go. The wherever is the load-bearing clause. The land is still held by fortified cities. The promise is not that the cities are imaginary. It is that God will be in them before Joshua is.

  • צָוָה Tsavah · “commanded” · Strong’s H6680

    Tsavah means to command, order, charge, or appoint. It appears about 495 times.

    The verse does not begin with a pep talk. It begins with tsavah — command, charge, appoint. The same verb that grounds the Torah: the LORD commanded Moses. Joshua’s nerve is not being recruited. It is being placed under an order that already exists.

  • חָזַק chazaq · “Be strong” · Strong’s H2388

    A primitive root, chazaq means to be or grow strong, firm, or courageous. It can also be used causatively, meaning to strengthen, uphold, or repair.

    Chazaq is to be or grow firm, to seize, to hold fast. God tells leaders to be strong because they will not feel strong. The strength is not self-derived. It is the grip of a man who has been told where to stand.

  • אָמַץ Amats · “courageous” · Strong’s H553

    The Hebrew verb amats means to be strong, to prevail, to make firm, or to be courageous. It is used both of physical strength and moral-spiritual courage, frequently in divine commissioning speeches where God commands His servants to take courage in the face of overwhelming challenge.

    Amats is the pair to chazaq in this chapter — be strong and courageous, four times. This is not psychological self-confidence. It is a moral category: active obedience in the face of fear, grounded in the word just given.

  • חָתַת chātat · “dismayed” · Strong’s H2865

    A primitive root meaning to be broken in pieces, dismayed, terrified, or to terrify. The word describes the collapse of courage under overwhelming threat or divine judgment.

    Chatat is to be shattered, broken in pieces, terrified. The command do not be dismayed is not a denial of real danger. It is the refusal to let the danger finish its work on the man. The antidote is not bravado. It is the last clause.

2. Where It Sits

Moses is dead. That is the first sentence of the book. Chapter 1 is God filling the vacancy. Three times Joshua is told to be strong and courageous — verse 6, verse 7, verse 9 — and the repetition is itself a diagnosis. The man is frightened, and God does not pretend otherwise.

Verse 8 sits inside the same speech: keep the book in your mouth, meditate day and night, do all that is written. Verse 9 is not a second speech. It is the same commissioning with the fear named out loud. The book and the courage are one instruction. The man is not told to summon nerve from his gut. He is told that the Lord who commanded him will be with him on the ground.

What follows is the crossing, the stones, Jericho. The promise is tested immediately. If verse 9 were a feeling, it would not survive the first wall. It is a fact about God’s location, issued before the first step.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin will not let a man treat courage as self-direction. The safe place is not inside your own will. It is behind the Lord who is already going.

For as the surest source of destruction to men is to obey themselves, so the only haven of safety is to have no other will, no other wisdom, than to follow the Lord wherever he leads.

Calvin, Institutes III.7

That is verse 9 from the other side. Joshua is not told to invent a path and then ask God to bless it. He is told to go where he was sent, and that the Lord is already there. The haven is not a quieted stomach. It is a followed command.

The Reformed reading of this verse has always refused two corruptions. First: that courage is a temperament some men have and others do not. The imperative makes it a duty. Second: that “God is with you” is a warm interior. The wherever is geography. Presence is for the road, not for the chair.

4. Confessional Anchor

God the good Creator of all things, in his infinite power and wisdom, does uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures and things, from the greatest even to the least, by his most wise and holy providence

1689 LBCF, ch. 5 — Of Divine Providence

From the greatest even to the least. The confession will not let providence stop at nations and skip the man crossing a river. Joshua 1:9 is that paragraph put in a commissioning speech. The Lord who governs all things is with this one man wherever he goes, which means the going is not a gap in the government.

God’s works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions.

The Baptist Catechism, Q. 14

Preserving and governing. Not watching from a distance and hoping Joshua finds his nerve. The God who commanded him also keeps him. Fear that treats the next mile as ungoverned has not yet believed this answer.

5. For the Man Reading This

You will not feel ready. Joshua did not. The verse does not wait for the feeling. It asks whether you were commanded, and then it tells you to go.

Do not turn this into a poster for general bravery. The command is attached to a mission and a Book. Strength that will not keep verse 8 is just noise. Courage that will not do what is written is not this verse. It is a man quoting Joshua at a life he will not obey.

And do not hide in the crowd of “we all get anxious.” The sentence has a subject. You. The Lord your God is with you wherever you go. The wherever includes the thing you would rather not name: the conversation, the job, the house, the diagnosis, the river. If He is not there, the verse is a lie. If He is, then dismay is no longer merely a feeling. It is a refusal to count Him present.

Take the question home. Have not I commanded you? If the answer is yes, the next word is not “once I feel stronger.” It is: be strong, and go.
