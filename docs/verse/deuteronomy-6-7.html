Verse Study · Deuteronomy

Deuteronomy 6:7

Verse 6 puts the words in the father. Verse 7 will not let him keep them there. A house is whetted, not decorated.

and you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, and when you walk by the way, and when you lie down, and when you rise up.— World English Bible (public domain)

And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. teach: Heb. whet, or, sharpen— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The KJV margin on this verse is the whole sermon: teach: Heb. whet, or, sharpen. Moses does not say put a lesson on the calendar. He says take the commanded words of verse 6 and put an edge on your sons with them.

The hours named are not a curriculum. Sit. Walk. Lie down. Rise up. That is a whole day. The father who only talks of the Lord at a set “devotion time” has not done this verse. He has scheduled God. Moses wants the words in the ordinary traffic of the house.

  • שָׁנַן shanan · “teach them diligently” · Strong’s H8150

    Shanan (שָׁנַן) means to whet or sharpen (as a sword or arrow), and by extension to teach sharply and repeatedly — to inculcate by diligent repetition. It appears about 9 times in the OT.

    Teaching that is shanan is not a once-a-week talk. It is a blade taken to the stone again. Related to shen (tooth). A dull father produces dull sons. The verse will not let you blame the youth group for an unsharpened house.

  • בֵּן Ben · “thy children” · Strong’s H1121

    Ben means son, child, grandson, or descendant. With nearly 5,000 occurrences, it is one of the most frequent Hebrew nouns.

    Ben is son, child, descendant — inheritance and identity, not a mascot. These are not generic “kids.” They are the next generation of the covenant house. What you fail to sharpen, the land will blunt.

  • דָּבַר Dabar · “shalt talk” · Strong’s H1696

    Dabar (verb) means to speak, say, declare, or command. It appears over 1,100 times.

    The verb dabar is to speak with weight — not chatter, not a slogan on the way out the door. The words already commanded in verse 6 become speech in verse 7. A silent house is not a peaceful house. It is an empty whetstone.

  • יָשַׁב Yashab · “when thou sittest” · Strong’s H3427

    Yashab means to sit, dwell, remain, inhabit, or settle. It appears over 1,000 times.

    Sit in the house — the table, the couch, the ordinary indoor quiet. If the words are only present in one room of the day, they are a hobby.

  • בַּיִת Bayith · “in thine house” · Strong’s H1004

    Bayith means house, household, dwelling, or temple. Appearing over 2,000 times, it covers physical buildings, family lineages ('house of David'), and the temple as God's dwelling place.

    The house is where the whetstone lives. Not the youth hall. Not a weekend retreat. The commanded words have to be talk under this roof.

  • יָלַךְ Yalak · “when thou walkest” · Strong’s H3212

    The Hebrew verb yalak means to go, walk, come, depart, or proceed. It is one of the most common verbs in the Old Testament, occurring over 1,500 times.

    Walk by the way — the commute, the errand, the walk to the truck. Moses named the road on purpose. The words travel, or they were never in the man.

  • שָׁכַב shakab · “when thou liest down” · Strong’s H7901

    To lie down — for sleep, rest, death, or sexual intimacy. The euphemism 'to lie with' describes sexual relations.

    The last words of the day. A house that only talks of the Lord at breakfast has not done the verse.

  • קוּם Qum · “when thou risest up” · Strong’s H6965

    Qum is a fundamental Hebrew verb meaning to arise, rise up, stand, or be established. One of the most frequent verbs in the Hebrew Bible (appearing over 600 times), it is used of physical rising (getting up), of enemies rising against one, of establishing covenants, and — most significantly — of resurrection.

    The first words of the morning. The four hours close the loophole. Sit. Walk. Lie down. Rise up. That is a whole day, not a scheduled slot.

2. Where It Sits

Deuteronomy 6 follows the Ten Words. Verse 4: one LORD. Verse 5: whole-self love. Verse 6: the words in the man’s own heart. Then this. Then bind them, write them, and when the son asks what the testimonies mean, tell him the exodus (6:20–25).

Start at verse 7 and you get a parenting technique. Keep verse 6 in front of it and you get a man who must first carry what he means to sharpen. The companion draft on Deuteronomy 6:6 already wrote that lock. This page assumes it.

The danger in the chapter is not wilderness ignorance. It is fullness in the land — houses you did not build, wells you did not dig (6:10–12). Verse 7 is how a father keeps the next generation from drowning in gift. He whets them while they are still in the ordinary hours, before prosperity makes the words optional.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed household instinct on this verse is the same as on Psalm 119:11: Scripture is not clergy property. It is the father’s daily speech. That is why the tradition built family worship instead of leaving the text to the pulpit.

Calvin hears Moses put the same charge on the dying day: the words go into the heart, then they are commanded to the children. The hours in verse 7 are not extra credit. They are the life of the house.

When about to die, he thus addressed the people, “Set your hearts unto all the words which I testify among you this day, which ye shall command your children to observe to do, all the words of this law. For it is not a vain thing for you; because it is your life”

Calvin, Institutes II.7

Moses already named the hours. The witnesses who built family worship were refusing to let verse 7 become a Sunday-school subcontract. Sit, walk, lie down, rise. That is the week, not a program.

4. Confessional Anchor

The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.

1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures

Obedience is the clause that ties the confession to this verse. A sufficient rule that never gets into a father’s mouth cannot sharpen a son. You do not need a second secret curriculum if the commanded words are actually talked when you sit and when you walk.

5. For the Man Reading This

Do not buy another family plan until you can name the last ordinary hour in which you talked the words to your sons.

Pick one stretch this week — table, truck, or bedtime — and shanan one commanded text there every day. Not a lecture. An edge. Say it. Ask them what it means. Tell them the exodus when they ask why it matters.

If you cannot do verse 6, you will fake verse 7. If you can do verse 6 and still stay silent in the house, you are hoarding what was given to be sharpened on your children.

The test is not whether the boys have heard you pray in public. The test is whether, when they sit and when they walk, the words that come out of you are the words that were commanded — or the words that happen to be in you instead.

In the Family Captain memorize pack — carry it.
