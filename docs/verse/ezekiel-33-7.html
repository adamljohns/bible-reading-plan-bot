Verse Study · Ezekiel

Ezekiel 33:7

The watchman is not hired for his opinions. He hears at the mouth, and he warns from the same God. Silence is not neutrality. It is blood.

So you, son of man, I have set you a watchman to the house of Israel; therefore hear the word at my mouth, and give them warning from me.— World English Bible (public domain)

So thou, O son of man, I have set thee a watchman unto the house of Israel; therefore thou shalt hear the word at my mouth, and warn them from me.— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The verse is a commissioning with a therefore in the middle, and the therefore is the whole job description. I have set you a watchman. Therefore you shall hear the word at my mouth, and warn them from me. If you skip the hearing, you have a man on a wall with nothing to blow. If you skip the warning, you have a man who heard and kept it for himself. Both fail the office.

From me is the other phrase that will not sit still. The warning is not the watchman’s temperature about the culture. It is God’s word, passed through. A man can be loud and still not be this. He can also be quiet and still be this, if the quiet is only until he has actually heard. What he cannot be is silent after the word has arrived.

  • Tsaphah · “watchman” · Strong’s H6822

    The Hebrew verb tsaphah (צָפָה) means to watch, keep watch, observe carefully, or serve as a lookout or watchman. It appears about 37 times in the OT, often in prophetic and military contexts.

    Tsaphah is to watch, keep watch, look out — the lookout on the wall or the hill. The noun from it is the tsopheh, the man stationed to see the sword coming and to sound. Ezekiel 3 already used this charge once. Chapter 33 gives it again. The metaphor is not a personality: some men are “watchman types.” It is a post. Someone is put where he can see, and he is not put there to enjoy the view.

    The city does not owe the watchman a pleasant hearing. The watchman owes the city a warning. Those are different debts.

  • שָׁמַע shama · “hear” · Strong’s H8085

    The Hebrew shama means to hear, listen, attend to, or obey. In Hebrew thought, true hearing always implies response — to hear God's word is to obey it.

    Shama is to hear in the Hebrew sense that already contains obedience. To hear and not do is, in this vocabulary, a failure to hear. The watchman’s first shama is not the people’s. It is his own, at God’s mouth. He does not invent the trumpet’s meaning on the wall. He receives it.

    That order humbles the office. A man who loves warning and hates hearing will become a scold. A man who loves hearing and hates warning will become a commentator. Ezekiel is allowed neither. The therefore runs from the setting to both verbs.

  • דָּבָר dabar · “word” · Strong’s H1697

    From the root H1696 (to speak), dabar is a common and significant Hebrew word. It encompasses a wide range of meanings, including a spoken word, a matter or affair, a thing, a command, or a promise.

    Dabar is word, matter, the spoken thing that has substance. The “word of the LORD” in this book is not advice. It comes, it lands, it does. The watchman is not a specialist in religious atmosphere. He is a man under a dabar that originated somewhere else. Hear the word. Then speak. If what you have is a mood, you do not yet have a dabar from the mouth.

  • פֶּה Peh · “mouth” · Strong’s H6310

    Peh means mouth, speech, edge, or opening. It appears about 500 times.

    Peh is mouth, speech, opening. Hear the word at my mouth. The picture is proximity, not a rumor chain. Ezekiel does not get a digest. He gets it where it is spoken. That is why the warning that follows can be from me. The mouth that gave it is the mouth that owns it. The prophet is a relay, not a source.

    Men in this work are always tempted to improve the mouth — make it kinder, sharper, more current. The verse does not ask for that. It asks for hearing at the actual mouth, and then a warning that can still be traced back.

  • זָהַר Zahar · “warn” · Strong’s H2094

    The Hebrew verb zahar means to warn, admonish, or give caution. In the Niphal stem it carries the sense of being warned or taking heed; in the Hiphil it means to cause to shine or to enlighten.

    Zahar is to warn, admonish, caution — and in another stem, to cause to shine, to enlighten. Warning here is not merely scolding. It is making a danger visible while there is still time to turn. Chapter 33 will spend the next verses on what happens if he does not: the wicked dies in his iniquity, and his blood I will require at your hand. If he does warn, and the man will not turn, the watchman has delivered his soul.

    So love, in this chapter, has a sound. It sounds like a trumpet. Silence while the sword is in view is not a gentle strategy. It is the crime the office exists to prevent.

2. Where It Sits

Ezekiel 33 opens with a parable anyone in a walled city would understand. When I bring the sword on a land, and the people set a watchman, and he sees and blows, the man who ignores the trumpet dies with his own blood on his head. If the watchman sees and does not blow, the dead man still dies in his iniquity, but I will require his blood at the watchman’s hand.

Then verse 7 applies the parable to the prophet: so you, son of man. Not a volunteer. Set. Unto the house of Israel. The sword in the parable is the LORD’s. The warning is the LORD’s. The blood-accounting is the LORD’s.

Verses 8-9 make it personal and moral, not only military: when I say to the wicked, you shall surely die, and you do not warn him from his way — blood at your hand. If you warn and he will not turn, he dies, and you have delivered your soul. Verse 10-11 then give the heart of the God who set the watchman: they are pining away in their sins, asking how they can live; and the Lord GOD swears He has no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn and live.

So the watchman charge is not a license to enjoy condemnation. It is how a God who wants the wicked to live gets a warning to them in time. The severity of verse 6-8 and the oath of verse 11 are the same God. Cut either half and you have a different book.

The chapter also has a date under it. Verse 21: a fugitive from Jerusalem arrives in the twelfth year and says the city is smitten. The first watchman charge in chapter 3 was before that news. This one stands at the hinge. The thing warned of has happened, and the office is not retired. There is still a house of Israel to hear, and still a wicked man to turn. Collapse does not end the trumpet. It proves why the trumpet existed.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading heard this as the ministry of the Word under a blood-earnestness the modern church is shy of. Pastors watch for souls as those who must give an account. Paul told the Ephesian elders he was clean from the blood of all men, because he had not shrunk from declaring the whole counsel. That is Ezekiel 33 in apostolic clothes. The office is not a helping profession that happens to use a Bible. It is a post from which a man will be asked, at the last, whether he blew.

They also refused to let the watchman be a freelance scold. Hear the word at my mouth. The authority is derivative. A man who warns from his own heat, his own party, his own fear of looking soft, is not covered by this verse. The blood-requirement assumes the word was God’s. Invented alarms do not get the same accounting. Neither does fashionable silence.

The tradition kept verse 11 attached, because a watchman without that oath becomes a prosecutor who needs convictions. God swears He does not want the death. The warning is a means of turning. If your “watchman” work never sounds like “turn ye, turn ye, why will ye die,” it may be a different metal — closer to the sword than to the trumpet.

4. Confessional Anchor

The work of pastors being constantly to attend the service of Christ in his churches in the ministry of the Word and prayer, watching for their souls as they that must give an account to him.

1689 LBCF, ch. 26 — Of the Church

Watching for their souls as they that must give an account is this chapter’s blood-language without the trumpet metaphor. The account is real. The Word and prayer are the means, which is Ezekiel’s order: mouth first, then warning. A pastor who will not hear cannot watch. A pastor who will not warn is not watching. He is staffing a wall.

This promise of Christ, and salvation by him, is revealed only by the Word of God. Neither do the works of creation or providence, with the light of nature, reveal Christ or grace by him, so much as in a general or obscure way — much less that men destitute of the revelation of him by the promise or gospel should be enabled thereby to attain saving faith or repentance.

1689 LBCF, ch. 20 — Of the Gospel, and of the Extent of the Grace Thereof

The watchman has no other instrument. Creation will not warn a wicked man to turn and live in the way this chapter means. The dabar at the mouth is the gospel’s ordinary way of arriving. That is why the silence is so costly. You are not withholding a preference. You are withholding the only outward means the confession will admit can reveal Christ. Ezekiel’s blood-accounting and the confession’s “only by the Word” are the same seriousness, spoken in two centuries.

5. For the Man Reading This

If you hold any post — pulpit, table, a son’s ear, a friend’s trust — start with the hearing. You do not get to warn from hunches. Open the book. Stay there until you have a dabar, not a mood. The wall is littered with men who loved the horn and starved the ear. Their noise did not deliver anyone’s soul. It only made the next true warning harder to hear.

Then blow when the sword is in view. The wicked man’s way, named as death, is not a private matter you are rude to mention. Verse 8 will require the blood at the silent man’s hand. You will not want that sentence. So speak — plainly, from the text, aimed at turning, not at winning the room. Verse 11 is your tone: God has no pleasure in the death. You are not recruiting for a ruin. You are begging a live man off a road.

Do not confuse this with a personality. Some of the best watchmen are quiet until they are not. The office is obedience, not volume. And do not take the title as a brand. A watchman who will not warn his own house, his own mouth, his own hidden way, is a hired lookout facing the wrong direction. The first shama is yours.

If you have been the man who heard the trumpet and did not take warning, the chapter has a word for you too. The blood is on your own head then, not the watchman’s. Turn. The God who set the watchman swears He wants that. The trumpet was mercy. Treating it as noise is the last mistake the parable is willing to describe.
