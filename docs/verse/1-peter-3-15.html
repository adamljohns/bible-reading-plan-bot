Romans 1:16-17

Set Christ apart as Lord in the heart first. Then be ready to give a reason. The order is the verse.

But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts; and always be ready to give an answer to everyone who asks you a reason concerning the hope that is in you, with humility and fear:

— World English Bible (public domain)

But sanctify the Lord God in your hearts: and be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear:

— King James Version (public domain)

κύριον δὲ τὸν Χριστὸν ἁγιάσατε ἐν ταῖς καρδίαις ὑμῶν, ἕτοιμοι ἀεὶ πρὸς ἀπολογίαν παντὶ τῷ αἰτοῦντι ὑμᾶς λόγον περὶ τῆς ἐν ὑμῖν ἐλπίδος, ἀλλὰ μετὰ πραΰτητος καὶ φόβου.

1. The Text and Its Words

Peter is not training a debate club. He is telling a suffering church how to live when the question comes. The verse has a hinge. First you sanctify the Lord in the heart. Then you are ready with an answer. Reverse those and you get a man who can win an argument and still have no Lord in the inner room.

  • ἁγιάζωhagiazō — "sanctify" · Strong's G37

    To make holy, to set apart, to treat as holy. Peter is not asking you to improve God’s reputation. He is telling you to treat the Lord as holy in your hearts — the inner person, the center of will. Isaiah 8:13 said the same thing of the LORD of Hosts: He is the one you must regard as holy. Peter takes that line and, in the best manuscripts, names Christ as the Lord you set apart. The fear of the Lord starts inside, before anyone asks you a question.

  • καρδίαkardia — "hearts" · Strong's G2588

    The inner person — not a feeling, the seat of will. If Christ is not holy there, the answer you give later is theater. A man can memorize a defense and still have a rival lord in the heart: comfort, reputation, the job, the wife’s approval. Peter starts where Proverbs starts. Guard the heart. Then open the mouth.

  • ἕτοιμοςhetoimos — "ready" · Strong's G2092

    Ready, prepared, at hand. Not frantic. Not waiting until the heat of the moment to invent a faith. Always. The word describes a state where the needful work has already been done. You do not get ready when they ask. You live ready, and the ask finds you already set.

  • ἀπολογίαapologia — "answer / defense" · Strong's G627

    A reasoned defense, a verbal vindication. It is the word behind “apologetics,” and it does not mean being sorry. Paul uses the same family of words when he makes his defense before rulers. Peter wants a logos — a reason — about the hope in you. Not a sneer, not a slogan, not a threat. A reason a man can follow.

  • ἐλπίςelpis — "hope" · Strong's G1680

    Not the English “I hope so.” Biblical hope is confident expectation grounded in God’s character. Peter has already blessed God for a living hope through the resurrection (1:3). The questioner is not asking why you like church. He is asking why you do not collapse when the heat comes. The answer is that hope, and it has a name.

  • πραΰτης / φόβοςprautēs / phobos — "meekness and fear" · Strong's G4240, G5401

    Meekness is strength under control, not softness. Fear here is reverence, not panic — the KJV margin even says “reverence.” You answer a man without crushing him, and you answer God without swagger. A defense that wins the room and loses the fear of the Lord is not this verse. Colossians 4:6 wants speech seasoned with salt. Same family of command.

The logos you owe is “concerning the hope that is in you.” If there is no hope in you, there is nothing to defend. The verse assumes a life that has already made people curious. Conduct first. Then words.

2. Where It Sits

1 Peter is a letter to elect exiles. They are suffering as Christians, not as busybodies. Chapter 2 told servants to endure unjust pain with eyes on Christ, who did not revile when He was reviled. Chapter 3 told wives and husbands how to live in the house. Then Peter turns to the whole church: do not repay evil for evil. Seek peace. Who will harm you if you are zealous for what is good? Even if you suffer for righteousness, you are blessed. Do not fear their fear. That is the runway. Verse 15 is the landing.

Verse 14 has already quoted Isaiah 8:12 in spirit — do not fear what they fear. Verse 15 quotes Isaiah 8:13 in substance: sanctify the Lord. The prophet told Judah to fear the LORD of Hosts instead of the conspiracy. Peter tells a Gentile church under local pressure to do the same with Christ as Lord. The old line is not retired. It is named more clearly.

What follows is as important as what precedes. Keep a good conscience, so that when you are slandered those who revile your good conduct in Christ may be put to shame. It is better to suffer for doing good, if God wills, than for doing evil. Then Christ, once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that He might bring us to God. The apologia is not a hobby. It sits between a blessing on the sufferer and the cross that explains the hope.

Cross-chains in the kit point the same way. 2 Timothy 2:15 wants a workman who does not need to be ashamed, handling the word rightly. Colossians 4:5–6 wants wisdom toward outsiders and an answer with grace. Matthew 10:19 promises that when you are delivered up, the Spirit will give you words in that hour — which is not an excuse to be unready, but a comfort that you are not alone when the hour comes. Peter’s “always be ready” and Jesus’ “do not be anxious how or what you will say” are not enemies. One is the life of preparation. The other is help in the dock.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin — on what knowing God is for

Peter starts with the heart’s reverence, not with a toolkit. Calvin says the same thing about the knowledge of God. Knowing Him is not a sport for the curious:

The effect of knowing him ought to be, first, to teach us reverence and fear; and second, to lead us, under his guidance and teaching, to ask every good thing from him, and, when we receive it, to credit it to him.

Calvin, Institutes I.2 (Beveridge 1845)

That is “sanctify the Lord God in your hearts” in Geneva English. Reverence first. Then you ask. Then you credit Him. A man who treats God as a mascot for his opinions has not begun the verse, however ready his talking points are.

Calvin’s further argument in that chapter runs like this. If you are His handiwork, you are bound by the law of creation to submit to His authority. Your life is owed to Him, and whatever you do should have reference to Him. Peter’s “always be ready” is one of those things. The answer you give is not your brand. It is reference to the Lord you have already set apart.

The meekness and fear keep the defense from becoming a second law. You do not crush the asker to feel safe. You do not flatter him to feel loved. You tell the truth about the hope, and you leave the result with the God you have sanctified in the heart. That is harder than winning. It is also the only way the answer matches the hope.

4. Confessional Anchor

1689 London Baptist Confession · Chapter 1, Paragraph 1

The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience. Although the light of nature and the works of creation and providence so far reveal the goodness, wisdom, and power of God as to leave men inexcusable, yet they are not sufficient to give that knowledge of God and his will which is necessary for salvation.

Read Chapter 1, Of the Holy Scriptures →

The reason you give is not a private vision. Nature can leave a man without excuse. It cannot give him the hope that is in you. So the apologia has a canon. You answer from the Word that is sufficient, not from the weather in your chest.

1689 London Baptist Confession · Chapter 14, Paragraph 2

By this faith a Christian believes to be true whatsoever is revealed in the Word, for the authority of God himself; and also apprehends an excellency therein above all other writings, and all things in the world … but the principal acts of saving faith have immediate relation to Christ, accepting, receiving, and resting upon him alone, for justification, sanctification, and eternal life, by virtue of the covenant of grace.

Read Chapter 14, Of Saving Faith →
1689 London Baptist Confession · Chapter 21, Paragraph 2

God alone is Lord of the conscience, and has left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men which are in any way contrary to his Word, or not contained in it. To believe such doctrines, or to obey such commands out of conscience, is to betray true liberty of conscience.

Read Chapter 21, Of Christian Liberty →
Baptist Catechism · Question 4

Q. What is the Word of God?
A. The holy Scriptures of the Old and New Testament are the Word of God, and the only certain rule of faith and obedience.

Work through the Catechism →

Peter wants a reason. The confession tells you where a reason that saves has to come from, and in whom it has to rest. If your answer is “my truth” or “what works for me,” you have not given Peter’s apologia. You have given a biography. The hope in you has a Lord, a cross, a resurrection, and a Word. Sanctify that Lord. Then speak.

5. For the Man Reading This

This verse is in the Unashamed memorize pack, right beside Romans 1:16–17. Paul tells you not to be ashamed of the gospel. Peter tells you what to do when someone asks why you aren’t. Learn both. One is the nerve. The other is the answer.

Do the heart work before the mouth work. If Christ is not holy in you on an ordinary Tuesday, you will not suddenly become a witness under pressure. You will become a salesman, or a coward, or a bully. Set Him apart in the morning. Then you can answer at lunch.

Get ready before you are asked. Ready always means you can say, in plain words, who Christ is, what He has done, and why you hope in Him — without a script in your pocket and without contempt in your voice. If you cannot do that for your son, you are not ready for a stranger. Write it once. Speak it until it is yours.

Keep meekness and fear in the same sentence. Meekness without truth is just agreeing. Fear of God without meekness toward the man is just winning. Peter will not let you split them. The man across from you is not the enemy. He is the one who asked. Answer him as someone who will give an account, and as someone who was himself asked and shown mercy.

The cost is real. A ready answer can cost you the room, the job, the easy peace at the table. Peter already said you might suffer for righteousness. The verse does not promise you will be liked. It promises a way to stand: the Lord holy in the heart, a reason in the mouth, and a hope that did not start with you.
