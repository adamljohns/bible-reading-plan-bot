Verse Study · Ephesians

Ephesians 5:14-16

Paul is not writing a poem about mornings. He is telling a church that already has the light to stop sleeping in it, to watch their step, and to buy back the hours because the week is not neutral.

Therefore he says, “Awake, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you.” Therefore watch carefully how you walk, not as unwise, but as wise; redeeming the time, because the days are evil.— World English Bible (public domain)

Wherefore he saith, Awake thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall give thee light. he: or, it See then that ye walk circumspectly, not as fools, but as wise, Redeeming the time, because the days are evil.— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

Three moves, and they run in order.

First a summons, quoted as if from Scripture: Awake, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will shine on you. Then a watch-order: see that you walk carefully, not as unwise but as wise. Then the market word: redeeming the time, because the days are evil.

The people being told to rise from the dead are already in the letter that said they were made alive. So this death is the other one — the sleep of a man who has light and will not use it. The shine is promised to the one who gets up. The careful walk is how a risen man spends a Tuesday. The buying-back of time is what that walk looks like when the days themselves are against him.

  • ἐγείρω egeirō · “Awake thou” · Strong’s G1453

    To awaken from sleep, to raise up, to cause to rise. In the NT, egeirō is the most common verb for resurrection — God raised Jesus from the dead.

    Egeiro is to wake, and it is the New Testament’s ordinary verb for resurrection. God raised Jesus. Here the sleeper is told to rise as if from among the dead. Paul is willing to use Easter language on a man who is already converted and currently numb. That is not insult. It is diagnosis. There is a sleep that can settle on a live church, and the cure named is not a nap. It is getting up.

  • ἐπιφαύσκω epiphausko · “shall give” · Strong’s G2017

    The Greek verb epiphausko (also epiphaino in some manuscripts) means to shine upon, to give light to, to illuminate. It is related to phos (light) and carries the sense of divine illumination breaking in.

    Epiphausko is to shine upon, to give light. The King James Christ shall give thee light and the WEB Christ will shine on you are the same promise. The light is not a mood you manufacture at dawn. It is Christ, turning His face on a man who has stopped playing dead. In this chapter they were already told they are light in the Lord. Verse 14 is what you do with a fact you have been treating as decoration.

  • ἀκριβῶς Akribōs · “circumspectly” · Strong’s G199

    The Greek adverb akribōs (ἀκριβῶς) means carefully, accurately, diligently, or exactly — describing action performed with precision, thoroughness, and scrupulous attention. Usage & Theological Significance Paul urged walking carefully, not as unwise but as wise (Ephesians 5:15).

    Akribos is carefully, accurately, exactly — the way you measure, not the way you stroll. Walk circumspectly is not “be vaguely spiritual.” It is watch your step. Fools in this sentence are not unintelligent. They are unwise: men who will not look at where the next foot lands. Wisdom here is moral precision in a dark street, not a personality type.

  • ἐξαγοράζω exagorazo · “Redeeming” · Strong’s G1805

    Exagorazo (ἐξαγοράζω) is a compound of ek (out of) and agorazo (to buy in the marketplace). It means to buy out of — specifically to redeem someone from slavery, legal condemnation, or the curse of the law by paying the purchase price.

    Exagorazo is to buy out of the market — ek plus agorazo. It is a redemption word: to purchase out of slavery, to buy back what was going to be lost. Time, in this verse, is not a feeling you manage. It is a thing that can be spent into nothing or bought back for a use. You redeem it the way you redeem a man: at cost, on purpose, because someone else was going to have him.

  • καιρός kairos · “the time” · Strong’s G2540

    The right time, the decisive moment, an appointed season. Distinguished from chronos (duration, clock-time), kairos is qualitative time — the moment ripe with meaning and opportunity.

    Kairos is not clock-time. It is the appointed season, the loaded moment, the hour that will not come again in the same shape. Paul does not tell you to redeem chronos — to get more hours. He tells you to buy back the kairos you already have, because the days are evil and will eat it if you do not.

Why the days being evil is the reason, not the excuse

Because the days are evil. That clause does not license withdrawal. It is why the buying must happen now. Evil days are not a pause in discipleship. They are the market in which the hours are being sold cheap. A man who waits for a better season to walk carefully has misunderstood the sentence. The evil is the reason for the haste.

2. Where It Sits

Chapter 5 has already named the people: once darkness, now light in the Lord; walk as children of light; the fruit is goodness, righteousness, truth; have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but expose them. Light makes things visible. Then verse 14, as the wherefore of that argument. If you are light, stop sleeping. If you expose darkness, do not live like it.

Verses 15-16 are the next therefore. Awake, then watch how you walk, then buy the time. Verse 17 follows immediately: so do not be foolish, but understand what the will of the Lord is. Then the Spirit-filling, the singing, the thanks, the submitting. Then husbands and wives. The household code does not replace this paragraph. It is where this paragraph goes to work.

So 14-16 is the hinge between identity (you are light) and the ordinary rooms of the church’s life. A sleeping Christian will not have a Christian marriage in the next section. A man who will not redeem Tuesday will not suddenly become wise at home.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading kept two things from flying apart: the already and the still.

You are already light. That is chapter 2 and 5:8. And you can still be asleep. The summons to rise does not deny the first gift. It refuses to let the first gift become a pillow. Regeneration has a public shape. Calvin named the object of that new life without romance.

We have said that the object of regeneration is to bring the life of believers into concord and harmony with the righteousness of God, and so confirm the adoption by which they have been received as sons.

Calvin, Institutes III.6

Concord and harmony with the righteousness of God is walk circumspectly in other clothes. Adoption is confirmed in the street, not in a private theory that you were raised. A man whose hours look like the old darkness has not yet let regeneration reach the calendar.

The tradition also heard redeeming the time as stewardship, not as panic. The days are evil under providence, not outside it. You do not redeem time by frantic usefulness. You buy it back into the will of the Lord, which is the next verse. Wisdom is not speed. It is accuracy.

4. Confessional Anchor

In which war, although the remaining corruption for a time may much prevail, yet through the continual supply of strength from the sanctifying Spirit of Christ, the regenerate part does overcome; and so the saints grow in grace, perfecting holiness in the fear of God, pressing after a heavenly life, in evangelical obedience to all the commands which Christ as Head and King, in his Word has prescribed them.

1689 LBCF, ch. 13 — Of Sanctification

For a time may much prevail is why a live man can still be told to wake. Corruption sleeps him. The Spirit supplies. The saints grow. Pressing after a heavenly life is not waiting to die. It is the careful walk of verses 15-16, in evil days, under a King who has already prescribed the commands.

To escape the wrath and curse of God due to us for sin, God requires of us faith in Jesus Christ, repentance to life, with the diligent use of all the outward means by which Christ communicates to us the benefits of redemption.

The Baptist Catechism, Q. 90

Diligent use. The catechism will not let faith and repentance sit in the air. There are means, and they take time, and they are to be used diligently. Redeeming the kairos is that diligence with the clock taken seriously. Word, prayer, the gathered church, the next obedience — those are not extras you get to when the evil days settle down. They are how a waking man spends the hours the days were trying to eat.

5. For the Man Reading This

If you are converted and currently dull, start at verse 14. Do not wait to feel awake. Get up. Open the Book. Go to the gathering. Confess the thing you have been too sleepy to name. The shine is promised to the one who rises, not to the one who lies there analyzing his fatigue.

Then look at the next step, literally. Circumspect walking is specific. What you watch, what you click, the tone in the kitchen, the hour after the children are down, the conversation you keep postponing. Fools in this passage are not cartoon villains. They are men who will not look. Wisdom is looking, then planting the foot.

Redeem a real piece of time, not time in general. One hour this week that usually gets sold — the phone hour, the leftover hour, the hour you call rest and spend on nothing that rests you — buy it back for a use that matches the light. The days being evil is the argument for doing it now. If you wait for a clean week, you have already believed a different gospel about how time works.

Christ shines on men who get up. The walk is how you stay in that light. The hours are the field. That is the whole charge, and it will still be the charge tomorrow, when the days are evil again.
