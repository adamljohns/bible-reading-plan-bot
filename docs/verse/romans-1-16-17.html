Romans 1:16-17

Paul is not ashamed of a crucified King. The gospel is not advice. It is God’s power, and the righteous live by faith.

For I am not ashamed of the Good News of Christ, for it is the power of God for salvation for everyone who believes; for the Jew first, and also for the Greek. For in it is revealed God’s righteousness from faith to faith. As it is written, “But the righteous shall live by faith.”

— World English Bible (public domain)

For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek. For therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith: as it is written, The just shall live by faith.

— King James Version (public domain)

Οὐ γὰρ ἐπαισχύνομαι τὸ εὐαγγέλιον, δύναμις γὰρ θεοῦ ἐστιν εἰς σωτηρίαν παντὶ τῷ πιστεύοντι, Ἰουδαίῳ τε πρῶτον καὶ Ἕλληνι. δικαιοσύνη γὰρ θεοῦ ἐν αὐτῷ ἀποκαλύπτεται ἐκ πίστεως εἰς πίστιν, καθὼς γέγραπται· Ὁ δὲ δίκαιος ἐκ πίστεως ζήσεται.

1. The Text and Its Words

These two verses are the thesis of the letter. Everything after them is Paul proving what he has just said. He has named Rome. He has named his debt to Greek and barbarian. Then he tells you why he is not embarrassed to show up in the capital with a message about a Jew who was crucified under Roman law.

  • ἐπαισχύνομαιepaischunomai — "I am ashamed" · Strong's G1870

    The verb means to be ashamed of, to shrink from something because it costs you face. Paul does not say he feels no fear. He says he will not treat the gospel as a thing that would make him duck. In a world where a cross was the empire’s way of humiliating a man, that sentence is a refusal. Shame is the tax Rome charges for a crucified Messiah. Paul will not pay it.

  • εὐαγγέλιονeuangelion — "gospel / good news" · Strong's G2098

    Good news, glad tidings. In the Roman world the word announced imperial victories and the emperor’s birthday. Paul takes the court’s word and uses it for a greater announcement: God has acted in Christ. The gospel is not a technique for improving yourself. It is news about something that happened.

  • δύναμιςdynamis — "power" · Strong's G1411

    Power, might, ability — the kind of force that actually does work. Paul does not call the gospel a theory, a slogan, or a brand. He calls it the power of God unto salvation. If it does not save, it is not this gospel. The word will later describe the Spirit’s power; here it names what the message itself is when God uses it.

  • σωτηρίαsoteria — "salvation" · Strong's G4991

    Rescue, deliverance, preservation. In this letter it is God’s complete work of delivering people from sin, death, and judgment. It is for everyone who believes — Jew first, and also Greek. The order is history, not favoritism: the promises came through Israel, and the nations are not an afterthought. There is one power and one door.

  • δικαιοσύνηdikaiosyne — "righteousness" · Strong's G1343

    Righteousness, justice, right standing. In Romans this is the master word. Verse 17 says it is revealed in the gospel. Not a righteousness you assemble, but God’s righteousness uncovered — both who He is and how He puts a sinner in the right. The verb apokalypto (G601) means to take the cover off. Paul is about to uncover wrath in verse 18. First he uncovers the righteousness that saves.

  • πίστιςpistis — "faith" · Strong's G4102

    Faith, trust, reliance. “From faith to faith” is Paul’s way of saying the whole road is trust — not faith as a first payment that you later replace with performance. The just man lives by it. The verb to believe (G4100) in verse 16 is the same family: not a nod of the head, but entrusting yourself to the news.

Paul then does what he always does when he wants a doctrine nailed down: he writes “as it is written,” and he reaches for Habakkuk. The prophet had watched a proud man puff himself up while Judah bled. The answer given him was not a new technique. It was this: the righteous will live by faith. Paul takes that line and makes it the spine of Romans. Galatians 3:11 and Hebrews 10:38 will do the same work. One sentence, three letters, one door.

2. Where It Sits

Romans opens with a man set apart for the gospel of God, promised beforehand in the holy Scriptures, concerning the Son who is David’s seed and declared Son of God in power by resurrection. Paul is a debtor. He wants fruit in Rome as among the rest of the nations. Verse 15 is eagerness: he is ready to preach there too. Verses 16–17 are why that eagerness is not a personality trait. They are the reason a man can walk into Rome without shrinking.

What comes next is not a change of subject. Verse 18 opens with the same kind of “for.” The righteousness of God is revealed in the gospel; the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men who suppress the truth. Two unveilings. One gospel. If you cut 16–17 out of the letter you would still have a diagnosis of the world. You would not have a power unto salvation. Paul will spend 1:18–3:20 showing that Jew and Greek alike are under sin. He plants the gospel first so you know where the argument is going before the floor drops.

“To the Jew first, and also to the Greek” is not a slogan for a missions brochure. It is the shape of the story. The promises, the law, the worship, and the Messiah belong to Israel. The nations are grafted in; they do not replace the root. A Gentile who treats Jewish unbelief as a punchline has not read this verse. A church that treats the Old Testament as optional has not read it either. The same power that saves a Greek does not skip the Jew, and it does not start with the Greek as if Abraham never happened.

The Habakkuk line also sits in a particular fight. Habakkuk 2:4 contrasts a puffed-up soul with the man who lives by faith. Paul will soon show that no one is justified by works of the law. The righteous live by faith because there is no other way to live before a holy God once the law has done its work. Genesis 15:6 is already waiting in the wings: Abraham believed the Lord, and it was credited to him for righteousness. Romans 4 will take that verse apart. Romans 1:17 is the door into that room.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin — on faith and the gospel as a pair

Calvin will not let you treat faith as a vague warmth toward God. Faith has an object, and the object is the free embassy of the gospel. He hears Paul using the two words as if they belong together:

Hence he often uses faith and the Gospel as correlative terms, as when he says, that the ministry of the Gospel was committed to him for “obedience to the faith;” that “it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth;” that “therein is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith,”

Calvin, Institutes III.2 (Beveridge 1845)

That is the point of verse 16 in one stroke. The ministry is for the obedience of faith. The gospel is power to the one who believes. The righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith. Take the gospel away and faith has nothing to rest on. Take faith away and the news is announced to a shut door.

Calvin — on what that faith actually does

Later, when he defines justification, he will not let faith become a work you offer God. Faith is the empty hand:

This is the meaning of faith by which the sinner comes into the possession of salvation, when, according to the doctrine of the Gospel, he perceives that he is reconciled by God; when, by the intercession of Christ, he obtains the pardon of his sins, and is justified; and, though renewed by the Spirit of God, considers that, instead of leaning on his own works, he must look solely to the righteousness which is treasured up for him in Christ.

Calvin, Institutes III.11 (Beveridge 1845)

That is “from faith to faith” in pastoral English. You see you are reconciled. You take pardon through Christ. You are even being renewed — and you still refuse to lean on that renewal as if it were your righteousness. The treasure is in Christ. Faith looks there, and keeps looking there.

Luther’s explosion over this verse is famous, and it is not in this repo, so it will not be quoted here. The argument is simple enough without his wording: a man who thinks he can stand by the law has not yet heard Habakkuk. The righteous live by faith because the law has already told the truth about them. Paul will say it without blushing in 3:20–22. Verse 17 is the seed of that later sentence.

4. Confessional Anchor

1689 London Baptist Confession · Chapter 20, Paragraph 2

This promise of Christ, and salvation by him, is revealed only by the Word of God. Neither do the works of creation or providence, with the light of nature, reveal Christ or grace by him, so much as in a general or obscure way — much less that men destitute of the revelation of him by the promise or gospel should be enabled thereby to attain saving faith or repentance.

Read Chapter 20, Of the Gospel and the Extent of Its Grace →

The confession cites Romans 1:17 right here. Creation can show there is a God. It cannot show Christ. That is why Paul is not ashamed of preaching. The power is in the news, not in the scenery. A man staring at the sky can become a philosopher. He cannot become a Christian without the gospel.

1689 London Baptist Confession · Chapter 11, Paragraph 1

Those whom God effectually calls, he also freely justifies; not by infusing righteousness into them, but by pardoning their sins, and by accounting and accepting their persons as righteous; not for anything wrought in them, or done by them, but for Christ’s sake alone; not by imputing faith itself, the act of believing, or any other evangelical obedience to them, as their righteousness; but by imputing Christ’s active obedience to the whole law, and passive obedience in his death for their whole and sole righteousness by faith, which faith they have not of themselves; it is the gift of God.

Baptist Catechism · Question 36

Q. What is justification?
A. Justification is an act of God’s free grace, in which he pardons all our sins, and accepts us as righteous in his sight, only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone.

Work through the Catechism →
Baptist Catechism · Question 91

Q. What is faith in Jesus Christ?
A. Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace, by which we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel.

Question 91 →

Notice what the confession will not let you do. It will not let you turn faith into the righteousness. Faith is the instrument, not the merit. It will not let you turn gospel into a mood you work up from nature. And the catechism will not let you rest on “faith” as a vague virtue. You receive and rest upon him, as he is offered in the gospel. Verse 16’s “everyone who believes” is not a participation trophy. It is the empty hand Calvin described, held out to a particular Christ.

5. For the Man Reading This

This pair of verses is in the Unashamed memorize pack. Carry it because shame is not a theory in your line of work. It shows up in the shop, in the group text, at the table when someone asks what you actually believe. Paul wrote this toward Rome. You live toward a milder empire that still knows how to sneer.

Stop treating the gospel as a brand you manage. If it is the power of God unto salvation, it does not need your cool. It needs your mouth. The man who is ashamed of it will sand it down until it can sit in a meeting without offense. That sanded thing is not this gospel. It has no dynamis left.

Stop using “faith” as a synonym for trying harder. From faith to faith means the whole life is trust in Christ’s righteousness, not a first leap followed by a career of self-rescue. If you are in Christ, you do not graduate from Habakkuk. You live by the same sentence on Monday that saved you on the day you believed.

Pay the Jew-first line more than lip service. It forbids two cheap moves: despising Israel as if the promises were a mistake, and treating Gentiles as a second-class add-on. The same power reaches both. If your gospel only works in people who already look like you, it is not Paul’s.

And under all three, the comfort: you do not have to be impressive to be unashamed. You have to be convinced that God has already acted in Christ, and that this news is enough. The righteous shall live by faith. That is not a slogan for a wall. It is how a man gets out of bed without lying about himself, and how he opens his mouth without waiting to feel brave.
