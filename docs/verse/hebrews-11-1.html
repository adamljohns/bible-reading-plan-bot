Verse Study · Hebrews

Hebrews 11:1

Not a definition you frame. A description of how the people in the next forty verses lived when they could not see what they had been promised.

Now faith is assurance of things hoped for, proof of things not seen.— World English Bible (public domain)

Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen. substance: or, ground, or, confidence— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The King James has substance and evidence. The World English Bible has assurance and proof. The margin in the King James already knew the argument: substance: or, ground, or, confidence. The verse will bear both, and the chapter will not let you pick the softer one and go home.

Faith is the underlying reality of what is hoped for — a kind of possession of it now — and it is the conviction of things you cannot yet point at. It is not a mood you work up in the absence of reasons. It is how a man stands on a promise while the thing promised is still offstage.

Then the writer proves the sentence by a list of names. If you isolate verse 1 from Abel and Noah and Abraham, you will make it a slogan about positivity. Those people did things. The description is for a life that moves.

  • πίστις Pistis · “faith” · Strong’s G4102

    The Greek noun pistis is one of the most important words in the New Testament, occurring approximately 243 times. It means faith, trust, reliance, or firm conviction — particularly the trust placed in God and in Jesus Christ.

    Pistis is trust, reliance, firm conviction. In Hebrews it is never a leap into the dark as a method. It is trust aimed at a speaker. Verse 6 will say the man who comes to God must believe that He is, and that He rewards those who seek Him. The faith of 11:1 has a God in it, not a technique.

  • Hypostasis · “substance” · Strong’s G5287

    The Greek noun hypostasis means substance, underlying reality, or confident assurance. Composed of hypo (under) and histemi (to stand), it literally means 'that which stands under' — the foundation, the substance, the underlying reality.

    Hypostasis is what stands underneath — substance, underlying reality, confident assurance. The writer of Hebrews has already used it of the Son: the exact imprint of God’s hypostasis. Here it is what faith is to the things hoped for. Calvin will call it a prop the mind leans on, or else confidence; he prefers the first. Either way the word is thick. Faith does not wish. It has something under it.

  • ἐλπίζω Elpizo · “hoped for” · Strong’s G1679

    The Greek verb elpizo means to hope, to place confidence in, or to have an expectation of good. It is the verbal form of elpis (G1680, hope) and describes the active, forward-looking trust that characterizes Christian hope.

    Elpizo is to hope, to expect, to wait for good from someone. In Scripture hope is not a thinner cousin of knowledge. It is faith stretched toward the future tense of the same God. The things hoped for in this chapter are not vague improvements. They are a city, a country, a resurrection, a rewarder.

  • ἔλεγχος elegchos · “evidence” · Strong’s G1650

    Proof, evidence that convinces, the conviction that comes from being shown the truth. Elegchos is the inner certainty produced by evidence — not blind faith but grounded assurance.

    Elegchos is proof, the conviction that comes from being shown. It is not a hunch. It is the inner certainty produced by evidence — here, the evidence of things that have not yet appeared to the eye. The unseen is not the unreal. It is the not-yet-visible, which is a different category, and the whole chapter exists to keep those two from being confused.

  • Βλέπω Blepō · “seen” · Strong’s G991

    The Greek verb blepō means to see, look at, observe, perceive, or beware. It is one of several Greek verbs for seeing — alongside horaō (to see/perceive), theōreō (to behold), and optanomai (to appear).

    Blepo is to see, look at, watch. The contrast is not between spiritual people and practical people. It is between what the eye can presently certify and what God has spoken. Verse 3 will apply it to creation itself: the things which are seen were not made of things which do appear. The first unseen in the chapter is the origin of the world you are standing in.

Description, not a complete definition

Calvin is careful here, and he is right. The apostle accommodates this wording to his discourse. It is not the whole doctrine of saving faith in one line. It is the heading for a catalog of people who treated the unseen as more solid than the seen. Saving faith also receives Christ, rests on Him, obeys. This verse is doing one job. Let it do that job.

2. Where It Sits

Chapter 10 has just said the just shall live by faith, and that we are not of those who shrink back to destruction, but of those who believe to the saving of the soul. Then: now faith is… The now is not a new topic. It is an explanation of the people who do not shrink back.

The readers are tired. They have need of patience. Some have taken plunder joyfully. Some are drifting. The writer does not give them a lecture on grit. He gives them a cloud of people who died without receiving the whole thing, and who were still right to live as if the promise were underneath them.

Verse 2: by it the elders obtained a good report. The rest of the chapter is that report. Verse 1 is not a trophy. It is the premise of every by faith that follows. Take it away and Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Moses become inspiring personalities. The writer will not have that. They are exhibits of this sentence.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

Calvin treats the verse as a description that fits his larger definition of faith, not as a rival to it. Faith is persuasion of God’s truth; therefore it is also possession of what God has promised and not yet shown.

As if he had said, that faith is a kind of certain and secure possession of those things which are promised to us by God.

Calvin, Institutes III.2

Certain and secure possession. That is hypostasis without romance. The land Abraham never fully held was, in this sense, already his, because the Promiser is not a speculation. The possession is of the promise, not of the map.

Calvin also ties the unseen to hope without making faith a consolation prize for people who cannot know.

For since we hope for what we see not, and faith, as is said in another passage, is “the evidence of things not seen” so long as we are imprisoned in the body we are absent from the Lord.

Calvin, Institutes III.25

Hope for what is not seen is not a defect in the Christian life. It is the present tense of it. You are absent from the Lord in one way and possessed of His promise in another, and both are true until the thing hoped for arrives. Hebrews 11 is what that looks like in a thousand ordinary obediences.

4. Confessional Anchor

The confession will not let faith be a human stunt. Before it is substance of things hoped for, it is a grace wrought in the heart.

The grace of faith, whereby the elect are enabled to believe to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of baptism and the Lord’s Supper, prayer, and other means appointed of God, it is increased and strengthened.

1689 LBCF, ch. 14 — Of Saving Faith

Enabled to believe. The catalog in Hebrews 11 is not a hall of the spiritually gifted. It is a hall of the enabled. The same Word that tells you what is unseen is the ordinary means by which the faith that treats it as substance is given.

Then the acts of that faith, which Hebrews 11 spends forty verses illustrating:

By this faith a Christian believes to be true whatsoever is revealed in the Word, for the authority of God himself; and also apprehends an excellency therein above all other writings, and all things in the world; as it bears forth the glory of God in his attributes, the excellency of Christ in his nature and offices, and the power and fullness of the Holy Spirit in his workings and operations: and so is enabled to cast his soul upon the truth thus believed.

1689 LBCF, ch. 14 — Of Saving Faith

Cast his soul upon the truth thus believed is Abraham leaving, Moses refusing, and a tired Hebrew congregation not shrinking back. The confession cites this chapter for that casting. Faith in Hebrews is never less than that.

5. For the Man Reading This

Faith here is not your intensity. It is not how strongly you can ignore facts. The unseen things in this verse are not hunches. They are promised things. If God has not spoken, you do not get to baptize a wish by calling it Hebrews 11. If He has spoken, you do not get to wait until the thing is visible before you treat it as underneath you.

The practical test is what you do next, which is how the chapter tests it. Abel offered. Noah built. Abraham went. None of them felt their way into the act after the evidence arrived. The evidence, in this vocabulary, was the faith itself — the conviction that the unseen word was the solid thing in the room.

So take the two old English words seriously. Substance: you already have, in the promise, what you do not yet hold in your hand. Evidence: you are not guessing; you have been shown enough to move. The showing is the Word, and the Spirit who works faith by it. When the week looks like chapter 10 — patience, delay, the temptation to draw back — this verse is not asking you to feel finished. It is telling you what you are standing on while you wait, and then it will spend the rest of the chapter introducing you to people who died still waiting and were not wrong.
