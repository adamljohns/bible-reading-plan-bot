Southern Baptist Convention — How It Is Set Up

Directory explainer · not a church listing · last researched 2026-08-22

Directory explainer · not a church listing · last researched 2026-08-22

This page is for a man standing in a Southern Baptist church — or looking at one on this directory — who needs to know what the letters actually mean. The SBC is not a pope, a bishop, or a headquarters that hires your pastor. It is a voluntary cooperative of autonomous local churches that pool money and messengers so they can send missionaries, train pastors, and plant churches together.

The logo does not disciple your sons. The local church does. Use this page to understand the machine. Then go back to the congregation that actually knows your name.

Related on this site: Baptist Faith & Message · Accountability Structure · Church Directory · Spotswood Baptist (home-church example)

1. The one sentence that unlocks everything

The local church is autonomous. The Convention is a meeting. The entities are hired help that the messengers own.

  • Your pastor does not report to Nashville.
  • Nashville cannot fire your pastor, seize your building, or overturn a congregational vote.
  • Churches choose to cooperate. Cooperation is measured mainly by giving through the Cooperative Program and by sending messengers to the annual meeting.
  • Between meetings, an Executive Committee keeps the lights on. It does not write doctrine and it does not command NAMB, the IMB, or the seminaries.

Official source: SBC Governance · SBC FAQ

3. How the money moves — Cooperative Program

The Cooperative Program (CP), launched in 1925, is the unified giving plan.

  1. You give through your local church.
  2. Your church votes what percentage of undesignated receipts to send as CP.
  3. That check goes to the state convention.
  4. State messengers vote what percent stays in-state and what percent is forwarded to the national SBC.
  5. SBC messengers vote how the national slice is split among the entities (IMB, NAMB, six seminaries, ERLC, EC, etc.).

Why it exists: before 1925 every board ran its own pledge campaign. Entities competed, ran deficits, and lived on emergency appeals. CP is the “we can do more together than alone” fix.

Two big special offerings sit beside CP, not inside it:

  • Lottie Moon Christmas Offering → International Mission Board (overseas)
  • Annie Armstrong Easter Offering → North American Mission Board (U.S. & Canada)

A church can give to CP, to those offerings, to both, or (poorly) to neither and still put “SBC” on the sign. This directory cares which one they actually do.

6. State conventions — especially Virginia

State conventions are partners, not subsidiaries. A church almost always cooperates with both a state convention and the national SBC. The SBC “does not encourage churches to practice national-only cooperation” (SBC FAQ).

Most states have one Southern Baptist convention. A few have two recognized on the official SBC state-convention directory. The two you will hit in this directory:

7. Local associations

Under each state convention sit local associations — the old Baptist word for a cluster of nearby churches. They elect a director of missions (titles vary). They do not hire your pastor.

On the BGAV card alone, SBC.net lists dozens of Virginia associations (Dover, Rappahannock, NorthStar, The Bridge Network, Richmond, etc.). Fredericksburg-area churches may sit in different association names depending on which state convention they joined. If the church page does not name the association, do not invent one.

8. What “SBC” does not mean

From the Convention’s own FAQ, in plain speech:

  • The SBC does not ordain. Your church does.
  • The SBC does not assign pastors. There is no bishop sending a man to Lafayette Boulevard.
  • The SBC cannot discipline your pastor. Only his congregation can.
  • Resolutions are opinions of that year’s messengers, not canon law.
  • Calvinism / tongues / elders / Lord’s Supper fencing / membership rules are local-church calls. BF&M 2000 is the confessional center of gravity; it is not a Book of Church Order.

This directory still scores SBC churches. Autonomy is not a hall pass. If a cooperating church publicly denies the gospel, installs a woman as senior/lead pastor, or affirms what Scripture forbids, we rate the church, not the Nashville logo.

9. Confession — Baptist Faith & Message

The SBC’s shared doctrinal statement is the Baptist Faith & Message 2000. It is a convention confession, not a creed forced on every member. Entities and seminary faculties are expected to work in its lanes. Churches “cooperate” rather than “subscribe” the way a Presbyterian session subscribes Westminster.

This site already hosts the text and a comparison:

  • usmcmin.org/bfm/ — 1925 · 1963 · 2000, plus our study notes
  • Official: bfm.sbc.net

For men on this directory, the articles that most often decide a score are Scripture (I), God / Christ (II), Man and gender (III + XVIII), Salvation (IV), The Church and the pastoral office (VI).

10. How to read an SBC church on this directory

  1. Pastor name — the shepherd God holds accountable. Not the state exec. Not Willy Rice.
  2. State convention letters — SBCV vs BGAV matters in Virginia.
  3. CP / missions evidence — do they actually give, or just wear the jersey?
  4. BF&M 2000 in practice — especially male pastoral office and sexual ethics.
  5. Local fruit — men’s discipleship, preaching, discipline. The brand will not raise your sons.

If you only remember one line: SBC is a cooperative, not a covering.
