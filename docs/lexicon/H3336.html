H3336 · Hebrew · Old Testament
יֵצֶר
yêtser
frame, thing framed, imagination, mind, work.

Definition

yêtser (יֵצֶר) is Hebrew Strong's H3336. Strong's defines it as a form; figuratively, conception (i.e. purpose). Strong's traces the form: from H3335 (יָצַר). The Authorised Version renders it frame, thing framed, imagination, mind, work. The tagged KJV carries this code 9 times, across 6 books: Genesis, Deuteronomy, 1 Chronicles, Psalms, Isaiah, Habakkuk.

Usage & Theological Significance

Every verse below is one the tagged KJV marks with H3336, so each is a place the Hebrew word itself stands behind the English rather than a translator's paraphrase. The first is Genesis 6:5. The code is spread over 6 books, which is worth weighing before treating any single rendering as the whole sense of the word. Where the Authorised Version gives more than one English word for it, that difference is the translators' judgement about context, not a second Hebrew word underneath.

Key Bible Verses

Genesis 6:5 And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually. every: or, the whole imagination: the Hebrew word signifieth not only the imagination, but also the purposes and desires continually: Heb. every day
