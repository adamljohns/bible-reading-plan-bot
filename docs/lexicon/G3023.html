G3023 · Greek · New Testament
λέων
leōn
Noun, masculine
a lion

Definition

The Greek noun leōn means a lion — the great hunting cat, not a mascot. In 1 Peter the word is a likeness for the devil: not a house cat, a predator that walks and seeks. KJV renders it lion. The point of the picture is teeth and hunger, not nobility.

Usage & Theological Significance

Hebrews 11:33: through faith they stopped the mouths of lions. 2 Timothy 4:17: Paul was delivered out of the mouth of the lion — imminent death, not a circus. 1 Peter 5:8: the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about. Revelation uses the same noun for the first living creature like a lion (4:7), the Lion of the tribe of Juda (5:5), teeth as of lions (9:8), heads as of lions (9:17), and a voice as when a lion roareth (10:3). One word holds both the devourer and the One who opens the book.

Revelation 10:3 And cried with a loud voice, as when a lion roareth: and when he had cried, seven thunders uttered their voices.
