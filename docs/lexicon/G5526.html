G5526 · Greek · New Testament
χορτάζω
chortazō
Verb
to fill; to feed until satisfied

Definition

The Greek verb chortazō means to fill — to feed a hungry man until the ache is gone. It is the language of a trough and a table, not of a nibble. KJV renders it filled. In the Beatitude the filling is God’s answer to hunger and thirst after righteousness. The empty man is not left empty.

Usage & Theological Significance

Matthew 5:6 promises that they which hunger and thirst after righteousness shall be filled. Luke 6:21 uses the same verb for those who hunger now: ye shall be filled. Philippians 4:12 then uses it for ordinary fullness: I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry. James 2:16 exposes the empty blessing that says be ye warmed and filled while giving nothing. The word therefore names both God’s real satisfaction and a man’s cheap talk. Only one of those actually feeds.

Key Bible Verses

Matthew 5:6 Blessed are they which do hunger and thirst after righteousness: for they shall be filled.
Luke 6:21 Blessed are ye that hunger now: for ye shall be filled. Blessed are ye that weep now: for ye shall laugh.
Philippians 4:12 I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: every where and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need.
James 2:16 And one of you say unto them, Depart in peace, be ye warmed and filled; notwithstanding ye give them not those things which are needful to the body; what doth it profit?
