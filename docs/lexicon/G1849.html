G1849 · Greek · New Testament
ἐξουσία
Exousia
Noun, feminine
Authority, delegated right

Definition

The Greek noun exousia means authority in the sense of a right that has been granted, not merely force that can be applied. Strong's spreads it across privilege, capacity, competency, freedom and mastery, and the KJV renders it authority, jurisdiction, liberty, power, right and strength. The distinction worth holding onto is that exousia is delegated: it answers the question of who gave it rather than how strong the holder is. A magistrate has it because of his office, and loses it when the office ends.

Usage & Theological Significance

Matthew introduces the word as the thing that set Jesus apart from the scribes, because He taught as one having authority and not as they did (Matthew 7:29). The centurion then supplies the definition from his own trade, explaining that he too is a man under authority with soldiers under him, and that a word is therefore enough (Matthew 8:9) — he grasps the chain of command better than the disciples do. Jesus claims the same right over what only God may do, saying the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins (Matthew 9:6), and the crowd glorifies God for giving such power unto men (Matthew 9:8). He then hands it on, giving the twelve authority against unclean spirits and over disease (Matthew 10:1), which is the pattern: delegated authority delegated further. The chief priests eventually challenge it directly, demanding to know by what authority He acts and who gave it to Him (Matthew 21:23), and He declines to answer men who will not answer about John. The question is finally settled after the resurrection, when He says all power is given unto Him in heaven and in earth (Matthew 28:18) — and only then does the Great Commission follow, because the commission rests on the authority rather than the other way round.

Matthew 7:29
For he taught them as one having authority, and not as the scribes. (KJV)

For I am a man under authority, having soldiers under me: and I say to this man, Go, and he goeth; and to another, Come, and he cometh; and to my servant, Do this, and he doeth it. (KJV)
Matthew 9:6
But that ye may know that the Son of man hath power on earth to forgive sins, (then saith he to the sick of the palsy,) Arise, take up thy bed, and go unto thine house. (KJV)

But when the multitudes saw it, they marvelled, and glorified God, which had given such power unto men. (KJV)

And when he had called unto him his twelve disciples, he gave them power against unclean spirits, to cast them out, and to heal all manner of sickness and all manner of disease. against: or, over (KJV)

And when he was come into the temple, the chief priests and the elders of the people came unto him as he was teaching, and said, By what authority doest thou these things? and who gave thee this authority? (KJV)
