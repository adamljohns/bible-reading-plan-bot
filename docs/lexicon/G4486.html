G4486 · Greek · New Testament
ῥήγνυμι
rhḗgnymi
break (forth), burst, rend, tear

Definition

rhḗgnymi (ῥήγνυμι) is Greek Strong's G4486. Strong's defines it as G2608 (κατάγνυμι) being its intensive (with the preposition in composition), and G2352 (θραύω) a shattering to minute fragments; but not a reduction to the constituent particles, like G3089 (λύω)) or disrupt, lacerate; by implication, to convulse (with spasms); figuratively, to give vent to joyful emotions. Strong's traces the form: both prolonged forms of (which appears only in certain forms, and is itself probably a strengthened form of (see in G2608 (κατάγνυμι))) to "break," "wreck" or "crack", i.e. (especially) to sunder (by separation of the parts. The Authorised Version renders it break (forth), burst, rend, tear. The tagged KJV carries this code 7 times, across 4 books: Matthew, Mark, Luke, Galatians.

Usage & Theological Significance

Every verse below is one the tagged KJV marks with G4486, so each is a place the Greek word itself stands behind the English rather than a translator's paraphrase. The first is Matthew 7:6. The code is spread over 4 books, which is worth weighing before treating any single rendering as the whole sense of the word. Where the Authorised Version gives more than one English word for it, that difference is the translators' judgement about context, not a second Greek word underneath.
