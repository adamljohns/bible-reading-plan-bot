H4687 · Hebrew · Old Testament
מִצְוָה
Mitsvah
Noun, feminine
Commandment, precept

Definition

The Hebrew noun mitsvah means a command, and Strong's notes it covers the human and the divine alike, and collectively stands for the Law itself. It is built from the verb tsavah, to charge or enjoin, so the noun carries the weight of the one issuing it rather than the preference of the one receiving it. The KJV renders it commandment, law, ordinance and precept. A mitsvah is not advice that happens to come from God; it is an obligation whose force is the character of the God who gave it.

Usage & Theological Significance

Kings is where the word is used to keep score. David's charge to Solomon is to keep the statutes and the commandments as written in the law of Moses, and the promise attached is that he may prosper in all that he doeth (1 Kings 2:3). When Solomon is given a long life on condition, the condition is the same: if thou wilt walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as thy father David did walk (1 Kings 3:14). The condition is repeated over the temple itself, so that even the house built for God's name is held to it (1 Kings 6:12). And the failure is measured by it too — the kingdom is torn from Solomon's son because he kept not the commandments, while David is remembered as the one who kept them and followed God with all his heart (1 Kings 11:34, 11:38). Elijah's charge against Ahab lands on the same word: ye have forsaken the commandments of the LORD (1 Kings 18:18). The commandments function throughout as the visible test of an invisible loyalty.

Key Bible Verses

1 Kings 2:3
And keep the charge of the LORD thy God, to walk in his ways, to keep his statutes, and his commandments, and his judgments, and his testimonies, as it is written in the law of Moses, that thou mayest prosper in all that thou doest, and whithersoever thou turnest thyself: prosper: or, do wisely (KJV)
1 Kings 3:14
And if thou wilt walk in my ways, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as thy father David did walk, then I will lengthen thy days. (KJV)
1 Kings 6:12
Concerning this house which thou art in building, if thou wilt walk in my statutes, and execute my judgments, and keep all my commandments to walk in them; then will I perform my word with thee, which I spake unto David thy father: (KJV)
1 Kings 11:34
Howbeit I will not take the whole kingdom out of his hand: but I will make him prince all the days of his life for David my servant's sake, whom I chose, because he kept my commandments and my statutes: (KJV)
1 Kings 11:38
And it shall be, if thou wilt hearken unto all that I command thee, and wilt walk in my ways, and do that is right in my sight, to keep my statutes and my commandments, as David my servant did; that I will be with thee, and build thee a sure house, as I built for David, and will give Israel unto thee. (KJV)

And he answered, I have not troubled Israel; but thou, and thy father's house, in that ye have forsaken the commandments of the LORD, and thou hast followed Baalim. (KJV)
