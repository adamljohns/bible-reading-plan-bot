H2974 · Hebrew · Old Testament
יָאַל
Yaal
Verb
To Be Willing / To Undertake

Definition

The Hebrew verb yaal means to show willingness or resolve, to undertake something, or to be pleased to act. Often translated 'was pleased' or 'was willing,' it captures the voluntary commitment of the will. It frequently introduces significant turning points — moments where someone chooses to stay, rise, or act.

Usage & Theological Significance

Yaal appears at pivotal moments of willing service. In Exodus 2:21, Moses 'was willing' to dwell with Jethro. Psalm 110:3: 'a people willing in the day of your power.' The word points toward the willing heart God desires — 'a people willing,' anticipating the Son who willingly laid down his life (John 10:18).

Key Bible Verses

Exodus 2:21 Moses agreed (was willing) to stay with the man, who gave him Zipporah in marriage.
Psalm 110:3 Your troops will be willing on your day of battle, arrayed in holy splendor.
2 Kings 5:23 'Be pleased to take them.' And he urged him, and tied two talents of silver.
Joshua 7:7 And Joshua said, 'Alas, Sovereign LORD, why did you ever bring this people across the Jordan?'
1 Samuel 17:39 'I cannot go in these,' he said to Saul, 'because I am not used to them.' So he took them off.
