H2814 · Hebrew · Old Testament
חָשָׁה
Chashah
Verb
To Be Silent; To Still; To Hold Peace

Definition

The Hebrew chashah means to be silent, to keep still, to hold peace, or to hush. It describes a deliberate quieting — whether of oneself before God, of God's people in response to divine command, or the stilling of turmoil. The word appears in moments of dramatic restraint: when silence is the right and holy response. It is related to but distinct from damam (H1826, to be still/dumb) and shaqat (H8252, to be quiet).

Usage & Theological Significance

The theology of chashah is the theology of reverential silence before God. Habakkuk 2:20 — 'The LORD is in his holy temple; let all the earth be silent before him' — uses this word. When God is present and speaking, the appropriate human response is not chatter but hushed attentiveness. Isaiah 41:1 opens with God's summons to the nations: 'Be silent (chashah) before me, you islands!' — a call to stop their striving and hear the divine word.

Yet chashah is not only passive. Job uses it of himself holding his peace rather than arguing with God (Job 13:13), and Nehemiah commands the people to be still when they weep at the public reading of the Law (Nehemiah 8:11). True silence is active — choosing restraint over reaction, trust over anxiety. It is the silence of those who know that God is in control and His word is sufficient.

Key Bible Verses

Habakkuk 2:20 The LORD is in his holy temple; let all the earth be silent before him.
Isaiah 41:1 Be silent before me, you islands! Let the nations renew their strength! Let them come forward and speak; let us meet together at the place of judgment.
Job 13:13 Keep silent and let me speak; then let come to me what may.
Nehemiah 8:11 The Levites calmed all the people, saying, 'Be still, for this is a holy day. Do not grieve.'
Psalm 46:10 He says, 'Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the nations, I will be exalted in the earth.'
