H3407 · Hebrew · Old Testament
יְרִיעָה
yeriah
Noun, feminine
Curtain, tent-cloth

Definition

Yeriah is a curtain or tent-cloth, occurring about fifty-four times and belonging above all to the tabernacle: the ten linen curtains of the dwelling and the goats' hair curtains spread over them (Exodus 26). The root carries the sense of trembling or quivering, which suits a cloth that moves in the wind. It is a different word from Yareach, the moon (H3394), which it resembles only when both are written in English letters.

Usage & Theological Significance

The curtains are the fabric of the place where God consented to live among His people, and Scripture treats them as worth describing thread by thread. The image then travels. In Psalm 104:2 the same word goes cosmic: God stretches out the heavens like a tent-curtain, so the sky is the canvas of His tent and creation is His dwelling. In Isaiah 54:2 the barren woman is told to lengthen her curtains, because her household is about to outgrow the tent. And in Jeremiah 4:20 the sudden ruin of the land is a tent whose curtains are destroyed in a moment. One word holds the tabernacle, the sky, the growing church and the fragility of everything made of cloth.

Key Bible Verses

Exodus 26:1 Moreover thou shalt make the tabernacle with ten curtains of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet : with cherubims of cunning work shalt thou make them. of cunning: Heb. the work of a cunning workman, or, embroiderer
Psalm 104:2 Who coverest thyself with light as with a garment: who stretchest out the heavens like a curtain:
Isaiah 54:2 Enlarge the place of thy tent, and let them stretch forth the curtains of thine habitations: spare not, lengthen thy cords, and strengthen thy stakes;
Jeremiah 4:20 Destruction upon destruction is cried; for the whole land is spoiled: suddenly are my tents spoiled, and my curtains in a moment.
Song of Solomon 1:5 I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Kedar, as the curtains of Solomon.
