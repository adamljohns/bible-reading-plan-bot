H5275 · Hebrew · Old Testament
נַעַל
naʻal
dryshod, (pair of) shoe((-latchet), -s).

Definition

naʻal (נַעַל) is Hebrew Strong's H5275. Strong's defines it as properly, a sandal tongue; by extension a sandal or slipper (sometimes as a symbol of occupancy, a refusal to marry, or of something valueless). Strong's traces the form: or (feminine) נַעֲלָה; from H5274 (נָעַל). The Authorised Version renders it dryshod, (pair of) shoe((-latchet), -s). The tagged KJV carries this code 22 times, across 11 books: Genesis, Exodus, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Ruth, 1 Kings, Psalms, Song Of Solomon, and others.

Usage & Theological Significance

Every verse below is one the tagged KJV marks with H5275, so each is a place the Hebrew word itself stands behind the English rather than a translator's paraphrase. The first is Genesis 14:23. The code is spread over 11 books, which is worth weighing before treating any single rendering as the whole sense of the word. Where the Authorised Version gives more than one English word for it, that difference is the translators' judgement about context, not a second Hebrew word underneath.
