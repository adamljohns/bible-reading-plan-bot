G4163 · Greek · New Testament
ποιητής
poiētēs
Noun, masculine
a doer; also a poet

Definition

The Greek noun poiētēs is a maker — from poieō, to do or make. In ordinary Greek it can mean a poet. In the tagged KJV it is doer five times and poets once (Acts 17:28). The word names the man who performs the law or the word, not the man who only hears it.

Usage & Theological Significance

Acts 17:28 is the secular sense: as certain also of your own poets have said. Romans 2:13 sets the moral sense: not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the doers of the law shall be justified. James drives it home: be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only (1:22); a hearer and not a doer is like a man beholding his face in a glass (1:23); the man that is a doer of the work shall be blessed (1:25); he that speaks evil of the law is not a doer of the law (4:11). Hearing without doing is self-deceit. The noun will not let a man stop at the ear.

Key Bible Verses

Acts 17:28 For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring.
Romans 2:13 (For not the hearers of the law are just before God, but the doers of the law shall be justified.
James 1:22 But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.
James 1:23 For if any be a hearer of the word, and not a doer, he is like unto a man beholding his natural face in a glass:
James 1:25 But whoso looketh into the perfect law of liberty, and continueth therein, he being not a forgetful hearer, but a doer of the work, this man shall be blessed in his deed.
James 4:11 Speak not evil one of another, brethren. He that speaketh evil of his brother, and judgeth his brother, speaketh evil of the law, and judgeth the law: but if thou judge the law, thou art not a doer of the law, but a judge.
