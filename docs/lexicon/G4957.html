G4957 · Greek · New Testament
συσταυρόω
systauroō
Verb
to crucify together with; to nail with another

Definition

The Greek verb systauroō means to crucify together with — to nail a man to the same cross as another. It is syn (with) plus stauroō (to crucify). KJV in Galatians 2:20 renders it I am crucified with. Paul does not say he admires the cross. He says he was put on it with Christ. The old “I” is not improved. It is executed. What lives after that sentence is not the same man with a better plan.

Usage & Theological Significance

Galatians 2:20 is the tagged use this desk confirmed. I am crucified with Christ: nevertheless I live; yet not I, but Christ liveth in me: and the life which I now live in the flesh I live by the faith of the Son of God, who loved me, and gave himself for me. The verb is perfect in force: the crucifixion with Christ is a finished fact that still defines the man. The next clauses refuse two errors. He is not a corpse. He is not the old self running the house. Christ lives in him. The life in the flesh is by faith in the Son who loved him and gave Himself. A man who wants Christ as helper and himself as the surviving boss has not taken this verb. The “I” that still negotiates with sin is claiming a life this sentence already ended.

Key Bible Verses
