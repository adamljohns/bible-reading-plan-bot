G4137 · Greek · New Testament
πληρόω
Plēroō
Verb
To fill, to fulfil

Definition

The Greek verb pleroo means to fill something completely — Strong's ranges from cramming a net and levelling up a hollow to finishing a task, executing an office, and verifying a prediction. The KJV accordingly renders it fill, fill up, complete, end, accomplish, perfect, supply and fulfil. The physical image governs the theological one. To fulfil a prophecy is not merely to satisfy a technicality; it is to fill a shape that was standing empty until the thing arrived that fit it.

Usage & Theological Significance

Matthew uses the word as the hinge of his whole argument, and he uses it relentlessly. The virgin birth happens that it might be fulfilled which was spoken of the Lord by the prophet (Matthew 1:22). The flight to Egypt fulfils Hosea (Matthew 2:15), the slaughter at Bethlehem fulfils Jeremiah's Rachel weeping for her children (Matthew 2:17), and the move to Nazareth fulfils what was spoken by the prophets (Matthew 2:23). The pattern is not decorative. Matthew is showing that the shapes were cut long before, and that one life fits all of them. The plainest non-prophetic use sits in a parable and quietly explains the rest: the net is cast into the sea, and when it was full they drew it to shore (Matthew 13:48) — filled to capacity, then hauled in and sorted. At the arrest the two senses meet, when Jesus asks how then shall the scriptures be fulfilled, that thus it must be (Matthew 26:54), and answers His own question a moment later: all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled (Matthew 26:56). He is not a victim of the text. He is filling it.
