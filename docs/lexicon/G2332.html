G2332 · Greek · New Testament
Θεσσαλονίκη
Thessalonikē
Noun, feminine · place name
Thessalonica — the Macedonian city on the Via Egnatia

Definition

The Greek name Thessalonikē is the city of Thessalonica — a Macedonian port on the Via Egnatia, capital of the Roman province. KJV keeps the English form Thessalonica. In Acts the name is not scenery. It is the first stop after Amphipolis and Apollonia, the synagogue where Paul reasoned three Sabbaths, and the contrast Luke uses when he praises Berea. A church can be named after a city and still fail the city’s test.

Usage & Theological Significance

Acts 17:1 is the arrival: they came to Thessalonica, where was a synagogue of the Jews. Acts 17:11 then uses the city as the measuring stick: these were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily. The Bereans are praised against this name. Philippians 4:16 remembers the same city from the other side of the ledger: even in Thessalonica ye sent once and again unto my necessity. The place that rioted also received gifts. A man who only knows Thessalonica as “the bad church in Acts 17” has not read the thank-you in Philippians. The city is a real street, a real synagogue, and a real test of whether a man will search the Book or raise a mob.

Key Bible Verses

Acts 17:1 Now when they had passed through Amphipolis and Apollonia, they came to Thessalonica, where was a synagogue of the Jews.
Acts 17:11 These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so.
Philippians 4:16 For even in Thessalonica ye sent once and again unto my necessity.
