H1826 · Hebrew · Old Testament
דָּמַם
Damam
Verb
To be silent, still, or cease

Definition

The Hebrew verb damam means to be silent, to cease, to rest still, or to be dumbfounded. It can express voluntary quietness before God as well as stunned speechlessness in the face of divine majesty.

Usage & Theological Significance

Damam carries the idea of a profound and reverent silence — the hush that falls when God acts or speaks. In Psalm 37:7, it is the posture of waiting silently for the LORD. It also describes death's silence (Psalm 31:17) and the speechlessness of judgment. Theologically, damam calls the believer to stop striving and trust God's sovereignty.

Key Bible Verses

Psalm 37:7 Be still (damam) before the LORD and wait patiently for him; do not fret when people succeed in their ways.
Lamentations 3:26 It is good to wait quietly (damam) for the salvation of the LORD.
Psalm 4:4 Tremble and do not sin; when you are on your beds, search your hearts and be silent (damam).
Amos 5:13 Therefore the prudent keep quiet (damam) in such times, for the times are evil.
Habakkuk 2:20 The LORD is in his holy temple; let all the earth be silent (damam) before him.
