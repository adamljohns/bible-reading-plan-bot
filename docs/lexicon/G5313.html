G5313 · Greek · New Testament
ὕψωμα
hypsōma
Noun, neuter
a height; a high thing raised up

Definition

The Greek noun hypsōma is a thing elevated — a height. From hypsoō, to lift up. The tagged KJV uses it twice: height in Romans 8:39 and high thing in 2 Corinthians 10:5. It can name space itself, opposite depth, or a raised structure — a rampart of the mind against the knowledge of God.

Usage & Theological Significance

Romans 8:39: Nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord. Here the word is cosmic scale. Nothing high enough can cut a man off from that love. 2 Corinthians 10:5 turns the same noun into a fortress: Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God. The height that cannot separate the saint is not the height that may still be raised in his own thoughts. One is creation’s reach; the other is pride’s wall.

Key Bible Verses
