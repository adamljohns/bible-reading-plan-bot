H251 · Hebrew · Old Testament
אָח
Ach
Noun, masculine
Brother, kinsman

Definition

The Hebrew noun ach means brother, and Scripture stretches it as far as the relationship will go. It covers the son of one's own parents, the member of one's tribe, the fellow Israelite bound by the same covenant, and the ally joined by treaty — Strong's notes the word is used of literal relationship and of metaphorical affinity alike. The KJV renders it brother, kindred, another and other, and the range is not looseness but a settled Hebrew conviction that obligation follows kinship outward.

Usage & Theological Significance

Because brotherhood carries duty, the Old Testament's worst betrayals are told as violations of it. Amnon plots against Tamar with the help of Jonadab, the son of Shimeah David's brother, and the narrator keeps naming the kinship as the outrage accumulates (2 Samuel 13:3-4). Tamar is sent to her brother Amnon's house precisely because a brother's house should have been the safest place for her (2 Samuel 13:7-8). Set against that ruin is David's lament for Jonathan, who was no blood relation at all: I am distressed for thee, my brother Jonathan (2 Samuel 1:26). Covenant had made them brothers, and the word does the work. The same logic runs through Israel's law, where the poor man, the debtor and the runaway slave are each called thy brother so that the hearer cannot treat them as strangers, and it is the logic the New Testament inherits when it calls the church a brotherhood rather than an association.
