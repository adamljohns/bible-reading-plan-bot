G5578 · Greek · New Testament
ψευδοπροφήτης
Pseudoprophetes
Noun (masculine)
False Prophet

Definition

One who falsely claims the prophetic office — a counterfeit voice pretending to speak for God. Compound of pseudes (false) + prophetes (prophet, forth-teller).

Theological Significance

Jesus warns of false prophets coming in sheep's clothing while inwardly being ravenous wolves (Matthew 7:15), and predicts their multiplication in the last days alongside counterfeit signs and wonders (Matthew 24:11, 24; Mark 13:22). John commands believers to test every spirit, since many false prophets have gone out into the world (1 John 4:1). 2 Peter 2:1 sets the historical pattern — false prophets among ancient Israel, false teachers among the church. In Revelation, "the false prophet" appears alongside the dragon and the beast as the third member of the satanic counterfeit-trinity, finally cast into the lake of fire (Revelation 16:13; 19:20; 20:10).

Discernment Test

Scripture gives three converging tests: prophetic accuracy (Deuteronomy 18:22), doctrinal fidelity to revealed truth (Deuteronomy 13:1–5), and the fruit produced by the prophet's life and followers (Matthew 7:16–20). A "false prophet" is not merely someone who got a prediction wrong; the term marks someone whose message and life pull people away from the true God.
