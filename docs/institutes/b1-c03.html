Book 1 · Chapter 3 · USMC modern English

The Knowledge of God Naturally Implanted in the Human Mind

▶ Listen — AI narration by Mr. Pemberton (modernized for 2026)
Sections in this chapter
  1. 1. The knowledge of God being manifested to all makes the reprobate without excuse. Universal belief and acknowledgement of the existence of God.
  2. 2. Objection—that religion and the belief of a Deity are the inventions of crafty politicians. Refutation of the objection. This universal belief confirmed by the examples of wicked men and Atheists.
  3. 3. Confirmed also by the vain endeavours of the wicked to banish all fear of God from their minds. Conclusion, that the knowledge of God is naturally implanted in the human mind.
1¶

We hold it beyond dispute that there exists in the human mind, and indeed by natural instinct, some sense of God. To keep anyone from pleading ignorance, God himself has given to every person some awareness of his own divine majesty. He constantly refreshes this awareness, and from time to time he enlarges it, so that everyone, knowing there is a God and that he is their Maker, may stand condemned by their own conscience when they neither worship him nor devote their lives to his service.

Surely, if there is anywhere we might expect God to be unknown, it would be among the most primitive tribes, farthest removed from civilization. Yet even a pagan writer tells us that there is no nation so barbarous, no people so savage, that they are not gripped by the conviction that there is a God. Those who in all other respects seem to differ little from the beasts still keep some seed of religion. So deeply does this common conviction take hold of every mind, and so firmly is it fixed in the heart of all. Since from the beginning of the world there has been no region, no city, indeed no household that could do without religion, this amounts to a tacit confession that a sense of God is inscribed on every heart.

2¶

It is therefore utterly absurd to claim, as some do, that religion was invented by the cunning and craft of a few clever men in order to keep ordinary people in line, while these very inventors, who taught others to worship God, believed least of all that any God exists. I readily grant that crafty men have introduced a great many fictions into religion in order to fill the common people with reverence, or to strike them with terror, and so make them more compliant. But they could never have succeeded in this if the minds of men had not already been filled with that settled conviction of God, which is the very seed from which religious feeling springs.

And it is altogether unbelievable that those who, in matters of religion, slyly imposed on their more ignorant neighbors were themselves wholly without any knowledge of God. For though in ancient times there were some, and even today there are not a few, who deny that God exists, yet whether they like it or not, they keep feeling again and again the very truth they wish they did not know. We do not read of anyone who broke out into more unbridled and reckless contempt of God than the emperor Caligula; yet no one trembled more wretchedly when any sign of God's anger appeared. So, against his own will, he shuddered before the God whom he openly tried to despise. You may see this same thing happening again and again in people like him. The boldest scoffer at God is the one most shaken by the rustling of a falling leaf. What accounts for this, except that God's majesty takes its revenge, and the more they try to flee from it, the more sharply it strikes their consciences?

They cast about for every possible hiding place where they might escape the presence of the Lord, and wipe it from their minds. But struggle as they will, they are still held fast. Though the awareness of God may sometimes seem to fade for a moment, it soon returns and breaks out again with fresh force. If for them this is any relief from anxiety, it is like falling into a brief, troubled sleep that is forever interrupted by terrors. Even the wicked themselves, then, serve as an example that some sense of God is always alive in every human mind.

3¶

All people of sound judgment will therefore agree that a sense of God is permanently engraved on the human heart. That this conviction is born naturally in everyone, and is fixed deep within us as though in our very bones, is strikingly proved by the stubbornness of the wicked. For all their furious struggling, they cannot break free from the fear of God.

Diagoras and others like him may make a joke of whatever religion the whole world has ever believed; Dionysius the tyrant may mock the judgment of heaven; but theirs is the laughter of madmen, for the worm of conscience, sharper than any branding iron, keeps gnawing at them within. I am not saying what Cicero said, that errors fade with time while true religion grows from age to age. For the world labors as hard as it can to shake off all knowledge of God, and to corrupt his worship in every possible way. I am only pointing out that the dull and stubborn insensitivity which the wicked long to have, as a way of despising God, still keeps working against them. They would dearly love to abolish all sense of God, yet that sense, which they wish were utterly extinguished, remains alive and breaks out again and again. From this we conclude that the knowledge of God is not something a person may freely accept or reject, but a conviction implanted in us by nature itself, one which cannot be uprooted without destroying our very humanity.

A Word for 2026 · U.S.M.C. Ministries

Calvin argues that no man is truly an atheist; God has stamped a sense of his own Godhead on every conscience, a seed that crafty politicians could exploit but never invent, and that even the most defiant cannot wholly shake. The fear of God surfaces in the scoffer despite himself. This is bracing for 2026, an age that wears unbelief as sophistication and assumes faith is a relic to be argued away. It is not. The man who insists there is no God is testifying against himself; his very rage betrays the truth he is fleeing. So do not let your children be cowed into thinking the believer is the odd one and the skeptic the default — the skeptic is suppressing what he knows. Yet bare awareness of God saves no one; the demons believe and tremble. Let this universal witness make us patient with neighbors who protest too loudly, and watchful that our own knowledge ripens past mere acknowledgment into worship and a consecrated life.
