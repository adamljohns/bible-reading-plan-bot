All the Principles of Piety Subverted by Fanatics, Who Substitute Revelations for Scripture

The Spirit of Christ seals the doctrine of the written Word on the minds of the godly.
  2. 2. Refutation continued. 3. The impositions of Satan cannot be detected without the aid of the written Word. First Objection. The Answer to it.

1¶

Those who reject Scripture and imagine that they have some special way of breaking through to God are to be judged as men driven not so much by error as by madness. For certain giddy men — Lactantius writes of such that they have corrupted the heavenly writings, so as to piece together for themselves a new doctrine with no root and no stability. See Calvin's Instruction against the Libertines, chapters 9 and 10.

Where the Latin reads "as if he were a raw beginner," the French has it this way: as if they were little children at their A, B, C.

2¶

From this it is easy to see that we must give careful attention both to the reading and to the hearing of Scripture, if we want to gain any benefit at all from the Spirit of God — just as Peter commends those who study the teaching of the prophets attentively (2 Peter 1:19), even though it might have been supposed that such study was set aside once the light of the gospel had dawned. And it is just as easy to see, on the other hand, that any spirit which bypasses the wisdom of God's Word and puts forward some other teaching is rightly suspected of emptiness and falsehood. Since Satan transforms himself into an angel of light, what authority can the Spirit have with us if he is not identified by some unfailing mark? And he is certainly pointed out to us by the Lord clearly enough; yet these wretched men go astray as though they were bent on their own ruin, since they look for the Spirit within themselves rather than from him. They object that it is an insult to place the Spirit, to whom all things ought to be subject, under the Scripture — as if it were a disgrace to the Holy Spirit to keep a perfect likeness throughout, and to be in every respect, without the slightest variation, consistent with himself. It is true that if he were made subject to a human standard, or an angelic one, or any standard outside himself, one might think he had been made subordinate, or, if you like, brought into bondage. But so long as he is compared with himself and considered in himself, how can anyone say that he is thereby wronged? I grant that he is brought to a test, but it is the very test by which he himself has been pleased to have his majesty confirmed. It ought to be enough for us once we have heard his voice; but so that Satan may not worm his way in under his name, he wants us to recognize him by the image he has stamped upon the Scriptures. The author of the Scriptures cannot shift and alter his likeness. As he first appeared there, so he will remain forever. There is nothing insulting to him in this, unless we are to suppose it would somehow be to his honor to degenerate and rebel against himself.

3¶

Their complaint that we cling to the dead letter carries within it the very punishment they deserve for despising Scripture. It is clear that Paul is there arguing against false apostles (2 Corinthians 3:6) who, by commending the law apart from Christ, robbed the people of the benefit of the New Covenant, in which the Lord pledges that he will write his law on the hearts of believers and engrave it on their inward parts. The letter, therefore, is dead, and the law of the Lord kills those who read it, when it is cut off from the grace of Christ and merely sounds in the ear without touching the heart. But if it is effectually pressed upon the heart by the Spirit, and if it sets Christ before us, then it is the word of life, converting the soul and making wise the simple. Indeed, in that very same passage the apostle calls his own preaching the ministration of the Spirit (2 Corinthians 3:8), indicating that the Holy Spirit clings so closely to his own truth, as he has set it down in Scripture, that he exerts and puts forth his strength only when the word is received with the honor and reverence that are due to it.

There is nothing here at odds with what was said a little earlier (chapter 7), that we have no firm certainty of the word itself until it is confirmed by the testimony of the Spirit. For the Lord has knit together the certainty of his word and of his Spirit so closely that our minds are rightly filled with reverence for the word when the Spirit, shining upon it, lets us behold the face of God there; and on the other hand, we embrace the Spirit with no danger of being deceived when we recognize him in his own image — that is, in his word. So indeed it stands. God did not set his word before men for the sake of a sudden display, meaning to do away with it the moment the Spirit should arrive; rather, he used that same Spirit, through whose working he had delivered the word, to finish his work by powerfully confirming that word. In this way Christ made it clear to the two disciples (Luke 24:27) not that they were to cast aside the Scriptures and lean on their own wisdom, but that they were to understand the Scriptures. In the same way, when Paul says to the Thessalonians, "Quench not the Spirit," he does not lift them up into empty speculation apart from the word; he immediately adds, "Despise not prophesying" (1 Thessalonians 5:19, 20). By this he surely means that the light of the Spirit is quenched the moment prophesying is held in contempt. What answer have those puffed-up enthusiasts to this — men in whose view the only true illumination lies in carelessly setting the Word of God aside and bidding it farewell, while with no less confidence than folly they seize upon whatever dreamy notion happens to have sprung up in their minds? Surely a very different sober-mindedness befits the children of God. Just as they are aware that without the Spirit of God they are utterly without the light of truth, so they are not ignorant that the word is the instrument by which the Spirit's illumination is given out. They know of no other Spirit than the one who dwelt and spoke in the apostles — the Spirit by whose own utterances they are daily invited to the hearing of the word.

A Word for 2026 · Uniting, Serving, Mentoring and Counseling Ministries

Against the fanatics who set aside Scripture and chase private revelations, Calvin lays down a rule we dare not lose — the Spirit and the Word are knit together by God himself, never to be torn apart; the Spirit we are to follow is the one who spoke in the prophets and apostles, recognized by the very image he stamped on the page. Our generation has its own enthusiasts, no less confident: the impression that felt like a leading, the inner voice baptized as guidance, the conviction that maturity means moving beyond the old written Word into something fresher. Satan transforms himself into an angel of light, and a spirit that bypasses Scripture is rightly suspected of vanity. Try every prompting by the Word, for that is the test God himself appointed. The letter without the Spirit kills, but the Spirit never contradicts what he has written. Quench not the Spirit, Paul says — then in the same breath, despise not prophesying. Keep them married in your home, and you will not be led astray.
