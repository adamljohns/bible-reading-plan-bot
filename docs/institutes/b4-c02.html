Book 4 · Chapter 2 · USMC modern English

Comparison Between the False Church and the True

The divisions of the chapter are,—I. Description of a spurious Church, resembling the Papacy vaunting of personal succession, of which a refutation is subjoined. sec. 1-4. II. An answer, in name of the orthodox Churches, to the Popish accusations of heresy and schism. A description of the Churches existing at present under the Papacy. 1. Recapitulation of the matters treated in the previous chapter. Substance of the present chapter—viz. Where lying and falsehood prevail, no Church exists. There is falsehood wherever the pure doctrine of Christ is not in vigour. 2. This falsehood prevails under the Papacy. Hence the Papacy is not a Church. Still the Papists extol their own Church, and charge those who dissent from it with heresy and schism. They attempt to defend their vaunting by the name of personal succession. A succession which abandons the truth of Christ proved to be of no importance. 3. This proof confirmed, 1. By examples and passages of Scripture; 2. By reason and the authority of Augustine. 4. Whatever the Papists may pretend, there is no Church where the word of God appears not. 5. The objection of personal succession, and the charge of heresy and schism, refuted, both from Scripture and Augustine. 6. The same thing confirmed by the authority of Cyprian. The anathemas of the Papists of no consequence. 7. The churches of the Papists in the same situation as those of the Israelites, which revolted to superstition and idolatry under Jeroboam. 8. The character of those Israelitish churches. 9. Hence the Papists act unjustly when they would compel us to communion with their Church. Their two demands. Answer to the first. Sum of the question. Why we cannot take part in the external worship of the Papists. 10. Second demand of the Papists answered. 11. Although the Papacy cannot properly be called a Church, still, against the will of Antichrist himself, there is some vestige of a Church in the Papacy, as Baptism and some other remnants. 12. The name of Church not conceded to the Papacy, though under its domination there have been some kind of churches. Herein is a fulfilment of Paul’s prophecy, that Antichrist would sit in the temple of God. Deplorable condition of such churches. Summary of the chapter.

1¶

It has been explained how much the ministry of the word and sacraments should weigh with us, and how far our reverence for it should extend, so that it may be a perpetual badge for distinguishing the Church. For we have shown, first, that wherever it exists whole and unimpaired, no errors of conduct and no defects should keep us from giving the name of Church; French, "Secondement, qu'encore il y ait quelques petites fautes, ou en la doctrine ou aux sacremens qu'icelui no laisse point d'avoir sa vigeur." — Secondly, that though there may be some small faults either in doctrine or in the sacraments, the Church does not cease to be in vigor.

109 D109 Calvin here answers the question, "How far into error can the (visible) Church go before it ceases to deserve the name?" Minor defects or trivial errors, whether in doctrine or in conduct, do not bring the Church to that point. But if the fundamental articles of religion are injured or suppressed, and the essential elements of the sacraments are destroyed, then the Church dies, and ceases to exist.

2¶

Since this is the state of affairs under the Papacy, we can understand how much of the Church survives there. See chapter 1, section 10; chapter 2, section 10; chapter 8, section 12.

French, "Je say bien que les flatteurs du Pape magnifient grandement leur Eglise." — I know well that the flatterers of the Pope greatly extol their Church.

3¶

In our day, therefore, the pretense of the Romanists is just the same as that which the Jews appear to have used in former times, when the prophets of the Lord charged them with blindness, ungodliness, and idolatry. For as the Jews proudly boasted of their temple, ceremonies, and priesthood, by which, with strong reason as they supposed, they measured the Church, so the Romanists, in place of the Church, present us with certain outward masks, which are often far from being connected with the Church, and without which the Church can perfectly well exist. Therefore we need no other argument to refute them than the one with which Jeremiah opposed the foolish confidence of the Jews — namely, "Trust ye not in lying words, saying, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord are these" (Jeremiah 7:4). The Lord recognizes nothing as his own except where his word is heard and religiously observed. Thus, though the glory of God sat in the sanctuary between the cherubim (Ezekiel 10:4), and he had promised that he would have his settled abode there, still, when the priests corrupted his worship by depraved superstitions, he moved it elsewhere and left the place without any holiness. If that temple, which seemed consecrated as the perpetual dwelling of God, could be abandoned by God and become profane, the Romanists have no ground to claim that God is so bound to persons or places, and so fixed to outward observances, that he must remain with those who have only the name and appearance of a Church. This is the question Paul discusses in the Epistle to the Romans, from the ninth to the twelfth chapter. Weak consciences were greatly disturbed when those who seemed to be the people of God not only rejected but even persecuted the doctrine of the gospel. Therefore, after setting out the doctrine, he removes this difficulty, denying that those Jews, the enemies of the truth, were the Church, though they lacked nothing that might otherwise have been desired for the outward form of the Church. The ground of his denial is that they did not embrace Christ. In the Epistle to the Galatians, when comparing Ishmael with Isaac, he says still more expressly that many hold a place in the Church to whom the inheritance does not belong, because they were not the offspring of a free mother. From this he goes on to draw a contrast between two Jerusalems: as the Law was given on Mount Sinai, but the gospel went forth from Jerusalem, so many who were born and brought up in bondage confidently boast that they are the sons of God and of the Church; indeed, though they are themselves degenerate, they proudly despise the true sons of God. Let us likewise, when we hear that it was once declared from heaven, "Cast out the bondmaid and her son," rely on this inviolable decree and boldly despise their empty boasts. For if they pride themselves on outward profession, Ishmael also was circumcised; if they rest on antiquity, he was the firstborn; and yet we see that he was rejected. If the reason is asked, Paul gives it (Romans 9:6): only those are counted sons who are born of the pure and lawful seed of doctrine. On this ground God declares that he was not bound to ungodly priests, though he had made a covenant with their father Levi to be their angel, or interpreter (Malachi 2:4); indeed, he turns back on them the false boast with which they used to rise up against the prophets — namely, that the dignity of the priesthood was to be held in singular esteem. This he himself readily admits, and he contends with them on the ground that he is ready to fulfill the covenant, while they, by failing to fulfill it on their part, deserve to be rejected. Here, then, is the worth of succession when it is not joined with imitation and corresponding conduct: as soon as the descendants are convicted of having revolted from their origin, they are stripped of all honor — unless, indeed, we are prepared to say that because Caiaphas succeeded many godly priests (indeed, the line from Aaron to him was unbroken), that accursed assembly deserved the name of Church. Even in earthly governments, no one would bear to see the tyranny of Caligula, Nero, Heliogabalus, and the like described as the true condition of a republic because they succeeded such men as Brutus, Scipio, and Camillus. French, "Or tent s'en faut que cela ait lieu, que mesmes aux gouvernemens terrestres il ne seroit point supportable. Comme il n'y a nul propos de dire que la tyrannie de Caligula, Neron, Heliogabale, et leurs semblables soit le vrai etat de la cité de Rome, pourcequ'ils ont succedé aux bons governeurs qui etoient establis par la peuple." — Now, so far is this from being the case that even in earthly governments it would not be bearable. Just as there is no ground for saying that the tyranny of Caligula, Nero, Heliogabalus, and the like is the true state of the city of Rome because they succeeded the good governors who were established by the people.

4¶

In this way the Romanists assail us today and terrify the unskilled with the name of Church, while they are the deadly adversaries of Christ. Therefore, although they display a temple, a priesthood, and other similar masks, the empty glare with which they dazzle the eyes of the simple should not move us in the least to admit that there is a Church where the word of God does not appear. The Lord has given us an unfailing test when he said, "Every one that is of the truth heareth my voice" (John 18:37). Again, "I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine." "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me." A little before, he had said that when the shepherd "putteth forth his own sheep, he goeth before them, and the sheep follow him: for they know his voice. And a stranger will they not follow, but will flee from him: for they know not the voice of strangers" (John 10:14, 4, 5). Why, then, do we of our own accord form so foolish an estimate of the Church, when Christ has marked it out by a sign in which there is nothing in the least ambiguous — a sign that is seen everywhere, whose presence infallibly proves the presence of the Church, while its absence proves the absence of everything that properly bears the name of Church? Paul declares that the Church is founded neither on the judgments of men nor on the priesthood, but on the doctrine of the apostles and prophets (Ephesians 2:20). Indeed, Jerusalem is to be distinguished from Babylon, and the Church of Christ from a conspiracy of Satan, by the distinguishing test which our Savior has applied to them: "He that is of God heareth God's words: ye therefore hear them not, because ye are not of God" (John 8:47). In short, since the Church is the kingdom of Christ, and he reigns only by his word, can there be any doubt about the falsehood of those claims by which the kingdom of Christ is represented without his scepter — in other words, without his sacred word?

5¶

As to their charge of heresy and schism, because we preach a different doctrine, do not submit to their laws, and meet apart from them for prayer, baptism, the administration of the Supper, and other sacred rites, it is indeed a very serious accusation, but one that does not need a long and labored defense. The name of heretics and schismatics is applied to those who, by dissenting from the Church, destroy its communion. This communion is held together by two bonds — namely, agreement in sound doctrine and brotherly love. Hence the distinction Augustine draws between heretics and schismatics is that the former corrupt the purity of the faith by false dogmas, whereas the latter sometimes, even while holding the same faith, break the bond of fellowship (Augustine, Questions on the Gospel of Matthew). But the thing to be observed is that this union of love so depends on unity of faith that it has in it its beginning, its end, and, in short, its only rule. Let us therefore remember that whenever church unity is commended to us, what is required is that, while our minds agree in Christ, our wills also be joined together by mutual goodwill in Christ. Accordingly Paul, when he urges us to it, takes as his foundational principle that there is "one God, one faith, one baptism" (Ephesians 4:5). Indeed, when he tells us to be "of one accord, of one mind," he immediately adds, "Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus" (Philippians 2:2, 5), indicating that where the word of the Lord is absent, it is not a union of believers but a faction of the ungodly.

6¶

Cyprian also, following Paul, derives the fountain of church concord from the one episcopate of Christ, and afterward adds: "There is one Church, which by the increase of its fruitfulness is spread more widely into a multitude, just as there are many rays of the sun but one light, and many branches of a tree but one trunk, upheld by its firm root. When many streams flow from one fountain, though there seems to be a wide-spreading multitude from the overflowing abundance of the supply, yet unity remains in the source. Pluck a ray from the body of the sun, and its unity suffers no division. Break a branch from a tree, and the branch will not bud. Cut off a stream from its fountain, and what is cut off dries up. So the Church, flooded with the light of the Lord, extends over the whole globe, and yet the light that is everywhere diffused is one" (Cyprian, On the Simplicity of Prelates). Words could not more elegantly express the inseparable connection that all the members of Christ have with one another. We see how he constantly calls us back to the head. Accordingly, he declares that when heresies and schisms arise, it is because men do not return to the source of the truth, because they do not seek the head, because they do not keep the doctrine of the heavenly Master. Let them now go and clamor against us as heretics for having withdrawn from their Church, since the only cause of our separation is that they cannot tolerate a pure profession of the truth. I say nothing of their having expelled us with anathemas and curses. That fact is more than enough to excuse us, unless they would also make schismatics of the apostles, with whom we share a common cause. Christ, I say, forewarned his apostles, "They shall put you out of the synagogues" (John 16:2). The synagogues of which he speaks were then held to be lawful churches. Since, then, it is certain that we were cast out, and we are prepared to show that this was done for the name of Christ, the cause ought first to be examined before any decision is given either for or against us. This, however, I am willing to leave to them if they choose; for me it is enough that we had to withdraw from them in order to draw near to Christ.

7¶

The place we ought to assign to all the churches which the tyranny of the Roman idol has seized will appear more clearly if we compare them with the ancient Church of Israel as the prophets portray it. So long as the Jews and Israelites held fast to the laws of the covenant, a true Church existed among them; in other words, by the kindness of God they obtained the benefits of a Church. True doctrine was contained in the Law, and its ministry was committed to the prophets and priests. They were initiated into religion by the sign of circumcision, and by the other sacraments they were trained and confirmed in the faith. There can be no doubt that the titles with which the Lord honored his Church applied to their society. After they forsook the law of the Lord and degenerated into idolatry and superstition, they partly lost this privilege. For who would dare to deny the title of Church to those with whom the Lord deposited the preaching of his word and the observance of his mysteries? On the other hand, who would dare to give the name of Church, without reservation, to an assembly by which the word of God is openly and with impunity trampled underfoot — where his ministry, its chief support and the very soul of the Church, is destroyed?

8¶

What then (someone will say)? Was not a particle of the Church left to the Jews from the time of their revolt to idolatry? The answer is easy. First, I say that in the defection itself there were several degrees; for we cannot hold that the lapses by which both Judah and Israel turned aside from the pure worship of God were the same. Jeroboam, when he made the calves against God's express prohibition and dedicated an unlawful place for worship, corrupted religion entirely. The Jews became degenerate in morals and superstitious opinions before they made any improper change in the outward form of religion. For although they had adopted many perverse ceremonies under Rehoboam, yet, since the doctrine of the Law, the priesthood, and the rites God had instituted continued at Jerusalem, the godly still had the Church in a tolerable state. As for the Israelites, matters that had certainly not been reformed up to the time of Ahab then grew worse. Those who succeeded him, until the overthrow of the kingdom, were partly like him, and partly (when they wished to be somewhat better) followed the example of Jeroboam, while all without exception were wicked and idolatrous. In Judea various changes took place from time to time, some kings corrupting the worship of God by false and superstitious inventions and others attempting to reform it, until at length the priests themselves polluted the temple of God with profane and abominable rites.

9¶

Now then, let the Papists, in order to excuse their vices as much as possible, deny if they can that the state of religion is as much vitiated and corrupted among them as it was in the kingdom of Israel under Jeroboam. They have a grosser idolatry, and in doctrine they are not one whit purer; rather, perhaps, they are even more impure. God, and indeed all who possess a moderate degree of judgment, will bear me witness, and the thing itself is too plain to require me to enlarge on it. When they would force us into the communion of their Church, they make two demands of us: first, that we join in their prayers, their sacrifices, and all their ceremonies; and secondly, that whatever honor, power, and jurisdiction Christ has given to his Church, we must attribute the same to theirs. As to the first, I admit that all the prophets who were at Jerusalem, when matters there were very corrupt, neither sacrificed apart nor held separate meetings for prayer. For they had the command of God, which directed them to meet in the temple of Solomon, and they knew that the Levitical priests, whom the Lord had appointed over sacred things, and who had not yet been set aside, however unworthy they might be of that honor, were still entitled to hold it French, "Ils savoient que les pretres Levitiques, combien qu'ils fussent indignes d'un tel office, neantmoins pourcequ'ils avoient eté ordonnez de Dieu, et n'etoient point encore deposés, devoient etre recognus pour ministres legitimes, ayant le degré de pretrise." — They knew that the Levitical priests, although they were unworthy of such an office, nevertheless, because they had been ordained by God and had not yet been deposed, were to be recognized as lawful ministers, having the rank of priesthood.

10¶

As to the second point, our objections are still stronger. For when the Church is considered in that particular sense — as the Church whose judgment we are bound to revere, whose authority we must acknowledge, whose admonitions we must obey, whose censures we must dread, and whose communion we must religiously cultivate in every respect — we cannot grant that they have a Church without binding ourselves to subjection and obedience. Still, we are willing to grant what the prophets granted to the Jews and Israelites of their day, when matters among them were in a similar or even a better condition. For we see how they constantly cry out against their assemblies as profane gatherings, to which it is no more lawful for them to consent than to renounce God (Isaiah 1:14). And certainly, if those were churches, it follows that Elijah, Micaiah, and others in Israel, and Isaiah, Jeremiah, Hosea, and men of like character in Judah — whom the prophets, priests, and people of their day hated and cursed more than the uncircumcised — were strangers to the Church of God. If those were churches, then the Church was no longer the pillar of the truth but the prop of falsehood, not the tabernacle of the living God but a storehouse of idols. They were therefore compelled to refuse consent to their assemblies, since consent was nothing else than an impious conspiracy against God. For the same reason, if anyone acknowledges as churches the assemblies of the present day which are defiled by idolatry, superstition, and impious doctrine — churches with which a Christian must maintain full communion, even to the point of agreeing with them in doctrine — he will greatly err. For if they are churches, the power of the keys belongs to them; yet the keys are inseparably joined to the word which they have driven away. Again, if they are churches, they can claim the promise of Christ, "Whatsoever ye bind," and so on; yet, on the contrary, they cast out of their communion all who sincerely profess themselves servants of Christ. Therefore, either the promise of Christ is vain, or in this respect at least they are not churches. In short, instead of the ministry of the word they have schools of ungodliness and sinks of every kind of error. Therefore, in this sense, either they are not churches, or no badge will remain by which the lawful assemblies of the faithful can be distinguished from the assemblies of the Turks.

11¶

Still, just as in ancient times certain special privileges of a Church remained among the Jews, so today we do not deny to the Papists those traces of a Church which the Lord has allowed to remain among them amid the ruin. When the Lord had once made his covenant with the Jews, it was preserved not so much by them as by its own strength, and supported by this it withstood their ungodliness. Such, then, is the certainty and constancy of the divine goodness that the covenant of the Lord continued there, and his faithfulness could not be blotted out by their treachery; nor could circumcision be so profaned by their impure hands as not still to be a true sign and sacrament of his covenant. Hence the Lord called the children born to them his own (Ezekiel 16:20), though, apart from a special blessing, they in no way belonged to him. So, having deposited his covenant in Gaul, Italy, Germany, Spain, and England, when these countries were oppressed by the tyranny of Antichrist, he, in order that his covenant might remain inviolable, first preserved baptism there as a testimony of the covenant — baptism, which, consecrated by his lips, keeps its power in spite of human depravity; and secondly, he provided by his providence that other remnants should also remain, to keep the Church from perishing utterly. And as in pulling down buildings the foundations and ruins are often left standing, so he did not allow Antichrist either to overturn his Church from its foundation or to level it to the ground (though, to punish the ingratitude of men who had despised his word, he permitted a fearful shaking and dismembering to take place), but was pleased that amid the devastation the building should remain, though half in ruins.

12¶

Therefore, while we are unwilling simply to grant the name of Church to the Papists, we do not deny that there are churches among them. The question we raise concerns only the true and lawful constitution of the Church, which implies communion in sacred rites, which are the signs of profession, and especially in doctrine. French, "Mais nous contendons seulement du vrai etat de l'Eglise, qui emporte communion, tant en doctrine, qu'en tout qui appartient à la profession de notre Chretienté;" — but we contend only for the true state of the Church, implying communion in doctrine as well as in everything that pertains to the profession of our Christianity.

The French adds, "pour le moins en l'Eglise Occidentale;" — at least in the Western Church.

A Word for 2026 · Uniting, Serving, Mentoring and Counseling Ministries

Calvin compares the false church with the true, refusing to grant the name 'Church' to any body that boasts an unbroken line of succession while abandoning the pure Word — for it is doctrine, not pedigree, that marks Christ's flock, and a lamp may keep its lampstand long after its light has gone out. For believers in 2026, this is a summons to discernment without a slide into cynicism. Not every assembly that wears the name is the true Church, yet Calvin will not let zeal harden into the schismatic's pride that condemns every imperfect congregation. The test is the gospel faithfully preached and the sacraments rightly kept — hold to that measure, and you can both flee the counterfeit and bear with the genuine in its weakness. Teach your household to weigh a church by its fidelity to Scripture rather than its size, antiquity, or polish. Watchfulness here is love, not suspicion: we guard the truth jealously precisely because we cherish the mother who feeds us, and we will not see her name claimed by those who have abandoned her Lord.
