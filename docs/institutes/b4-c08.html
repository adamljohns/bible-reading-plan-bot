Of the Power of the Church in Articles of Faith — The Unbridled Licence of the Papal Church in Destroying Purity of Doctrine

This chapter is divided into two parts,—I. The limits within which the Church ought to confine herself in matters of this kind, sec. 1-9. II. The Roman Church convicted of having transgressed these limits, sec. 10-16. 1. The marks and government of the Church having been considered in the seven previous chapters, the power of the Church is now considered under three heads—viz. Doctrine, Legislation, Jurisdiction. 2. The authority and power given to Church-officers not given to themselves, but their office. This shown in the case of Moses and the Levitical priesthood. 3. The same thing shown in the case of the Prophets. 4. Same thing shown in the case of the Apostles, and of Christ himself. 5. The Church astricted to the written Word of God. Christ the only teacher of the Church. From his lips ministers must derive whatever they teach for the salvation of others. Various modes of divine teaching. 1. Personal revelations. 6. Second mode of teaching—viz. by the Law and the Prophets. The Prophets were in regard to doctrine, the expounders of the Law. To these were added Historical Narratives and the Psalms. 7. Last mode of teaching by our Saviour himself manifested in the flesh. Different names given to this dispensation, to show that we are not to dream of anything more perfect than the written word. 8. Nothing can be lawfully taught in the Church, that is not contained in the writings of the Prophets and Apostles, as dictated by the Spirit of Christ. 9. Neither the Apostles, nor apostolic men. nor the whole Church, allowed to overstep these limits. This confirmed by passages of Peter and Paul. Argument a fortiori. 10. The Roman tyrants have taught a different doctrine—viz. that Councils cannot err, and, therefore, may coin new dogmas. 11. Answer to the Papistical arguments for the authority of the Church. Argument, that the Church is to be led into all truth. Answer. This promise made not only to the whole Church, but to every individual believer. 12. Answers continued. 13. Answers continued. 14. Argument, that the Church should supply the deficiency of the written word by traditions. Answer. 15. Argument founded on Mt. 18:17 . Answer. 16. Objections founded on Infant Baptism, and the Canon of the Council of Nice, as to the consubstantiality of the Son. Answer.

1¶

We come now to the third division, namely, the power of the Church, as it exists either in individual bishops or in councils, whether provincial or general. I speak only of the spiritual power which belongs properly to the Church, and which consists either in doctrine, or in jurisdiction, or in making laws. In regard to doctrine there are two divisions, namely, the authority to deliver dogmas, and the interpretation of them. Before we begin to treat each one in particular, I wish to remind the godly reader that whatever is taught about the power of the Church ought to be referred to the end for which Paul declares (2 Corinthians 10:8; 13:10) that it was given, namely, for edification and not for destruction; those who use it lawfully regard themselves as nothing more than servants of Christ, and at the same time servants of the people in Christ. Moreover, the only way in which ministers can edify the Church is by striving to maintain the authority of Christ, which cannot remain intact unless that which he received from the Father is left to him, namely, to be the only Master of the Church. For it was said of no other but of himself alone, "Hear him" (Matthew 17:5). Church power, therefore, is not to be decked out to men's harm, but is to be confined within definite limits, so as not to be drawn this way and that at the whim of men. For this purpose it will be of great use to observe how it is described by the Prophets and Apostles. For if we grant to men without reserve all the power they see fit to assume, it is easy to see how soon it will degenerate into a tyranny which is altogether foreign to the Church of Christ.

2¶

Therefore it is necessary here to remember that whatever authority and dignity the Holy Spirit in Scripture confers on priests, or prophets, or apostles, or successors of the apostles, is given wholly not to the men themselves, but to the ministry to which they are appointed; or, to speak more plainly, to the word, to whose ministry they are appointed. For if we were to go over the whole in order, we would find that they were not invested with authority to teach or to give answers except in the name and word of the Lord. For whenever they are called to office, they are commanded not to bring anything of their own, but to speak by the mouth of the Lord. Nor does he bring them forward to be heard by the people before he has instructed them what they are to speak, so that they may speak nothing but his own word. Moses, the prince of all the prophets, was to be heard in preference to others (Exodus 3:4; Deuteronomy 17:9); but he is first furnished with his orders, so that he may not be able to speak at all except from the Lord. Accordingly, when the people embraced his teaching, they are said to have believed the Lord and his servant Moses (Exodus 14:31). It was also provided under the severest penalties that the authority of the priests should not be despised (Exodus 17:9). But at the same time the Lord shows on what terms they were to be heard, when he says that he made his covenant with Levi so that the law of truth might be in his mouth (Malachi 2:4-6). A little after he adds, "The priest's lips should keep knowledge, and they should seek the law at his mouth; for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts." Therefore, if the priest would be heard, let him show himself to be the messenger of God; that is, let him faithfully deliver the commands he has received from his Maker. When the manner of hearing is treated, then, it is expressly said, "According to the sentence of the law which they shall teach you" (Deuteronomy 17:11).

3¶

The nature of the power conferred on the prophets in general is elegantly described by Ezekiel: "Son of man, I have made you a watchman unto the house of Israel: therefore hear the word at my mouth, and give them warning from me" (Ezekiel 3:17). Is not he who is ordered to hear at the mouth of the Lord forbidden to devise anything of himself? And what is meant by giving a warning from the Lord, but to speak in such a way as to be able to declare with confidence that the word he delivers is not his own but the Lord's? Jeremiah expresses the same thing in other terms: "The prophet that has a dream, let him tell a dream; and he that has my word, let him speak my word faithfully" (Jeremiah 23:28). Surely God here lays down the law for all, and it is a law which does not allow anyone to teach more than he has been commanded. He afterward gives the name of chaff to whatever has not come from himself alone. Accordingly, none of the prophets opened his mouth unless the word of the Lord went before him. Hence we so often meet the expressions, "The word of the Lord," "The burden of the Lord," "Thus says the Lord," "The mouth of the Lord has spoken it." The French adds, "Vision receue du Seigneur; Le Seigneur des armees l'a dit," that is: A vision received from the Lord; The Lord of hosts has spoken it.

4¶

Now if you look to the apostles, they are commended by many distinguished titles: they are the Light of the world and the Salt of the earth, they are to be heard in Christ's stead, and whatever they bound or loosed on earth is bound or loosed in heaven (Matthew 5:13, 14; Luke 10:16; John 20:23). But they declare in their own name what authority their office conferred on them, namely, that if they are apostles they must not speak at their own pleasure, but must faithfully deliver the commands of him by whom they are sent. The words in which Christ defined their mission are clear enough: "Go therefore and teach all nations, teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:19, 20). Indeed, so that no one might be permitted to evade this law, he received it and imposed it on himself: "My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me" (John 7:16). He who always was the only and eternal counselor of the Father, who was appointed by the Father Lord and Master of all, yet because he performed the ministry of teaching, prescribed to all ministers by his example the rule they ought to follow in teaching. The power of the Church, therefore, is not unlimited, but is subject to the word of the Lord, and, as it were, enclosed within it.

5¶

But though the rule which has always existed in the Church from the beginning, and ought to exist today, is that the servants of God are to teach only what they have learned from him, yet according to the variety of times they have had different ways of learning. The way that exists now differs greatly from that of former times. First, if it is true, as Christ says, that "Neither knows any man the Father save the Son, and he to whomsoever the Son will reveal him" (Matthew 11:27), then those who wished to attain to the knowledge of God had always to be guided by that eternal wisdom. For how could they have grasped the mysteries of God in their minds, or declared them to others, except by the teaching of him to whom alone the secrets of the Father are known? The only way, therefore, in which holy men in ancient times knew God was by beholding him in the Son as in a mirror. When I say this, I mean that God never revealed himself to men by any other means than by his Son, that is, his own only wisdom, light, and truth. From this fountain Adam, Noah, Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and others drew all the heavenly doctrine they possessed. 116 D116 This section admirably expresses Calvin's view of the centrality of Christ in divine revelation. From Creation to Consummation, Christ is God's only wisdom, light, and truth, the only mirror in which God has Himself as Redeemer, the only fountain of heavenly doctrine, and the only way by which man can come to know God. Here there is no room for the notion that Christ was completely concealed in the Old Testament, whereas He is completely revealed in the New; nor for the idea that Old Testament saints trusted in God, whereas New Testament saints trust in Christ.

6¶

But when God determined to give the Church a more illustrious form, he was pleased to commit and entrust his word to writing, so that the priests might seek there what they were to teach the people, and every doctrine delivered might be brought to it as to a test (Malachi 2:7). Accordingly, after the publication of the Law, when the priests are commanded to teach from the mouth of the Lord, the meaning is that they are not to teach anything foreign or alien to that kind of doctrine which God had summed up in the Law, since it was unlawful for them to add to it or take from it. Next came the prophets, through whom God published new oracles which were added to the Law; yet not so new that they did not flow from the Law and have regard to it. For as far as doctrine is concerned, they were only interpreters of the Law, adding nothing to it but predictions of future events. With this exception, all that they delivered was pure exposition of the Law. But as the Lord was pleased that doctrine should exist in a clearer and fuller form, the better to satisfy weak consciences, he commanded the prophecies also to be committed to writing and to be held as part of his word. To these were added at the same time historical accounts, which are also the composition of prophets, but dictated by the Holy Spirit; 117 D117 Although Calvin uses the term "dictated" in connection with this explanation of the way in which the body of Old Testament Scripture was formed, this should not be taken to express the mode of Inspiration, but rather to call attention to the result of Inspiration. That this is his intention may be seen in the preceding assertion that historical accounts "are also the composition of prophets," which assertion takes into account the human factor in the process by which revelation was put into Scripture.

7¶

But when at last the Wisdom of God was revealed in the flesh, he fully unfolded to us all that the human mind can grasp, or ought to think, concerning the heavenly Father. Now, therefore, since Christ, the Sun of Righteousness, has risen, we have the full radiance of divine truth, like the brightness of noonday, whereas before the light was dim. It was no ordinary blessing which the apostle meant to proclaim when he wrote: "God, who at sundry times and in divers manners spoke in time past unto the fathers by the prophets, has in these last days spoken unto us by his Son" (Hebrews 1:1, 2). For he indicates, indeed openly declares, that God will not from now on speak, as formerly, by this one and by that one; that he will not add prophecy to prophecy or revelation to revelation, but has so completed all the parts of teaching in the Son that this is to be regarded as his last and eternal testimony. For this reason the whole period of the new dispensation, from the time when Christ appeared to us with the preaching of his Gospel until the day of judgment, is called the last hour, the last times, the last days, so that, content with the perfection of Christ's doctrine, we may learn to frame no new doctrine for ourselves and to admit none devised by others. With good reason, therefore, the Father appointed the Son our teacher, with a special prerogative, commanding that he and no human being should be heard. When he said, "Hear him" (Matthew 17:5), he commended his office to us in few words indeed, but in words of more weight and force than is commonly supposed; for it is just as if he had drawn us away from all the doctrines of men and confined us to him alone, ordering us to seek the whole doctrine of salvation from him alone, to depend on him alone, and to cleave to him alone; in short (as the words express it), to listen only to his voice. And indeed, what can now be expected or desired from man, when the very Word of life has appeared before us and has explained himself to us familiarly? Indeed, every mouth should be stopped once he has spoken in whom, according to the good pleasure of our heavenly Father, "are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge" (Colossians 2:3), and has spoken as befitted the Wisdom of God (which is in no part lacking) and the Messiah (from whom the revelation of all things was expected) (John 4:25); in other words, has so spoken as to leave nothing to be spoken by others after him.

8¶

Let this, then, be a sure axiom: that no word of God is to be given place in the Church except that which is contained, first, in the Law and the Prophets, and secondly, in the writings of the Apostles; and that the only proper method of teaching in the Church is according to the prescription and rule of his word. From this we also infer that nothing else was permitted to the apostles than was formerly permitted to the prophets, namely, to expound the ancient Scriptures and to show that the things delivered there are fulfilled in Christ. This, however, they could not do except from the Lord; that is, unless the Spirit of Christ went before them and in a manner dictated the words to them. 118 D118 This assertion that the Spirit of Christ "in a manner dictated words to them" implies at least three ideas: (1) The guidance of the Spirit in the inspiration of Scripture extends to the very words which the apostles and prophets used; (2) The words of Scripture express the very thoughts which God wished expressed, so that the result of the Spirit's inspiration is, with regard to truth, the same as if the words had been dictated; (3) Yet the words of Scripture were only "in a manner" dictated, since the Spirit used the faculties peculiar to the human instruments, thereby ensuring a Scripture marked by differences of vocabulary, syntax, grammar, literary style, historical setting, and theological approach.

9¶

Accordingly Peter, who was perfectly instructed by his Master as to the extent of what was permitted to him, leaves nothing more to himself or to others than to dispense the doctrine delivered by God: "If any man speak, let him speak as the oracles of God" (1 Peter 4:11); that is, not hesitantly, as those are accustomed to speak whose convictions are imperfect, but with the full confidence that befits a servant of God who is furnished with a sure message. What else is this than to banish all the inventions of the human mind (whatever the head that may have devised them), so that the pure word of God may be taught and learned in the Church of the faithful; than to discard the decrees, or rather the fictions, of men (whatever their rank), so that the decrees of God alone may remain steadfast? These are "the weapons of our warfare," which "are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strongholds; casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:4, 5). Here is the supreme power with which the pastors of the Church, by whatever name they are called, should be invested: namely, to dare all things boldly for the word of God, compelling all the strength, glory, wisdom, and rank of the world to yield to and obey its majesty; to command all, from the highest to the lowest, trusting in its power to build up the house of Christ and overthrow the house of Satan; to feed the sheep and drive away the wolves; to instruct and exhort the teachable; to accuse, rebuke, and subdue the rebellious and insolent; to bind and loose; and finally, if need be, to thunder and hurl lightning, but all in the word of God. Although, as I have observed, there is this difference between the apostles and their successors: they were sure and authentic amanuenses of the Holy Spirit; 119 D119 The expression "sure and authentic amanuenses" would at first glance seem to imply that the apostles were mere scribes and copyists, and that Inspiration should be defined as "infallible supernatural superintendence of mechanical human reproduction of divinely communicated words." But Calvin would have subscribed to such a "dictation theory" of Inspiration is abundantly clear from the many relevant portions of the Institutes. (See also the notes on sections 6 and 8 of this chapter.)

10¶

But if this power of the Church described here is compared with the power which spiritual tyrants, falsely calling themselves bishops and religious prelates, have now for several ages exercised among the people of God, there will be no more agreement than that of Christ with Belial. It is not my intention here to set out the manner, the shameful manner, in which they have exercised their tyranny; I will only state the doctrine which they maintain at present, first in writing, and then by fire and sword. Taking it for granted that a universal council is a true representation of the Church, they start from this principle, and at the same time lay it down as beyond dispute that such councils are under the immediate guidance of the Holy Spirit and therefore cannot err. But since they rule councils, indeed make them up, they in fact claim for themselves whatever they maintain to be due to councils. They will therefore have our faith stand and fall at their pleasure, so that whatever they have determined either way must be firmly settled in our minds: what they approve must be approved by us without any doubt; what they condemn we too must hold to be justly condemned. Meanwhile, at their own whim and in contempt of the word of God, they coin doctrines to which they demand our assent in this way, declaring that no one can be a Christian unless he assents to all their dogmas, affirmative as well as negative, if not with explicit, then with implicit faith, because it belongs to the Church to frame new articles of faith.

11¶

First, let us hear by what arguments they prove that this authority was given to the Church, and then we shall see how far their claims about the Church help them. The Church, they say, has the noble promise that she will never be forsaken by Christ her bridegroom, but will be guided by his Spirit into all truth. But of the promises which they are accustomed to cite, many were given no less to individual believers than to the whole Church. For although the Lord spoke to the twelve apostles when he said, "Lo, I am with you always, even unto the end of the world" (Matthew 28:20), and again, "I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever: even the Spirit of truth" (John 14:16, 17), he made these promises not only to the twelve together, but to each of them separately, and indeed in like manner to other disciples whom he had already received or was afterward to receive. When they interpret these promises, which are full of comfort, as if they were given not to any individual Christian but to the whole Church together, what else is this than to rob Christians of the confidence they ought to have drawn from them to encourage them in their course? I do not deny that the whole body of the faithful is furnished with a manifold variety of gifts, and endowed with a far larger and richer treasure of heavenly wisdom than each Christian by himself; nor do I mean that this was said of believers in general as implying that all possess the spirit of wisdom and knowledge in equal degree. But we must not give the adversaries of Christ leave to defend a bad cause by twisting Scripture from its proper meaning. Setting this aside, however, I simply hold what is true, namely, that the Lord is always present with his people and guides them by his Spirit. He is the Spirit, not of error, ignorance, falsehood, or darkness, but of sure revelation, wisdom, truth, and light, from whom they can learn without deception the things that have been given to them (1 Corinthians 2:12); in other words, "what is the hope of their calling, and what the riches of the glory of their inheritance in the saints" (Ephesians 1:18). But since believers, even those of them who are endowed with more excellent graces, obtain in this present life only the firstfruits and, as it were, a foretaste of the Spirit, nothing better remains for them than, conscious of their weakness, to keep themselves carefully within the limits of the word of God, lest by following their own judgment too far they at once stray from the right path, being left without that Spirit by whose teaching alone truth is distinguished from falsehood. For all confess with Paul that "they have not yet reached the goal" (Philippians 3:12). Accordingly, they aim at daily progress rather than glory in perfection.

12¶

But it will be objected that whatever is attributed in part to any of the saints belongs in complete fullness to the Church. Although this has some appearance of truth, I deny that it is true. God does indeed measure out the gifts of his Spirit to each of the members, so that nothing necessary to the whole body is lacking, since the gifts are bestowed for the common benefit. The riches of the Church, however, are always such that much is lacking to that supreme perfection of which our opponents boast. Still, the Church is not left destitute in any part, but always has as much as is sufficient, for the Lord knows what her needs require. But to keep her in humility and godly modesty, he bestows on her no more than he knows to be fitting. I am aware that it is usual to object here that Christ has cleansed the Church "with the washing of water by the word: that he might present it to himself a glorious Church, not having spot or wrinkle" (Ephesians 5:26, 27), and that it is therefore called the "pillar and ground of the truth" (1 Timothy 3:15). But the former passage shows rather what Christ is daily doing in it than what he has already completed. For if he daily sanctifies all his people, purifies and refines them, and wipes away their stains, it is certain that they still have some spots and wrinkles, and that their sanctification is in some measure incomplete. How vain and fanciful it is to suppose that the Church, all of whose members are somewhat spotted and impure, is completely holy and spotless in every part! It is true, therefore, that the Church is sanctified by Christ, but here only the beginning of her sanctification is seen; the end and full completion will come when Christ, the Holy of holies, truly and completely fills her with his holiness. It is true also that her stains and wrinkles have been wiped away, but in such a way that the process goes on every day, until Christ at his coming entirely removes every remaining defect. For unless we admit this, we shall be forced to hold with the Pelagians that the righteousness of believers is perfected in this life; like the Cathari and Donatists, we shall tolerate no weakness in the Church. The French adds, "Or, nos adversaires mesmes tiennent tous ceux-la pour heretiques," that is: Now, our opponents themselves regard all those as heretics.

13¶

And so that my readers may better understand the hinge on which the question chiefly turns, I will briefly explain what our opponents demand and what we resist. When they deny that the Church can err, their aim and meaning come to this: Since the Church is governed by the Spirit of God, she can walk safely without the word; in whatever direction she moves, she cannot think or speak anything but the truth; and therefore, if she decides anything without or beside the word of God, it must be regarded in no other light than as a divine oracle. If we grant the first point, namely, that the Church cannot err in things necessary to salvation, our meaning is that she cannot err because she has altogether renounced her own wisdom and submits to the teaching of the Holy Spirit through the word of God. Here, then, is the difference. They place the authority of the Church outside the word of God; we bind it to the word and do not allow it to be separated from it. And is it strange if the bride and pupil of Christ is so subject to her Lord and Master as to hang carefully and constantly on his lips? In every well-ordered house the wife obeys the command of her husband; in every well-run school only the teaching of the master is listened to. Therefore let the Church not be wise in herself, nor think anything of herself, but let her consider her wisdom at an end when he ceases to speak. In this way she will distrust all the inventions of her own reason; and when she leans on the word of God, she will not waver in distrust or hesitation, but will rest in full assurance and unwavering constancy. Relying on the generous promises she has received, she will have the means of nobly maintaining her faith, never doubting that the Holy Spirit is always present with her to be the perfect guide of her path. At the same time she will remember the use which God wishes to be made of his Spirit: "When he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth" (John 16:13). How? "He shall bring to your remembrance all things whatsoever I have said unto you." He declares, therefore, that nothing more is to be expected from his Spirit than that he should enlighten our minds to perceive the truth of his doctrine. Hence Chrysostom very shrewdly observes: "Many boast of the Holy Spirit, but with those who speak their own words it is a false pretense. As Christ declared that he did not speak of himself (John 12:50; 14:10), because he spoke according to the Law and the Prophets, so if anything contrary to the Gospel is thrust upon us under the name of the Holy Spirit, let us not believe it. For as Christ is the fulfillment of the Law and the Prophets, so the Spirit is the fulfillment of the Gospel" (Chrysost. Serm. de Sancto et Adorando Spiritu). Thus far Chrysostom. We may now easily infer how wrongly our opponents act in boasting of the Holy Spirit for no other purpose than to lend the credit of his name to strange doctrines, foreign to the word of God; whereas he himself desires to be inseparably joined to the word of God, and Christ declares the same thing of him when he promises him to the Church. And so indeed it is. The sobriety which our Lord once prescribed to his Church he wishes to be observed perpetually. He forbade that anything should be added to his word or that anything should be taken from it. This is the inviolable decree of God and of the Holy Spirit, a decree which our opponents try to annul when they pretend that the Church is guided by the Spirit without the word.

14¶

Here again they mutter that the Church had to add something to the writings of the apostles, or that the apostles themselves had to supply by word of mouth what they had taught less clearly, since Christ said to them, "I have yet many things to say unto you, but you cannot bear them now" (John 16:12); and they claim that these are the points which have been received without writing, merely by use and custom. But what effrontery is this! The disciples, I admit, were ignorant and almost unteachable when our Lord spoke to them in this way; but were they still in this condition when they committed his doctrine to writing, so that they were afterward obliged to supply by word of mouth what they had failed to write through ignorance? If they were guided by the Spirit of truth into all truth when they published their writings, what prevented them from grasping a full knowledge of the Gospel and setting it down there? But let us grant them what they ask, provided they point out the things which had to be revealed without writing. Should they presume to attempt this, I will answer them in the words of Augustine: "When the Lord is silent, who of us may say, this is so, or that is so? Or if we should presume to say it, how do we prove it?" (August. in Joann. 96). But why do I argue needlessly? Every child knows that in the writings of the apostles, which these men represent as mutilated and incomplete, is contained the fruit of that revelation which the Lord then promised them.

15¶

What, they say, did not Christ declare that nothing which the Church teaches and decrees can be contradicted, when he commanded that everyone who presumes to oppose her should be regarded as a heathen man and a publican (Matthew 18:17)? First, there is no mention of doctrine here; what is asserted is her authority to censure, for correction, so that none who had been admonished or rebuked might resist her judgment. But to say nothing of this, it is very strange that these men are so lost to all sense of shame that they do not hesitate to pride themselves on this declaration. For what, I ask, will they make of it, except that the consent of the Church, a consent never given except to the word of God, is not to be despised? The Church is to be heard, they say. Who denies this, since she decides nothing except according to the word of God? If they demand more than this, let them know that the words of Christ give them no support. I ought not to seem contentious when I insist so strongly that we cannot grant the Church any new doctrine; in other words, allow her to teach and to deliver as an oracle more than the Lord has revealed in his word. Men of sense see how great the danger is if so much authority is once granted to men. They see also how wide a door is opened to the jeers and cavils of the ungodly if we admit that Christians are to receive the opinions of men as if they were oracles. We may add that our Savior, speaking according to the circumstances of his times, gave the name of Church to the Sanhedrin, so that the disciples might afterward learn to revere the sacred assemblies of the Church. Hence it would follow that individual cities and districts would have equal freedom to coin dogmas.

16¶

The examples they bring do not help them. They say that infant baptism rests not so much on a plain command of Scripture as on a decree of the Church. It would be a miserable refuge if, in defense of infant baptism, we were obliged to take shelter in the bare authority of the Church; but it will be made plain enough elsewhere (chapter 16) that the case is far otherwise. In the same way, when they object that we nowhere find in the Scriptures what was declared in the Council of Nicaea, namely, that the Son is consubstantial with the Father (see August. Ep. 178), they do a grievous injustice to the Fathers, as if they had rashly condemned Arius for not swearing to their words, though he professed the whole of that doctrine which is contained in the writings of the Apostles and Prophets. I admit that the expression does not occur in Scripture; but since it is declared there so often that there is one God, and Christ is so often called true and eternal God, one with the Father, what do the Nicene Fathers do, when they affirm that he is of one essence, but simply declare the genuine meaning of Scripture? Theodoret relates that Constantine, in opening their assembly, spoke as follows: "In the discussion of divine matters, the teaching of the Holy Spirit stands on record. The Gospels and the apostolic writings, together with the oracles of the prophets, fully show us the mind of the Deity. Therefore, laying aside discord, let us take the resolution of our questions from the words of the Spirit" (Theodoret. Hist. Eccles. Lib. 1 c. 5). There was no one who opposed this sound advice; no one who objected that the Church could add something of her own, that the Spirit had not revealed all things to the apostles, or at least that they had not delivered them to posterity, and so on. If the point on which our opponents insist is true, then Constantine, first, erred in robbing the Church of her power; and secondly, when none of the bishops rose to defend it, their silence was a kind of treachery and made them traitors to church law. But since Theodoret relates that they readily embraced what the Emperor said, it is evident that this new dogma was then wholly unknown.

The Church has real authority, Calvin insists, but it is not infinite — it is bound to the written Word, for Christ alone is the Master whose voice we are commanded to hear. Prophets, apostles, even the Son in his earthly ministry spoke not their own pleasure but the words given them; the Church may expound Scripture, never coin new articles of faith above it. This is the bedrock of every faithful home and pulpit in 2026. We are surrounded by voices claiming fresh revelation, novel doctrines dressed in the language of the Spirit — yet the Spirit, as Calvin reminds us through Chrysostom, never speaks contrary to the Word he himself inspired. Teach your children to weigh every teaching against the Law, the Prophets, and the apostles, and to rest there. The Church is the spouse who hangs upon her Husband's lips; she is wise only when he speaks. Anchor your family to Scripture, and no wind of doctrine will carry them off.
