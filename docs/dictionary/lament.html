Lament
/ lə-ˈment /
noun / verb
From Latin lamentum — a wailing, mourning cry; Hebrew qinah (קִינָה) — a dirge, lamentation; anah (אָנַח) — to groan, sigh; Greek thrēneō (θρηνέω) — to wail, sing a dirge

📖 Biblical Definition

Lament is the honest, anguished cry of the soul to God in the face of suffering, loss, injustice, or sin. Far from being a failure of faith, lament is a form of faith — it presupposes that God hears, cares, and can act. The Bible devotes an entire book to lament (Lamentations) and more than a third of the Psalms are lament psalms. Job lamented. Jeremiah lamented. Jesus wept (John 11:35) and cried out from the cross quoting a lament psalm (Psalm 22:1). Lament holds grief and trust together — it does not deny pain, but it brings pain directly to the One who can redeem it.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

LAMENT', v.i. To mourn; to grieve; to weep or wail; to express sorrow. LAMENT', v.t. To bewail; to mourn for; to bemoan. LAM'ENT, n. Grief expressed in outcries; sorrow audibly expressed; a passionate expression of grief; lamentation.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Two opposing errors destroy lament in modern Christianity. The first is toxic positivity: churches suppress lament as a sign of weak faith, insisting believers must always be happy, victorious, and cheerful. This silences honest suffering and creates spiritual performance culture. The second is therapeutic wallowing: secular culture encourages endless grief without resolution, turning pain into identity and victimhood into a permanent status. Biblical lament does neither — it cries out boldly to God and trusts Him to answer, always moving toward renewed hope (see Lamentations 3:21-24).
