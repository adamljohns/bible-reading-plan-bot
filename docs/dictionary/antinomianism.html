Antinomianism
/ ˌan·tē·ˈnō·mē·ə·ˌni·zəm /
noun
Greek anti (against) + nomos (νόμος, law). "Against the law." The theological error that grace abolishes the moral obligation of believers to obey God's law. Coined formally during the Reformation by Luther's opponent Johannes Agricola, though the error appears in Scripture itself.

📖 Biblical Definition

Antinomianism is the false teaching that, since Christians are saved by grace and not by works, they are free from any obligation to the moral law. Scripture explicitly anticipates and refutes this: "Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? By no means!" (Romans 6:1–2). The grace that saves also transforms — it does not license. Christ did not come to abolish the law but to fulfill it (Matthew 5:17), and the Spirit writes God's law on the believer's heart (Jeremiah 31:33; Ezekiel 36:27). The law no longer justifies the Christian but still guides, convicts, and directs him in righteousness. True grace produces obedience; cheap grace produces antinomianism.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

ANTINO'MIAN, n. [Gr. anti, against, and nomos, law.] One of a sect who maintain, that, under the gospel dispensation, the law is of no use or obligation; or who hold doctrines which supersede the necessity of good works and a virtuous life. This sect was founded by Johannes Agricola, about 1535.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Contemporary evangelical culture is saturated with functional antinomianism — not as a formal doctrine, but as a lived assumption. "Don't judge." "God just wants you to be happy." "The New Testament is only about love, not rules." These slogans produce Christians who know they are saved but have no framework for what a saved life looks like. The result is a church that mirrors the culture rather than challenging it. Ironically, this pseudo-grace is not liberating — it is enslaving, because without the law's guidance, the believer is left to navigate holiness by feeling rather than by God's revealed standard.

📖 Key Scripture

• Romans 6:1–2 — "Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? By no means! We are those who have died to sin; how can we live in it any longer?"

• Matthew 5:17 — "Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them."

• 1 John 3:4 — "Everyone who sins breaks the law; in fact, sin is lawlessness."

• Jude 4 — "...ungodly people, who pervert the grace of our God into a license for immorality."

• Titus 2:11–12 — "For the grace of God...teaches us to say 'No' to ungodliness and worldly passions."

✍️ Usage

• The congregation had drifted into antinomianism — not by formal decision, but by decades of preaching grace without ever defining what a grace-transformed life should look like.

• Paul was so aware of the antinomian misreading of his gospel that he addressed it explicitly: the same faith that justifies also sanctifies.

• Dietrich Bonhoeffer named it "cheap grace" — forgiveness without repentance, baptism without discipline, absolution without confession.
