Ordo Salutis
/ˈɔːr.doʊ səˈluː.tɪs/
Latin phrase / noun
Latin: ordo (order, sequence) + salutis (genitive of salus — salvation, health, deliverance). Literally: the order of salvation. A systematic theological framework mapping the logical or chronological sequence of the various acts of grace by which God brings a sinner from election to glorification. The phrase was formalized in Reformed scholasticism (17th century) but the underlying pattern derives from the Golden Chain of Romans 8:29–30.

📖 Biblical Definition

The ordo salutis is the theological framework describing the ordered stages through which divine grace applies the benefits of Christ's redemption to the individual sinner. It is not a salvation-by-stages system (as if each step could be lost), but a map of the logical and causal relationships among saving acts. The classic Reformed ordo (from Romans 8:29–30 as foundation) is: Election → Effectual Calling → Regeneration → Faith & Repentance → Justification → Adoption → Sanctification → Perseverance → Glorification. The Arminian ordo differs primarily at the start: God's foreknowledge of faith precedes election, and prevenient grace enables human response. The Lutheran ordo emphasizes: Calling → Illumination → Conversion → Regeneration → Justification → Mystical Union → Sanctification → Preservation → Glorification. The ordo debate centers on: Does regeneration precede and cause faith (Reformed)? Or does faith precede and occasion regeneration (Arminian)? This sequence determines whether salvation is fundamentally monergistic or synergistic.

📖 The Golden Chain (Romans 8:29–30)

  • Foreknew — God's intimate, purposeful foreknowing of those he would save
  • Predestined — marked out beforehand for conformity to Christ's image
  • Called — effectually summoned through the Gospel
  • Justified — declared righteous on the basis of Christ's work
  • Glorified — (past tense!) so certain is the final outcome, Paul speaks it as accomplished

• Romans 8:29–30 — The anchor text for the entire ordo.

📖 Key Scripture

• John 3:3–8 — Regeneration as prerequisite for seeing/entering the kingdom — born again before one believes, in the Reformed reading.

• Ephesians 2:1–10 — Dead → Made alive → Raised → Saved by grace through faith. Regeneration precedes faith in the logical sequence here.

• Acts 13:48 — "And as many as were appointed to eternal life believed." — Election as the ground of faith.

• Philippians 1:6 — "He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ." — Perseverance grounded in God's faithfulness.

Reformed Ordo: Election (unconditional) → Effectual calling → Regeneration → Faith → Repentance → Justification → Adoption → Sanctification → Perseverance (certain) → Glorification

Arminian Ordo: Prevenient grace (universal) → Human faith → Election (conditional, based on foreseen faith) → Regeneration → Justification → Sanctification → Perseverance (possible to lose) → Glorification

The hinge question: Does God regenerate the sinner so that he believes, or does God justify the sinner because he believed? The Reformed say regeneration precedes and causes faith. The Arminian says faith precedes and occasions regeneration/justification.
