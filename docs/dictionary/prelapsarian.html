Prelapsarian
/ ˌprē-lap-ˈser-ē-ən /
adjective / noun
Latin prae- ("before") + lapsus ("a slip, fall") + -arian (pertaining to). Lapsus derives from labi ("to fall, slip"). The theological compound was coined by scholastic and Reformed theologians to denote the state before the Fall (lapsus) of Adam and Eve in Genesis 3. Its antonym is postlapsarian ("after the Fall"). The term also appears in the compound infralapsarian / supralapsarian, which debate the logical order of God's decrees relative to the Fall in the ordo decretorum.

📖 Biblical Definition

Prelapsarian refers to the original condition of humanity as created by God before sin entered through the Fall — the state of Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden as described in Genesis 1–2. In this state, human beings were created in the image of God (imago Dei, Genesis 1:26–28), endowed with true righteousness and holiness (Ephesians 4:24, Colossians 3:10), possessing original integrity (posse non peccare — "able not to sin"), and existing in unbroken fellowship with God who walked with them in the garden (Genesis 3:8). The prelapsarian condition was one of genuine creaturely goodness, not perfection in the absolute eschatological sense — Adam was placed in a probationary state under the Covenant of Works (Genesis 2:15–17) and could have moved from that initial goodness to confirmed, irreversible glory through obedience. Understanding the prelapsarian state is theologically essential because it defines what was lost in the Fall (postlapsarian depravity), what redemption must recover (the restoration of the image in Christ), and what the New Creation will surpass (the eschatological state will exceed Eden, bringing humanity to what Adam forfeited and more — confirmed non posse peccare, "not able to sin").

LAPSE — A sliding, gliding, or gradual fall. A fall or apostasy; as the lapse of our first parents. [Webster 1828]

Webster's does not list "prelapsarian" as such (a technical theological term), but defines lapse in direct relation to Adam's fall. The compound prelapsarian entered common theological usage through the Reformed scholastic debates of the 17th century and was well established by the 19th. Webster's entry on the Fall reads: "The fall of our first parents from a state of innocence."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture uses "prelapsarian" sentimentally — as romantic nostalgia for an innocent past (a lost childhood, a simpler era, a pre-industrial paradise). This strips the term of its theological weight and turns it into an emotional aesthetic. More dangerously, evolutionary theology denies any actual prelapsarian state, treating Genesis 1–2 as myth and reinterpreting the Fall as merely the dawning of moral consciousness rather than a catastrophic historical event. This destroys the logic of the gospel: if there was no actual original goodness lost, there is nothing for redemption to restore; if Adam's fall did not introduce real death and sin into creation (Romans 5:12), Paul's argument in Romans 5 collapses. The prelapsarian state is not nostalgia — it is the theological baseline from which the entire drama of creation, fall, redemption, and new creation makes sense.

📖 Key Scripture

• Genesis 1:31 — "And God saw everything that He had made, and behold, it was very good."

• Genesis 2:25 — "And the man and his wife were both naked and were not ashamed" — the prelapsarian state of innocence before shame entered.

• Ecclesiastes 7:29 — "God made man upright, but they have sought out many schemes."

• Romans 5:12 — "Sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin" — the hinge between prelapsarian and postlapsarian states.

• Colossians 3:10 — "The new self…is being renewed in knowledge after the image of its creator" — redemption restoring what was prelapsarian.

Hebrew טוֹב (tov, H2896) — good, pleasant, right, beautiful
  → Genesis 1:31: "very good" (טוֹב מְאֹד) — the prelapsarian verdict
  → The fall is precisely the movement away from tov into evil

Hebrew יָשָׁר (yashar, H3477) — upright, straight
  → Ecclesiastes 7:29: "God made man upright (yashar)" — prelapsarian moral rectitude

Greek εἰκών (eikōn, G1504) — image, likeness
  → Colossians 3:10: the image damaged by the Fall, now being restored
  → 2 Corinthians 3:18: progressive renewal toward the prelapsarian + eschatological image

Latin theological terms:
  posse non peccare — "able not to sin" (prelapsarian human freedom)
  posse peccare — "able to sin" (the creaturely limitation that made the Fall possible)
  non posse peccare — "not able to sin" (the eschatological confirmed state of the redeemed)
  non posse non peccare — "not able not to sin" (the postlapsarian state — total depravity)

imago Dei (image of God):
  Narrow sense: original righteousness, holiness, knowledge (lost in Fall)
  Broad sense: personality, rationality, moral capacity (retained, though corrupted)
