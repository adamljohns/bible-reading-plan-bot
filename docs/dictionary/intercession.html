Intercession
/ˌɪntəˈsɛʃən/
noun
Latin: intercessio — from inter (between) + cedere (to go). Greek: enteuxis (ἔντευξις) — a meeting with, petition; entynchanō (ἐντυγχάνω) — to approach, appeal on behalf of another.

📖 Biblical Definition

Prayer on behalf of another, in which the petitioner stands in the gap between God and the one in need, appealing to divine mercy, provision, or justice. Intercession is supremely the work of Christ, who "always lives to intercede" for His people at the right hand of the Father (Heb 7:25), and of the Holy Spirit, who intercedes through the believer with "groanings too deep for words" (Rom 8:26). It is also the calling of every believer — to stand before God for others, bearing their burdens into the throne room of grace. Biblical intercession is not manipulation of God but cooperation with His redemptive purposes through the ordained means of prayer.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

INTERCES'SION, n. A passing between; interposition. In theology, prayer or entreaty in favor of another. The intercession of Christ consists in his appearing in the presence of God and making requests for his people, founded on the merit of his obedience and atonement. The prayers of believers for one another are also called intercessions. Any mediation or solicitation for another.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Roman Catholic doctrine extends intercession to deceased saints as mediators alongside Christ — a direct contradiction of 1 Timothy 2:5 ("there is one mediator between God and mankind, the man Christ Jesus"). In charismatic excess, "intercession" is sometimes confused with spiritual warfare techniques, binding demons, or claiming territorial authority beyond what Scripture warrants. In secular usage, "intercession" is reduced to legal intervention or diplomatic mediation, losing its vertical dimension entirely.

📖 Key Scripture

• Romans 8:26–27 — "The Spirit himself intercedes for us through wordless groans..."

• Hebrews 7:25 — "Therefore he is able to save completely those who come to God through him, because he always lives to intercede for them."

• 1 Timothy 2:1–2 — "I urge, then, first of all, that petitions, prayers, intercession and thanksgiving be made for all people..."

• Ezekiel 22:30 — "I looked for someone among them who would build up the wall and stand before me in the gap on behalf of the land..."

• John 17:20 — "My prayer is not for them alone. I pray also for those who will believe in me through their message..."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G1783 — enteuxis (ἔντευξις): intercession, petition; used in 1 Timothy 2:1 as one of four types of prayer for all people.

G1793 — entynchanō (ἐντυγχάνω): to plead, appeal, intercede; used of Christ in Romans 8:34 and Hebrews 7:25, and of the Spirit in Romans 8:26–27.

H6293 — pāga' (פָּגַע): to encounter, meet, fall upon; used in Isaiah 53:12 of the Servant who "made intercession for the transgressors."
