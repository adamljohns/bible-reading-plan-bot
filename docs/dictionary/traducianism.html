Traducianism
/trəˈdjuː.ʃɪ.ə.nɪ.z(ə)m/
noun (theological position)
From Latin tradux — a vine cutting propagated from a parent vine; from traducere (to lead across, to propagate) → trans- (across) + ducere (to lead). The view that the human soul is propagated from the parents' souls at conception, just as the body is generated biologically. Contrasted with creationism (God directly creates each new soul at conception) and the heretical pre-existentialism (souls exist before bodies, Origen).

📖 Biblical Definition

Traducianism holds that the soul, like the body, is transmitted from parent to child — that Adam's soul begat all human souls just as his body begat all human bodies. This view has significant theological traction because it provides the most natural explanation for the transmission of original sin: if the soul is inherited, then Adam's guilt and corruption flows through all his descendants in both body and soul, not just body. God breathed life into Adam (Genesis 2:7), and from that act all subsequent souls derive. Tertullian was the most prominent patristic traducianist. The main competitor, creationism, struggles to explain how a newly created pure soul becomes immediately guilty of original sin. Traducianism is held by a minority of Reformed scholars but is a legitimate and serious position. Most Christians leave the question open — Scripture is not explicit either way.

📖 Key Scripture

• Genesis 2:7 — "The LORD God formed the man of dust from the ground and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life, and the man became a living creature." — The unique creation of Adam's soul; all subsequent souls derive from this.

• Genesis 5:3 — "Adam…fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image." — Traducianists note that this includes spiritual likeness — soul and moral nature — not just physical.

• Romans 5:12 — "Sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned." — The mechanism of original sin transmission is naturally explained by soul-propagation.

• Hebrews 7:9–10 — Levi "was still in the loins of his ancestor" Abraham when Melchizedek was met — implying the real, federal, and even substantial presence of descendants in their ancestor.

• Job 14:4 — "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? There is not one." — Suggests corruption passes through natural generation completely.

PRE-EXISTENTIALISM (Origen, ~185–253 AD)
  Souls existed before bodies and were placed into bodies.
  → Condemned as heretical. Inconsistent with creation ex nihilo
    and the goodness of embodiment. Denies that creation is new.

CREATIONISM (Dominant Western view — Catholic, many Reformed)
  God directly creates a new, fresh soul for each person at conception.
  Strength: preserves the direct work of God in every human life.
  Weakness: How does a newly-created soul immediately bear Adam's guilt?
    Requires a purely legal/federal explanation of original sin.
  Held by: Thomas Aquinas, Francis Turretin, Louis Berkhof (tentatively)

TRADUCIANISM (Tertullian; held by many Lutherans)
  Soul propagated from parents, as body is.
  Strength: Naturally explains the transmission of original sin
    in both body and soul; consistent with Gen 5:3 and Heb 7:9-10.
  Weakness: Risk of viewing soul as material/divisible; complications
    for Christ's sinless human soul (answer: miraculous conception
    by the Spirit breaks the chain of corruption).
  Held by: Tertullian, Augustine (tentative), many Lutherans
