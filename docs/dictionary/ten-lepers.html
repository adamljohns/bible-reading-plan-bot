Ten Lepers
/TEN LEP-erz/
proper noun phrase (figures)
Composite. The ten lepers Christ healed at the border of Samaria and Galilee (Lk 17:11-19).

📖 Biblical Definition

The Ten Lepers were a group of ten leprous men (one a Samaritan, the others presumably Jewish) who met Christ at the border of Samaria and Galilee. Standing afar off, they cried Jesus, Master, have mercy on us. He told them to go show themselves to the priests; on the way, they were cleansed. Only one returned to give thanks — and he was the Samaritan. Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine? Christ asked. The story is a parable in narrative form: the gospel of grace, the rarity of gratitude, the foreigner's witness against insiders.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Ten leprous men healed by Christ; only the Samaritan returned to give thanks (Lk 17:11-19).

expand to see more

Luke 17:11-19 records the only Gospel account. Significant details: the men kept their distance (Levitical law required); Christ's instruction sent them to the priests for the legal cleansing-declaration (Lev 14); they were healed on the way, not at the moment of speaking; only one returned.

The Samaritan's return is theologically loaded. Jews would not have visited Samaritan priests; Christ's command sent the Jewish lepers to Jewish priests for declaration. The Samaritan, having no Jewish priest to consult, returned directly to Christ — and was honored for it.

📖 Key Scripture

Luke 17:14 — "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity often skips the ten lepers' ratio: 10 healed, 1 grateful. The household's gratitude-rate is a measure of its spiritual health.

expand to see more

10:1 grateful is grim. The 90% — cleansed, restored, given a new life — did not return. They went on to their priests, their families, their normal life, their ordinary blessings; they did not stop to thank.

The household's implication: gratitude is rare. Cultivate it. The Samaritan saw that he was healed; turned back; with a loud voice glorified God; fell on his face at Christ's feet. The four-step gratitude is the saint's posture toward grace received.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

Greek deka leproi (ten lepers).

expand to see more

Greek lepros — leprous; behind English leprosy.

Note: biblical leprosy covered a range of skin conditions, not all of them what modern medicine calls Hansen's disease.
