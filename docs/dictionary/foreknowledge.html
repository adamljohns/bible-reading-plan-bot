Foreknowledge
/ˈfɔːrˌnɒlɪdʒ/
noun
Middle English: fore- (before) + knowledge (from Old English cnāwan — to know intimately). God's eternal, perfect, and personal knowledge of all things before they occur in time.

📖 Biblical Definition

Foreknowledge in Scripture is not merely God's advance awareness of future events — it is His intimate, covenantal knowing of persons before time began. "For those God foreknew he also predestined" (Romans 8:29) — the Greek proginōskō carries relational depth: to know beforehand in the sense of choosing and setting apart. Christ was foreknown as the Lamb slain "before the creation of the world" (1 Peter 1:20). Foreknowledge is an attribute of divine omniscience and a ground of assurance: nothing surprises God, nothing falls outside His sovereign knowledge, and His purposes cannot be frustrated.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

FOREKNOWL'EDGE, n. Knowledge of a thing before it happens; prescience. In theology, God's foreknowledge of human actions is distinguished from the necessity of those actions; foreknowledge does not compel, though it comprehends. The foreknowledge of God extends to all future events and is the ground of prophecy, assurance, and the execution of the divine decree.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Some theologians reduce divine foreknowledge to "simple foreknowledge" — God merely observes future free choices without ordaining them — in an attempt to preserve human libertarian freedom. This creates a God who is surprised by nothing but also sovereign over nothing: a heavenly bystander who watches and responds but does not direct. Biblical foreknowledge, properly understood, is inseparable from God's sovereign purpose: He foreknew not as a spectator but as the Author. Open Theism takes this further, denying even exhaustive foreknowledge — a God who genuinely doesn't know what will happen tomorrow. Scripture's God knows the end from the beginning (Isaiah 46:10).

📖 Key Scripture

Romans 8:29 — "For those God foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son."

1 Peter 1:20 — "He was chosen before the creation of the world, but was revealed in these last times for your sake."

Acts 2:23 — "This man was handed over to you by God's deliberate plan and foreknowledge; and you, with the help of wicked men, put him to death by nailing him to the cross."

Isaiah 46:10 — "I make known the end from the beginning, from ancient times, what is still to come. I say, 'My purpose will stand, and I will do all that I please.'"

Psalm 139:16 — "Your eyes saw my unformed body; all the days ordained for me were written in your book before one of them came to be."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G4267 — proginōskō (προγινώσκω) — to know beforehand, foreknow; carries covenantal intimacy, not mere cognitive awareness.

G4268 — prognōsis (πρόγνωσις) — foreknowledge; the noun form used in Acts 2:23 and 1 Peter 1:2 of God's foreknowing purpose.

H3045 — yāda' (יָדַע) — to know deeply and personally; the OT root for covenantal knowing — "before I formed you in the womb I knew you" (Jer. 1:5).

✍️ Usage

God's foreknowledge is not cold omniscient surveillance — it is the warm, prior, choosing knowledge of a Father who named and claimed His children before time began. A believer's assurance rests not in their own constancy but in the fact that God foreknew them, predestined them, called them, justified them, and will glorify them (Romans 8:29–30). The chain cannot be broken because God forged it before the world was.
