Lost Sheep
/lɒst ʃiːp/
phrase / parable
From Greek probaton apoloulos — probaton (sheep) and apollumi (to destroy, to lose, to perish). The parable of the lost sheep in Luke 15 and Matthew 18 uses the universal pastoral image of a shepherd seeking a wandering sheep to illustrate God's relentless pursuit of sinners.

📖 Biblical Definition

The parable of the lost sheep reveals the heart of God toward sinners. In Luke 15:3-7, Jesus tells of a shepherd who leaves ninety-nine sheep to search for the one that is lost, and upon finding it, rejoices more over that one than over the ninety-nine who did not go astray. The context is essential: Jesus told this parable in response to the Pharisees grumbling that He received sinners and ate with them. The lost sheep is not merely someone who has wandered socially — it is a sinner in danger of perishing, helpless to save itself, desperately in need of a Shepherd who will seek, find, and carry it home. Isaiah declared "All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned — every one — to his own way" (Isaiah 53:6). Every human being is a lost sheep apart from the seeking grace of the Good Shepherd.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Lost: not to be found; missing; ruined; destroyed. Sheep: a ruminant animal raised for wool, milk, and meat.

expand to see more

LOST, adj. Not to be found; missing; that has wandered from the way; perplexed. In theology, not recovered from sin; unregenerate. SHEEP, n. In Scripture, the people of God, His spiritual flock. Note: Webster's theological definitions capture the full weight of "lost" — not merely misplaced but perishing and in need of divine rescue.

📖 Key Scripture

• Luke 15:3-7 — "What man of you, having a hundred sheep, if he has lost one of them, does not leave the ninety-nine ... and go after the one that is lost?"

• Isaiah 53:6 — "All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned — every one — to his own way."

• Ezekiel 34:11-12 — "I myself will search for my sheep, and will seek them out."

• Matthew 18:12-14 — "It is not the will of my Father who is in heaven that one of these little ones should perish."

• John 10:11 — "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The lost sheep has been sentimentalized into a story about God's unconditional acceptance of everyone as they are.

expand to see more

Modern preaching often uses the parable of the lost sheep to teach that God loves everyone just as they are and that no change is necessary. But the sheep was lost — it was in danger, wandering away from safety, heading toward destruction. Being found meant being brought back to the fold, not being affirmed in the wilderness. The shepherd does not celebrate the sheep's independence — he rescues it from peril. The parable ends with a call to repentance: "There will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents" (Luke 15:7). A gospel that finds the lost sheep and leaves it in the ditch, affirming its autonomy, has missed the entire point.

Usage

• "The parable of the lost sheep is not about God affirming our wandering — it is about a Shepherd who risks everything to bring sinners back to the fold."

• "Every one of us is a lost sheep apart from the seeking grace of Christ, the Good Shepherd who laid down His life to find us."
