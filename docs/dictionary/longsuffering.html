Longsuffering
/ˈlȯŋ-ˈsə-fə-riŋ/
noun / adjective
Old English compound: long + suffering (enduring). Translates Greek makrothumia — long-tempered, slow to wrath. A distinctly biblical word that secular English has largely abandoned.

📖 Biblical Definition

Patient endurance toward people who sin against you or try your spirit — bearing long with difficulty, provocation, or injury without retaliation or despair. Longsuffering is listed among the fruit of the Spirit (Gal. 5:22) and is first and foremost an attribute of God Himself: His longsuffering toward sinners is what keeps judgment from falling immediately, making space for repentance (2 Pet. 3:9). It is distinct from mere patience with circumstances (hupomone) — longsuffering is specifically patience with persons. It is love in its most tested form.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

LONG-SUFFERING, a. Bearing injuries or provocation for a long time; patient; not easily provoked. "The Lord God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth." — Exodus 34:6.

LONG-SUFFERING, n. Patience of offense; forbearance of resentment.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture has replaced longsuffering with a zero-tolerance, boundary-first ethic that treats endurance as weakness or codependency. While healthy boundaries have their place, the secular age has lost the concept of bearing with difficult people out of love and hope for their transformation. Longsuffering is misread as doormat behavior rather than recognized as one of God's most glorious attributes — the very patience that kept Him from destroying us before Christ came.
