Lordship
/ˈlôrd-ship/
noun
From Old English hlāford (lord, "loaf-guardian," one who provides and protects) + -scipe (condition, state). The state, authority, and dominion belonging to a lord — one who holds sovereign ownership and command.

📖 Biblical Definition

The Lordship of Christ is the claim that Jesus does not merely offer salvation as a benefit to be received — He demands total ownership of the life that receives it. To call Jesus "Lord" (Greek: Kyrios) in the first century was to invoke the divine name of God (the Greek translation of YHWH) and to submit to absolute sovereign authority. Lordship is not a higher tier of discipleship for the serious believer; it is the fundamental content of the gospel itself. "No one can say 'Jesus is Lord' except in the Holy Spirit" (1 Corinthians 12:3). The debate over "Lordship salvation" is, at its root, a debate about whether one can accept Christ's benefits while rejecting His authority — the Bible says no.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

LORDSHIP, n. The state or condition of a lord; dominion; power; authority; the title of address given to noblemen. Applied to the Supreme Being, it denotes sovereignty and dominion over all created beings.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern evangelical culture has bifurcated Jesus into "Savior" and "Lord" as if they were optional add-ons. Prosperity theology speaks of Jesus as a provider and life-enhancer but recoils from His claims as absolute owner and commander. Progressive Christianity redefines Lordship as a metaphor for love and service while stripping it of authority, judgment, and demand. The result: millions who call Jesus "Lord" but live as functional atheists — "Lord, Lord," He will say, "I never knew you" (Matthew 7:23).

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G2962 — Kyrios (Κύριος): Lord, Master, Owner; the Greek equivalent of YHWH in the Septuagint. Applied to Jesus, it asserts full divine sovereignty.

H3068 — YHWH (יְהוָה): The personal name of the God of Israel; when the NT applies "Lord" (Kyrios) to Jesus, it often quotes OT texts about YHWH.

✍️ Usage

You cannot have a Savior who is not your Lord — redemption and ownership are inseparable in Scripture.

The Lordship of Christ over a marriage means both husband and wife are accountable to a higher authority than themselves — a truth that humbles both and protects both.

Lordship is not a burden but a liberation: to have Christ as Lord is to be freed from every lesser master — fear, lust, money, approval.
