Lordship
/ˈlɔːrd.ʃɪp/
noun
Old English hlāford (lord, bread-guardian) | Hebrew אָדוֹן (adon — master, lord) | Greek κυριότης (kyriotēs — lordship, dominion)

📖 Biblical Definition

Lordship is the rightful exercise of supreme authority and ownership. To confess Jesus as Lord (Kyrios) is to acknowledge His absolute claim on every area of life — not merely His role as Savior of the soul. The New Testament declaration "Jesus is Lord" (Romans 10:9; 1 Corinthians 12:3) was a politically charged proclamation in the Roman world, where "Caesar is Lord" was the reigning confession. The Lordship of Christ means He rules not just in heaven but in the home, the marketplace, the bedroom, and the ballot box. Lordship salvation holds that saving faith necessarily involves submission to Christ's authority, not just intellectual assent to His atoning work.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

LORD'SHIP, n. 1. The state or condition of a lord; the territory over which a lord holds jurisdiction; a manor. 2. Dominion; power; authority. "They that are great exercise lordship over them." (Luke 22:25) 3. A title of honor applied to noblemen, and to judges and certain other persons in authority and office.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity has been infected by what Dietrich Bonhoeffer called "cheap grace" — a version of salvation that accepts Jesus as Savior but defers or denies His Lordship. The consumer church sells a gospel of personal benefits (peace, purpose, heaven) while downplaying the cost of discipleship: "Take up your cross and follow me" (Luke 9:23). Culture further erodes the concept by treating lordship as inherently oppressive — a synonym for domination or abuse of power. But Christ's lordship is servant leadership perfected; He is Lord precisely because He laid down His life for His people (Philippians 2:6–11).

✍️ Usage

• "You call me Teacher and Lord, and you are right, for so I am" — Jesus (John 13:13), establishing His authority even as He washed His disciples' feet.

• A husband who understands Christ's lordship leads his family as a steward, not an owner — accountable to the Lord who owns them all.

• Lordship is not domination — it is ordered authority exercised in love, for the flourishing of those under its care.
