Lordship
/ˈlôrd·ship/
noun
Old English hlāfordscipe — the estate and authority of a lord; from hlāford (bread-guardian, protector). Greek: kyrios (κύριος) — lord, master, owner; the word used for both the Roman emperor and, in the Septuagint, for YHWH Himself.

📖 Biblical Definition

Lordship is the comprehensive claim of ownership, authority, and rule over a person or domain. In the New Testament, kyrios carries the full weight of divine sovereignty — it is the title exalted above every name, given to the risen and ascended Christ (Philippians 2:9–11). To confess "Jesus is Lord" (Kyrios Iēsous) was not a spiritual preference in the first century; it was a totalizing political and personal declaration that overrode Caesar's claim to ultimate allegiance. The Lordship of Christ means He has rightful authority over every domain — family, finances, vocation, body, mind, and soul. There is no "secular/sacred" split in His kingdom; all of life is under His rule.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

LORDSHIP, n. The state or condition of a lord. Dominion; power; authority. "They which are accounted to rule over the Gentiles, exercise lordship over them." In theology, the supreme authority and dominion of God over all his creatures, and of Christ as Mediator over all things committed to his charge.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The "Lordship salvation" debate in evangelical circles has at times reduced Christ's lordship to an add-on — an optional discipleship tier for the spiritually ambitious, while "salvation" requires only intellectual assent. But the New Testament knows no "savior without Lord." You cannot receive Jesus as Savior while rejecting Him as Lord, any more than a soldier can accept rescue while refusing the General's command. Contemporary Christianity also domesticates Christ's lordship by confining it to Sunday mornings, ignoring its claims on political life, economics, sexuality, and culture. "Every knee will bow" is not a church attendance metaphor.
