High Priestly Garments

/haɪ ˈpriːstli/
noun / vestments

Etymology & Webster 1828

The set of eight sacred vestments of the Aaronic high priest, detailed in Exodus 28 and 39. Four "garments of gold" specific to the high priest: (1) the ephod with shoulder stones naming the twelve tribes; (2) the breastpiece of judgment with twelve jewels (one for each tribe) and the Urim and Thummim; (3) the robe of the ephod — a blue seamless tunic with pomegranates and bells around the hem; (4) the turban with a gold plate engraved "Holy to the LORD." Plus four "white linen garments" shared with other priests: tunic, sash, undergarments, and cap. "You shall make holy garments for Aaron your brother, for glory and for beauty" (Exodus 28:2).

Biblical Meaning

The high priest's vestments are the OT's most elaborate sacred wardrobe — and every piece preaches. Four observations. (1) Glory and beauty. Scripture is explicit about the aesthetic intention: for glory and for beauty (Exodus 28:2, 40). Israel's worship was not austere; it involved craftsmanship, color, and intentional beauty in service of reverence. (2) Every garment has a typological function. The bells on the hem rang as the priest moved within the Holy Place, letting the people know he was alive and at work (they couldn't see in). Pomegranates symbolized the fruitful land of promise. The breastpiece jewels carried Israel on the high priest's heart; the shoulder stones carried them on his shoulders. The turban's plate "Holy to the LORD" on the priest's forehead covered the entire ministry with the declaration of holiness. (3) Christ fulfills every garment. Hebrews 4:14-16 identifies Christ as our great high priest; Revelation 1:13 pictures Him in a long robe with a golden sash, a clear high-priestly image. His "seamless robe" at the crucifixion (John 19:23) echoes the robe of the ephod, which was woven in one piece. (4) Believers are made a kingdom of priests (Revelation 1:6, 1 Peter 2:9). Not every believer is a pastor, but every believer is a priest — with access to God, a ministry to offer, and the imputed beauty of Christ's holiness covering us. Our garments are clean because He has washed them (Revelation 7:14).

Key Scriptures
