Soon
/suːn/
adverb
Old English sōna, at once. Webster: in a short time; early; readily. The adjective “speedy” he marks not in use.

Biblical Definition

Soon measures a short interval after a named time — or a shamefully short interval. Paul marvels that the Galatians are turning away so soon from Him who called them in the grace of Christ, to a different gospel. The Lord of the churches says He is coming quickly. Scoffers treat delay as disproof. Scripture treats soon as a warning: apostasy does not need a century, and the coming does not need your calendar permission.

Webster 1828 Definition

SOON, adv. In a short time; shortly after any time specified or supposed; early; readily.

SOON, adverb

1. In a short time; shortly after any time specified or supposed; as soon after sunrise; I shall soon return.

2. Early; without the usual delay; before any time specified. How is it that ye have come so soon to-day?

3. Readily; willingly. But in this sense it accompanies would, or some other word expressing will.

AS SOON AS, SO SOON AS, immediately at or after another event. As soon as the mail arrives, I will inform you.

SOON, adjective Speedy; quick. [Not in use.]

Key Scripture

• Galatians 1:6 — “I marvel that you are turning away so soon from Him who called you in the grace of Christ, to a different gospel,” (BTE NKJV)

• Revelation 22:7 — “Behold, I am coming quickly! Blessed is he who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book.”” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

Scoffers say “soon” failed. Paul says soon is how fast a church can leave the gospel.

expand to see more

2 Peter’s mockers ask where the promise of His coming is, as if delay canceled the word. That is one drift. The other is treating soon as a branding adverb — “coming soon” with no keep. Galatians 1:6 is the pastoral use: so soon means you did not even last. Revelation’s quickly is not a missed deadline. It is the Lord’s own nearness. Keep the words.

Usage

• “Soon is either a desertion timer or a coming. Do not yawn at either.”
