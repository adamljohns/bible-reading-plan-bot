Imputation
/ ˌim-pyoo-ˈtā-shən /
noun
Latin imputare — "to reckon, charge to one's account" | from in- (into) + putare (to reckon, calculate)

📖 Biblical Definition

The divine act by which God credits or reckons something to a person's account — either sin charged to the innocent, or righteousness granted to the guilty. Scripture teaches a threefold imputation: Adam's sin imputed to all mankind (Rom 5:12), the sins of the elect imputed to Christ on the cross, and Christ's perfect righteousness imputed to believers through faith. Imputation is not infusion (righteousness put into us) but forensic declaration — God legally pronounces the sinner righteous on the basis of Christ's merits. It is the heartbeat of the gospel: "He made Him who knew no sin to be sin on our behalf, so that we might become the righteousness of God in Him" (2 Cor 5:21). Without imputation, there is no substitutionary atonement and no justification by faith alone.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

IMPUTA'TION, n. 1. The act of imputing or charging; attribution; as the imputation of crimes or faults to the true authors of them. 2. Charge or attribution of evil; censure; reproach. 3. In theology, the charging of one with the sin of another, or crediting one with another's righteousness. The imputation of the sin of Adam to his posterity; the imputation of the righteousness of Christ to believers.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern theology — particularly progressive Christianity and the New Perspective on Paul — denies or minimizes forensic imputation, recasting justification as "covenant membership" or moral transformation rather than the legal crediting of Christ's righteousness. This strips the gospel of its objective foundation. If righteousness is merely infused (as Rome teaches) rather than imputed, then salvation becomes a process we contribute to rather than a gift fully received. Culture further confuses imputation by treating all "blame assignment" as toxic — yet imputation is not blame-shifting; it is the only basis by which guilty sinners can stand before a holy God.
