Imputation

/ˌɪm.pjʊˈteɪ.ʃən/

Etymology

From Latin imputare, meaning "to reckon, to credit, to attribute." It carries the idea of logging something to someone's account.

Biblical Definition

Imputation is a theological concept referring to the act of crediting or attributing something to a person. In Christian theology, it most often refers to three key acts: 1) the imputation of Adam's sin to all humanity, 2) the imputation of humanity's sin to Christ on the cross, and 3) the imputation of Christ's righteousness to believers.

Webster's 1828 Definition

The act of imputing, or charging; attribution; the act of ascribing to one what is done by another. In theology, the imputation of Adam's sin to his posterity, and the imputation of Christ's righteousness to believers, are doctrines of high importance.

Modern Corruption

In modern usage, "impute" has a mostly negative connotation, often implying blame or accusation, sometimes unfairly. The theological richness of the term as a double-entry accounting of sin and righteousness is almost entirely lost in secular conversation.
