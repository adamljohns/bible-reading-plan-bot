Koinonia
/ˌkɔɪ.noʊˈniː.ə/
noun
Greek koinōnia (κοινωνία) from koinos (κοινός) — common, shared, held in common. Root: Proto-Indo-European *kom- (together with). Noun of action from koinōneō (κοινωνέω) — to share, to have in common, to participate. Translated variously as "fellowship," "communion," "sharing," "participation," "partnership."

📖 Biblical Definition

Koinonia is the shared participation in Christ that constitutes the church's deepest bond — simultaneously vertical (with the Triune God) and horizontal (with one another). It is not mere social gathering or friendly association but a common life forged through union with Christ. The Jerusalem church was characterized by koinonia: shared meals, shared goods, shared prayer, shared suffering (Acts 2:42-44). Paul's benediction names it the gift of the Spirit: "the fellowship (koinonia) of the Holy Spirit be with you all" (2 Corinthians 13:14). John grounds it in truth: "our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ" (1 John 1:3). Koinonia also describes the Lord's Supper as "participation" (koinōnia) in Christ's body and blood (1 Corinthians 10:16) — not merely a memorial but a real sharing.

📖 Key Scripture

• Acts 2:42 — "They devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching and the fellowship (koinōnia), to the breaking of bread and the prayers."

• 1 Corinthians 10:16 — "The cup of blessing… is it not a participation (koinōnia) in the blood of Christ?"

• 1 John 1:3 — "So that you too may have fellowship with us; and indeed our fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ."

• 2 Corinthians 13:14 — "The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ and the love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all."

• Philippians 3:10 — "That I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share (koinōnia) his sufferings."

FELLOWSHIP — "1. The state of being an associate; association; society; consort; mutual intercourse. 2. Association on equal and friendly terms; familiarity. 3. A society; a company; a partnership." Webster's definition captures the relational dimension but lacks the theological depth of koinonia's participation — the ontological sharing in the divine life through the Spirit.

Proto-Indo-European *kom- (together, with) → Latin com- / Greek koinos

Greek κοινός (koinos) — common, shared, public, unclean (also used in ritual purity context)
→ κοινωνέω (koinōneō, G2841) — to share, participate, have in common
→ κοινωνία (koinōnia, G2842) — fellowship, communion, sharing, participation (used 19x in NT)
→ κοινωνός (koinōnos, G2844) — partner, sharer, companion

Contrast: κοινός used negatively = "common/unclean" (Acts 10:14) — the same root
  carries both the richest fellowship and the lowest ritual impurity.
  Peter's vision (Acts 10) redeems the koinos — now all nations share in koinōnia.

Latin: communio — communion (from com- + unio, oneness) — used for the Eucharist
English: "fellowship," "communion," "sharing," "participation" (no single word captures it)

Modern Christianity has reduced koinonia to coffee hour. "Fellowship" has come to mean potlucks, small talk, and church socials — the warm feeling of being around people who share your preferences. But biblical koinonia is forged through shared participation in Christ's suffering (Phil 3:10), shared goods (Acts 2:44-45), shared confession (1 John 1:7), and shared mission. It costs something. Comfortable churchgoers who "fellowship" Sunday mornings but share nothing of their resources, burdens, or lives are experiencing something far thinner than what the New Testament commands.

• "The Lord's Supper is not a symbolic ceremony — Paul calls it koinonia: a real participation in Christ's body and blood. That is why he warns so gravely against eating it unworthily."

• "You cannot have koinonia with the Father while walking in darkness (1 John 1:6). Fellowship with God and fellowship with sin are mutually exclusive."

• "The early church didn't just attend the same service — they had all things in common. That's koinonia. The modern church attends the same service and drives separate cars home."
