Rise
/raɪz/
verb
Old English rīsan, to get up. Webster: preterit rose; participle risen. Distinct from raise (to lift another).

See also: Rise

Biblical Definition

Rise is to leave the place of rest or death and stand. The sun rises. A man rises from sleep. The gospel’s center is not a metaphor of “getting up”: the crucified Jesus of Nazareth is risen. The tomb is empty because He rose. The church does not raise Him by enthusiasm. The Father raised Him; He is risen.

Webster 1828 Definition

RISE, v.i. To move upward; to get up; to appear. Preterit rose; participle risen.

RISE, verb intransitive rize. preterit tense rose; participle passive risen.

1. To move to pass upward in any manner; to ascend.

2. To get up; to leave the place of sleep or rest; as, to rise from bed.

3. To get up or move from any recumbent to an erect posture; as, to rise after a fall.

8. To appear above the horizon; to shine; as, the sun or a star rises. He maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good.

Key Scripture

• Mark 16:6 — “But he said to them, ‘Do not be alarmed. You seek Jesus of Nazareth, who was crucified. He is risen! He is not here. See the place where they laid Him.’” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

“Rise and grind” is self-start. Easter is not a motivational poster.

Hustle English steals the verb and leaves the tomb full. A man may rise from bed by alarm. No man rises from death by grit. The angel’s word is history: He is risen. Do not baptize a workout slogan as resurrection.

Usage

• “He is not here. That is not poetry. That is an empty place.”
