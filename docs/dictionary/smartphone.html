Smartphone
/ˈsmɑːrtfoʊn/
noun
Late twentieth-century coinage: a telephone with a computer in it. Webster 1828 has no lemma — no paraphrase of a missing page.

Biblical Definition

Smartphone is a pocket window. It can carry a Bible, a call, a map. It can also set wickedness before the eyes until it clings. Let your eyes look straight ahead. All things are lawful; not all are helpful; do not be brought under the power of any. The device is not the sin. The mastered gaze is.

Key Scripture

• Psalm 101:3 — “I will set nothing wicked before my eyes; I hate the work of those who fall away; It shall not cling to me.” (BTE NKJV)

• 1 Corinthians 6:12 — “All things are lawful for me, but all things are not helpful. All things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any.” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

“I need my phone” talk baptizes a master as a tool.

expand to see more

The slab is useful. The drift is treating it as an organ: always on, always near, always first. A man who cannot sit through a meal without it is already under its power. The other drift is Luddite theater — smashing the tool while the heart still wants the feed. Psalm 101 is about what you set before your eyes. That is a choice, even with a computer in your pocket.

Usage

• “A smartphone is a window. Guard what you set in it.”
