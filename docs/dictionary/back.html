Back
/bæk/
noun / adverb
Old English bæc, the hinder part of the body. Homograph: the body-ridge, and the return to a former place or state.

Biblical Definition

Back holds two ordinary senses that Scripture uses without mash.

1. The hinder part. The body opposite the face. To cast sins behind the back is to put them out of sight in mercy — not to pretend they never happened, but to refuse to keep them in front of the sinner.

2. Return. To go back is to return to the place from which one came. A man may go back in repentance, or go back in unbelief. The adverb does not decide the moral; the direction of the heart does.

Webster 1828 Definition

BACK, n. The hinder part of the body. BACK, adv. To the place from which one came.

BACK, noun

BACK, adverb To the place from which one came; as, to go back is to return.

Key Scripture

• Isaiah 38:17 — “Indeed it was for my own peace That I had great bitterness; But You have lovingly delivered my soul from the pit of corruption, For You have cast all my sins behind Your back.” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

“I’ve got your back” is loyalty talk; it is not the same as God casting sin behind His back.

expand to see more

Street English uses back for support, revenge (“payback”), or retreat. None of those erase Webster’s Scripture sense: to cast behind the back is to forgive. Do not baptize a gang slogan as gospel, and do not treat divine forgetting as divine indifference to holiness.

Usage

• “Sins behind His back are not sins He failed to see. They are sins He refuses to keep in the sinner’s face.”
