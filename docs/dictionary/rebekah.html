Rebekah
/rɪˈbɛk.ə/
proper noun
From Hebrew Rivqah (רִבְקָה), meaning "to bind, to tie firmly" or "captivating beauty." Some connect it to a word for a young cow or heifer — a term of beauty and vitality. Her providential meeting with Abraham's servant at the well displays God's sovereign orchestration of the covenant line.

📖 Biblical Definition

Rebekah is the wife of Isaac, the mother of Esau and Jacob, and a pivotal figure in the transmission of the covenant promise. Her story begins with one of the most beautiful providence narratives in Scripture — Abraham's servant praying for a specific sign at the well, and Rebekah appearing to fulfill it exactly (Genesis 24). She received the oracle from God that "the older shall serve the younger" (Genesis 25:23), and she acted to ensure Jacob received the blessing. While her method (deception) was sinful, her conviction that Jacob was the covenant heir was correct — God had told her so before the twins were born. Rebekah demonstrates the complexity of God's sovereign purposes working through flawed human instruments. Paul uses her in Romans 9:10-12 to teach unconditional election — God's choice of Jacob over Esau was declared to Rebekah before either child had done anything good or bad.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

The wife of Isaac, chosen by divine providence at the well; mother of Esau and Jacob.

REBEK'AH, n. [Heb. רבקה, to bind.] The daughter of Bethuel, sister of Laban, providentially brought to be the wife of Isaac. She received the divine oracle concerning her twin sons and played a key role in the transfer of the patriarchal blessing to Jacob.

📖 Key Scripture

• Genesis 24:14-15 — "Before he had finished speaking, behold, Rebekah... came out with her water jar on her shoulder."

• Genesis 25:23 — "The LORD said to her, 'Two nations are in your womb... the older shall serve the younger.'"

• Genesis 24:58 — "They called Rebekah and said to her, 'Will you go with this man?' She said, 'I will go.'"

• Romans 9:10-12 — "When Rebekah had conceived children by one man... she was told, 'The older will serve the younger.'"

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Rebekah is either condemned as a manipulative schemer or praised as a proto-feminist subverting patriarchy.

expand to see more

Modern readings of Rebekah split into two errors. Moralistic readings condemn her as a deceitful schemer who manipulated her blind husband — turning the narrative into a cautionary tale about dishonesty. Feminist readings celebrate her as a woman who subverted patriarchal authority to ensure the "right" outcome — making her a heroine of female agency. Both miss the point. Rebekah was the recipient of a divine oracle. God told her that Jacob, not Esau, was the chosen one. Her sin was not in the conviction but in the method — she used deception instead of trusting God to fulfill His own word in His own way. The narrative is about divine sovereignty working through human agency, both faithful and unfaithful. Paul's use of Rebekah in Romans 9 is the definitive interpretation: God's election was sovereign and unconditional, declared before either child had done anything.
