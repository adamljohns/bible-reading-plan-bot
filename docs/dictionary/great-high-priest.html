Great High Priest

/ɡreɪt haɪ priːst/
Christological title

Etymology & Webster 1828

Greek archiereus megas (Hebrews 4:14). In Israel's cult, the high priest alone entered the Most Holy Place on Yom Kippur, carrying blood that was not his own. The epistle to the Hebrews argues that Jesus surpasses every human high priest in five ways: (1) His priesthood is after the order of Melchizedek, not Aaron — older, universal, kingly (Heb 7); (2) He is sinless and has no sins of His own to atone for (Heb 7:27); (3) He offered not bulls and goats but His own blood (Heb 9:12); (4) He entered not the earthly copy but heaven itself (Heb 9:24); (5) His priesthood is permanent "by the power of an indestructible life" (Heb 7:16).

Biblical Meaning

The Great High Priest title anchors Christian assurance. Every Old Covenant sacrifice had to be repeated — the very repetition proved its insufficiency (Heb 10:1-4). Christ offered Himself once for all and sat down at God's right hand (Heb 10:12) — priests in the temple never sat, because the work was never done; Christ sat because He finished it. And unlike the OT high priest who only entered once a year, our Great High Priest continually intercedes for us (Heb 7:25). Because He shared our flesh, He sympathizes with our weaknesses (Heb 4:15); because He conquered temptation, He can help those being tempted (Heb 2:18); because He ever lives to intercede, "He is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through Him" (Heb 7:25). Boldness at the throne of grace is not arrogance — it is a correct reading of who stands at the Father's right hand.

Key Scriptures

"Since then we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus, the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession. For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses... Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace."— Hebrews 4:14-16
"He holds His priesthood permanently, because He continues forever. Consequently, He is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them."— Hebrews 7:24-25
"He entered once for all into the holy places, not by means of the blood of goats and calves but by means of his own blood, thus securing an eternal redemption."— Hebrews 9:12
