Force
/fɔːrs/
noun / verb
Latin fortis, strong. Webster: strength; violence against consent; validity; power to bind. Homograph: the noun (might / legal force) and the verb (to compel).

Biblical Definition

Force is power that produces motion — in muscle, in war, in argument, and in law. Jesus: from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force. That is holy urgency, not a warrant for coercion of souls. Hebrews: a testament is in force after men are dead. Homograph, one entry: might, and binding validity. Do not mash gospel force into state violence, or legal force into a shrug.

Webster 1828 Definition

FORCE, n. Strength; active power; violence; validity; power to bind or hold.

FORCE, noun [Latin fortis.]

1. Strength; active power; vigor; might; energy that may be exerted.

4. Violence; power exerted against will or consent; compulsory power.

5. Strength; moral power to convince the mind. There is great force in an argument.

6. Virtue; efficacy.

7. Validity; power to bind or hold. If the conditions of a covenant are not fulfilled, the contract is of no force. A testament is of force after the testator is dead.

8. Strength or power for war; armament; troops.

11. In law, any unlawful violence to person or property.

Key Scripture

• Matthew 11:12 — “And from the days of John the Baptist until now the kingdom of heaven suffers violence, and the violent take it by force.” (BTE NKJV)

• Hebrews 9:17 — “For a testament is in force after men are dead, since it has no power at all while the testator lives.” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

“Don’t force your religion” treats persuasion as assault. Scripture distinguishes kingdom urgency from unlawful violence, and force as legal validity.

expand to see more

Webster already listed violence against consent and unlawful force in law — those are sins. The mash is to call every hard claim “violence” so that Matthew 11:12 becomes embarrassing. Taking the kingdom by force is pressing in, not baptizing a mob. And Hebrews will not let “in force” mean mood: a testament binds because the testator died.
