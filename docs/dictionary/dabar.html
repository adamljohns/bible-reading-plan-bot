Dabar
/ dä·ˈvär /
noun
Hebrew dāvār (דָּבָר) — "word, thing, matter, event." From the root d-b-r (to speak, to drive forward). Remarkably, dabar means both "word" and "thing" — in Hebrew thought, a word spoken with authority is the thing itself. When God speaks, reality happens. The "word of the LORD" (devar-YHWH) appears over 240 times in the Old Testament, always as a creative and commanding force.

📖 Biblical Definition

Dabar is the dynamic, creative, effective word of God that accomplishes what it declares. It is not idle speech or mere information — it is speech that creates reality. "By the word [dabar] of the LORD were the heavens made" (Psalm 33:6). When God speaks, things come into being. When prophets deliver the dabar of the LORD, they are not sharing opinions; they are releasing the active, sovereign word of the living God into history. Dabar also means "thing" or "matter" because in the Hebrew mind, a word and its corresponding reality are inseparable. God's word does not return to Him void; it accomplishes the purpose for which He sent it (Isaiah 55:11). This is why the Ten Commandments are literally the "Ten Words" (ʿaseret ha-devarim) — they are not suggestions but creative decrees that establish moral reality. The New Testament equivalent is logos — "In the beginning was the Word" (John 1:1) — the dabar made flesh.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

WORD — An articulate or vocal sound, or a combination of articulate and vocal sounds, uttered by the human voice, and by custom expressing an idea or ideas. In theology, the second person of the Trinity; Christ. "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." John 1:1. Also, Scripture; the Holy Scriptures. "He that heareth my word and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The modern world has drained words of their power. Speech is considered merely symbolic — labels we attach to things that exist independently of language. This is the opposite of the Hebrew worldview. When "words are just words," promises become breakable, vows become provisional, and truth becomes negotiable. The therapeutic culture treats words as expressions of feeling rather than commitments of will. But dabar insists that words create, bind, and accomplish. A man's word was once his bond because the Hebrew understanding of dabar assumed that speech carries real force. The charismatic abuse of "speaking things into existence" is a distortion, not a correction — it turns human words into magic incantations. But the answer is not to strip words of power altogether; it is to recognize that God's dabar alone has creative authority, and our words carry weight precisely because we are made in the image of the speaking God.
