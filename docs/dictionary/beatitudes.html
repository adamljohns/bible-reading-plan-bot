Beatitudes
/biˈæt.ɪ.tjuːdz/
noun phrase
From Latin beatitudo — "blessedness, happiness." The opening eight (or nine) declarations of the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5:3-12), each beginning "Blessed are..." The word "blessed" translates the Greek makarios — "truly happy, fortunate, in a state of divine favor."

📖 Biblical Definition

The Beatitudes are the opening salvo of the Sermon on the Mount — the great manifesto of the kingdom of God. Each begins "Blessed are..." The word makarios does not mean "lucky" or "cheerful"; it describes the deep, objective state of being in God's favor, whatever the surface circumstances. The Beatitudes are not a ladder of moral achievements; they are a portrait of the kingdom citizen — the kind of person Christ is shaping all His people into. They are also deeply counter-cultural: every Beatitude describes as "blessed" what the world considers miserable. The list (Matthew 5:3-12): (1) "Blessed are the poor in spirit" — those who know their spiritual bankruptcy, for theirs is the kingdom; (2) "Blessed are those who mourn" — over their sin and the world's brokenness, for they will be comforted; (3) "Blessed are the meek" — strength submitted to God, for they will inherit the earth; (4) "Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness" — for they will be filled; (5) "Blessed are the merciful" — for they will obtain mercy; (6) "Blessed are the pure in heart" — for they will see God; (7) "Blessed are the peacemakers" — for they will be called sons of God; (8) "Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake" — for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. The Beatitudes are not optional; they describe the character Christ creates in all His disciples. They are also an indictment of American Christianity, which often prizes the exact opposite qualities: self-confidence, ease, dominance, satisfaction, and popularity.

📖 Key Scripture

Matthew 5:3-6 — "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted. Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth. Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be filled."

Matthew 5:7-9 — "Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy. Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God. Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God."

Matthew 5:10-12 — "Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. Blessed are you when they revile and persecute you, and say all kinds of evil against you falsely for My sake. Rejoice and be exceedingly glad, for great is your reward in heaven."

Luke 6:20-23 — "Blessed are you poor, for yours is the kingdom of God."
