Kingdom of God

Biblical Definition

The Kingdom of God is the reign of God in Christ — His right to rule, His realm, and His redemptive rule breaking into a rebel world. Jesus taught His disciples to pray: “Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven” (Matthew 6:10). The kingdom is not first a political program or a human utopia. It is the Father’s rule through the Son by the Spirit: sinners transferred “from the domain of darkness” into “the kingdom of his beloved Son” (Colossians 1:13). Christ reigns now; He will reign openly. The church does not *become* the kingdom by activism; the church bears witness to the King, preaches His gospel, and obeys His Word until He returns.

Webster 1828 Definition

KINGDOM, n. The territory or country subject to a king; the inhabitants or population subject to a king; government; royal authority.

KING'DOM, n. 1. The territory or country subject to a king; the inhabitants subject to a king. 2. Royal authority; supreme power. 3. A region; a tract. 4. In Scripture, the government or universal dominion of God.

Note: Webster’s instinct is right on the main point — a kingdom is about *rule*, not branding. Modern church marketing often keeps the word and loses the King.

Key Scripture

• Matthew 6:9–10 — “Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.”

• Colossians 1:13–14 — transferred from darkness into the kingdom of His beloved Son.

• John 18:36 — “My kingdom is not of this world.”

• Matthew 6:33 — “Seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness.”

• Mark 1:15 — “The kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel.”

• Daniel 7:13–14 — dominion given to the Son of Man.

Modern Corruption

“Kingdom” is hijacked by rival gospels: social programs without repentance, dominion maps without the cross, and prosperity schemes that crown the self.

expand to see more

Three common corruptions: (1) the social gospel turns the kingdom into political liberation and identity frameworks (DEI/CRT) while muting sin, wrath, and new birth; (2) NAR / dominion language turns the kingdom into territorial conquest under self-appointed apostles; (3) prosperity turns the kingdom into cashflow and “breakthrough.” Jesus refused a kingdom advanced by worldly force (John 18:36). Any “kingdom” that does not require the cross, the Word, and holiness is not His.
