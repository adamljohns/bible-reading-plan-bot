Awe
/ô/
noun / verb
Old Norse agi — fear, terror; Old English ege — fear, dread; cognate with Hebrew yir'ah (יִרְאָה) — fear, reverence, awe before the Lord.

📖 Biblical Definition

Awe is the reverential dread produced by an encounter with something overwhelmingly holy, powerful, and beyond human comprehension — supremely, the living God. Biblical awe is inseparable from fear of the Lord: it combines terror at God's infinite holiness and power with profound reverence and wonder at His goodness and grace. It is the only appropriate response to a theophany — God's self-revelation. Isaiah fell undone (Isa. 6:5); Job was silenced (Job 40:4); John fell as dead (Rev. 1:17). Awe is not a warm spiritual feeling — it is the creature's recognition of the Creator's absolute otherness. Scripture calls this the "beginning of wisdom" (Ps. 111:10). Where awe is absent, idolatry fills the vacuum.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Fear mingled with admiration or reverence; the emotion inspired by something grand, sublime, or extremely powerful. "The grandeur of God's creation struck him with awe." Also: the reverential fear of God's majesty which restrains men from sin.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The word "awe" has been thoroughly domesticated. In modern usage, one feels "awe" at a sunset, a sports play, or a TED talk. Stripped of its divine referent, awe becomes mere aesthetic wonder — an emotional experience available through any sufficiently stimulating spectacle. Psychologists now study "awe" as a positive emotion that promotes "self-transcendence," completely divorcing it from the God who is its only proper object. The result: people are perpetually awed by everything — and therefore truly awed by nothing, especially not by God.
