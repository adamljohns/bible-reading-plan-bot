Nihilism
/NYE-uh-liz-um/
noun
From Latin nihil — "nothing." Coined in the early 19th century. Popularized by Ivan Turgenev's novel Fathers and Sons (1862) to describe the radical rejection of all established authority, values, and institutions. Philosophically developed by Friedrich Nietzsche, who diagnosed nihilism as the inevitable consequence of the death of God in Western consciousness.

📖 Biblical Definition

Nihilism is the belief that life has no objective meaning, purpose, or intrinsic value. It is the philosophical endpoint of rejecting God — the logical destination of a road that begins with doubt and ends in the void.

Scripture describes the nihilistic condition with devastating accuracy. Ecclesiastes records Solomon's experiment with meaning apart from God: "Vanity of vanities; all is vanity" (Ecclesiastes 1:2). The Hebrew word hevel (הֶבֶל) means "vapor, breath, meaninglessness." Solomon tried pleasure, wisdom, wealth, and achievement — and found them all hevel when separated from God. This is the most honest nihilistic text ever written — and its conclusion is not despair but faith: "Fear God, and keep His commandments: for this is the whole duty of man" (Ecclesiastes 12:13).

Paul describes the gentile condition apart from God as being "without hope, and without God in the world" (Ephesians 2:12). This is nihilism in a single phrase. The psalmist describes the fool who says "there is no God" (Psalm 14:1) — and the immediate result is moral corruption, because meaning and morality are inseparable from the God who grounds them both.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

The term "nihilism" was not yet in common English usage in 1828.

expand to see more

No entry exists in Webster 1828. The concept would not gain philosophical prominence until Nietzsche's work in the 1880s. Webster did define NIHIL (Latin: "nothing") and would have recognized the philosophical danger of building on nothingness: his entire dictionary project was built on the conviction that words have real meanings grounded in real things — the opposite of nihilism.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Nihilism is no longer a philosophical diagnosis — it is the operating system of Western culture, disguised as entertainment, irony, and "living your truth."

expand to see more

Nietzsche predicted that the death of God would produce nihilism, and he was terrified by his own prediction. He understood that without a transcendent ground of meaning, everything — morality, purpose, human dignity — would dissolve. He was right.

Modern nihilism does not announce itself with Nietzsche's philosophical rigor. It wears a smile. It shows up as the ironic detachment that mocks everything and commits to nothing. It appears as the "nothing matters" shrug of a generation raised on screens and starved of purpose. It manifests as the depression epidemic, the suicide crisis, the opioid plague — all symptoms of a civilization that has removed God and found nothing to replace Him with.

The phrase "live your truth" is nihilism in costume. If there is no objective truth, then "your truth" is just your preference — and preferences provide no foundation for enduring meaning, sacrifice, or hope. You cannot live on your own breath.

The church's answer to nihilism is not an argument but a Person. Ecclesiastes tried every human path to meaning and declared them all hevel — except one: fearing God and keeping His commandments. Jesus told His disciples "I am the vine; apart from me you can do nothing" (John 15:5). Nihilism is what "nothing" looks like when it is lived. Christ is what "everything" looks like when it arrives.
