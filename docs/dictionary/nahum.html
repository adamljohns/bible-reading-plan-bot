Nahum

/ˈneɪəm/
proper noun / prophet

Etymology & Webster 1828

Hebrew Nachum, "comfort." Seventh-century BC prophet (c. 650 BC), from the obscure Elkosh (location unknown). Nahum prophesied the impending fall of Nineveh, the capital of Assyria, which occurred in 612 BC when a coalition of Babylonians and Medes overthrew the city. Where Jonah had preached repentance a century earlier and Nineveh had (briefly) turned, now the Assyrians had reverted to brutal tyranny — raping nations, deporting populations, flaying enemies alive and displaying their skins on city walls. Nahum announces the end.

Biblical Meaning

Nahum is the prophet of God's vengeance against unrepentant tyranny. The book opens with one of the most terrifying theophanies in Scripture: "The LORD is a jealous and avenging God; the LORD is avenging and wrathful; the LORD takes vengeance on his adversaries and keeps wrath for his enemies. The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and the LORD will by no means clear the guilty. His way is in whirlwind and storm, and the clouds are the dust of his feet" (1:2-3). Then — right in the middle of wrath — a single gospel verse: "The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble; he knows those who take refuge in him" (1:7). The God who destroys Nineveh is the God who shelters the oppressed. Nahum's book answers the cry of Jonah 4 in reverse — God's patience with evil is not endless. A generation pleading with God to act against monstrous regimes can find comfort in Nahum; judgment is coming. Modern application: take heart when evil seems to prevail. The Assyrias of every age are on divine borrowed time.

Key Scriptures
