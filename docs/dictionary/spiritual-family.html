Spiritual Family / Household of God
/ˈspɪr.ɪ.tʃu.əl ˈfæm.ə.li/ · HOWS-hohld uhv god
noun phrase
Household of God is the New Testament phrase (Eph 2:19; 1 Tim 3:15). Spiritual family is ordinary pastoral English for the same reality. Also called the family of God and the household of faith.
Also filed as: Household of God · Spiritual Family · Family of God (related)

📖 Biblical Definition

Spiritual family and household of God name one thing: the church as God’s family and dwelling — all who are born again, adopted by the Father, united to Christ, and bound as brothers and sisters under one Lord. Scripture’s controlling language is household of God and house of God; pastoral speech often says spiritual family. This dictionary keeps them as one entry so the vernacular and the text stay locked together.

Paul writes that believers are “no more strangers and foreigners, but fellowcitizens with the saints, and of the household of God” (Ephesians 2:19), and that the house of God is “the church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth” (1 Timothy 3:15). Jesus marks the bond: “Whoever does the will of my Father in heaven is my brother and sister and mother” (Matthew 12:50). The image gathers adoption, dwelling, and order: sons under one Father; a habitation of God through the Spirit; officers, duties, and discipline — not a club of customers.

Nuance that must be kept: this family is not identical to every visible congregation, every membership roll, or every room that uses the word “church.” The true spiritual family / household of God is the company of the regenerate — what theology has long called the invisible church. That household is made visible, trained, and ordered through the visible church. On earth that people still wars against sin, the world, and the devil (church militant); in glory the same people rest with Christ (church triumphant). Local church membership is the ordinary commanded form of household life — not optional, and not a consumer upgrade.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Webster has no dual headword; the sense is recovered from family, household, and church as God’s people.

expand to see more

FAM'ILY, n. The collective body of persons who live in one house and under one head or manager; a household. Also, those who descend from one common progenitor; a race; a tribe.

HOUSE'HOLD, n. Those who dwell under the same roof and compose a family.

CHURCH, n. In its most general sense, the collective body of Christians, or of those who profess to believe in Christ.

HOUSEHOLD OF GOD (biblical usage) — the church as the family and dwelling of God; believers fellowcitizens and members of His house, built on Christ the corner stone, indwelt by the Spirit, ordered with officers, duties, and discipline.

Note: “Spiritual family” is later pastoral English for that same household — entered by regeneration and adoption, not by natural descent alone.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The household is reduced to a club of preference, or “spiritual family” is stretched into warm vibes that rewrite marriage, natural family, and holiness.

expand to see more

1. Consumer church. The household of God is treated as a voluntary service-provider left at will. Believers are not customers but children, with duties to the Father and to one another (Eph 2:19; 1 Tim 3:15).

2. Sentiment without new birth. “Spiritual family” becomes any affirming group that uses Jesus-language. Entrance is by regeneration and adoption, not by feelings of belonging.

3. Rival to creation ordinances. Some use spiritual-family language to neglect spouse, children, or parents, or to redefine marriage. Scripture honors both the natural household and God’s household. A man who will not provide for his own has denied the faith (1 Timothy 5:8).

4. Soft cover for soft churches. When “family” means unconditional affirmation without repentance, truth, or discipline, the house of God is swapped for a club. Love that blesses what God calls sin is not household loyalty; it is house-wrecking. Context, marriage, and church order matter because God’s family is ordered under His Word.

Recover one reality under two names: the ordered, Spirit-indwelt household of the living God — entered by new birth, lived in the visible church, fighting as the church militant until the church triumphant.
