Sanctity
/ˈsaŋk-tə-tē/
noun
From Latin sanctitas — holiness, sacredness; from sanctus (holy, consecrated). Related to sanctification, sanctuary, and saint. The word carries the sense of something possessing inherent, inviolable worth because it belongs to God.

📖 Biblical Definition

The quality of being holy — set apart, inviolable, possessing sacred worth that must not be transgressed. Sanctity is grounded in God's own holiness communicated to His creation. Most significantly, the sanctity of human life flows from the imago Dei: every person bears God's image, and to shed innocent blood is to attack the image of God (Gen. 9:6). Sanctity implies both positive worth (to be honored and protected) and negative prohibition (not to be violated or treated as merely utilitarian). The sanctity of marriage, of the Lord's Day, of vows, of the body — all rest on the same foundation: God has declared these things holy, and what God has declared holy, no human authority may profane.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

SANCTITY, n. Holiness; the state of being holy; purity of heart and life; moral goodness. Also, the state of anything reverenced and held sacred; as the sanctity of an oath, or of a vow. Inviolability; as the sanctity of conscience. That which is sacred; a holy thing.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The doctrine of the sanctity of human life is the great battleground of our era. Abortion reduces the unborn to a non-person; euthanasia redefines the value of the aged and ill by their utility. Both rest on the same foundation: that human worth is assigned rather than inherent — and the strong may assign it or remove it as they see fit. This is the inversion of sanctity. Sexual ethics similarly: the sanctity of the body as a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor. 6:19) is replaced by the body as a personal instrument for pleasure. Wherever God's authority to declare something holy is denied, sanctity collapses.
