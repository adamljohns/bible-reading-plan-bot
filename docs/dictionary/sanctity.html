Sanctity
/ ˈsaŋ(k)-tə-tē /
noun
From Latin sanctitas — "holiness, sacredness"; from sanctus (holy, sacred) — past participle of sancire (to make sacred, to ratify). Related to sanctify, sanctuary, and saint. The state of being set apart as holy, inviolable, and sacred before God.

📖 Biblical Definition

The quality of being holy, consecrated, and set apart — belonging to God and therefore possessing inherent sacred worth that must not be violated. The sanctity of human life is grounded in the Imago Dei: every person is made in the image of God (Genesis 1:26–27), giving each life immeasurable, non-negotiable value from conception to natural death. The sanctity of marriage is grounded in its divine institution and covenantal nature (Genesis 2:24; Matthew 19:6). Sanctity is not earned or given by the state; it is conferred by the Creator. To violate what is holy is to transgress against God himself.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

SANC'TITY, n. Holiness of life and disposition; godliness; saintliness. Also, the quality of being sacred or inviolable; sacredness. "The sanctity of an oath." The sanctity of life refers to the sacred character of human life as divinely created and therefore not to be violated by man.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The "sanctity of life" — particularly of the unborn — has been explicitly challenged by modern bioethics, which grounds human value in function (sentience, viability, productivity) rather than in the image of God. When sanctity is replaced by utility, the weakest become expendable: the unborn, the disabled, the elderly. "Sanctity" also collapses in marriage discourse, where the permanent, exclusive, covenant of one man and one woman is redefined as any consensual arrangement. Without a Creator, there is no sanctity — only preference.
