Fruit of the Spirit
/fruːt əv ðə ˈspɪr.ɪt/
noun phrase (doctrine)
Paul's nine-fold description in Galatians 5:22-23 of the character qualities the Holy Spirit produces in every believer as a consequence of His indwelling: love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. Contrasted with the works of the flesh (5:19-21).

📖 Biblical Definition

The Fruit of the Spirit is the signature passage of Christian sanctification. Paul lists the works of the flesh — adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lewdness, idolatry, sorcery, hatred, contentions, jealousies, outbursts of wrath, selfish ambitions, dissensions, heresies, envy, murders, drunkenness, revelries, and the like — and then announces the counter-catalog: "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Against such there is no law" (Galatians 5:22-23). Several details matter. First, "fruit" is singular in the Greek (karpos, not karpoi). These are not nine separate fruits to be cultivated piece by piece; they are one ninefold fruit that grows together in the life of the Spirit-filled believer. Second, this is not a list of Christian virtues we produce by effort; it is what the Spirit produces in us. The fruit of an apple tree is apples — it is the natural outflow of what the tree is. The fruit of the Spirit in a believer is the natural outflow of the Spirit's indwelling. Third, the nine qualities are not interchangeable — each adds something distinct: love (self-giving for another's good), joy (settled delight independent of circumstances), peace (wholeness and right ordering), longsuffering (patience under provocation), kindness (active goodness in concrete action), goodness (moral excellence), faithfulness (reliable covenant-keeping), gentleness (strength under control), self-control (mastery of one's desires). Fourth, "against such there is no law" — the Mosaic law could condemn wickedness but could not produce righteousness. Only the Spirit can produce fruit. The Christian life is not a cleanup operation; it is a gardening operation. We do not trim sins off a dying plant; we cultivate the new life of the Spirit and the fruit grows.

📖 Key Scripture

Galatians 5:22-23 — "But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control. Against such there is no law."

Galatians 5:16 — "I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh."

Galatians 5:24-25 — "And those who are Christ's have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires. If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit."

John 15:4-5 — "Abide in Me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in Me. I am the vine, you are the branches."
