Fine
/faɪn/
adjective / noun
Homograph. Sense 1: refined, pure, not coarse — fine linen, fine gold. Sense 2: a money penalty. Keep both; mash neither.

Biblical Definition

Fine is one spelling with two honest senses.

1. Refined, pure, not coarse. The bride is granted to be arrayed in fine linen, clean and bright; the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints. Fine here is quality under holiness, not fashion.

2. A penalty paid in money. Ordinary English still uses the noun for a court mulct. That is not Revelation 19. Do not read the wedding garment as a traffic ticket.

Webster 1828 Definition

FINE, adj. Small; thin; clear; pure; free from feculence. (Penalty sense is a separate English use of the same spelling.)

FINE, adjective

1. Small; thin; slender; minute; of very small diameter; as a fine thread; fine silk; a fine hair.

4. Made of fine threads; not coarse; as fine linen or cambric.

5. Clear; pure; free from feculence or foreign matter; as fine gold or silver; wine is not good till fine.

Key Scripture

• Revelation 19:8 — “And to her it was granted to be arrayed in fine linen, clean and bright, for the fine linen is the righteous acts of the saints.” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

“I’m fine” means unbothered. “Fine linen” means granted purity, not a shrug.

expand to see more

Casual English flattened fine to “okay.” Court English kept the penalty. Neither sense should steal Revelation’s garment. The linen is granted, clean, and bright — righteous acts, not a mood and not a fee.

Usage

• “Fine linen is given, not bought at the mall and not paid as a ticket.”
