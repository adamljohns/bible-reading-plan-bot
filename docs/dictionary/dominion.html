Dominion
/dəˈmɪn.jən/
noun
Latin: dominium — ownership, lordship; from dominus — lord, master; from domus — house. Hebrew: רָדָה (rādāh) — to have dominion, rule, tread down

📖 Biblical Definition

Dominion is the God-given authority entrusted to mankind to govern, steward, and cultivate creation under God's ultimate sovereignty. In Genesis 1:26–28, God commissions humanity as His image-bearers to exercise dominion over the earth — not as autonomous rulers, but as vice-regents accountable to the Creator-King. Dominion is not exploitation but stewardship; it carries responsibility proportional to the authority granted. The Fall corrupted humanity's exercise of dominion, introducing tyranny, abuse, and mismanagement. Christ, the Last Adam, restores true dominion through His resurrection and reign, and calls His people to exercise godly authority in every sphere of life — family, church, culture, and creation.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

DOMIN'ION, n. [L. dominium, from dominus.] 1. Sovereign or supreme authority; the power of governing and controlling. 2. Right of possession and use without being accountable; as the private dominion of individuals. 3. Territory under a government; region; country; district governed, or within the limits of the authority of a prince or state. 4. Government; right of governing. 5. A celestial order of beings.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern environmentalism and secular progressivism treat the biblical mandate of dominion as the root cause of ecological destruction, branding it as "speciesism" or anthropocentric arrogance. Some dismiss Genesis 1:28 as a license for exploitation that has ravaged the planet. This misreads Scripture — dominion in the Bible is always accountable stewardship, not license for destruction. Conversely, some misapply dominion to justify authoritarian political control or religious nationalism divorced from the servant-leadership modeled by Christ. True dominion looks like faithful gardening, not conquest.
