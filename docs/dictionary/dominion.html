Dominion
/də-ˈmin-yən/
noun
Latin dominium — ownership, lordship, from dominus — lord, master. Hebrew radah (רָדָה) — to have dominion, rule over, tread down; kabash (כָּבַשׁ) — to subdue, bring into subjection.

📖 Biblical Definition

Dominion is the God-delegated authority of humanity to rule, subdue, and steward the created order under God's sovereign lordship. In Genesis 1:26–28, God commissions mankind — created in His image — to exercise dominion over all living creatures and the earth itself. This is the Cultural Mandate: not exploitation, but vice-regent rule under the Owner of all things. Dominion carries the weight of responsibility: to rule as God rules — wisely, justly, for the flourishing of what is entrusted. The Fall distorted dominion into exploitation and abdication. Christ, the last Adam, restores true dominion (Ps. 8; Heb. 2:6–8), and the church is commissioned to extend His kingdom rule into every domain of life.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Sovereign or supreme authority; the power of governing and controlling; territory under a government; right of ownership. "God gave man dominion over the fish of the sea, the fowl of the air, and every living creature."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Environmentalist ideology has redefined biblical dominion as the root cause of ecological destruction — turning the Cultural Mandate into an excuse for abuse. Some theologians, embarrassed by the charge, have capitulated by replacing dominion language with mere "stewardship," effectively stripping humanity of its vice-regent status. On the other side, some Christians misuse dominion theology to justify authoritarian political control. The biblical vision is neither exploitation nor abdication nor theocratic conquest — it is image-bearers ruling wisely under the authority of the Creator, for His glory and creation's good.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

H3533 — כָּבַשׁ (kabash) — to subdue, bring under control; the Cultural Mandate's second charge (Gen. 1:28).

G2904 — κράτος (kratos) — dominion, power, might; used in doxologies ascribing dominion to Christ (1 Tim. 6:16; Rev. 1:6).
