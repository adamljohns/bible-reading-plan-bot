Dominion
/dəˈmɪnjən/
noun
Latin: dominium — from dominus (lord, master, owner). Hebrew: rādāh (רָדָה) — to rule, have dominion, tread down. Greek: katakurieuō (κατακυριεύω) — to exercise authority over.

📖 Biblical Definition

The God-given authority and responsibility of humanity to govern, cultivate, and care for the created order as vice-regents under the sovereign rule of God. In the creation mandate (Gen 1:28), God commissions mankind — male and female together — to rule over every living creature and fill the earth. This dominion is not exploitative tyranny but stewardly kingship: authority exercised on God's behalf, for God's glory, and according to God's character. Dominion is marred by the Fall but restored in principle by Christ, the Last Adam, who reclaims rightful lordship over all things.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

DOMIN'ION, n. Sovereign or supreme authority; the power of governing and controlling. Government; territory under a government. Predominance; ascendancy; power. In Scripture, it sometimes denotes an order of angels. The right of possession and use without being accountable, as the dominion of the Creator over his creatures. Lordship; ownership.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Environmentalism often rejects the biblical dominion mandate as the root of ecological destruction, preferring to see humanity as merely one species among many rather than the designated stewards of creation. This overcorrection denies human uniqueness and fails to account for the stewardship obligation within the mandate. Conversely, some have used "dominion" to justify extractive exploitation of creation — a distortion that ignores the servant-lordship model exemplified by Christ. The word has also been politicized around "Dominionism," a caricature that conflates faithful cultural engagement with theocratic power-seeking.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

H7287 — rādāh (רָדָה): to have dominion, rule, tread; used in Genesis 1:28 for mankind's commission to rule over the animal kingdom.

H3533 — kābaš (כָּבַשׁ): to subdue, bring into bondage; used in Genesis 1:28 alongside rādāh — taming and ordering creation for flourishing.

G2634 — katakurieuō (κατακυριεύω): to exercise lordship over; used negatively in Matthew 20:25 of Gentile rulers lording it over others — the opposite of servant dominion.
