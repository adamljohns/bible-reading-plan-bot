Dominion
/də-ˈmin-yən/
noun
From Latin dominium — ownership, lordship, from dominus (lord, master). Related to domain and dominate. The Hebrew radah (to rule, have dominion) carries a sense of treading down, governing.

📖 Biblical Definition

The authority and responsibility given to humanity by God to rule over creation as His vice-regents (Gen. 1:26–28). Dominion is not exploitation but stewardship — the exercise of God-delegated authority in service of His purposes. The Cultural Mandate (fill the earth, subdue it, have dominion) was given at creation and was never revoked; it is fulfilled through faithful work, family, governance, art, science, and culture-building. The Fall corrupted how dominion is exercised (now involving toil and conflict), but Christ — the Last Adam — is restoring dominion as it was intended. Redeemed humanity is called to rule rightly under the King of Kings.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

DOMINION, n. Sovereign or supreme authority; the power of governing and controlling. In Scripture, dominion denotes the authority given to man over the creatures, and the right to use them for his benefit. It also signifies the territory or country over which sovereignty is exercised.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The word "dominion" has become toxic in secular environmental discourse, blamed for human exploitation of the natural world. The solution offered is to reduce human authority over creation. But the problem is not dominion itself — it is corrupt dominion exercised by fallen humanity. The answer is not abdication but redemption: humans exercising God-reflecting stewardship rather than self-serving exploitation. The other corruption is theological — some reduce dominion to a personal inner kingdom of self-mastery, losing its cosmic, cultural, and social dimensions.
