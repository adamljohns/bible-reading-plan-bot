Depravity
/ di-ˈprav-ə-tē /
noun
Latin depravare — "to distort, corrupt" | from de- (completely) + pravus (crooked, wicked)

📖 Biblical Definition

The fallen condition of human nature inherited from Adam's transgression, by which every faculty of man — mind, will, affections, conscience — is morally corrupted and bent away from God. Scripture teaches total depravity: not that every person is as wicked as they could possibly be, but that every part of man is tainted by sin such that, apart from divine grace, no one seeks God, chooses righteousness, or contributes anything to their salvation (Rom 3:10–12). Depravity explains why man needs new birth (John 3:3), why regeneration must precede faith, and why the gospel is not advice to improve but power to resurrect (Eph 2:1–5). It is the uncomfortable diagnosis that makes the gospel's cure comprehensible — the sicker the patient, the greater the Physician.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

DEPRAV'ITY, n. A vitiated or corrupt state of moral character; the want of holiness or correspondence to the divine will; moral corruption; wickedness; as the depravity of mankind; depravity of the heart; total depravity. The doctrine of total depravity teaches that sin has corrupted every faculty and every part of human nature, so that man, in his natural state, is altogether sinful and capable of no spiritual good.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture militantly rejects the doctrine of human depravity. Secular humanism insists man is fundamentally good — corrupted only by bad environments, unjust systems, or lack of education. Progressive theology follows suit, reducing sin to structural injustice while excusing personal accountability. This produces a civilization that builds elaborate social programs to fix human problems while refusing to diagnose the root cause. The irony: denying depravity guarantees its proliferation. The MOOP worldview holds the opposite — acknowledging total depravity is the beginning of wisdom, because it destroys pride, demands grace, and opens the heart to the gospel.
