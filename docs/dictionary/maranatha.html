Maranatha
/ˌmer-ə-ˈnä-thə/
Aramaic exclamation / prayer
Aramaic מָרַן אֲתָא (maran atha) — "Our Lord, come!" Alternatively parsed as marana tha — same meaning. One of the few Aramaic words preserved in the Greek New Testament, indicating it was a living liturgical expression of the early church. Also found in the Didache (early Christian manual, c. AD 50–120).

📖 Biblical Definition

Maranatha is the primal eschatological prayer of the early church — the cry of the bride longing for her Bridegroom's return. Found in 1 Corinthians 16:22, it closes the letter with a solemn invocation: those who do not love the Lord are accursed; then immediately, "Maranatha" — Come, Lord Jesus! The same cry surfaces at the close of Revelation (22:20): "Even so, come, Lord Jesus." It is at once a confession of faith (Jesus is Lord, Maran), a petition (come!), and an ethical posture (we live looking for His appearing). The church is defined partly by this cry — those who love His appearing (2 Tim. 4:8) are those who pray Maranatha with genuine longing.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

An Aramaic expression signifying "Our Lord cometh," or "Our Lord is coming." Used as a solemn form of devotion and as a signal of impending divine judgment. The expression was sometimes used as an imprecation — invoking the Lord's coming as judgment upon the ungodly.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

In a church increasingly at home in the world, Maranatha has become largely decorative — a liturgical relic rather than a living cry. When Christians prioritize building comfortable earthly lives, the prayer "Come, Lord Jesus!" becomes awkward or even unwelcome (it would interrupt too much). Prosperity gospel reduces eschatology to present blessing; progressive Christianity is embarrassed by a literal return of Christ. Both result in a church no longer genuinely longing for Christ's appearing — which Paul lists as a mark of those who will receive "the crown of righteousness" (2 Tim. 4:8).

📖 Key Scripture

1 Corinthians 16:22 — If anyone has no love for the Lord, let him be accursed. Our Lord, come!

Revelation 22:20 — He who testifies to these things says, "Surely I am coming soon." Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!

2 Timothy 4:8 — Henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord... will award to me on that day, and not only to me but also to all who have loved his appearing.

Philippians 3:20 — But our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G2962 — κύριος (kurios) — Lord; the Greek equivalent of the Aramaic Maran — confessing Jesus as divine Lord.

G2064 — ἔρχομαι (erchomai) — to come; Rev. 22:20 — "Come, Lord Jesus" — the final promise and final prayer of Scripture.
