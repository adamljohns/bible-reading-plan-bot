Maranatha
/ ˌmar-ə-ˈna-thə /
exclamation / prayer
Aramaic māranā thā (מָרָנָא תָא) — "Our Lord, come!" or alternatively, as māran ʾethā, "Our Lord has come." One of the few Aramaic phrases preserved untranslated in the NT (1 Corinthians 16:22), suggesting it was a well-known liturgical cry of the early church — the prayer on every believer's lips as they longed for Christ's return.

📖 Biblical Definition

Maranatha is the prayer of the longing church — a two-word petition that captures the entire eschatological hope of Christianity: "Our Lord, come!" It appears in 1 Corinthians 16:22 as Paul's closing prayer, and the same cry echoes in the final verses of Revelation: "Come, Lord Jesus!" (Revelation 22:20). That the early church retained this Aramaic phrase — the mother tongue of Jesus — suggests it carried emotional and liturgical weight from the very beginning. It is the prayer of the exile who has not forgotten home. It is the prayer of suffering believers who know that history is moving toward a Person, not a policy. Every time the church prays maranatha, it declares: this world is not the end.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

MARANATHA, n. A word used by the apostle Paul (1 Corinthians 16:22) as an expression of solemn warning. It is an Aramaic phrase signifying "the Lord cometh" or "our Lord, come." It was used in solemn devotion by the early Christians to express their ardent desire and prayer for the coming of the Lord Jesus Christ.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The disappearance of maranatha from the modern church's vocabulary signals something deeper: we have stopped longing for Christ's return. Comfortable Christianity has made this world livable enough that we no longer cry "Come, Lord Jesus!" with urgency. We have built our towers, settled into our suburbs, and begun to act as though the age is not ending. The early church, marked by suffering and expectancy, prayed this prayer with desperate longing. It is also significant that Revelation ends with both maranatha (22:20) and a warning not to add or subtract from the book — suggesting that those who diminish the eschatological hope of Scripture are precisely those who most need the rebuke it contains.

📖 Key Scripture

1 Corinthians 16:22 — "If anyone has no love for the Lord, let him be accursed. Our Lord, come!"

Revelation 22:20 — "He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!"

Philippians 3:20 — "But our citizenship is in heaven, and from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ."

Revelation 22:17 — "The Spirit and the Bride say, 'Come.' And let the one who hears say, 'Come.'"
