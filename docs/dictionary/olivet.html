Olivet
OL-i-vet
proper noun
Latin olivetum, olive grove. Hebrew Har ha-Zeitim, “Mount of Olives.” The ridge east of Jerusalem from which Christ wept over the city, taught His longest prophetic discourse, prayed in Gethsemane, and ascended.

📖 Biblical Definition

Olivet — the Mount of Olives — is the ridge running north-south east of Jerusalem across the Kidron Valley, rising about 2,700 feet. It was the geographic stage of the most decisive moments of Christ’s passion week and resurrection. He wept over the city from its western slope: "O Jerusalem, Jerusalem... how often would I have gathered thy children together!" (Luke 19:41; Matthew 23:37). He delivered the Olivet Discourse on its crest (Matthew 24-25). He prayed in Gethsemane at its foot (Matthew 26:36-46). He was arrested there. And He ascended bodily into heaven from Olivet (Acts 1:9-12) — and Zechariah prophesies His feet shall stand on the same mount at His return (Zechariah 14:4).

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The hill that saw the tears, the prayer, and the ascension will see Him return.

expand to see more

Olivet is the most concentrated geography in the Gospels. Its slope saw Jesus weep over Jerusalem; its summit saw the Olivet Discourse where He answered the disciples about the end of the age; its base held the garden where He sweat blood. Three days later, the same ridge saw Him risen and walking; forty days after that, the same hill watched Him ascend. The angels who said This same Jesus... shall so come in like manner were standing on Olivet.

Zechariah 14:4 closes the loop: His feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives. The mountain that absorbed His tears will host His return. Modern Christianity has spiritualized Zechariah out of its plain reading; the ground itself disagrees. The literal Olivet that Christ literally ascended from will see literal feet again. Look up.
