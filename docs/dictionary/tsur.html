Tsur (Rock)
TSOOR
Hebrew noun
Hebrew tsur (צוּר) — rock, cliff, stronghold; the immovable foundation.

📖 Biblical Definition

The Hebrew word for rock, especially used as a divine title. Tsur is the great cliff-face, the strong-rock, the immovable foundation. "He is the Rock, his work is perfect" (Deut 32:4). Distinct from even (a stone). Christ is the spiritual Rock that followed Israel (1 Cor 10:4).

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Hebrew "rock" — YHWH as immovable foundation.

The Hebrew word for rock, especially the large cliff or stronghold-rock (distinct from even, a stone). Used extensively as a divine title: "the LORD is my rock" (Ps 18:2). The Song of Moses calls God "the Rock" repeatedly (Deut 32). Paul identifies the rock that gave water in the wilderness as Christ (1 Cor 10:4).

📖 Key Scripture

Deuteronomy 32:4 — "He is the Rock, his work is perfect: for all his ways are judgment: a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he."

Psalm 18:2 — "The LORD is my rock, and my fortress, and my deliverer; my God, my strength, in whom I will trust."

1 Corinthians 10:4 — "And did all drink the same spiritual drink: for they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them: and that Rock was Christ."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Reduced to a stable-image without the cliff-face / stronghold-rock force the Hebrew carries; the divine-title weight thinned.

expand to see more

Modern "rock" suggests pebble or boulder. Hebrew tsur suggests cliff-face, stronghold, immovable bulk. When the Song of Moses calls God "the Rock," the picture is the great stone wall in the desert — you can climb it, hide behind it, build on it.

Recover the bulk: YHWH is not pebble-stable; He is cliff-stable. Christ is the Rock that gave water in the wilderness — the same Rock that follows the saints across all the deserts.
