Teshuvah
/ tə·ˈshü·və /
noun
Hebrew teshûvâh (תְּשׁוּבָה) — "return, repentance, turning back." From shûv (to turn, to return). The root idea is not merely feeling sorry but physically turning around — reversing direction. It implies that sin is a departure from God and repentance is the journey home. The word carries the full weight of covenant restoration: the prodigal turning his face back toward the father's house.

📖 Biblical Definition

Teshuvah is the act of turning back to God after having wandered. It is not merely regret or emotional distress over sin — it is a complete reversal of direction, a reorientation of the whole life toward the Lord. The prophets thundered teshuvah as God's primary demand: "Return unto me, and I will return unto you" (Malachi 3:7). It requires three things: recognition of sin, confession before God, and a changed course of action. Teshuvah is what the father in the parable was watching for — not a letter of apology from the far country, but the sight of his son walking home (Luke 15:20). It is the foundation of covenant life. Without teshuvah, there is no restoration. God does not negotiate with rebels who remain in rebellion; He receives sons who come home. The entire Old Testament prophetic corpus can be summarized in one word: shûv — return.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

REPENTANCE — Sorrow for any thing done or said; the pain or grief which a person experiences in consequence of the injury or inconvenience produced by his own conduct. In theology, the pain, regret or affliction which a person feels on account of his past conduct, because it exposes him to punishment. Real penitence; sorrow or deep contrition for sin, as an offense and dishonor to God, a violation of his holy law, and the basest ingratitude towards a Being of infinite benevolence.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity has softened teshuvah into mere "feeling bad" about sin. Repentance has become an emotion rather than an action. People claim repentance while continuing in the same direction — they feel guilty but do not turn. This is not teshuvah; it is remorse without change, the sorrow of Judas rather than Peter. Therapeutic culture has also reframed sin as "brokenness" and repentance as "healing" — passive language that removes personal responsibility. You do not "heal" from adultery; you repent of it. You do not "process" your rebellion; you turn from it. The Hebrew is stubbornly physical: teshuvah means your feet move in a different direction. Any theology that allows a man to claim repentance while his life remains unchanged has abandoned the biblical concept entirely.

📖 Key Scripture

• Isaiah 55:7 — "Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts: and let him return unto the LORD."

• Ezekiel 18:30 — "Repent, and turn yourselves from all your transgressions; so iniquity shall not be your ruin."

• Joel 2:12–13 — "Turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping...rend your heart, and not your garments."

• Luke 15:20 — "And he arose, and came to his father."
