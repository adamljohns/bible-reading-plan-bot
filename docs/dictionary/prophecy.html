Prophecy
/ˈprä-fə-sē/
noun
From Greek prophēteia — "gift of interpreting the will of God," from pro- (before, on behalf of) + phēmi (to speak). To speak forth — primarily to declare God's word to the present, and secondarily to foretell the future.

📖 Biblical Definition

Prophecy is primarily the proclamation of God's word — declaring what God has said — and secondarily (but not exclusively) the foretelling of future events. The OT prophets were not primarily futurists but preachers of God's covenant demands and warnings: "Return to me, and I will return to you" (Malachi 3:7). Foretelling served forthtelling — future predictions were meant to call the present to repentance and faith. In the NT, prophecy continues as a gift of the Spirit (1 Corinthians 14) — but all prophecy must be tested against Scripture (1 Thessalonians 5:20-21). Jesus Christ is the fulfillment of all OT prophecy; the "testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (Revelation 19:10).

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

PROPHECY, n. A foretelling; prediction; a declaration of something to come. In Scripture, it denotes the gift of expounding Scripture, or the gift of declaring the divine will, as well as foretelling events. "Despise not prophesyings." 1 Thessalonians 5:20.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The charismatic movement has over-inflated prophecy into personal "words" delivered in church services, often indistinguishable from encouragement — and rarely tested. Accountability for failed prophecies has virtually disappeared ("I may have heard wrong"). Cessationism overcorrects by eliminating the prophetic gift altogether, leaving the church without the Spirit's voice. Both errors neglect Paul's balanced instruction: "Do not despise prophecies, but test everything" (1 Thessalonians 5:20-21). Secular culture uses "prophecy" to mean any lucky prediction — divorcing it entirely from divine revelation.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G4396 — prophētēs (προφήτης): prophet; one who speaks on behalf of God; the office from which the gift derives.

H5030 — nābî' (נָבִיא): prophet; one called and sent to speak God's word; 300+ times in OT for the prophetic office.

✍️ Usage

The prophet's primary job was not prediction but proclamation: "Thus says the LORD" demanded a hearing regardless of any forecast.

Scripture itself is the supreme prophetic word — "more fully confirmed" than even an eyewitness voice from heaven (2 Peter 1:19).

All prophecy must bow to Scripture; any word that contradicts the Bible is not from the Spirit who inspired the Bible.
