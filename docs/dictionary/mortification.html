Mortification
/ ˌmôr·tə·fəˈkā·shən /
noun
Latin mortificatio — from mortificare, "to put to death"; from mors (death) + facere (to make). The Puritan theologians, especially John Owen, developed mortification as the central discipline of the Christian life — the ongoing, Spirit-empowered killing of indwelling sin. Not self-punishment; not penance. The violent, decisive, repeated putting to death of the flesh's sinful desires and habits.

📖 Biblical Definition

Mortification is the Spirit-empowered discipline of actively putting to death the sinful desires and habits of the flesh. Paul commands it without apology: "Put to death the deeds of the body" (Romans 8:13). "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you" (Colossians 3:5). The language is violent on purpose — nekrōsate, "kill it." Not negotiate with it. Not manage it. Not balance it with grace. Kill it. John Owen's axiom is irreplaceable: "Be killing sin, or it will be killing you." Mortification is not self-flagellation, asceticism, or penance — it is the Spirit-powered, Word-directed, faith-fueled discipline of refusing to let the flesh have its way. It is the active, ongoing, daily crucifixion of the old man. It presupposes the indicative of the gospel ("you are dead to sin" — Romans 6:11) and flows from it into the imperative ("put to death" — Colossians 3:5). You do not mortify sin to become accepted; you mortify sin because you are accepted. Grace is not the enemy of mortification; grace is its only power source.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

MORTIFICA'TION, n. [L. mortificatio.] In theology, the act of subduing the passions and appetites by penance, abstinence, or painful severities inflicted on the body. More specifically, the destruction or subduing of the corrupt affections by prayer, repentance, fasting, and religious exercises; the keeping under of the flesh and its lusts by spiritual means. "Through the Spirit ye do mortify the deeds of the body." Romans 8:13.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern evangelicalism has largely abandoned mortification in two directions. The therapeutic church replaces killing sin with understanding sin — tracing its origins in your family of origin, processing your trauma, validating your struggle, and managing it with better coping mechanisms. Killing is too aggressive, too judgmental, too harsh. The result: Christians carry the same addictions, the same bitterness, the same sexual patterns for decades, having never killed anything — only studied it. On the other side, hyper-grace theology argues that focusing on sin at all is "sin-consciousness" that negates the gospel. Just believe harder and sin will fade. The result: the same sins with a theological justification for passivity. Owen saw both errors. The answer is not therapeutic self-understanding (though self-knowledge matters) and not passive faith (though faith is essential) — it is violent, intentional, Spirit-powered, daily war against the flesh. The Christian life was never marketed as comfortable.

📖 Key Scripture

• Romans 8:13 — "For if you live according to the flesh you will die, but if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live."

• Colossians 3:5 — "Put to death therefore what is earthly in you: sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry."

• Galatians 5:24 — "Those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires."

• Romans 6:11 — "So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus."

• Matthew 5:29 — "If your right eye causes you to sin, tear it out and throw it away."

✍️ Usage

• John Owen: "Do you mortify? Do you make it your daily work? Be always at it whilst you live. Cease not a day from this work. Be killing sin or it will be killing you."

• Mortification without vivification (the positive filling of the Spirit) produces legalistic misery — you must kill sin and fill the vacancy with Christ.

• True mortification attacks sin at the root (the desire) not just the branch (the behavior) — behavior management without heart transformation is Pharisaism, not sanctification.
