Firstborn
/ ˈfərst-ˌbȯrn /
noun / adjective
Hebrew bekhor (בְּכוֹר) — firstborn son, with rights of primogeniture; Greek prōtotokos (πρωτότοκος) — firstborn, preeminent one. The concept carries legal, covenantal, and Christological significance throughout Scripture.

📖 Biblical Definition

The firstborn holds a place of supreme honor, inheritance, and consecration in Scripture. Under the Mosaic law, every firstborn male — human and animal — was consecrated to God (Ex 13:2), a perpetual reminder of the Exodus when God spared Israel's firstborn while slaying Egypt's. Israel itself is called God's "firstborn son" (Ex 4:22), expressing covenantal primacy. Most profoundly, Jesus Christ is "the firstborn of all creation" (Col 1:15) — not meaning He was created, but that He holds the preeminent position over all things. He is also "the firstborn from the dead" (Col 1:18; Rev 1:5), the pioneer of resurrection who secures the inheritance for all His brothers. Believers share in the "assembly of the firstborn who are enrolled in heaven" (Heb 12:23).

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

FIRST'BORN, a. First brought forth; eldest. n. The first child born of any parent; the eldest child. Among the Jews, the first-born son had a right to a double portion of his father's inheritance, and the first-born among cattle was consecrated to the Lord. The first-born of Israel was slain by the destroying angel in Egypt, as a judgment on Pharaoh.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The Colossians 1:15 title "firstborn of all creation" has been misused by Arian and Jehovah's Witness theology to argue that Christ is a created being — the first and greatest creature. This misreads prōtotokos (firstborn = preeminent) as prōtoktistos (first-created). Paul's argument in Colossians 1 is the opposite: Christ is the firstborn precisely because "all things were created through him and for him" — the Creator cannot be part of creation. The title expresses sovereign preeminence, not chronological origin. This is one of the most important Christological errors to understand and correct.
