Valley of Dry Bones
/ˈvæli əv draɪ boʊnz/
noun / biblical vision
Ezekiel 37:1-14. The prophet Ezekiel, ministering to the exiled Jews in Babylon c. 585 BC, is brought in a vision to a valley strewn with dry human bones — the remains of a slaughtered army long dead. The LORD asks him, "Son of man, can these bones live?" Ezekiel answers, "O Lord GOD, you know." God commands him to prophesy to the bones, and as he speaks, the bones come together — bone to bone, sinew, flesh, skin — but no breath. God commands Ezekiel to prophesy to the breath (Hebrew ruach, also meaning Spirit), and breath enters them. They stand on their feet, "an exceedingly great army." God interprets: "these bones are the whole house of Israel."

Biblical Definition

The valley of dry bones is one of the Bible's most arresting pictures of spiritual resurrection. Four observations. (1) Hope for the hopelessly dead. Israel in exile had lost land, temple, kingship, identity. The bones pictured their own lament: "Our bones are dried up, and our hope is lost; we are indeed cut off" (37:11). Into that despair God speaks resurrection. No situation is too dead for God's word. (2) Two-stage revival. First the bodies reconstruct — but they remain lifeless. Then the breath/Spirit enters them. This is the pattern of real revival: doctrinal recovery matters, ministry structures matter, but without the Spirit's living breath the result is a well-ordered corpse. Many churches and denominations need exactly this diagnosis: bone to bone has happened but breath has not. (3) The Word and the Spirit together. Ezekiel is commanded to prophesy twice — to the bones (Word) and to the breath (Spirit). Both are needed. Word without Spirit is dead letter; Spirit without Word is emotional chaos. (4) Historical and eschatological fulfillment. The immediate fulfillment was the return from exile — Israel physically restored to the land under Zerubbabel and Ezra. The larger fulfillment is the eschatological resurrection of Israel and of all believers (Romans 11; 1 Corinthians 15). Christians facing a dead church, a dead family member, a dead culture — bring the word. Prophesy to the bones. Then prophesy to the breath.
