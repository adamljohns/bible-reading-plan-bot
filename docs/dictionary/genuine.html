Genuine

/ˈdʒɛnjuɪn/
adjective

Etymology & Webster 1828

From Latin genuinus, "native, natural, innate" — from gignere, "to beget." Webster 1828: "of the original stock; native; hence, real; pure; not spurious; proceeding from its reputed source or author." The opposite of counterfeit, pretended, or artificial. In biblical usage the concept is captured by Greek dokimos (approved after testing, genuine as metal approved by assay — Romans 16:10, 2 Corinthians 10:18, 13:7, James 1:12) and anypokritos (literally "without hypocrisy, unfeigned" — Romans 12:9, 2 Corinthians 6:6, 1 Timothy 1:5, 2 Timothy 1:5, 1 Peter 1:22).

Biblical Meaning

The Bible cares intensely about the genuine versus the counterfeit. Four observations. (1) Genuine faith survives testing. 1 Peter 1:7 — "the tested genuineness of your faith, more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire, may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ." Fire separates fool's gold from real gold; trials separate genuine faith from the performative kind. (2) Genuine love. Romans 12:9 — "Let love be genuine [anypokritos]." Literally "without a stage mask" — no playacting, no curated performance. The Christian love command is not "appear to love"; it is "actually love, all the way down." (3) Genuine faith in Timothy. 2 Timothy 1:5 — Paul rejoiced at Timothy's "sincere [anypokritou] faith, a faith that dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice." Genuine faith is generational; it passes from grandmother to mother to son when it is the real thing. (4) The counterfeit danger. Matthew 7:21-23 — not everyone who says "Lord, Lord" will enter; some will produce impressive religious work and be told "I never knew you." Cultural Christianity, performative faith, and Pharisaic religion are the counterfeits the Lord warns against. The question is not whether your faith looks convincing to observers; it is whether it is genuine before God. Pray to be the real thing. The Spirit is the author of genuineness, not the cultivator of masks.

Key Scriptures

"So that the tested genuineness of your faith — more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire — may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ."— 1 Peter 1:7
"Let love be genuine. Abhor what is evil; hold fast to what is good."— Romans 12:9
"I am reminded of your sincere faith, a faith that dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and now, I am sure, dwells in you as well."— 2 Timothy 1:5
