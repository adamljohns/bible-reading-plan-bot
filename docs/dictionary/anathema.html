Anathema
/əˈnæθ.ə.mə/
noun
Greek anathema — from ana- ("up") + tithemi ("to set, to place"). Originally: something set up, offered, or dedicated to a god. In the Septuagint and New Testament, it came to mean something devoted to divine destruction — accursed, set apart for judgment.

📖 Biblical Definition

Anathema in the New Testament carries the weight of divine condemnation — something or someone devoted to destruction by God's curse. Paul uses it to declare that anyone who preaches a gospel different from the one he received is "anathema" — under divine curse (Gal. 1:8–9). He even uses it of himself hypothetically (Rom. 9:3) and of the apostolic formula "if anyone has no love for the Lord" (1 Cor. 16:22). The word carries the gravest theological weight: to be anathema is to be cut off from Christ and handed over to divine judgment. The Church historically used "Let him be anathema" at councils to formally condemn heretical teaching.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

ANATHEMA, n. Excommunication with curses. An ecclesiastical curse, importing that a person is devoted to divine wrath and cut off from the church. In a more general sense, a curse or execration.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern usage has trivialized "anathema" to mean merely "something strongly disliked" — "That proposal is anathema to me." The word has been entirely detached from its theological gravity. In doing so, culture also loses the category of things that are genuinely, objectively cursed before God. A worldview that cannot say "this teaching is condemned by God" has no framework for anathema — and no framework for truth.

📖 Key Scripture

Galatians 1:8–9 — "But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed [anathema]."

1 Corinthians 16:22 — "If anyone has no love for the Lord, let him be accursed [anathema]. Our Lord, come!"

Romans 9:3 — "For I could wish that I myself were accursed [anathema] and cut off from Christ for the sake of my brothers."

Joshua 6:17 — "And the city and all that is within it shall be devoted to the LORD for destruction [Hebrew: cherem]."

Zechariah 14:11 — "It shall be inhabited, for there shall never again be a decree of utter destruction [cherem]."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G331 — anathema — "accursed, devoted to destruction"; the New Testament term for divine condemnation of false teaching and lovelessness toward Christ.

G332 — anathematizo — "to declare anathema, to bind with a curse"; the verbal form used in Acts 23:12 of those who vowed to kill Paul.

H2764 — cherem — "devoted thing, thing under the ban"; the Hebrew equivalent — things or persons set apart for God's judgment and destruction.
