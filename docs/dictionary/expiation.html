Expiation
/ˌɛk.spiˈeɪ.ʃən/
noun
From Latin expiare — to atone for, make amends: ex- (out, away) + piare (to appease, make good, from pius, dutiful, holy). Distinguished from propitiation: expiation focuses on removing the stain and guilt of sin; propitiation focuses on satisfying the wrath of a holy God. Both occur at the cross.

📖 Biblical Definition

Expiation is the removal and cancellation of the guilt and defilement of sin through sacrificial atonement. Where propitiation is God-ward (turning away wrath), expiation is sin-ward (washing away the stain). When Christ died, He did not merely satisfy God's righteous anger — He also removed the pollution, the debt, and the record of sin. The scapegoat ritual of Leviticus 16 pictures expiation: the priest laid hands on the goat, confessing the people's sins, and the goat carried them away "into a remote area" — sins removed, forgotten, covered. The NT describes expiation through language of washing, cleansing, and the cancellation of the "record of debt" (Col 2:14). Expiation without propitiation is incomplete — it removes the debt but does not address the offended Judge. Together, they constitute the fullness of what Christ accomplished.

EXPIATION, n. The act of expiating or atoning for a crime; the means by which atonement for crime is made; satisfaction or atonement. Among pagans, expiation was made by sacrifices or other rites. Among christians, expiation for the sins of men is made by the obedience and sufferings of Christ.

EXPIATE, v.t. To atone for; to make satisfaction or reparation for; to extinguish the guilt of a crime by subsequent acts of piety or worship. To expiate sin. To expiate a crime.

📖 Key Scripture

• Leviticus 16:21–22 — "Aaron shall lay both his hands on the head of the live goat, and confess over it all the iniquities… carrying all their iniquities on itself to a remote area."

• Colossians 2:14 — "Canceling the record of debt that stood against us with its legal demands. This he set aside, nailing it to the cross."

• Isaiah 43:25 — "I, I am he who blots out your transgressions for my own sake, and I will not remember your sins."

• Psalm 103:12 — "As far as the east is from the west, so far does he remove our transgressions from us."

• Hebrews 9:14 — "How much more will the blood of Christ… purify our conscience from dead works to serve the living God."

  ex- (out, away) + piare (to appease, from pius — dutiful, good)
  → expiare = to make good, clean up, atone
  → expiation = the act of removing sin's stain and debt

Two aspects of what Christ accomplished:
  Expiation = removal of sin's guilt and defilement (sin-ward)
  Propitiation = satisfaction of God's righteous wrath (God-ward)
  Reconciliation = restored relationship (relational result)
  Redemption = freedom from bondage (liberation framing)

When liberal scholarship (following C.H. Dodd, 1935) translated hilasmos as "expiation" rather than "propitiation," they removed the God-ward dimension of the atonement. Expiation alone is not wrong — sin is indeed removed. But if there is no wrath to be satisfied, forgiveness becomes God simply deciding to overlook sin, making divine justice arbitrary. The Bible's God is not merely an offended deity whose feelings are hurt — He is a righteous Judge whose law has been violated and who cannot be unjust. Expiation without propitiation can produce a therapeutic gospel: Jesus helps you feel less guilty, but the holy God's justice is left unaddressed. The full atonement addresses both: the stain is removed (expiation) and the wrath is absorbed (propitiation).
