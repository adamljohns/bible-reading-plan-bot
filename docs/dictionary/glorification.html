Glorification
/ˌɡlɔːrɪfɪˈkeɪʃən/
noun
Latin: glorificatio — from gloria (glory, fame, renown) + facere (to make). Greek: doxazō (δοξάζω) — to magnify, honor, bring into radiance.

📖 Biblical Definition

The final and complete transformation of the redeemed believer into perfect conformity with Christ, occurring at the resurrection of the body. Glorification is the culminating step in the ordo salutis (order of salvation) — the moment when the believer is fully freed from all sin, corruption, and mortality, and made to share in the very glory of God. It is God's definitive "yes" to His creative and redemptive purposes, the consummation of adoption, and the full realization of all that grace began.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

GLORIFICA'TION, n. The act of glorifying or of giving glory to. The state of being glorified. In theology, the exaltation of a believer to the full fruition of the divine favor and the joys of the heavenly kingdom; the final state of the redeemed in which they are perfectly conformed to the image of Christ and enjoy the beatific vision of God.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Secular culture co-opts "glorification" to mean celebrity worship, fame-seeking, or the elevation of harmful behaviors ("glorifying violence"). In therapeutic Christianity, glorification is reduced to a vague sense of "feeling good about yourself" or "being your best self." The doctrine's radical promise — that God will literally transform the physical body of every believer — is either ignored or sentimentalized into mere spiritual improvement.

📖 Key Scripture

• Romans 8:30 — "And those he predestined, he also called; those he called, he also justified; those he justified, he also glorified."

• 1 Corinthians 15:42–44 — "The body that is sown is perishable, it is raised imperishable..."

• Philippians 3:20–21 — "...he will transform our lowly bodies so that they will be like his glorious body."

• 1 John 3:2 — "We know that when Christ appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is."

• Romans 8:18 — "I consider that our present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that will be revealed in us."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G1392 — doxazō (δοξάζω): to glorify, honor, magnify; to clothe with splendor; used of God's transformative work on believers.

G1391 — doxa (δόξα): glory, splendor, radiance; the manifest presence and weightiness of God.
