Perichoresis
/ ˌper·i·kəˈrē·sis /
noun
Greek perichorein — "to make room for one another, to rotate around, to interpenetrate." From peri (around) + chorein (to contain, to give space). John of Damascus (c. 675–749) formalized the term to describe the mutual indwelling of the three Persons of the Trinity. Latin equivalent: circumincessio or circuminsessio.

📖 Biblical Definition

Perichoresis is the theological term for the mutual indwelling and interpenetration of the three Persons of the Trinity — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — in a perfect, eternal, and dynamic communion. Each Person fully contains and is contained by the others without confusion or merger. The Persons do not merely coexist; they coinhere. The Father is in the Son and the Son is in the Father (John 10:38; 14:11). The Spirit proceeds from the Father and is sent by the Son and dwells in believers, making them partakers of this divine fellowship (John 14:16–17). This is not three gods; nor is it one God wearing three masks. It is one God in three eternal, co-equal, mutually interpenetrating Persons — a fellowship of love so complete that relationship itself is the nature of God. Creation, then, is not God's loneliness solved but the overflow of love that already existed within the Godhead from eternity.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

PERICHORESIS — (Not listed in Webster 1828; a technical theological term). Closest entry: CIRCUMINSESSION — In theology, the mutual inexistence of the three persons of the Trinity in each other; the being of each in the others; their coinherence. This truth is received by orthodox divines as necessary to preserve the unity of the Godhead.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Contemporary theologians have stretched perichoresis beyond its Trinitarian meaning to support "open" or "relational" theologies in which God enters into mutual dependence with creation — even suggesting that humanity participates in the divine perichoretic dance in such a way that God is somehow incomplete without us. This is a dangerous expansion. Perichoresis describes what happens within the Godhead, not between God and the creature. Borrowing the term to baptize a semi-panentheistic theology — where God and creation mutually constitute each other — is an abuse of a precise theological concept. God invites us into fellowship (1 John 1:3), but we are not ontologically absorbed into the divine Being. Participation in Christ is real and transforming; it is not the erasure of Creator/creature distinction.

📖 Key Scripture

• John 10:38 — "The Father is in me, and I am in the Father."

• John 14:11 — "Believe me when I say that I am in the Father and the Father is in me."

• John 17:21 — "That they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You; that they also may be one in Us."

• John 14:16–17 — "He will give you another Helper...the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive...he dwells with you and will be in you."

• 1 John 4:8 — "God is love" — not merely that God loves, but that love is His eternal nature, expressed in perichoretic communion.

🔗 Greek Roots

G4013 — perichorein (περιχωρεῖν) — to encompass, to interpenetrate; root of the theological term

G3306 — menō (μένω) — to abide, to remain, to dwell in — the word Jesus uses for mutual indwelling (John 15)

✍️ Usage

• The doctrine of perichoresis explains how the Trinity can be three Persons without being three Gods — each Person is fully God, fully distinct, and fully united in the others.

• Prayer is not a monologue delivered to a distant deity but an entry into the relational life of the Triune God — made possible because perichoresis is real.

• The perichoretic dance of the Trinity is the eternal archetype of all true community — why isolation is unnatural and love is the ground of being.
