Luke
/luːk/
proper noun
Greek Loukas (probably short for Lucanus). The "beloved physician" (Colossians 4:14), a Gentile by background — the only non-Jewish author of any New Testament book — and the writer of the longest portion of the NT after Paul: the Gospel of Luke (24 chapters) and the Acts of the Apostles (28 chapters) together comprise roughly 27% of the New Testament by word count. Luke was a traveling companion of Paul during the latter half of Paul's missionary career (the "we" passages of Acts begin at 16:10) and was with him in his final imprisonment: "Luke alone is with me" (2 Timothy 4:11).

See also: Luke

Biblical Definition

Luke is the historian's historian. He opens his Gospel by announcing his method: "Forasmuch as many have taken in hand to set forth in order a declaration of those things which are most surely believed among us, Even as they delivered them unto us, which from the beginning were eyewitnesses, and ministers of the word..." (Luke 1:1-4). His distinctives: (1) Careful sourcing — he interviewed eyewitnesses and used existing written accounts; (2) Literary Greek — Luke's prose is the most polished in the NT; (3) Universal scope — his genealogy of Jesus goes back to Adam (not just Abraham as Matthew's does), emphasizing Jesus as the savior of all humanity; (4) Attention to the marginalized — women, the poor, Samaritans, Gentiles, tax collectors, and the sick all receive prominent treatment; (5) Prayer and the Spirit — Luke highlights Jesus praying at every major juncture and records more Holy Spirit references per chapter than any other Gospel; (6) Acts as continuation — Luke writes a two-volume work; the Gospel tells what Jesus began to do, and Acts tells what He continued through the Spirit-filled Church. Without Luke we would know nothing of the shepherds at Bethlehem, the Prodigal Son, the Good Samaritan, the Emmaus road, Zacchaeus, the thief on the cross's repentance, or most of the book of Acts.

Key Scripture

Luke 1:3-4 — "It seemed good to me also, having had perfect understanding of all things from the very first, to write unto thee in order, most excellent Theophilus, That thou mightest know the certainty of those things, wherein thou hast been instructed."

Colossians 4:14 — "Luke, the beloved physician, and Demas, greet you."

2 Timothy 4:11 — "Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with thee: for he is profitable to me for the ministry."
