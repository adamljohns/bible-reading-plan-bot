Just
/dʒʌst/
adjective / adverb
Homograph. Sense 1: Latin justus, straight, due, righteous. Sense 2: adverb of exactness — nearly, barely, precisely. Webster also records a noun just for a tournament tilt; Scripture does not traffic in that sport.

Biblical Definition

Just is one English spelling with two honest senses.

1. Upright. A man aligned to God’s law and, in the gospel, made right by faith. Habakkuk and Paul lock the same sentence: the just shall live by faith. The word is moral before it is political. A just weight, a just judge, a just man — all mean due, true, not crooked.

2. Exactly / barely. The adverb of nearness in place or time: just then, just so, just enough. This sense is precision, not righteousness. Do not read “just now” as if the speaker had been justified.

Webster 1828 Definition

JUST, adjective. Upright; righteous. JUST, adverb. Exactly; nearly. JUST, noun. A mock encounter on horseback.

JUST, adjective [Latin justus. The primary sense is probably straight or close, from the sense of setting, erecting, or extending.]

5. In a moral sense, upright; honest; having principles of rectitude; or conforming exactly to the laws, and to principles of rectitude in social conduct; equitable in the distribution of justice; as a just judge.

6. In an evangelical sense, righteous; religious; influenced by a regard to the laws of God; or living in exact conformity to the divine will. There is not a just man on earth, that doeth good, and sinneth not.

7. Conformed to rules of justice; doing equal justice. JUST balances, just weights, a just ephah and a just hin shall ye have.

10. Innocent; blameless; without guilt. How should man be just with God?

JUST', adverb Close or closely; near or nearly, in place. He stood just by the speaker, and heard what he said.

2. Exactly; nicely; accurately. They remain just of the same opinion.

3. Merely; barely; exactly. And having just enough, not covet more.

JUST, noun A mock encounter on horseback; a combat for sport or for exercise; a tilt; one of the exercises at tournaments.

Key Scripture

• Habakkuk 2:4 — “Behold the proud, His soul is not upright in him; But the just shall live by his faith.” (BTE NKJV)

• Romans 1:17 — “For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith; as it is written, ‘The just shall live by faith.’” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

“Just” is drafted into slogans, while the adverb sense (“I just feel”) smuggles a shrug into moral speech.

expand to see more

Webster’s evangelical lock still holds: the just man is righteous toward God, not merely aligned with a crowd. A cause can be named “justice” and still be crooked. The other drift is filler-talk — “I just think,” “I’m just saying” — which uses the adverb of barely to dodge a charge. Habakkuk’s just man lives by faith, not by a hashtag. Keep the two senses; do not mash them.

Usage

• “The just live by faith; the proud live by their own uprightness, which is none.”

• “If you ‘just feel’ it, you have not yet said whether it is true.”
