Resurrection
/ ˌre-zə-ˈrek-shən /
noun
From Latin resurrectio — "a rising again"; from resurgere — "to rise again" (re- again + surgere to rise). The Greek equivalent is anastasis (ἀνάστασις) — "a standing up again."

📖 Biblical Definition

The bodily raising of the dead to life — most supremely, the physical resurrection of Jesus Christ from the grave on the third day, which is the cornerstone of the Christian faith. The Resurrection is not a metaphor for spiritual renewal or the survival of memory; it is the literal, historical, bodily event in which the crucified Jesus walked out of the tomb in a glorified physical body. Christ's resurrection is the firstfruits and guarantee of the future resurrection of all believers, when the dead shall be raised incorruptible and given glorified, immortal bodies. Scripture also teaches a resurrection of the unjust unto judgment.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

RESURREC'TION, n. A rising from the dead; the revival of the dead; the rising of a dead body to life. This is one of the most glorious doctrines of the gospel. "I am the resurrection and the life." The resurrection of Christ from the dead is the foundation of the Christian's hope of immortality.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Liberal theology has systematically stripped the Resurrection of its physical reality, recasting it as a "resurrection of hope," a subjective experience in the disciples' hearts, or merely Christ "rising" in their memory and teaching. Modern culture uses "resurrection" to mean any comeback — a career resurrection, a brand's resurrection — trivializing the term. The denial of a bodily resurrection is ancient heresy (1 Corinthians 15:12) and renders the gospel empty. Paul is unambiguous: "If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins" (1 Cor. 15:17).
