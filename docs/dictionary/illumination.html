Illumination
/i-ˌlü-mə-ˈnā-shən/
noun
Latin illuminatio, from illuminare — to light up, make clear; in- + luminare (to illuminate, from lumen, light). Greek photismos (φωτισμός) — illumination, enlightenment (2 Cor. 4:6).

📖 Biblical Definition

In theology, illumination is the supernatural work of the Holy Spirit by which He opens the eyes of the spiritually blind to understand, receive, and apply the revealed Word of God. It is distinct from inspiration (the Spirit's work in producing Scripture) and revelation (God's disclosure of truth). Illumination is the Spirit's internal work in the reader, enabling what is objectively true in Scripture to become subjectively understood and personally transforming. Without illumination, the natural mind cannot receive the things of the Spirit of God — they are foolishness to him (1 Cor. 2:14). The same Spirit who inspired the Word also illumines it to each believer. This is why prayer must accompany Bible study: only the Author can fully open the meaning to our darkened hearts.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

The act of illuminating; the state of being illuminated. In theology, the special enlightening of the understanding by the Holy Spirit, enabling a person to understand and embrace divine truth. "The illumination of the Spirit is necessary for saving knowledge of Scripture."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Post-Enlightenment culture equates illumination with human reason — the "light" of intellectual inquiry and scientific method. The Enlightenment itself was named as though human reason replaced divine revelation. In charismatic circles, "illumination" is sometimes misused to claim new revelations beyond Scripture — bypassing the text entirely. The Reformation doctrine is precise: illumination does not add to Scripture, it opens the meaning of Scripture. It is the difference between a room with the lights off (word without Spirit) and a room with lights on (word with Spirit) — the furniture hasn't changed, but now you can see it clearly.
