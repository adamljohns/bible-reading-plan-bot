Image-Bearer
/ˈɪm.ɪdʒ ˌbɛr.ər/
noun

📖 Biblical Definition

An image-bearer is every human being, created in the image and likeness of God (imago Dei). "God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them" (Genesis 1:27). This image includes rationality, moral agency, relational capacity, dominion, and the capacity to know and worship God. The image is not erased by the fall — even after sin, murder is prohibited because man is made in God's image (Genesis 9:6). The image is marred but not destroyed, and believers are being progressively renewed in Christ, who is Himself "the image of the invisible God" (Colossians 1:15). Every human life possesses inherent dignity because every human bears God's image.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Image: a representation or likeness of any person or thing. Bearer: one who bears or carries.

IM'AGE, n. [L. imago.] A representation or likeness of any person or thing, formed of a material substance. In theology, the likeness of God in man, in knowledge, righteousness, and holiness. Note: Webster recognizes the theological sense — the image of God in man as consisting of knowledge, righteousness, and holiness.

📖 Key Scripture

• Genesis 1:27 — "So God created man in His own image, in the image of God He created Him; male and female He created them."

• Genesis 9:6 — "Whoever sheds the blood of man, by man shall His blood be shed, for God made man in His own image."

• Colossians 1:15 — "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation."

• James 3:9 — "With it we curse people who are made in the likeness of God."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

"Image-bearer" is co-opted to affirm anything a person desires rather than what God designed.

expand to see more

The phrase "image-bearer" has been weaponized in progressive theology to mean that whatever a person feels, desires, or identifies as must be affirmed because they bear God's image. But bearing God's image does not sanctify every human desire — it establishes a standard against which human fallenness is measured. We bear the image of a holy God, which means our sin is not an expression of the image but a violation of it. The imago Dei grounds human dignity, not human autonomy. It means every person has inherent worth — not that every choice, desire, or identity claim is valid. To say "you are an image-bearer" and therefore affirm sin is to use a true doctrine to reach a false conclusion.

Usage

• "Every human is an image-bearer — which is precisely why abortion, racism, and dehumanization of any kind are abominations before God."

• "Being an image-bearer means we have inherent dignity — it does not mean every human desire reflects the character of the God whose image we bear."
