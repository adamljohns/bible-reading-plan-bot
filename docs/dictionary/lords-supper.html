Lord's Supper (Communion, Eucharist)
/lɔːrdz ˈsʌp.ər/
noun

Biblical Definition

The Lord's Supper (also called Communion, the Eucharist, and the Breaking of Bread) is the meal Christ instituted on the night He was betrayed — bread broken as His body, cup poured as His blood of the new covenant — celebrated by the church till He comes (Matthew 26:26–28; Mark 14:22–24; Luke 22:19–20; 1 Corinthians 11:23–26).

Names of the ordinance. Lord's Supper is Paul's household name (1 Cor 11:20). Communion translates the koinōnia of the cup and loaf (1 Cor 10:16–17) — participation in Christ's blood and body, and the one-loaf unity of the church. Eucharist is from Greek eucharistia, thanksgiving — the meal is gratitude before it is argument. Breaking of Bread is the Acts name for the apostolic table (Acts 2:42; 20:7).

What it does. It proclaims the Lord's death until He comes (1 Cor 11:26); seals covenant participation; feeds faith on the finished work of Christ; binds the body together; and calls for self-examination and discerning the body (1 Cor 11:27–29).

What it is not. Not a bare memorial so thin that Christ is absent from His own table — and not a re-sacrifice of Christ. The cross was once for all (Hebrews 10:10–14). Believers truly commune with the living Lord who was crucified and is risen, without denying the finished work.

Wider sense of communion. Outside the table, koinōnia still names fellowship with the Father and Son (1 John 1:3) and the shared life of the saints. That wider fellowship is real — but it must not become a second dictionary card that splits the ordinance. One entry holds both the meal and the brief wider note.

Watchman / pastoral. Come as a household under the blood of the covenant. Examine yourself; discern the body; do not treat holy things as common. Cross-link: Blood of the New Covenant, Blood of Christ (Precious Blood), Blood of Sprinkling, Covenant, Church.

Webster 1828 Definition

Lord's Supper: the sacrament of the eucharist; the commemoration of the death and sufferings of Christ.

LORD'S SUPPER, n. The sacrament of the eucharist; the commemoration of the death and sufferings of our Savior, by the use of bread and wine, as emblems of His body broken and His blood shed for the remission of sins. Note: Webster recognizes the Lord's Supper as a sacrament of commemoration — bread and wine as emblems of Christ's sacrifice.

Modern Corruption

The Lord's Supper is either rarified into an occasional ritual or stripped of its covenantal gravity.

expand to see more

The Lord's Supper suffers from opposite errors. Many evangelical churches treat it as an occasional afterthought — a quarterly appendage to the sermon, rushed through with crackers and grape juice while a worship song plays. The early church devoted themselves to the breaking of bread as a central act of worship, not a footnote. On the other side, Roman Catholic theology transforms the Supper into a re-sacrifice of Christ — the Mass — contradicting Hebrews' insistence that Christ was offered "once for all." The memorial view strips the Supper of any real spiritual nourishment, while transubstantiation adds what Scripture does not teach. The biblical Lord's Supper is a genuine means of grace — not an empty symbol, not a re-sacrifice, but a real participation in Christ that nourishes faith and proclaims His death until He comes.

Usage

• "The Lord's Supper is not an optional add-on to the worship service — it was central to the apostolic church's gathering and should be central to ours."

• "Paul warns that eating and drinking in an unworthy manner brings judgment — the Table is sacred, requiring self-examination and repentance."

• "Every time we eat the bread and drink the cup, we proclaim the Lord's death until He comes — it is both remembrance and anticipation."
