Lust
/lʌst/
noun / verb
Old English: lust — pleasure, appetite, desire (originally neutral); German: Lust — pleasure; Greek: ἐπιθυμία (epithymia) — strong desire, craving; Hebrew: תַּאֲוָה (taʾăwāh) — longing, desire, lust

📖 Biblical Definition

In Scripture, lust is disordered desire — craving that has broken free from God's order and become consuming, idolatrous, and destructive. While desire itself is God-given and good when rightly directed, lust is desire turned inward and downward: coveting what belongs to another, craving forbidden pleasures, or treating people as objects for gratification. Jesus intensified the standard by teaching that lust in the heart is already adultery (Matthew 5:28). The New Testament uses epithymia broadly for sinful craving — lust of the flesh, lust of the eyes, and the pride of life (1 John 2:16) — all forms of desire that bypass God in pursuit of created things. The antidote is not suppression but redirection: filling the heart with greater desires for God Himself.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

LUST, n. [Sax. lust, lyst; G. lust; D. lust; Dan. lyst.] 1. Longing desire; eagerness to possess or enjoy. 2. Carnal appetite; unlawful desire of carnal pleasure. 3. Evil propensity; depraved affections and desires. v.i. 1. To desire earnestly. 2. To have carnal desire; to desire the gratification of carnal appetite. 3. To covet what is unlawful; to desire eagerly.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture has rehabilitated lust as a virtue — "owning your desires," "sexual liberation," and "following your heart" are the new moral vocabulary. Lust is reframed as authentic self-expression and its restraint as repression or prudishness. Pornography, hook-up culture, and advertising all monetize lust and normalize it. The catastrophic result is the objectification of persons, the destruction of marriages, and the spiritual deadening of those who habitually indulge disordered desire. Scripture's diagnosis is precise: lust wages war against the soul (1 Pet. 2:11). What the culture calls freedom, God calls bondage.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G1939 — epithymia — strong desire, craving, lust; used both neutrally (Luke 22:15) and for sinful desire; the root of the verb epithymeō used by Jesus in Matthew 5:28

G4561 — sarx — flesh; "lusts of the flesh" refers to desires operating from the fallen, unregenerate nature rather than the Spirit

H8378 — taʾăwāh — longing, desire, lust; used in Numbers 11:34 ("graves of lust") and Proverbs 21:25–26 for destructive craving
