Ecclesiastes
i-klee-zee-AS-teez
proper noun (book of Bible)
Greek ekklesiastes (G1577 family), “the assembler, preacher, convener.” Translates Hebrew Qoheleth — the title-character of the book, traditionally identified with Solomon in his old age.

📖 Biblical Definition

Ecclesiastes is the Old Testament wisdom book commonly ascribed to Solomon in his old age — written from the perspective of Qoheleth ("the Preacher" or "Assembly-leader"). The book is structured around the keyword hevel (Hebrew "breath, vapor, smoke") — translated "vanity" in the KJV — appearing 38 times. The Preacher tests one domain of human life after another (wisdom, pleasure, work, wealth, power, religion) and pronounces each "vanity and vexation of spirit" when considered "under the sun" apart from God. The book closes with its only sufficient verdict: "Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: Fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man. For God shall bring every work into judgment" (12:13-14). Vapor terminates in fearing God.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Pop-existentialism quotes Ecclesiastes for the despair and skips the conclusion.

expand to see more

Ecclesiastes is one of the great philosophical books in world literature. Solomon (in his old age) walks the reader through every counterfeit answer to the meaning of life: pleasure, learning, wealth, power, accomplishment, sex. Each is tested. Each is named hevel — vapor, breath, untraceable smoke. The reader is meant to nod in recognition: yes, that is exactly what life under the sun feels like.

Modern existentialism stops here and calls it courageous despair. Ecclesiastes refuses. Chapter 12 closes the book with two clauses that make the entire diagnosis useful: Fear God, and keep his commandments... for God shall bring every work into judgment. Vapor under the sun is real; vapor before the throne is not. The corrective is not optimism but reverence. Read the whole book or you will misuse it.
