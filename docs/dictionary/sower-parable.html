Parable of the Sower
/ˈsoʊ.ər/
noun phrase (parable)
Jesus' parable of Matthew 13:1-23, Mark 4:1-20, and Luke 8:4-15 — the first and interpretive key to all His parables. A sower throws seed on four kinds of ground, and only one produces fruit. The seed is the Word of God; the soils are four kinds of human hearts.

📖 Biblical Definition

The Parable of the Sower is the interpretive key to all of Jesus' parables. When His disciples asked why He spoke in parables, Jesus first answered the question about parables in general (hidden from those who refuse to hear, revealed to those who earnestly seek), then told them: "Do you not understand this parable? How then will you understand all the parables?" (Mark 4:13). This parable is foundational. A sower scatters seed. Some falls by the wayside — birds eat it immediately. Some falls on stony ground — it springs up quickly but has no root and withers in the sun. Some falls among thorns — it grows up but the thorns choke it. And some falls on good ground — and bears fruit: some thirty, some sixty, some a hundredfold. Jesus explains: the seed is the Word of God. The four soils are four kinds of hearts. (1) The wayside: those who hear but Satan immediately snatches the word away — never even germinated. (2) The stony ground: those who receive the word with joy, but having no depth of root, they fall away when tribulation or persecution comes. (3) The thorny ground: those who hear, but "the cares of this world, the deceitfulness of riches, and the desires for other things" (Mark 4:19) choke the word. (4) The good ground: those who hear, receive, and bear fruit. The parable is deeply sobering: three out of four soils do not produce fruit. The same sermon, the same Bible reading, the same gospel — different outcomes, because different hearts. The parable is also a call to self-examination: Which soil am I? And what can be done to soften stony ground, pull out the thorns of worldly care, and become the kind of heart that bears fruit thirty, sixty, a hundredfold?

📖 Key Scripture

Mark 4:3-8 — "Listen! Behold, a sower went out to sow. And it happened, as he sowed, that some seed fell by the wayside... Some fell on stony ground... Some seed fell among thorns... But other seed fell on good ground and yielded a crop that sprang up, increased, and produced: some thirtyfold, some sixty, and some a hundred."

Mark 4:14-20 — "The sower sows the word... These are the ones sown on good ground, those who hear the word, accept it, and bear fruit."

Matthew 13:23 — "But he who received seed on the good ground is he who hears the word and understands it, who indeed bears fruit and produces: some a hundredfold, some sixty, some thirty."

Luke 8:15 — "But the ones that fell on the good ground are those who, having heard the word with a noble and good heart, keep it and bear fruit with patience."
