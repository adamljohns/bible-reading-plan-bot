Sovereignty
/ˈsɒv.rɪn.ti/
noun
Old French soveraineté | Latin superanus (supreme, chief) | from super (above) | Hebrew מֶלֶךְ (melek — king)

📖 Biblical Definition

The sovereignty of God is His absolute, supreme authority over all creation — His right and power to do whatever He pleases with what He has made, accountable to no one above Himself (Daniel 4:35). God's sovereignty is not tyranny; it is the perfectly wise, perfectly just rule of the One who is infinitely good. It encompasses His decretive will (what He ordains to happen) and His preceptive will (what He commands). God's sovereignty does not negate human responsibility — Scripture holds both in tension without apology. The sovereignty of God is the bedrock of Christian comfort: if God is not sovereign over all things, then nothing is truly secure.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

SOV'EREIGNTY, n. 1. Supreme power; supremacy; the possession of the highest power, or of uncontrollable power. Sovereignty, applied to God, denotes his infinite perfection and right to govern all things. Applied to kings or other rulers, sovereignty denotes supreme power; as the sovereignty of a king. 2. The territory of a state; as the sovereignty of the United States.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern individualism has effectively transferred sovereignty from God to the self. The phrase "my body, my choice" is not merely a political slogan — it is a theological claim of absolute personal sovereignty that displaces God's ownership and authority over what He created. Progressive political philosophy extends this to national and institutional governance, treating human rights as the ultimate sovereign above which no law or deity may make claims. The biblical worldview insists: all human authority is delegated authority; God alone is truly sovereign.

✍️ Usage

• "The LORD reigns; let the earth rejoice" (Psalm 97:1) — sovereignty proclaimed as cause for worship, not dread.

• To submit to God's sovereignty is not fatalism — it is faith that the One who holds all things is perfectly good and cannot err.

• Human governments derive their legitimate authority from God; when they act contrary to His law, they transgress the bounds of their delegated sovereignty.
