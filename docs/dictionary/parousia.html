Parousia
/pəˈruː.zi.ə/
noun (Greek theological term)
From Greek παρουσία (parousia) — presence, arrival, coming. From πάρειμι (pareimi) — to be present, to have arrived. In secular Greek, parousia denoted the royal visit or official arrival of a king or emperor. In the NT, it is the technical term for Christ's return in glory.

📖 Biblical Definition

The Second Coming of the Lord Jesus Christ — His glorious, bodily, visible return to judge the living and the dead, consummate His Kingdom, and make all things new. The parousia is not a spiritual metaphor or a gradual process; it is a definitive, cataclysmic event that terminates history as we know it (Matt. 24:27; 1 Thess. 4:16; Rev. 19:11–16). The NT authors use parousia 24 times, always with urgency and expectancy. It is the "blessed hope" (Titus 2:13) toward which all of redemptive history points and for which the church is to live in constant readiness.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Webster 1828 did not include "Parousia" as a common English entry. The concept was universally taught under "Second Coming" or "Advent of Christ." Theologians of that era described it as the literal, bodily return of Christ in great power and glory, to raise the dead, judge all mankind, and establish His eternal reign — events clearly prophesied throughout both Testaments and affirmed in the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Liberal theology has "demythologized" the parousia into a symbol of spiritual progress or social transformation — Christ "coming" whenever justice advances or the church grows. Others spiritualize it as a subjective inner experience. Both moves evacuate the parousia of its literal, cosmic, bodily content and strip the church of its eschatological urgency. Additionally, date-setting movements (from William Miller to Harold Camping) have repeatedly brought the parousia into disrepute by ignoring Christ's own words: "No one knows the day or the hour" (Matt. 24:36). Faithful Christianity holds the parousia as certain, imminent, and unknowable in timing.

📖 Key Scripture

Matthew 24:27 — "For as the lightning comes from the east and shines as far as the west, so will be the coming [parousia] of the Son of Man."

1 Thessalonians 4:16–17 — "The Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a cry of command…and we will always be with the Lord."

Titus 2:13 — "Waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ."

Revelation 19:11 — "Then I saw heaven opened, and behold, a white horse! The one sitting on it is called Faithful and True."

Acts 1:11 — "This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw Him go into heaven."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G3952 — παρουσία (parousia) — presence, coming, arrival; the primary NT term for Christ's Second Coming; 24 occurrences.

G2015 — ἐπιφάνεια (epiphaneia) — appearing, manifestation; used alongside parousia for the visible, glorious revelation of Christ at His return.

✍️ Usage

The parousia shapes Christian ethics: "Everyone who thus hopes in Him purifies himself as He is pure" (1 John 3:3). Expecting Christ's return produces holiness.

The parousia is the ultimate vindication of the Gospel — all history's injustices, all suffering, all ambiguity will be resolved when the King arrives.

To live in light of the parousia is to treat every day as potentially the last — not in fatalism, but in faithful urgency.
