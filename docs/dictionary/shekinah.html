Shekinah
/ ʃɪˈkaɪ·nə /
noun (theological term)
Hebrew shekhinah (שְׁכִינָה) — from shakan (שָׁכַן), to dwell, to tabernacle, to settle. The word itself does not appear in Scripture but is a rabbinic term for the manifest, visible glory of God's dwelling presence — the luminous cloud, the consuming fire, the brilliant radiance that signals where God has chosen to make Himself known among His people.

📖 Biblical Definition

The Shekinah is the radiant, tangible manifestation of God's holy presence dwelling among His covenant people. It is not God in His essence — no man can see God and live (Ex. 33:20) — but the visible, created expression of His nearness: the pillar of cloud by day and the pillar of fire by night (Ex. 13:21), the dense cloud and consuming fire that settled upon Sinai (Ex. 24:16–17), and above all the glory that filled the Tabernacle when it was dedicated (Ex. 40:34–35) and the Temple of Solomon at its consecration (1 Ki. 8:10–11). In each case, the priests could not stand to minister — the holiness of God's manifest presence was overwhelming.

The Shekinah is the story of God closing the distance between Himself and man — first in the Garden, then in the Tabernacle, then in the Temple, then supremely in the Incarnation. John 1:14 announces the climax: "The Word became flesh and dwelt (eskēnōsen — pitched His tent) among us, and we beheld His glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father." Christ is the true Shekinah — God tabernacling among men in human flesh. The Shekinah departed Ezekiel's Temple before the Babylonian destruction (Ezek. 10:18; 11:23), but it returned in the person of Jesus of Nazareth, and it will fill the New Jerusalem in fullness when God makes His dwelling with man forever (Rev. 21:3).

For the father of a household, the Shekinah teaches that the Lord who fills the Tabernacle with His glory also promises to dwell with those who fear Him (Ps. 91:1). The patriarch leads his family toward the presence of God — not away from it.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

SHEKINAH, n.

SHEKINAH, n. [Heb. from shakan, to dwell.] A word used by Jewish writers to denote the divine presence, or the symbol of it — particularly the luminous cloud or visible glory of God that rested between the cherubim over the mercy seat, signifying the dwelling of God among His people. The term does not appear in the Hebrew text of Scripture but is derived from the rabbinic tradition to describe the dwelling or settling of the divine majesty in a visible, manifested form.

📖 Key Scripture

Exodus 40:34–35 — "Then the cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle… for the cloud of the LORD was on the tabernacle."

1 Kings 8:10–11 — "The cloud filled the house of the LORD, so that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud, for the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD."

John 1:14 — "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."

Ezekiel 10:18 — "Then the glory of the LORD went out from the threshold of the house…" — The departure of the Shekinah: the most devastating line in the Old Testament.

Revelation 21:3 — "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

In charismatic and word-of-faith circles, "Shekinah" has been reduced to a buzzword for emotional experience — an atmosphere of worship, a feeling in the room, a spiritual "vibe." People chase the sensation and call it the presence of God. This inverts the biblical reality: the Shekinah of Scripture was so holy that trained priests fled (1 Ki. 8:11). It was not a warm feeling — it was terrifying holiness that leveled all human pretension. Moses had to remove his sandals; Isaiah cried "Woe is me, I am undone." Modern Christianity has domesticated the glory of God into a therapeutic experience. The corrective is not to stop pursuing God's presence, but to approach it as Scripture demands — in reverence, through the blood of the covenant, clothed in Christ's righteousness, with trembling and joy.
