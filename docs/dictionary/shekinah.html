Shekinah
/ʃɪˈkaɪnə/
noun
Hebrew: shekhinah (שְׁכִינָה) — from shakan (שָׁכַן) — to dwell, to tabernacle, to settle. Not used as a noun in the Hebrew Bible itself, but developed in rabbinic literature as the technical term for God's visible, glorious presence dwelling among His people. Directly related to the Greek skēnoō (σκηνόω) — to tabernacle — used of the Incarnation in John 1:14.

📖 Biblical Definition

Shekinah refers to the manifest, glorious, visible presence of God dwelling among His people — the pillar of cloud by day and fire by night that led Israel through the wilderness (Exod 13:21), the glory cloud that filled the Tabernacle (Exod 40:34–35) and Solomon's Temple (1 Kgs 8:10–11), the terrifying brightness that knocked Saul to the ground on the Damascus road (Acts 9:3). The departure of the Shekinah from the Temple before Babylon's destruction (Ezek 10–11) was the most catastrophic event in Israel's history. The Incarnation is the supreme Shekinah event: "The Word became flesh and tabernacled (eskēnōsen) among us, and we beheld his glory" (John 1:14). In the new creation, the Shekinah fills all things: God Himself dwells with His people forever (Rev 21:3, 22–23).

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

SHEKI'NAH, n. [Heb. from shakan, to dwell.] A word used by Jewish writers to denote the visible symbol of the divine presence — the luminous cloud or pillar of glory which rested on the mercy seat in the tabernacle and temple, and which was the token of God's peculiar presence with Israel. This term does not occur in Scripture itself but is used by theologians to describe the glorious manifestation of God's immanent presence, as distinguished from His omnipresence.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Evangelical Christianity has largely reduced the presence of God to an emotional experience — a warm feeling during worship, a subjective sense of nearness. The biblical Shekinah was something else entirely: an objective, physical, terrifying reality that caused priests to flee, prophets to fall as dead men, and foreigners to tremble. The domestication of God's presence has produced a Christianity that sings "Your presence is all I need" while structuring church to be maximally comfortable and non-confrontational. Meanwhile, New Age spirituality has co-opted Shekinah as a feminine divine energy concept — completely alien to its biblical meaning. Both distortions reflect the same root error: making God's presence manageable.

📖 Key Scripture

• Exodus 40:34–35 — "The cloud covered the tent of meeting, and the glory of the LORD filled the tabernacle. Moses could not enter…because the cloud had settled on it."

• 1 Kings 8:10–11 — "When the priests withdrew from the Holy Place, the cloud filled the temple…and the priests could not perform their service because of the cloud."

• Ezekiel 10:18 — "Then the glory of the LORD departed from over the threshold of the temple…" (The Shekinah departs before judgment.)

• John 1:14 — "The Word became flesh and made his dwelling (tabernacled) among us. We have seen his glory, the glory of the one and only Son."

• Revelation 21:3 — "God's dwelling place is now among the people, and he will dwell with them. They will be his people, and God himself will be with them."

🔗 Hebrew & Greek Roots

H7931 — shakan (שָׁכַן): to settle, to dwell, to tabernacle; the root of Shekinah and of mishkan (Tabernacle — the dwelling place).

H3519 — kavod (כָּבוֹד): glory, weight, heaviness; the visible manifestation of God's presence — often appearing as fire, cloud, or blinding light.

G4637 — skēnoō (σκηνόω): to tabernacle, to dwell in a tent; used in John 1:14 of the Incarnation and in Revelation 21:3 of the final dwelling of God with His people.
