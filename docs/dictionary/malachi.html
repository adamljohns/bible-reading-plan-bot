Malachi

/ˈmæləˌkaɪ/
proper noun / prophet

Etymology & Webster 1828

Hebrew Malachi, "my messenger." The last of the writing prophets and the final book of the Old Testament canon. Dates to approximately 430 BC — about a century after the return from exile under Zerubbabel, probably during or just after Nehemiah's second term as governor. The temple had been rebuilt, but spiritual zeal had cooled. Priests offered defective sacrifices, men divorced their Jewish wives to marry foreigners, people withheld tithes, and the prevailing mood was a cynical "What's the use of serving God?" (3:14). Malachi's book is structured as a series of six disputations — God makes a charge, the people protest, God answers.

Biblical Meaning

Malachi is God's closing word to Israel for 400 years — after him, the heavens fall silent until John the Baptist. The themes of the book are the themes Jesus and the apostles would walk straight into. (1) Marriage and family — God "hates divorce" (2:16) and rebukes the men of Judah for betraying their wives; fathers' hearts need to be turned to their children (4:6). (2) Tithing and worship — "Bring the full tithe into the storehouse... test me in this, says the LORD of hosts, if I will not open the windows of heaven for you and pour down for you a blessing until there is no more need" (3:10). (3) The coming of the Lord — "Behold, I send my messenger, and He will prepare the way before me. And the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to His temple" (3:1). (4) The messianic forerunner — "Behold, I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the LORD comes" (4:5). This prophecy is quoted explicitly by Jesus about John the Baptist (Matthew 11:14, 17:12) — John is Elijah who was to come. (5) The sun of righteousness — "For you who fear my name, the sun of righteousness shall rise with healing in its wings" (4:2), one of the most beautiful Christological prophecies in the OT. Then silence. Four centuries. Then a strange wilderness preacher in camel's hair quoting Malachi's promise, and after him the Sun of Righteousness Himself.
