Aseity
/ā-ˈsē-ə-tē/
noun
Medieval Latin aseitas, from Latin a se — "from itself, by itself"; scholastic theological term for God's self-existence and self-sufficiency. Related to the divine name YHWH (הוה יָ) — "I AM WHO I AM" (Exod. 3:14), which implies self-existent, uncaused being.

📖 Biblical Definition

Aseity is the divine perfection by which God exists entirely from Himself, by Himself, and for Himself — underived, uncaused, and utterly self-sufficient. He is the only uncreated being. Everything else that exists has its being from God; God has His being from no one. This is what God declared to Moses in His most fundamental name: "I AM WHO I AM" — pure, self-existent being. Aseity means God has no needs (Acts 17:25), was not brought into existence, cannot be destroyed, and does not depend on creation for His joy, identity, or existence. Creation did not "complete" God or add to Him. He is the fountain of all being; He drinks from no other. This is why He alone deserves ultimate worship.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

Aseity does not appear in Webster 1828, as it was primarily scholastic/theological vocabulary. The concept is captured under "self-existence" — the property of existing without any external cause or ground; the divine attribute of being uncaused and underived.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Process theology directly denies aseity by making God dependent on and shaped by creation — God grows, changes, and in some sense needs the world to be fully God. This "needy God" is immensely popular because it makes God seem more relatable, more like us. But it collapses the Creator-creature distinction and ultimately makes God untrustworthy — if He depends on creation, His existence is as contingent as ours. Worship of a needy God is pity, not adoration. Additionally, popular evangelical sentiment sometimes implies God "needs" our worship or "wants" our love as if He were lonely — language that subtly erodes aseity by framing God as emotionally incomplete without us.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

H1961 — הָיָה (hayah) — to be, exist, become; the root of YHWH — God's name is derived from pure existence itself.

G3801 — ὁ ὢν καὶ ὁ ἦν (ho ōn kai ho ēn) — "He who is and who was and who is to come" (Rev. 1:4, 8) — the New Testament unpacking of the divine name into past, present, and future self-existence.

G2222 — ζωή (zōē) — life; John 5:26 — the Father has life in himself — the divine quality of self-existent, underived life.
