Contrition
/kən-ˈtri-shən/
noun
From Latin contritio — a grinding, crushing; from conterere (to grind together, bruise), from con- + terere (to rub, wear down). The word carries the image of a heart ground down to powder before God.

📖 Biblical Definition

Deep, genuine sorrow over sin — not merely regret over consequences, but grief over the offense against God Himself. Contrition is the heart condition God specifically promises to dwell with: "I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit" (Isa. 57:15). Psalm 51 — David's great cry of contrition after his adultery with Bathsheba — shows what it looks like: acknowledgment of the sin as ultimately against God, a broken spirit, and a plea not for leniency but for cleansing. Contrition is the soil from which repentance grows; without it, repentance is merely behavioral modification. The contrite heart does not perform sorrow — it is crushed by it, and finds in that very crushing the nearness of God (Ps. 34:18).

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

CONTRITION, n. The act of grinding or wearing; in theology, deep sorrow and penitence for sin, proceeding from a consideration of the heinous nature of sin as an offense against a holy God, and a detestation of it. Contrition is distinguished from attrition, which is sorrow arising only from fear of punishment. True contrition springs from love of God and hatred of the sin itself.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The age of therapy has replaced contrition with emotional processing — we are encouraged to "work through" guilt feelings, not to be broken before a holy God. The category of offense against God has collapsed into offense against self or others, so guilt becomes a psychological problem to resolve rather than a spiritual reality to address through repentance. Public apologies (celebrity or political) are carefully crafted to manage perception, not express genuine brokenness. The result is a culture of performative remorse without transformation — people sorry for being caught, not sorry for sinning.
