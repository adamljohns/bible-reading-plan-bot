New Apostolic Reformation (NAR)

Biblical Definition

Scripture teaches that the church is “built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the cornerstone” (Ephesians 2:20). The foundational apostolic office bore unique, once-for-all authority tied to the risen Christ’s eyewitness commission and the inscripturated Word. The faith was “once for all delivered to the saints” (Jude 3). No modern network may reinstall a governing caste of “apostles” and “prophets” over the churches as if the canon were open and Christ’s foundation incomplete. Local churches are led by qualified elders/pastors under Scripture (Titus 1:5–9; 1 Timothy 3:1–7) — not by extra-biblical “covering” from a living apostle.

Webster 1828 Definition

APOS'TLE, n. A person deputed to execute some important business; among Christians, a disciple charged by Christ with the gospel commission.

APOS'TLE, n. [Gr. apostolos, one sent away.] A person deputed to execute some important business; but appropriately, a disciple of Christ commissioned to preach the gospel.

Note: Webster assumes *sending under Christ*. He does not imagine a permanent, self-replicating office that outranks Scripture and local elders.

Key Scripture

• Ephesians 2:20 — foundation of the apostles and prophets; Christ the cornerstone.

• Jude 3 — faith once for all delivered.

• Acts 17:11 — examine everything by the Scriptures.

• 2 Peter 1:19 — the prophetic word more sure.

• Matthew 24:24 — false christs and false prophets.

• Revelation 22:18–19 — do not add to the words of the prophecy.

• Galatians 1:8–9 — another gospel is accursed.

Modern Corruption

The New Apostolic Reformation (NAR) rebrands Christianity as a hierarchy of modern “apostles” and “prophets,” often fused with dominion language and prosperity fuel.

expand to see more

Public markers include: staff titles of Apostle/Prophet as governing offices; required “alignment” or covering under a network apostle; Seven Mountains / dominion branding as the church’s core mission; “new revelation” that functionally outranks the Bible; and — commonly — prosperity theology ingrained in the movement (seed-faith, breakthrough, wealth-as-covenant right). This is not Spirit-filled biblical Christianity. It is a rival authority structure. When the public gospel becomes success, elevation, and apostolic brand loyalty instead of Christ crucified and risen, the church has left the Kingdom of Light for the Kingdom of Darkness.

• “Test every prophet by the written Word. The canon is closed; the King has spoken.”
