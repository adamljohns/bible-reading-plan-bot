Supralapsarian
/ˌsuː.prə.læpˈsɛər.i.ən/
adjective / noun
Latin supra — above, before + lapsus — fall (from labi — to fall) + -arian. Literally: "above/before the fall." The supralapsarian holds that in God's eternal decree, election and reprobation logically preceded (were "above") the decree to permit the Fall. Its counterpart is infralapsarianism (Latin infra — below/after + lapsus) — election occurs logically after the decree to permit the fall.

📖 Biblical Definition

Supralapsarianism and infralapsarianism address the logical (not temporal) ordering of God's eternal decrees — specifically, when in God's sovereign plan does election occur relative to the Fall?

Supralapsarian order: (1) Elect/reprobate some men → (2) Create them → (3) Permit the Fall → (4) Provide redemption for the elect → (5) Call and glorify the elect.

Infralapsarian order: (1) Create men → (2) Permit the Fall → (3) Elect some fallen men → (4) Provide redemption for the elect.

Both affirm unconditional election and God's sovereignty. The debate is about the logical relationship within God's eternal decree — not a sequence in time. Infralapsarianism is the majority Reformed position (Westminster Standards, Synod of Dort); supralapsarianism is the minority view historically associated with Theodore Beza, William Twisse, and to some extent Calvin.

Neither term appears in Webster 1828 by name (the debate was largely internal to Reformed scholasticism). However, the underlying issue — the ordering of God's eternal decrees — was hotly contested at the Synod of Dort (1618–1619). The Canons of Dort adopted an infralapsarian framework while allowing supralapsarian sympathizers within the Reformed tent. Francis Turretin's Institutes of Elenctic Theology (1679) provides the most thorough historical engagement with both positions.

Both positions can be misused. Supralapsarianism, pressed to its logical extreme, can make God appear to create humans for the purpose of damnation — making reprobation a positive decree rather than a permissive one. Infralapsarianism, if not carefully qualified, can suggest that God merely "looks ahead" to see who will believe — drifting toward Arminian foreknowledge election rather than unconditional sovereign election. The practical danger of both: turning election into a cold logical puzzle rather than the warm pastoral comfort Paul intends in Romans 8–9. God's decrees are not a flowchart — they are the eternal counsels of infinite wisdom and love.

Latin:
  supra (above, before) + lapsus (fall) + -arian (one who holds)
  infra (below, after) + lapsus (fall) + -arian

  lapsus ← labi (to fall, to slip)
    → "lapse" in English — a slip, a fall
    → "lapsarian" = relating to the Fall of Adam

Key terms in the debate:
  electio (election) — God's choice of the saved
  reprobatio (reprobation) — God's passing over / hardening
  decretum (decree) — God's eternal will/plan
  praedestinatio (predestination) — foreordaining of ends

ekloge (ἐκλογή, G1589) — election, selection, choice; the divine act at the center of this debate (Romans 9:11; 11:5,7,28).

proorizo (προορίζω, G4309) — to predetermine, to foreordain; the eternal decree in view (Romans 8:29–30; Ephesians 1:5,11).
