Miraculous Catch of Fish
KACH of FISH
miracle
Two miracle accounts in the Gospels. Luke 5:1-11 records the calling of the first disciples after a great catch following an empty night; John 21:1-14 records a parallel catch after the resurrection, drawing the disciples back from their fishing nets to ministry.

📖 Biblical Definition

Two miraculous-catch accounts frame the public ministry of Jesus. Luke 5:1-11 records Christ teaching from Peter's boat after a fruitless night of fishing, then commanding Peter to launch out into the deep and let down the nets; the catch was so great the nets brake. Peter fell at His knees: depart from me; for I am a sinful man, O Lord. John 21:1-14 records the parallel post-resurrection scene at the Sea of Tiberias: another empty night, another command from the shore, another great catch — this time exactly 153 fish — and the restoration of Peter over breakfast.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity often returns to old occupations after failure; Christ comes again to the empty boat.

expand to see more

The two great catches frame Peter's discipleship. The first call was from an empty boat after an empty night; the post-resurrection restoration was also from an empty boat after an empty night. The pattern is intentional: without me ye can do nothing. The man who walked away from his nets in Luke 5 returned to them in John 21 in a moment of failure-shaped grief; Christ came again to the empty boat and called him out a second time.

Modern Christianity often returns to old occupations after spiritual failure. Christ comes again. The fire is lit on the shore; the breakfast is prepared; the question is asked: lovest thou me? Three denials, three restorations; three commands: feed my lambs, feed my sheep, feed my sheep. Leave the nets again; the call has not been rescinded.
