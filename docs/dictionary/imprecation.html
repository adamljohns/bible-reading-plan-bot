Imprecation
/ ˌim·prə·ˈkā·shən /
noun
Latin imprecatio — from in (upon) + precari (to pray, entreat). "To pray down upon." Hebrew cognate concept found in arar (אָרַר) — to curse; to bind with a pronouncement of judgment.

📖 Biblical Definition

An imprecation is a prayer calling upon God to judge, curse, or bring calamity upon the wicked. The imprecatory psalms (e.g., Psalms 35, 69, 109, 137) are not personal vendettas but judicial appeals to God's righteousness — cries from the oppressed that God would vindicate His people and execute justice on those who war against Him. The psalmist does not take vengeance himself; he hands the matter to God. Imprecation is rooted in a robust belief in divine justice: God is holy, sin has real consequences, and the enemies of God's people are enemies of God Himself. Jesus and Paul both quote imprecatory psalms approvingly (John 15:25; Romans 11:9–10).

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

IMPRECA'TION, n. [L. imprecatio.] The act of imprecating, or invoking evil on any one; a prayer that a curse or calamity may fall on any one; a curse. He was answered with a volley of imprecations.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern sensibilities have nearly excised the imprecatory psalms from worship and preaching, dismissing them as sub-Christian, embarrassing, or "Old Testament." This reflects not a higher ethic but a diminished view of divine justice and human evil. A Christianity stripped of imprecation cannot take seriously the reality of persecution, oppression, or God's wrath. It also produces a flattened view of prayer — as if the only approved petition is one that makes everyone feel good. The imprecatory psalms are God's inspired provision for those who have nowhere else to turn but to Him for justice.

📖 Key Scripture

• Psalm 109:6–13 — David's imprecation against his enemy; quoted in Acts 1:20 regarding Judas.

• Psalm 69:22–28 — "May their table become a snare..."; quoted by Paul in Romans 11:9.

• Revelation 6:10 — Martyrs under the altar cry: "How long, Sovereign Lord...until you judge the inhabitants of the earth and avenge our blood?"

• Galatians 1:8–9 — Paul's apostolic imprecation: "Let them be anathema."

✍️ Usage

• The persecuted church in the book of Revelation does not retaliate — they offer their suffering to God in imprecation, trusting that He will act.

• Praying the imprecatory psalms does not make a Christian bloodthirsty; it makes him honest about evil and confident in divine justice.

• The man who cannot pray an imprecation has likely not been wronged deeply enough, or does not believe deeply enough that God will judge.
