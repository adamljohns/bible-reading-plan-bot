Consecration
/ˌkän-si-ˈkrā-shən/
noun
From Latin consecratio — the act of making sacred, from con- (completely) + sacrare (to make holy, dedicate). Related to sacred and sacrifice.

📖 Biblical Definition

The solemn act of setting apart a person, object, or place for exclusive holy use — removing it from common use and dedicating it wholly to God. In the Old Testament, priests, the tabernacle, its vessels, and offerings were consecrated through elaborate rites (Ex. 29; Lev. 8). In the New Testament, every believer is called to a living consecration: presenting themselves as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God (Rom. 12:1). Consecration is the visible expression of the inner reality of sanctification — the life fully surrendered, nothing held back, set apart entirely for God's purposes.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

CONSECRATION, n. The act or ceremony of separating from a common to a sacred use, or of devoting and dedicating a person or thing to the service and worship of God, by certain rites or solemnities. Consecration does not make a person or thing intrinsically holy, but it is the designation of persons or things to holy purposes.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The concept of consecration has been either clericalized (reserved for priests and sacraments, removing it from ordinary believers) or dissolved entirely in therapeutic Christianity, which focuses on self-fulfillment rather than self-surrender. Culture celebrates the dedicated life only when it serves personal ambition. The radical New Testament call for total consecration — no divided loyalties, no private reservations — is softened into optional spiritual extras rather than the basic posture of Christian discipleship.
