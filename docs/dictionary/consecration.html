Consecration
/ ˌkän(t)-sə-ˈkrā-shən /
noun
From Latin consecratio, from consecrare — "to make wholly sacred"; con- (intensive) + sacer (sacred, holy); Hebrew qadash (קָדַשׁ) — to set apart as holy

📖 Biblical Definition

Consecration is the act of setting apart a person, place, or object entirely for God's use and glory — removing it from common or profane use and dedicating it wholly to the sacred. In the Old Testament, priests, altars, vessels, and the tabernacle were consecrated through elaborate rites involving blood, oil, and sacrifice (Exodus 29). The New Testament applies this principle to the believer's entire life: by presenting our bodies as living sacrifices (Romans 12:1), we consecrate ourselves to God as temples of the Holy Spirit. Consecration is not a single moment but a posture of continual surrender.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

CONSECRA'TION, n. The act or ceremony of separating from a common to a sacred use, or of devoting and dedicating a person or thing to the service and worship of God, by certain rites or solemnities. Consecration does not make a person or thing intrinsically holy, but is an outward separation to a holy office or use, and an outward token of an internal dedication of heart.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The concept of consecration has been largely evacuated from Protestant life. Where it survives, it is often misunderstood as a special feeling for super-spiritual Christians rather than the normative call for every believer. In broader culture, the word is reduced to ceremonial pomp (consecrating a building, a stadium, an event) divorced from any genuine surrender to the living God. The Roman Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation also uses "consecration" in a way that conflates the symbolic with the physical, obscuring the once-for-all sacrifice of Christ.
