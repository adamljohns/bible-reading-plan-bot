Consecration
/ˌkän(t)-sə-ˈkrā-shən/
noun
Latin consecratio, from consecrare — to make sacred, dedicate wholly; con- (together, completely) + sacrare (to make holy, from sacer, sacred). Hebrew qadash (קָדַשׁ) — to be set apart, sanctified, made holy.

📖 Biblical Definition

Consecration is the solemn act of setting apart a person, place, or object for exclusive use in God's service. It is not merely dedication in a general sense but a total, irrevocable transfer of ownership — from the common to the sacred, from the world to the Lord. In the Old Testament, priests were consecrated through anointing and sacrifice (Exodus 29); the Tabernacle and its instruments were consecrated before service began. In the New Testament, believers are called living sacrifices (Romans 12:1) — a continuous consecration of the whole self to God. Consecration flows from the character of a holy God who demands that what belongs to Him be fully His.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

The act or ceremony of separating from a common to a sacred use; the act of devoting or setting apart for the service of God with appropriate rites. "The consecration of the priests was performed by Moses under divine direction."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

In contemporary usage, "consecration" has been flattened into a generic word for dedication or seriousness — athletes "consecrate" themselves to a sport, leaders to a cause. The church itself has largely lost the category: the notion that certain things, places, or persons are set apart as wholly belonging to God is treated as superstition. In liturgical traditions, consecration is sometimes reduced to a ritual formula stripped of its transformative meaning. The result is a Christianity with no sacred/common distinction — where everything is equally ordinary and nothing is truly set apart for God.

📖 Key Scripture

Exodus 29:1 — Now this is what you shall do to them to consecrate them, that they may serve me as priests.

Romans 12:1 — Present your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and acceptable to God, which is your spiritual worship.

2 Timothy 2:21 — If anyone cleanses himself from what is dishonorable, he will be a vessel for honorable use, set apart as holy, useful to the master of the house.

Hebrews 10:10 — We have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

H6942 — קָדַשׁ (qadash) — to be holy, set apart, consecrated; the foundational holiness root.

G37 — ἁγιάζω (hagiazō) — to sanctify, make holy, set apart; used of Christ consecrating believers through His sacrifice.

H4390 — מִלֵּא יָד (mille' yad) — lit. "to fill the hand"; the idiom for priestly consecration (ordination) in the OT.
