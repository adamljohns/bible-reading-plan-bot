Wrath
/ræθ/ or /rɑːθ/
noun
Old English wrǣththu (anger, fury) | Hebrew אַף (aph — nostril, anger) | Greek ὀργή (orgē — settled indignation)

📖 Biblical Definition

In Scripture, wrath is first and foremost an attribute of God — His settled, holy indignation against sin. God's wrath is not a temper tantrum; it is the righteous response of infinite holiness to moral evil. The wrath of God (Romans 1:18) is being revealed from heaven against all ungodliness. Human wrath is addressed in Scripture as often dangerous and sinful, yet even man may experience righteous anger — anger directed at genuine injustice, as modeled by Christ driving out the money-changers (Mark 11:15–17). The Bible consistently warns against quick-tempered, selfish wrath while affirming that God's wrath is just, measured, and ultimately glorifying.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

WRATH, n. [Sax. wrath.] 1. Violent anger; vehement exasperation; indignation. 2. The effects of anger. The wrath of God is often used in Scripture for the punishment he inflicts on sinners, or his holy displeasure with sin. 3. Any strong passion or excitement of displeasure.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture has reduced wrath entirely to a character flaw — anger management fodder. The concept of divine wrath has been systematically excised from progressive theology in favor of a "loving God" who takes no issue with sin. This false dichotomy pits love against wrath as if they are opposites, when in fact God's wrath and love are both expressions of His perfect holiness. A God incapable of wrath is incapable of true justice — and therefore incapable of truly loving what is good.

✍️ Usage

• "Flee from the wrath to come" — John the Baptist, Matthew 3:7

• A father disciplines with firmness, not wrath; correction proceeds from love, not wounded pride.

• The Day of the Lord is described as a day of wrath — the final culmination of divine justice against unremedied sin.

• Christ bore the full wrath of God on the cross, becoming a propitiation for those who believe (Romans 3:25).
