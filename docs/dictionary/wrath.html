Wrath
/ rath /
noun
Old English wrǣththu — from wrāth (angry). Hebrew: aph (אַף) — nostril, anger; chemah (חֵמָה) — heat, burning anger. Greek: orgē (ὀργή) — settled indignation; thumos (θυμός) — passionate anger. The righteous anger of a holy God against sin and evil.

📖 Biblical Definition

The wrath of God is His holy, righteous, and settled indignation against sin — not a loss of emotional control, but the necessary and just reaction of a perfectly holy nature to moral evil. Scripture is unambiguous: "The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the godlessness and wickedness of people" (Romans 1:18). God's wrath is not capricious or malicious; it is the inevitable corollary of His love and holiness. A God who was not angry at injustice, cruelty, and rebellion would not be good. The cross does not eliminate wrath — it absorbs it: Christ became a propitiation, bearing the full weight of God's judgment so that those in Him receive mercy instead.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

WRATH, n. [Sax. wraethu, from wrath, adj., wroth.] Violent anger; vehement exasperation; indignation; as the wrath of Achilles. In theology, the just punishment of sin; the vengeance of God. God is a righteous judge, and God is angry with the wicked every day. Ps. 7. The wrath of God is the just expression of his holy nature reacting against wickedness and rebellion.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity has largely purged divine wrath from its preaching, replacing the biblical God with a therapeutic deity whose defining attribute is acceptance. The result is a gospel without urgency — no wrath means no need for rescue, no need for propitiation, no need for repentance. A generation has grown up hearing that "God is love" without understanding that His love and His wrath are not in tension — both flow from His holiness. When wrath disappears, so does the cross. The good news that "Christ died for our sins" requires the prior bad news that sin incurs wrath; without both, the gospel is sentimentalism, not salvation.

📖 Key Scripture

• Romans 1:18 — "The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the godlessness and wickedness of people."

• John 3:36 — "Whoever rejects the Son will not see life, for God's wrath remains on them."

• 1 Thessalonians 1:10 — "Jesus, who rescues us from the coming wrath."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G3709 — orgē (ὀργή) — wrath, settled indignation; the most common NT word for God's wrath

G2372 — thumos (θυμός) — passionate anger, fury; used in Revelation for eschatological wrath

H639 — aph (אַף) — nose, nostril; by extension, anger (anger was expressed through flared nostrils)

H2534 — chemah (חֵמָה) — heat, burning anger, fierce wrath

✍️ Usage

• Jonathan Edwards preached "Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God" not to terrorize but to awaken — wrath preached faithfully drives sinners to the mercy that absorbs it.

• The wrath of God displayed at the cross is simultaneously the greatest demonstration of both His justice and His love.

• A Christian who never trembles before God's wrath has not yet understood his own sin; a Christian who only trembles has not yet understood the cross.
