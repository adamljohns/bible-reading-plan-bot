Wrath
/rɒθ/
noun
Old English wrǣththu — anger, fury; from Proto-Germanic root meaning "to twist, turn." Greek: orgē (ὀργή) — settled indignation, wrath; thymos (θυμός) — fierce outburst of anger, passion. Hebrew: af (אַף) — nostril, anger (anger expressed through snorting); chemah (חֵמָה) — heat, burning wrath.

📖 Biblical Definition

The wrath of God is His settled, righteous, holy opposition to everything that violates His nature and law — not a divine temper tantrum, but the necessary and just response of a morally perfect Being to sin. God's wrath and God's love are not in tension; they are the same holiness expressing itself differently toward different objects. His love cannot approve what destroys those He loves; His wrath is the jealous protection of all that is good. The NT speaks of a present wrath (the moral consequences of sin built into creation — Romans 1:18–32), and a future, eschatological wrath (the final judgment). The cross is where wrath and mercy meet: Christ absorbed the full wrath of God due to sinners, satisfying both justice and love.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

WRATH, n. Violent anger; vehement exasperation; indignation; as the wrath of Achilles. The effects of anger. In scripture, the wrath of God is his holy and just indignation against sin. The punishment which God inflicts on sinners, or will inflict on the finally impenitent.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Liberal theology has for two centuries been embarrassed by divine wrath, redefining God as a grandfatherly figure who would never actually judge anyone. "God is love" (true) is used to negate "God is just" (also true). But a God without wrath is not loving — He is indifferent. A judge who lets every murderer go free is not merciful; he is complicit. The doctrine of divine wrath is actually the foundation of human dignity: it says your actions matter, evil has consequences, and the universe is not morally neutral. Without wrath, there is no real justice — and without justice, love becomes sentimentality. The cross only makes sense if wrath is real. Christ didn't die to avoid a minor inconvenience; He died to absorb infinite holy fury on behalf of sinners.
