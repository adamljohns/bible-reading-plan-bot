Sola Gratia
/ˈsoʊ.lə ˈɡrɑː.tsi.ə/
Latin phrase
Latin: sola (alone, only) + gratia (grace, favor, gift — from gratus, pleasing, beloved). One of the five solas of the Protestant Reformation (alongside sola fide, sola scriptura, solus Christus, soli Deo gloria). Formally articulated in the 16th century though the substance is as old as Augustine contra Pelagius. The claim: salvation in its entirety — from election to glorification — is the work of God's grace alone, not a cooperative project between divine grace and human merit.

📖 Biblical Definition

Sola gratia teaches that every stage of salvation is the unilateral gift of God. The sinner contributes nothing to their justification, election, or regeneration — indeed, the sinner is incapable of contributing anything, being "dead in trespasses and sins" (Ephesians 2:1). God does not save because we were wise enough to choose him, good enough to deserve him, or persistent enough to find him. He saves because he is gracious — freely, sovereignly, unconditionally. The Reformation insistence on sola gratia was not a novelty; it was a recovery of Pauline theology obscured by centuries of merit-based accretions. The key distinction: grace is not the assistance God gives to help our free will reach salvation — it is the resurrection power God applies to our dead will to produce faith, repentance, and new life. Even the faith by which we receive salvation is itself a gift (Ephesians 2:8).

Webster 1828 — GRACE: "The free unmerited love and favor of God, the spring and source of all the benefits men receive from him… In theology: the divine influence operating in man to renew, regenerate, sanctify, and strengthen."

Augustine (c. 418): "Our will is free when it is not servile to vices and sins. Such freedom was given by grace… What we have from ourselves is sin; what we have from God is grace." (De gratia et libero arbitrio)

Luther (1525): "I frankly confess that, for myself, even if it could be, I should not want 'free-will' to be given to me… for I should be forced to labour with no guarantee of success… But now that God has taken my salvation out of the control of my own will, and put it under the control of His, and promised to save me, not according to my working or running, but according to His own grace and mercy, I have the comfortable certainty that He is faithful and will not lie to me." (The Bondage of the Will)

Modern American Christianity has largely traded sola gratia for what Bonhoeffer called "cheap grace" — grace as a background assumption rather than a staggering miracle. When people say "God helps those who help themselves" (nowhere in Scripture), they are denying sola gratia. When salvation is presented as God doing his part and you doing yours, it is no longer grace alone — it is a transaction. More subtly, the evangelical altar call that centers the human decision as the decisive factor in salvation quietly elevates human will above divine grace. Sola gratia does not deny human response — it insists that even that response is God's gift.

📖 Key Scripture

• Ephesians 2:8–9 — "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast."

• Romans 9:16 — "So then it depends not on human will or exertion, but on God, who has mercy."

• Titus 3:5 — "He saved us, not because of works done by us in righteousness, but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Spirit."

• John 1:13 — "Who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God."

• 2 Timothy 1:9 — "Who saved us and called us to a holy calling, not because of our works but because of his own purpose and grace, which he gave us in Christ Jesus before the ages began."

G5485 — charis (χάρις): grace, favor, gift freely given; used 155 times in NT. Always unmerited — charis by definition cannot be earned or it ceases to be grace (Romans 11:6: "But if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works; otherwise grace would no longer be grace").

G1435 — dōron (δῶρον): gift; used in Ephesians 2:8 to clarify that even saving faith is a divine gift, not a human contribution — closing the last loophole for human boasting.

G1653 — eleeō (ἐλεέω): to have mercy, to show compassion; the verb behind Romans 9:16 — salvation depends on God who mercies, not man who wills or runs.
