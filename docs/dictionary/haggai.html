Haggai

/ˈhæɡaɪ/
proper noun / prophet

Etymology & Webster 1828

Hebrew Chaggai, possibly "festal" or "my feast." Postexilic prophet whose ministry is precisely dated — his four oracles fall in a span of less than four months in 520 BC, the second year of the Persian king Darius. The returned exiles had laid the temple foundation sixteen years earlier (Ezra 3) but had then stopped work under opposition from neighboring peoples and distraction with their own houses. Haggai was sent, along with Zechariah, to shame and encourage them back to the task. The temple was completed four years later, in 516 BC (Ezra 6:15).

Biblical Meaning

Haggai's theology is simple and piercing: priorities reveal allegiances, and misplaced priorities invite covenantal discipline. "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?" (1:4). The people complained of poor harvests, economic hardship, broken dreams — and Haggai showed that their hardships were the direct outcome of neglecting God's house: "You have sown much, and harvested little. You eat, but you never have enough; you drink, but you never have your fill... you have looked for much, and behold, it came to little. And when you brought it home, I blew it away. Why? declares the LORD of hosts. Because of my house that lies in ruins, while each of you busies himself with his own house" (1:6, 9). Within three weeks of the sermon, work on the temple resumed. The book has a sustained messianic undercurrent: God will "shake the nations, so that the treasures of all nations shall come in, and I will fill this house with glory" (2:7) — a reference quoted in Hebrews 12:26 and traditionally read as the coming of Christ to the Second Temple. Modern application: when finances tighten, marriages strain, and plans miscarry, ask whether your priorities have God's house (the Church, His work) in first place or in last.

Key Scriptures

"Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?"— Haggai 1:4
"I will shake all nations, so that the treasures of all nations shall come in, and I will fill this house with glory... The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former."— Haggai 2:7-9
"Then Haggai, the messenger of the LORD, spoke to the people with the LORD's message, "I am with you, declares the LORD.""— Haggai 1:13
