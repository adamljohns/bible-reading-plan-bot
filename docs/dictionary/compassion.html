Compassion
/kəm·ˈpa·shən/
noun
From Latin compassio — "co-suffering"; com- (together) + pati (to suffer). Greek: splanchnizomai (σπλαγχνίζομαι) — moved in one's bowels, the seat of deep emotion.

📖 Biblical Definition

Compassion is the visceral movement of the soul toward the suffering of another, issuing in concrete action. In Scripture it is first and most perfectly an attribute of God — He is "compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love" (Psalm 103:8). The Hebrew racham (רַחַם) derives from rechem, meaning womb — a mother's tender love that cannot abandon its child. Compassion is not sentiment alone; every instance in the Gospels where Jesus is said to be "moved with compassion" is immediately followed by a healing, a feeding, or a raising. True compassion sees suffering, is moved by it, and spends itself to relieve it.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

COMPASSION, n. A suffering with another; painful sympathy; a sensation of sorrow excited by the distress or misfortunes of another; pity; commiseration. Compassion is a mixed passion, compounded of love and sorrow; at least some portion of love generally attends the pain or regret, or is excited by it. Extended empathy is the foundation of all the social virtues.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture has diluted compassion into mere feeling — the therapeutic "I feel your pain" that requires no sacrifice and changes nothing. Worse, political compassion has been weaponized: "compassion" now means expanding government programs that create dependency rather than restoration. True biblical compassion is costly, personal, and transformative. It walks into Lazarus's tomb. It touches the leper. It does not post a hashtag from a safe distance.
