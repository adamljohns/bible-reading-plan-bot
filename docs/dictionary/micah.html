Micah

/ˈmaɪkə/
proper noun / prophet

Etymology & Webster 1828

Hebrew Mikayah, "Who is like Yahweh?" Eighth-century BC prophet from Moresheth in the Judean lowlands, a rough contemporary of Isaiah in Jerusalem and of Amos and Hosea in the north. Prophesied during the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah (c. 735-700 BC). His book moves in three cycles of judgment-and-hope alternation, aimed primarily at the southern kingdom of Judah but with oracles against Samaria as well. Micah predicted the fall of Samaria (fulfilled 722 BC) and the siege of Jerusalem (fulfilled in Hezekiah's day and finally in 586 BC).

Biblical Meaning

Micah's two most famous passages show the sweep of his theology. (1) Micah 5:2 — "But you, O Bethlehem Ephrathah, who are too little to be among the clans of Judah, from you shall come forth for me one who is to be ruler in Israel, whose coming forth is from of old, from ancient days." Seven hundred years before the birth of Christ, Micah names the village where the Messiah would be born and identifies Him as a ruler whose origin is eternal. When Herod asked the scribes where the Christ was to be born, they quoted this verse (Matthew 2:5-6). (2) Micah 6:8 — the most quoted summary of biblical ethics: "He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?" Three things: mishpat (justice, right judgment), chesed (covenant loyalty and mercy), and humble walking with God. Not religious ceremony without ethics, not ethics without worship — all three held together. Micah also contains some of the fiercest denunciations in the prophets of corrupt judges, covetous landlords, false prophets-for-hire, and priests who teach for money (3:11). Yet the book ends with one of the warmest gospel statements in the OT: "Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity and passing over transgression for the remnant of his inheritance? He does not retain his anger forever, because he delights in steadfast love. He will again have compassion on us; he will tread our iniquities underfoot. You will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea" (7:18-19).
