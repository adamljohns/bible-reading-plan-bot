Mark (Book)
MAHRK
Bible book
Greek Markos (G3138). The second Gospel, traditionally written by John Mark from Peter's preaching. The shortest of the four Gospels; fast-paced, action-driven; uses immediately over forty times; portrays Jesus as the suffering Servant who came to give His life a ransom for many.

📖 Biblical Definition

The second Gospel and the shortest, traditionally written by John Mark, the cousin of Barnabas, drawing on the eyewitness preaching of the apostle Peter. Mark's pace is brisk — the adverb euthus (immediately, straightway) appears over forty times. The book emphasizes Jesus' actions over discourse and is structured around two halves: chapters 1-8 (who is Jesus?) climaxing in Peter's confession, and chapters 9-16 (what kind of Messiah is He?) climaxing in the cross and resurrection.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity prefers long teaching Gospels; Mark is the action-pace Gospel and was likely Peter's sermon material.

expand to see more

Mark is the shortest Gospel and the easiest to read at one sitting. Tradition says it preserves Peter's preaching as the apostle described his years with Jesus to the Roman church. The pace is brisk and visceral; Jesus heals, casts out demons, walks on water, calms storms, and rebukes the religious establishment in rapid sequence. Mark 10:45 is the thesis statement: the Son of man came... to give his life a ransom for many.

Modern Christianity often prefers the long teaching Gospels (Matthew, John). Mark is for action and confrontation. Read Mark in one sitting at least once; you will see the Lord move at His ground-level pace. The Christ of Mark is no detached philosopher; He moves toward Jerusalem with set face, gives His life a ransom, and rises on the third day.
