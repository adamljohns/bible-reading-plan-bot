Kerygma
/ kə-ˈrig-mə /
noun
From Greek kērygma — proclamation, announcement; from kēryssein — to herald, to proclaim publicly; from kēryx — a herald, a public crier. In the ancient world, the kēryx was the official messenger who announced the decrees of a king. The kerygma is the herald's shout — not advice, not suggestion, but royal announcement.

📖 Biblical Definition

Kerygma is the core proclamation of the Christian gospel — the non-negotiable announcement that Jesus of Nazareth, the Son of God, died for sins, was buried, was raised on the third day, and is now Lord and Christ. Theologian C.H. Dodd identified its earliest form in the apostolic sermons of Acts: Christ died according to the Scriptures, was buried, was raised, appeared to witnesses, and now calls all people to repentance and faith. This is not teaching (didachē), which explains and applies; it is proclamation — an event announced that demands a response. The kerygma is offensive by nature: it announces that God has acted decisively in history, that this action has cosmic implications, and that no one is neutral before it. You are either reconciled or still estranged, repentant or still refusing.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

KERYGMA, n. Not in Webster 1828 (a Greek theological term that entered English theological usage primarily in the 20th century through C.H. Dodd and Rudolf Bultmann). The concept, however, is thoroughly biblical. Webster's era knew the thing without the technical term: the preaching of the gospel as a public proclamation of historical facts — Christ crucified and risen — rather than merely a set of moral instructions or a system of doctrine.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Rudolf Bultmann, the 20th-century German theologian, attempted to "demythologize" the kerygma — stripping away what he considered the mythological framework (virgin birth, bodily resurrection, ascension) to reach a "kernel" of existential encounter. The result was a kerygma with no historical content: a proclamation with nothing to proclaim. Contemporary evangelicalism faces the opposite corruption: a kerygma so trimmed for palatability that it announces "God has a wonderful plan for your life" rather than "Jesus is Lord and you are not." Neither version produces the crisis of decision the true kerygma demands. The gospel is not lifestyle advice — it is a royal decree that changes the ontological status of the hearer. You were under judgment; through faith in the crucified and risen Christ, you are no longer. That is the kerygma.

📖 Key Scripture

• 1 Corinthians 15:3–5 — Paul's own summary of the kerygma: "Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures, and that he appeared..." (The earliest creedal kerygma)

• Acts 2:22–36 — Peter's Pentecost sermon: the template kerygma — Jesus' ministry, crucifixion, resurrection, exaltation, and Lordship, with a call to repentance.

• Romans 10:8–9 — "The word is near you... that is, the message (rhēma) of faith we are proclaiming: if you confess with your mouth, 'Jesus is Lord,' and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved."

• 1 Corinthians 1:21 — "God was pleased through the foolishness of what was preached (kērygmatos) to save those who believe." (The kerygma is divinely foolish and divinely powerful)

• Mark 1:14–15 — Jesus' own kerygma: "The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news." (The shortest, densest kerygma in Scripture)

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G2782 kērygma — proclamation, preaching; the content of what is heralded. Used in Matthew 12:41, Luke 11:32, Romans 16:25, 1 Corinthians 1:21, 2:4, 15:14, 2 Timothy 4:17, Titus 1:3.

G2784 kēryssō — to herald, to proclaim as a crier; used over 60 times in the NT for the proclamation of Jesus, John the Baptist, Paul, and the disciples.

G2783 kēryx — a herald, official messenger; used in 1 Timothy 2:7 and 2 Timothy 1:11 of Paul as a herald of the gospel.
