Rich Man and Lazarus

/rɪtʃ mæn ənd ˈlæzərəs/
parable

Etymology & Webster 1828

Luke 16:19-31. A rich man dressed in purple and fine linen feasted sumptuously every day; a poor man named Lazarus lay at his gate full of sores, desiring scraps from the table. Both died. Lazarus was carried by angels to "Abraham's side"; the rich man went to Hades in torment. From across the chasm the rich man begged Abraham to send Lazarus with a drop of water for his tongue, then to warn his five brothers. Abraham's answer: "If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be convinced if someone should rise from the dead."

Biblical Meaning

This is the one parable in which Jesus names a character by name (Lazarus; not to be confused with the actual Lazarus raised in John 11, though the overlap is striking). That feature has led some interpreters to read it as a historical account rather than a parable, but either way the lessons are identical. Three themes. (1) Post-mortem reality is real and fixed. Jesus depicts conscious existence after death for both the righteous and the unrighteous, with a "great chasm" fixed between them that "none may cross." No second chances; no purgatory; no annihilation. This is one of the strongest passages in Scripture on the conscious, eternal state of the unregenerate dead. (2) Wealth is not neutral. The rich man's sin is not specified beyond his lifestyle — he feasted, he wore finery, he ignored Lazarus at his gate. Jesus repeatedly warns that the comforts of wealth blind the heart (Luke 12:15-21; 1 Timothy 6:9-10). Lazarus's poverty is not meritorious in itself; the point is that the rich man had opportunities for mercy and did not take them. (3) If Moses and the prophets are not enough, miracles will not be enough. The rich man thinks a visit from the dead would change his brothers' minds; Abraham says no. Scripture is the sufficient means of persuasion; people who reject it will reject miracles too. The resurrection of Jesus proves Abraham right — most who heard the news rejected it anyway. The Bible is enough. Preach it.
