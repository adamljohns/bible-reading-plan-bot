Election
/i-ˈlek-shən/
noun
From Latin electio — "a choosing, selection," from eligere (to pick out, select), from ex- (out) + legere (to choose). The sovereign act of choosing — applied in Scripture to God's choosing of persons and peoples for His purposes.

📖 Biblical Definition

Election is God's sovereign choice, made before the foundation of the world, to set His saving love upon particular individuals — not on the basis of their foreseen merit or faith, but according to His own purpose and grace (Ephesians 1:4-5, Romans 9:11). Israel was elect as a nation — not because they were great, but because God loved them (Deuteronomy 7:6-7). The church is elect in Christ — chosen in Him before creation to be holy and blameless. Election is not fatalism; it is the foundation of assurance. The one who is in Christ need not fear: "those whom he predestined he also called, and those whom he called he also justified, and those whom he justified he also glorified" (Romans 8:30). Election is not about who is excluded — it is about why anyone is included: grace alone.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

ELECTION, n. The act of choosing; choice; the act of selecting one or more from others. In theology, the sovereign act of God, by which he selects certain individuals from among mankind for salvation. "There is a remnant according to the election of grace." Romans 11:5.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture uses "election" almost exclusively for political voting — stripping the word of its theological depth. Within the church, the doctrine of election is widely avoided because it offends human pride and the therapeutic gospel of self-determination. Arminian revisions soften it to "God chose those He foresaw would choose Him" — but this makes election contingent on human will, not divine grace. Open theism goes further, denying God's foreknowledge altogether. The discomfort with election is ultimately a discomfort with the sovereignty of God.

📖 Key Scripture

Ephesians 1:4 — "He chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him."

Romans 9:11 — "Though they were not yet born and had done nothing either good or bad — in order that God's purpose of election might continue, not because of works but because of him who calls."

Romans 8:33 — "Who shall bring any charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies."

Deuteronomy 7:6 — "For you are a people holy to the LORD your God. The LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession."

2 Peter 1:10 — "Be diligent to confirm your calling and election, for if you practice these qualities you will never fall."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G1589 — eklogē (ἐκλογή): election, choosing; from ek (out of) + legō (to pick, choose). Used in Romans 9–11 for God's sovereign selection.

G1588 — eklektos (ἐκλεκτός): chosen, elect, selected; applied to Israel, Christ, and the church throughout NT.

H977 — bāchar (בָּחַר): to choose, select; used for God's choosing of Israel, David, and Jerusalem; the primary OT word for divine election.

✍️ Usage

Election is not a cold decree — it is a love letter written before time, addressed to every person who will one day find themselves in Christ.

The doctrine of election does not make evangelism pointless; it makes it certain. God will call His people through the preaching of the gospel.

You did not choose God first — this is the humbling truth that makes grace so staggering (John 15:16).
