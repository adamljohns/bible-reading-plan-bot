Godspeed
GOD-speed
interjection / noun
Middle English God spede — “may God cause you to prosper / succeed.” One word in modern English: Godspeed. Also written godspeed, god-speed, and KJV two-word God speed. All three spellings land here.

Biblical Definition

Godspeed is not “good luck.” It is a blessing: may God give you success on the road. The older English is a prayer that the living God would speed — prosper, further — a traveler or a work. That is why the King James puts two words in 2 John 10–11: “If there come any unto you, and bring not this doctrine, receive him not into your house, neither bid him God speed: For he that biddeth him God speed is partaker of his evil deeds.” To say Godspeed to a false teacher is not politeness. It is partnership. You are asking God to prosper a lie.

The Christian may say Godspeed to a brother sent on a true mission — gospel, marriage, a lawful journey — because the telos of the trip is under God. He may not say it over rebellion, syncretism, or a man running from covenant.

Webster 1828 / spelling

Canonical headword: Godspeed (one word). Variants: godspeed · god-speed · God speed (KJV).

expand to see more

Webster 1828 lists GODSPEED as a compound of God + speed: a wish that God would prosper. Related: SPEED — “to make haste; to succeed; to prosper; to aid.” The blessing is not velocity for its own sake. It is success granted by God.

Do not confuse with slang speedrun (racing a course by skipping wisdom). Godspeed is the opposite: you go, but you go under His hand.

Key Scripture

2 John 10–11 — "…neither bid him God speed: For he that biddeth him God speed is partaker of his evil deeds."

Genesis 24:12 — Abraham’s servant: "O LORD God of my master Abraham, I pray thee, send me good speed this day…"

Joshua 1:8 — "…then thou shalt make thy way prosperous, and then thou shalt have good success."

3 John 6 — send gospel workers forward “after a godly sort.”

Modern Corruption

Reduced to “good luck,” NASA slang, or a blank farewell that baptizes whatever the traveler is doing.

expand to see more

Culture hears Godspeed as luck-with-a-halo — a send-off with no doctrine. 2 John forbids that. You do not wish God to prosper a man who denies Christ. You also do not treat Godspeed as a cute period on an email. If you mean it, you are invoking God over the path. If you do not mean it, say goodbye.

Usage

"Godspeed — may the Lord prosper the work, not the ego."

"Do not bid God speed to a doctrine that denies the Son."

"Godspeed is a prayer, not luck."
