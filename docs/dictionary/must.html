Must
/mʌst/
verb
Auxiliary of necessity. Old English mōste, past of mōtan, to be allowed or obliged. Webster also records a noun for new wine and a verb for growing moldy — different roots, same spelling. Scripture’s load is the auxiliary.

Biblical Definition

Must names what cannot be otherwise. Jesus told Nicodemus he must be born again — not as a suggestion for the spiritually curious, but as the only door. Paul told Timothy a bishop must be blameless — fitness required by the office, not a personality type. The word is necessity, not mood.

Webster’s two auxiliary senses still serve: physical necessity (a man must eat) and moral fitness (a bishop must be grave). The gospel uses both. New birth is not optional. Office is not a costume.

Webster 1828 Definition

MUST, verb intransitive. To be obliged; to be necessitated. Also, new wine; to grow moldy.

MUST, verb intransitive

1. To be obliged; to be necessitated. It expresses both physical and moral necessity. A man must eat for nourishment, and he must sleep for refreshment. We must submit to the laws or be exposed to punishment. A bill in a legislative body must have three readings before it can pass to be enacted.

2. It expresses moral fitness or propriety, as necessary or essential to the character or end proposed. ‘Deacons must be grave,’ ‘a bishop must have a good report of them that are without.’

MUST, noun [Latin mustum; Heb. to ferment.] New wine; wine pressed from the grape but not fermented.

MUST, verb transitive To make moldy and sour.

MUST, verb intransitive To grow moldy and sour; to contract a fetid smell.

Key Scripture

• John 3:7 — “Do not marvel that I said to you, ‘You must be born again.’” (BTE NKJV)

• 1 Timothy 3:2 — “A bishop then must be blameless, the husband of one wife, temperate, sober-minded, of good behavior, hospitable, able to teach;” (BTE NKJV)

Modern Corruption

Therapeutic English turns “must” into “should, if it resonates,” as if new birth were a wellness option.

expand to see more

Jesus did not invite Nicodemus to explore rebirth as a metaphor. He named a necessity. Soft pulpits recast the word as preference, then wonder why the church is unborn. The office-must is likewise flattened: blameless becomes “authentic,” one wife becomes “complicated,” able to teach becomes “good energy.” Must is not branding. It is the door that will not open another way.

Usage

• “You must be born again is not a slogan. It is the only entrance.”

• “If the office can be held without the musts, it is not the office.”
