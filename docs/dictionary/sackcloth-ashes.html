Sackcloth & Ashes
/SAK-kloth and ASH-iz/
noun phrase
Hebrew saq (coarse goat-hair garment) plus epher (ashes). The combined biblical sign of profound grief or repentance.

📖 Biblical Definition

Sackcloth and ashes is Scripture's combined sign of profound grief or repentance: a coarse, scratchy garment worn next to the skin and ashes scattered or sat in. The pair appears together repeatedly — Mordecai, Daniel, Job, the king of Nineveh — whenever a soul is stripped to the bone before God.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

(Composite.) The traditional Hebrew sign of mourning or contrition; coarse cloth worn on the body and ashes upon the head.

expand to see more

Webster: sackcloth — “a sort of coarse cloth, of which sacks are made; in Scripture, garments of this stuff were worn in mourning, distress, or as a sign of humiliation.”

Ashes were gathered from the household fire or sacrificial altar and either scattered on the head or sat upon. The combination signaled total inward grief manifest in the body.

📖 Key Scripture

Job 42:6 — "Wherefore I abhor myself, and repent in dust and ashes."

Daniel 9:3 — "And I set my face unto the Lord God, to seek by prayer and supplications, with fasting, and sackcloth, and ashes."

Esther 4:1 — "When Mordecai perceived all that was done, Mordecai rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes."

Jonah 3:6 — "And he arose from his throne, and he laid his robe from him, and covered him with sackcloth, and sat in ashes."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern Christianity has neither the practice nor a substitute; corporate fasting in sackcloth is so far gone we hardly know what we have lost.

expand to see more

Nineveh repented in sackcloth from king to cattle (Jonah 3:6-8); the city was spared. Daniel approached intercession in sackcloth and ashes (Dan 9:3); the seventy weeks were revealed. The biblical correlation between humbled bodies and answered prayer is uncomfortable but real.

Modern Christians cannot wear what they do not own; the recovery is not necessarily the cloth itself, but the willingness to humble the body when humbling the soul. Skip a meal, sit on the floor, cover the mirror — let the body confess what the soul confesses.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

Two Hebrew words name the pair; their pairing is one of Scripture's great fixed phrases.

expand to see more

H8242 — שַׂק (saq) — sackcloth; coarse goat-hair garment for mourning.

H665 — אֵפֶר (epher) — ashes; gathered from the hearth and scattered upon the head.
