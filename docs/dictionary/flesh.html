Flesh
/flesh/
noun
From Old English flæsc — "muscle, body, animal food." In Scripture, carries multiple meanings from the literal (physical body) to the theological (the sinful human nature in rebellion against God).

📖 Biblical Definition

In Scripture, "flesh" operates on at least three levels. (1) Literally: physical human or animal tissue — "the Word became flesh" (John 1:14) affirms the full bodily Incarnation of Christ. (2) Figuratively: humanity in its weakness and mortality — "all flesh is grass" (Isaiah 40:6) captures human frailty before the eternal God. (3) Theologically (especially in Paul): the sinful nature — the principle of self-centered rebellion that remains in the believer even after regeneration. The flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh (Galatians 5:17). To "walk in the flesh" is to live oriented toward self and sin; to "walk in the Spirit" is to be oriented toward God. The flesh is not the body per se (which will be resurrected); it is the disposition of the old Adam.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

FLESH, n. The soft part of the bodies of animals; the muscles. In theology, the carnal part of man; the body, as the seat of carnal desires; the corrupt nature of man; human nature. "The flesh lusteth against the Spirit." Galatians 5:17.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern culture worships the flesh — the body, its pleasures, its appearance, its desires. "Follow your heart" and "trust your gut" are secular commands to obey the flesh. The fitness and wellness industry sells body-optimization as the highest good. Simultaneously, progressive theology has blurred the biblical distinction between flesh (sinful nature) and body (created good) — using the body's goodness as an argument against sexual ethics. This is a category error: Paul did not condemn the body; he condemned the sarx — the disposition of self-sovereignty.

📖 Key Scripture

Galatians 5:16–17 — "Walk by the Spirit, and you will not gratify the desires of the flesh. For the desires of the flesh are against the Spirit, and the desires of the Spirit are against the flesh."

Romans 8:5 — "For those who live according to the flesh set their minds on the things of the flesh, but those who live according to the Spirit set their minds on the things of the Spirit."

John 1:14 — "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory."

Galatians 5:24 — "Those who belong to Christ Jesus have crucified the flesh with its passions and desires."

Isaiah 40:6 — "All flesh is grass, and all its beauty is like the flower of the field."

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G4561 — sarx (σάρξ): flesh; in Paul, frequently the sinful nature or the sphere of self-centered human existence opposed to God. One of the richest and most contested words in NT theology.

H1320 — bāśār (בָּשָׂר): flesh, body, skin; used for physical flesh, human nature in its weakness, and kinship bonds ("bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh").

✍️ Usage

The flesh is not your body — it is the old orientation toward self that was crucified with Christ and must be mortified daily by the Spirit.

When John says "the Word became flesh," he is not using a euphemism — he is making the most audacious claim in history: God took on human biology.

Every sin begins with a choice to trust the flesh over the Spirit — to let appetite, pride, or fear drive the moment.
