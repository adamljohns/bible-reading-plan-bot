Propitiation
/prəˌpɪʃiˈeɪʃən/
noun
Latin: propitiatio — from propitiare (to appease, render favorable). Greek: hilastērion (ἱλαστήριον) — mercy seat; the place or means of turning aside divine wrath through a sacrifice.

📖 Biblical Definition

The satisfaction of God's righteous wrath against sin through the substitutionary sacrifice of Jesus Christ. Propitiation is not merely covering or canceling sin (expiation) but specifically the turning aside of divine judgment — Christ absorbing in Himself the full penalty that sinners deserve, so that God's wrath is fully and finally satisfied. This is why the cross is not a tragedy but a triumph: God Himself, in Christ, provides the propitiation that His own holy law demands. The mercy seat (hilastērion) in the tabernacle was the physical type pointing to this ultimate reality.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

PROPITIA'TION, n. The act of appeasing wrath and conciliating the favor of an offended person; the act of making propitious. In theology, the atonement or atoning sacrifice; the sacrifice which Christ offered to appease the wrath of God and render him propitious to sinners. Christ is the propitiation for our sins.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Liberal theology rejects propitiation as "cosmic child abuse" or as making God vengeful and primitive. Modern translations often replace "propitiation" with the weaker "atoning sacrifice" (NIV) or "expiation" (RSV), losing the specific idea of wrath-satisfaction. Process theology and open theism deny that God has genuine settled wrath against sin, making propitiation nonsensical. The result is a cross that is merely a moral example or display of love — stripped of its judicial and substitutionary core, and therefore unable to actually save anyone.

🔗 Greek & Hebrew Roots

G2435 — hilastērion (ἱλαστήριον): propitiation, mercy seat; used in Romans 3:25 of Christ as the fulfillment of the tabernacle's mercy seat where blood was sprinkled on the Day of Atonement.

G2434 — hilasmos (ἱλασμός): propitiation, expiation; used in 1 John 2:2 and 4:10 for Christ's satisfaction of divine wrath on behalf of sinners.

H3727 — kappōret (כַּפֹּרֶת): the mercy seat; the gold lid of the Ark of the Covenant where the high priest sprinkled blood on Yom Kippur — the type of Christ's propitiation.
