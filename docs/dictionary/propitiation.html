Propitiation
/ prə·ˌpi·shē·ˈā·shən /
noun
Latin propitiatio — from propitiare (to appease, to render favorable). Hebrew: kaphar (כָּפַר) — to cover, to atone; Greek: hilasmos (ἱλασμός) — an appeasing sacrifice, the turning away of wrath.

📖 Biblical Definition

Propitiation is the satisfying of God's righteous wrath against sin through the atoning sacrifice of Jesus Christ. It is stronger than mere "expiation" (the removal of guilt) — propitiation includes the appeasement of a personal, holy God whose nature demands justice. The mercy seat of the Ark of the Covenant (hilasterion) was literally the "place of propitiation," where blood was sprinkled to turn back divine wrath (Exodus 25:22; Romans 3:25). Christ is our hilasmos — He absorbs the wrath we deserved, rendering God propitious (favorably inclined) toward those who are in Him. This is not God's love being overcome by His wrath, nor His wrath being overridden by His love — it is both perfectly satisfied at the cross.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

PROPITIA'TION, n. [L. propitiatio.] The act of appeasing wrath and conciliating the favor of an offended person; the act of making propitious. In theology, the atonement or atoning sacrifice offered to God to assuage his wrath and render him propitious to sinners. Christ is the propitiation for our sins. 1 John 2:2.

⚠️ Modern Corruption

Modern liberal theology, uncomfortable with a God who has wrath, replaces propitiation with "expiation" — framing the atonement as merely cleaning up humanity's guilt rather than satisfying divine justice. This is not a minor translation dispute; it fundamentally changes the nature of God and the gospel. A God without wrath needs no propitiation. A God without holiness needs no atonement. When churches preach the cross as only a "demonstration of love" without propitiation, they produce a gospel that cannot explain why Christ had to die — only that it was moving and inspiring. The cross without wrath is sentiment, not salvation.

📖 Key Scripture

• 1 John 2:2 — "He is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only but also for the sins of the whole world."

• Romans 3:25 — "God presented Christ as a sacrifice of atonement [hilasterion], through the shedding of his blood."

• 1 John 4:10 — "This is love: not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as an atoning sacrifice for our sins."

• Hebrews 2:17 — "That he might become a merciful and faithful high priest...to make propitiation for the sins of the people."

✍️ Usage

• The Levitical priest's annual sprinkling of blood on the mercy seat was a shadow of Christ's once-for-all propitiation at Calvary.

• Understanding propitiation is not optional theology — it is the difference between knowing why Christ died and merely believing that He did.

• The hymn writer understood it: "In my place condemned He stood, sealed my pardon with His blood" — propitiation made personal.
