Submission (Wife)
/səbˈmɪʃ.ən/
theological concept
Greek hypotasso — to arrange under, to order oneself under appointed authority; applied to wives in Ephesians 5, Colossians 3, Titus 2, and 1 Peter 3.

📖 Biblical Definition

Wifely submission is the voluntary, intelligent, and joyful ordering of a Christian wife's strength under her own husband's God-appointed headship. It is not a declaration of female inferiority: the Son submits to the Father in the economy of redemption without being less than God, and the church submits to Christ without being worthless. It is also not mindless compliance or permission for sin. Scripture bounds the command with the Lord Himself: "as is fitting in the Lord" (Colossians 3:18). No wife is called to submit to abuse, lawlessness, idolatry, or any command that contradicts Christ.

In its positive form, wifely submission is covenantal order: a strong woman willingly aligns her counsel, gifts, labor, and loyalty under a husband who is himself under Christ and commanded to love her sacrificially. It is not a husband's weapon; Scripture addresses the command to wives, not to husbands as a license to coerce. The husband is commanded to love, nourish, cherish, and lay down his life; the wife is commanded to respect and order herself under that headship. Together they display the mystery of Christ and His church.

📜 Webster 1828 Definition

SUBMIS'SION, n.

SUBMIS'SION, n. The act of yielding to power or authority; the acknowledgment of dependence; humble or suppliant behavior. In Christian usage, submission is not servility but the ordered yielding of the will to lawful authority under God.

📖 Key Scripture

Ephesians 5:22-24 — "Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord."

Colossians 3:18 — "Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as it is fit in the Lord."

1 Peter 3:1 — "Likewise, ye wives, be in subjection to your own husbands."

Titus 2:4-5 — "That they may teach the young women to be sober, to love their husbands, to love their children..."

⚠️ Modern Corruption

The modern error is to treat all hierarchy as oppression and all autonomy as virtue.

expand to see more

Modern culture caricatures wifely submission as erasure, abuse, or weakness, then uses that caricature to reject biblical marriage altogether. Scripture gives a sharper answer: authority can be abused, but abuse does not abolish authority; passivity can masquerade as submission, but biblical submission is active obedience to God; domineering men can misuse the doctrine, but their sin does not cancel God's design. The counterfeit is coercion. The biblical virtue is ordered strength.
