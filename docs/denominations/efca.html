Gender and leadership: the nuance

The EFCA's own statements draw three different lines, and they are not the same line:

  1. Ordination is for men. The 1988 General Conference decision and the credentialing booklet restrict the Certificate of Ordination to qualified men. That is binding on the denomination's own credentials.
  2. Women serve in non-ordained vocational ministry. The EFCA grants women a Certificate of Christian Ministry. That is a staff role, not the pastoral office.
  3. Elders are a local decision. Most EFCA churches restrict elders to men, but the EFCA states there is no official policy for local churches; each congregation decides.

So a given EFCA church can still have women elders. Leadership is graded YELLOW (mixed) at the denomination level for that reason, and the church's own page decides it.

“The EFCA does not ordain women, which is a general conference decision (1988) based on our understanding of the biblical text” — efca.org
“This credential is designed for qualified males who serve in pastoral ministry in the local church whose primary ministry responsibility is preaching and teaching the Word.” — efca.org
“Women can and do serve in vocational ministry, and the EFCA recognizes this by offering a Certificate of Christian Ministry” — efca.org
“women usually do not serve as an elder in the EFCA, as most EFCA churches recognize that the biblical qualifications for the pastor (serving vocationally in this capacity) are the same qualifications for all other elders as well” — efca.org
“even though there is no official EFCA policy for local churches” — efca.org
“because of our congregational form of church government, the local church is free to make its own decision on this matter.” — efca.org
