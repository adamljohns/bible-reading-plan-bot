Day 22: Proverbs 22

R — Reject Passivity

REAL MAN Focus

Nothing exposes a passive man like fatherhood and excuses. Proverbs 22 hands you the most active command in parenting — train up a child — and then mocks the sluggard who will not leave the house because there is a lion outside. Reject Passivity means you do the deliberate work of shaping what is entrusted to you, and you stop manufacturing reasons to stay in bed.

Key Verse

“Train up a child according to the way he should go, and even when he is old he will not turn from it.”
— Proverbs 22:6

Observation

"Train up a child according to the way he should go, and even when he is old he will not turn from it" (v.6). The verb is active and intentional — to train is to aim, to shape, to point in a direction on purpose. A passive father assumes his kids will just absorb good character by being nearby, the way you would assume a garden grows itself. Solomon says no; the way a child should go is a way you deliberately train him into. The most important thing you will ever build requires the death of passivity — showing up on purpose, aiming the arrow, doing the daily formative work no one applauds.

The chapter then names the enemy of every man's productivity: the excuse. "The sluggard says, There is a lion outside! I shall be killed in the streets!" (v.13). It is absurd on purpose. There is no lion; there is only a man who has become so skilled at manufacturing reasons that he can conjure a predator to justify staying inside. Passivity is rarely honest about itself — it dresses up as caution, as timing, as waiting for the right moment. The man who rejects passivity learns to recognize his own lions for what they are: excuses with fur glued on.

And Proverbs 22 quietly warns about the passivity that gets a man enslaved: "the borrower is slave to the lender" (v.7). Drifting into debt is a passive act — a hundred small unexamined yeses — and it hands your future to someone else. But the chapter also shows the reward of the opposite man: "have you noticed a man quick and practiced at his craft? It is before kings he will take his stand" (v.29). The diligent, deliberate, non-passive man rises. He trains his kids on purpose, refuses his own excuses, guards his freedom, and hones his craft — and that active life opens doors a passive one never sees.

Application

Do one deliberate, formative thing today with someone entrusted to you — a real conversation, a lesson, a correction you have been avoiding with a child or someone you mentor. Then name one lion you have been using as an excuse, walk out the door anyway, and do the thing you have been telling yourself you could not.

Prayer

Father, expose the lions I have invented to justify my passivity, and give me the honesty to call my excuses what they are. Make me a man who trains on purpose — who aims the arrow, who does the daily formative work with the people You have entrusted to me instead of assuming they will turn out fine on their own. Free me from the drift that enslaves and the excuses that paralyze. In the name of Your Son Jesus — Amen.
