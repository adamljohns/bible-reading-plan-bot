Chapter 30

Of the Lord's Supper

The supper of the Lord was instituted for the perpetual remembrance of Christ — a bond and pledge of his people's communion with him and with one another.

1

The supper of the Lord Jesus was instituted by him the same night in which he was betrayed, to be observed in his churches unto the end of the world for the perpetual remembrance and the showing forth of the sacrifice of himself in his death; for the confirmation of the faith of believers in all the benefits thereof; for their spiritual nourishment and growth in him; for their further engagement in and to all the duties which they owe unto him; and as a bond and pledge of their communion with him and with each other.

Proof-texts
1 Cor 11:23-26 · 1 Cor 10:16-17,21
2

In this ordinance Christ is not offered up to his Father, nor any real sacrifice made at all for the remission of sins of the living or the dead, but only a memorial of that one offering up of himself by himself upon the cross, once for all, and a spiritual oblation of all possible praise unto God for the same. So that the popish sacrifice of the mass, as they call it, is most abominable, and injurious to Christ's own sacrifice, which is the only propitiation for all the sins of the elect.

Proof-texts
Heb 9:25-26,28 · 1 Cor 11:24 · Matt 26:26-27
3

The Lord Jesus has, in this ordinance, appointed his ministers to pray and bless the elements of bread and wine, and thereby to set them apart from a common to a holy use; to take and break the bread, to take the cup, and (they communicating themselves) to give both to the communicants.

Proof-texts
1 Cor 11:23-26
4

The denial of the cup to the people, worshiping the elements, the lifting them up or carrying them about for adoration, and the reserving them for any pretended religious use, are all contrary to the nature of this ordinance and to the institution of Christ.

Proof-texts
Matt 26:26-28 · Matt 15:9 · Ex 20:4-5
5

The outward elements in this ordinance, duly set apart to the uses ordained by Christ, have such relation to him crucified that truly, although in terms used figuratively, they are sometimes called by the names of the things they represent — namely, the body and blood of Christ — even though in substance and nature they still remain truly and only bread and wine, as they were before.

Proof-texts
1 Cor 11:27 · 1 Cor 11:26-28
6

That doctrine which maintains a change of the substance of bread and wine into the substance of Christ's body and blood (commonly called transubstantiation) by consecration of a priest, or by any other way, is repugnant not only to Scripture but even to common sense and reason; it overthrows the nature of the ordinance, and has been and is the cause of manifold superstitions, yea, of gross idolatries.

Proof-texts
Acts 3:21 · Luke 24:6,39 · 1 Cor 11:24-26
7

Worthy receivers, outwardly partaking of the visible elements in this ordinance, do then also inwardly by faith, really and indeed — yet not carnally and corporally, but spiritually — receive and feed upon Christ crucified, and all the benefits of his death. The body and blood of Christ are then not corporally or carnally, but spiritually, present to the faith of believers in that ordinance, as the elements themselves are to their outward senses.

Proof-texts
1 Cor 10:16 · 1 Cor 11:23-26
8

All ignorant and ungodly persons, as they are unfit to enjoy communion with Christ, so are they unworthy of the Lord's table, and cannot, without great sin against him, while they remain such, partake of these holy mysteries or be admitted to them. Yea, whoever shall receive unworthily is guilty of the body and blood of the Lord, eating and drinking judgment to himself.

Proof-texts
2 Cor 6:14-15 · 1 Cor 11:29 · Matt 7:6
