Chapter 29

Of Baptism

Baptism is an ordinance of the New Testament, administered by immersion in water in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, to those who profess faith in Christ.

1

Baptism is an ordinance of the New Testament, ordained by Jesus Christ. To the one baptized it is a sign of his fellowship with Christ in his death and resurrection; of his being engrafted into him; of the remission of sins; and of his giving up unto God, through Jesus Christ, to live and walk in newness of life.

Proof-texts
Rom 6:3-5 · Col 2:12 · Gal 3:27 · Mark 1:4 · Acts 22:16 · Rom 6:4
2

Those who actually profess repentance toward God, and faith in and obedience to our Lord Jesus Christ, are the only proper subjects of this ordinance.

Proof-texts
Mark 16:16 · Acts 8:36-37 · Acts 2:41 · Acts 8:12 · Acts 18:8
3

The outward element to be used in this ordinance is water, in which the party is to be baptized in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit.

Proof-texts
Matt 28:19-20 · Acts 8:38
4

Immersion, or the dipping of the person in water, is necessary to the due administration of this ordinance.

Proof-texts
Matt 3:16 · John 3:23
