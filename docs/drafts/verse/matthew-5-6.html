  • μακάριος makarios · Blessed · Strong's G3107

    The Greek word for blessedness — not emotional happiness dependent on circumstances, but the deep well-being of those who are right with God. The Beatitudes (Matt 5) open with makarios.

    [WRITE — what "Blessed" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ὁ, ἡ, τό Ho, Hē, To · are they which · Strong's G3588

    The Greek definite article ho/hē/to is the most common word in the New Testament, appearing over 19,800 times. Unlike English "the," the Greek article is fully declined — it has gender (masculine, feminine, neuter), number (singular, plural), and case (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, vocative).

    [WRITE — what "are they which" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • διψάω Dipsao · thirst · Strong's G1372

    The Greek verb dipsao (G1372) means to thirst or to be thirsty — both literally and in its powerful metaphorical uses describing spiritual longing and the promise of ultimate satisfaction in Christ. Usage & Theological Significance Dipsao is one of Scripture's most evocative spiritual metaphors.

  • δικαιοσύνη dikaiosyne · after righteousness · Strong's G1343

    The Greek dikaiosyne is one of the central theological terms of the New Testament, meaning righteousness, justice, or right standing. It denotes both the character of being right (morally upright) and the legal status of being declared right (justified).
