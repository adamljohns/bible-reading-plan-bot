1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • ἐπαισχύνομαι Epaischunomai · I am · Strong's G1870

    The Greek verb epaischunomai (ἐπαισχύνομαι) means to be ashamed of or to feel shame about. It appears 11 times in the New Testament and is theologically decisive: Paul and other writers invoke it to address the temptation to be ashamed of the Gospel in a culture that viewed crucifixion as ultimate shame.

  • εὐαγγέλιον euangelion · of the gospel · Strong's G2098

    Good news, glad tidings. From eu (good) + angelos (messenger).

  • Χριστός Christos · of Christ · Strong's G5547

    The Anointed One — the Greek translation of Hebrew Mashiach (Messiah). In the OT, kings, priests, and prophets were anointed with oil as a sign of God's commissioning.

    [WRITE — what "of Christ" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ἐστί esti · it is · Strong's G2076

    The form esti (ἐστί, also written ἐστίν) is the third person singular present indicative of eimi — meaning "is, it is, he/she/it is." It is one of the most frequently occurring words in the Greek NT, appearing hundreds of times. In the mouth of Jesus, its theological weight is staggering — particularly in the seven great "I AM" (ἐγώ εἰμι) declarations of John's Gospel.

  • δύναμις dynamis · the power · Strong's G1411

    The Greek dynamis means power, might, ability, or miraculous power. It is the root of the English words 'dynamic' and 'dynamite'.

4. Confessional Anchor

This promise of Christ, and salvation by him, is revealed only by the Word of God. Neither do the works of creation or providence, with the light of nature, reveal Christ or grace by him, so much as in a general or obscure way — much less that men destitute of the revelation of him by the promise or gospel should be enabled thereby to attain saving faith or repentance.
