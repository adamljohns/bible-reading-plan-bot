  • διό Dio · Wherefore · Strong's G1352

    The Greek conjunction dio (a combination of dia + ho, 'through which') introduces a logical conclusion drawn from what preceded. It is slightly stronger than oun ('therefore') and signals a tight causal connection between premise and conclusion.

  • ἀποτίθημι apotithēmi · lay apart · Strong's G659

    The Greek verb apotithēmi means to put away, to lay aside, to take off, or to set down. Like removing a garment, it carries vivid imagery of deliberate and complete separation from something previously worn.

  • ῥυπαρία rhyparia · filthiness · Strong's G4507

    From rhyparos (G4508, filthy, dirty, wretched). Rhyparia means filth, moral impurity, squalor — the accumulated grime of sin and degraded living.

  • Περισσεια Perisseia · superfluity · Strong's G4050

    From perissos (exceeding). Abundance, surplus, that which overflows beyond what is needed.

  • κακία Kakia · of naughtiness · Strong's G2549

    The Greek noun kakia (κακία) means wickedness, malice, moral evil, or depravity. It derives from kakos (bad/evil) and represents not merely wrongdoing but the disposition of a malicious heart — a settled orientation toward evil.
