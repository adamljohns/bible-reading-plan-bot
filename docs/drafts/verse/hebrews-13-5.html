1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • ἀφιλάργυρος Aphilargyros · be without covetousness · Strong's G866

    The Greek adjective aphilargyros is formed from the negative alpha, philos (loving), and argyros (silver/money). It means not a lover of money, free from greed, or not greedy for financial gain.

    [WRITE — what "be without covetousness" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ἀρκέω Arkeo · and be content · Strong's G714

    The Greek arkeo (ἀρκέω) means 'to be sufficient,' 'to be enough,' 'to satisfy,' or 'to be content.' It appears in John 6:7 (Philip: 'eight months' wages would not be enough'), John 14:8 (Philip: 'Lord, show us the Father and that will be enough for us'), 2 Corinthians 12:9 ('My grace is sufficient for you'), and 1 Timothy 6:8 ('godliness with contentment is great gain'). Usage & Theological Significance The word arkeo reaches its theological summit in 2 Corinthians 12:9: 'My grace is sufficient (arkei) for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness.' This is God's direct answer to Paul's three-fold prayer for the removal of his thorn.

    [WRITE — what "and be content" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ἐρέω ereō · hath said · Strong's G2046

    Ereō serves as the future tense of legō — 'I will say' or 'it will be said.' It is a defective verb, supplying the future forms that legō lacks. It appears in contexts where someone will speak a future word — often in eschatological judgment contexts.

    [WRITE — what "hath said" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ἀνίημι Aniemi · leave · Strong's G447

    In Ephesians 6:9, Paul commands masters: "do the same to them, and stop threatening (aniemi)" — literally, loosen your grip on threats. The Christian employer is to let go of the power-through-fear model.

    [WRITE — what "leave" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • οὐδέ Oude · nor · Strong's G3761

    The Greek word oude (οὐδέ) is a strong compound negative meaning 'neither,' 'nor,' or 'not even.' It intensifies negation beyond the simple ou (not). It appears about 143 times in the NT.
