1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • γρηγορέω gregoreo · Watch ye · Strong's G1127

    Gregoreo (γρηγορέω) means to be awake, alert, or watchfully on guard. It appears about 22 times in the New Testament and is one of the key eschatological words — the posture required of disciples in light of Christ's return.

    [WRITE — what "Watch ye" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • στήκω Stēkō · stand fast · Strong's G4739

    Stēkō (G4739) means to stand firm or stand fast — a later form derived from the perfect of histēmi (to stand). It is used exclusively in the NT for the posture of faith under pressure: holding one's ground in the face of opposition, temptation, or trial.

    [WRITE — what "stand fast" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • πίστις Pistis · the faith · Strong's G4102

    The Greek noun pistis is one of the most important words in the New Testament, occurring approximately 243 times. It means faith, trust, reliance, or firm conviction — particularly the trust placed in God and in Jesus Christ.

  • ἀνδρίζομαι Andrizomai · quit you like men · Strong's G407

    "Be watchful, stand firm in the faith, act like men (andrizomai), be strong" (1 Corinthians 16:13). This four-part command builds to its climax in andrizomai — embodying faith with courageous action.
