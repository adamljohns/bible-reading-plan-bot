Verse Study · 2 Timothy

2 Timothy 3:16

One word in this verse appears nowhere else in Greek literature before Paul. He seems to have built it himself, because nothing available said what he meant.

Every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness,— World English Bible (public domain)

All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness:— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

The verse makes two claims and they are different in kind. The first is about origin: Scripture is God-breathed. The second is about use: it is profitable, and then four specific uses are listed. Origin and function, in that order, because the second depends entirely on the first. A book is only profitable in these particular ways if it came from where Paul says it came from.

Notice also that the four uses are not four ways of feeling better. Teaching, reproof, correction, training. Two of the four are corrective, and one of those means being shown you are wrong. This is not a description of devotional reading.

  • γραφή graphe · “scripture” · Strong’s G1124

    Graphe (γραφή) means a writing or document, but in the New Testament it almost always refers specifically to the sacred Scriptures — the Old Testament writings understood as authoritative divine communication. It appears about 51 times and is the standard New Testament term for Scripture.

    Graphe simply means a writing, a document. But in the New Testament it is almost always the technical term for the sacred writings — the Old Testament, understood as authoritative divine speech. That is what Paul primarily has in view here, which is worth saying plainly: the first application of this verse is to the Hebrew Scriptures Timothy learned as a child, mentioned two verses earlier.

  • θεόπνευστος Theopneustos · “is given by inspiration of God” · Strong’s G2315

    The Greek adjective theopneustos (θεόπνευστος) is a compound of theos (God) and pneustos (breathed). It means God-breathed or divinely inspired and appears only once in the New Testament — in the foundational text for the doctrine of biblical inspiration, 2 Timothy 3:16.

    Here is the word Paul appears to have coined. Theopneustos is theos (God) plus a form of pneo (to breathe) — God-breathed. It occurs exactly once in the New Testament and is not found in surviving Greek literature before this.

    The image is not that God inflated an existing book with divine significance, and not that inspired men breathed particularly well. Scripture is what God exhaled. That is why the older translation “given by inspiration” can mislead slightly — inspiration suggests breathing in, and Paul’s word is the other direction. The text is not man’s word that God elevated; it is God’s breath that men wrote down.

  • διδασκαλία Didaskalia · “doctrine” · Strong’s G1319

    The Greek noun didaskalia (διδασκαλία) refers to teaching, doctrine, or the body of instruction — both the act of teaching and the content taught. It appears 21 times in the New Testament, concentrated especially in the Pastoral Epistles (1 and 2 Timothy, Titus), where 'sound doctrine (didaskalia)' is one of the defining markers of a healthy church and faithful ministry.

    Didaskalia is teaching — both the act and the body of content taught. It heads the list, and its position matters. Before Scripture corrects you it informs you. A man who reaches for the Bible only to fix problems will use it badly; its first office is to tell you what is true.

  • ἔλεγχος elegchos · “reproof” · Strong’s G1650

    Proof, evidence that convinces, the conviction that comes from being shown the truth. Elegchos is the inner certainty produced by evidence — not blind faith but grounded assurance.

    Elegchos is proof, evidence that convinces — the conviction produced by being shown. This is the word behind “reproof,” and it is not scolding. It is the moment the evidence lands and you can no longer maintain your position. Scripture reproves the way a fact reproves, not the way an angry man does.

“All Scripture” or “every Scripture”?

The Greek can be rendered either way, and translations split — the King James has “all scripture,” the World English Bible “every Scripture.” The difference is smaller than it looks. “All Scripture” says the whole collection is God-breathed; “every Scripture” says each individual passage is. Both refuse the position Paul is excluding, which is that some parts are inspired and others are filler you may set aside.

That is the practical force of the sentence. Not that the Bible is inspiring, but that there is no page of it you get to discount.

2. Where It Sits

2 Timothy is almost certainly the last letter Paul wrote. He is in prison again, and this time he does not expect release — in chapter 4 he says the time of his departure is at hand. He asks Timothy to come before winter and to bring his cloak and the books.

That is the setting. Chapter 3 opens with a warning about the last days: men will be lovers of self, lovers of money, boasters, having a form of godliness but denying its power. Then, in verse 12, all who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution. Then verse 13: evil men and impostors will grow worse and worse.

And Paul’s answer to all of it, in verse 14, is but you, continue in the things which you have learned — and from a child you have known the holy Scriptures.

So this verse is not an abstract doctrine of inspiration dropped into a letter. It is a dying man telling his successor what will hold when everything else is deteriorating. The doctrine of Scripture here is a survival instruction.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

This verse sits underneath the Reformation’s formal principle. If Scripture is God-breathed, then it carries God’s own authority, and no church council or tradition can stand above it — because nothing else in the argument was breathed out by God.

The Reformers were careful, though, to pair authority with sufficiency and clarity. A book can be authoritative and still leave you dependent on experts to tell you what it says. Their claim was stronger: that Scripture is clear enough in what is necessary that an ordinary believer, reading it, can be taught, reproved, corrected and trained by it.

That conviction is why they translated. Everything in the Reformation about vernacular Bibles is downstream of taking this verse at face value.

4. Confessional Anchor

The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.

1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures

Three adjectives, and each one answers a different anxiety.

Sufficient — you do not need a supplement. Paul’s list of four uses is presented as adequate to make the man of God complete, and the next verse says exactly that: thoroughly furnished unto all good works.

Certain — it will not shift under you. That is the promise a man in Paul’s position needed, writing from a cell to a nervous younger pastor in a deteriorating age.

Infallible — it does not fail in what it sets out to do. Which follows directly from theopneustos. If it is God’s breath, its reliability is His.

5. For the Man Reading This

Take the four uses as a diagnostic on how you actually read. Most men accept teaching and quietly opt out of reproof. We read for comfort, insight, and material to use on other people. Paul lists being shown wrong as one of the four purposes, which means a Bible that has never contradicted you is a Bible you have been managing.

So read the parts you avoid. Everyone has them — the passages about money, or the tongue, or the way you treat your wife, or the ones that are simply boring. “All Scripture” is a claim with teeth precisely there.

And notice the last word: training in righteousness. The Greek is the word for raising and disciplining a child. It is not a quick fix; it is formation over years. Which means the honest measure of your Bible reading is not what you got out of this morning. It is who you are becoming across a decade of it — the same slow, unglamorous logic as Luke’s daily.

In the Foundations and The Wheel memorize packs — carry it.
