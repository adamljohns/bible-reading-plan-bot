1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • Χριστός Christos · with Christ · Strong's G5547

    The Anointed One — the Greek translation of Hebrew Mashiach (Messiah). In the OT, kings, priests, and prophets were anointed with oil as a sign of God's commissioning.

  • δέ de · nevertheless · Strong's G1161

    De is the second most common word in the Greek New Testament (after kai), appearing about 2,792 times. It is a postpositive particle — meaning it cannot stand first in a sentence — and it signals a mild contrast ("but"), a continuation ("and, now"), or a transition ("then").

    [WRITE — what "nevertheless" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ζάω zaō · I live · Strong's G2198

    To live, to be alive, to have vitality. Zaō covers physical life, spiritual life, and eternal life.

    [WRITE — what "I live" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ὅς, ἥ, ὅ Hos, Hē, Ho · the life which · Strong's G3739

    The Greek relative pronoun hos/hē/ho introduces relative clauses and refers back to an antecedent noun. It is fully declined and agrees with its antecedent in gender and number, but takes its case from its function within the relative clause.

    [WRITE — what "the life which" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • σάρξ sarx · the flesh · Strong's G4561

    Flesh — the physical body, human nature in its weakness, or the sinful disposition opposed to the Spirit. Sarx has a wide range in the NT: neutral (physical body), positive (the Word became flesh), and negative (the works of the flesh).
