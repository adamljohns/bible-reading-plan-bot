Verse Study · Psalm

Psalm 1:2-3

The blessed man is not the man who found a better crowd. He is the man whose delight got attached to a law, and whose roots were put where water already runs.

but his delight is in the LORD’s law. On his law he meditates day and night. He will be like a tree planted by the streams of water, that produces its fruit in its season, whose leaf also does not wither. Whatever he does shall prosper.— World English Bible (public domain)

But his delight is in the law of the LORD; and in his law doth he meditate day and night. And he shall be like a tree planted by the rivers of water, that bringeth forth his fruit in his season; his leaf also shall not wither; and whatsoever he doeth shall prosper. wither: Heb. fade— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

Verse 1 has already named the refusals: he does not walk in the counsel of the ungodly, stand in the path of sinners, or sit in the seat of the scornful. Verses 2–3 are the positive. Blessing is not only what a man will not join. It is what he loves, what he murmurs, and where he has been planted.

Notice the order. Delight, then meditation, then the tree. You cannot start at “whatever he does shall prosper” and work backward into a technique. The fruit is the issue of a root.

  • Chephets · “delight” · Strong’s H2656

    The Hebrew noun chephets denotes delight, pleasure, desire, or what is pleasing and desirable. It can describe human delight in things or people, but most profoundly it appears in contexts describing what God desires and what pleases Him.

    The blessed man is not grimly compliant. His want has been retied to the law. A man who only handles Scripture as a duty has not yet reached verse 2, even if his streak looks impressive.

  • תּוֹרָה torah · “law” · Strong’s H8451

    One of the most significant Hebrew words, appearing about 220 times. Torah means instruction, teaching, or law.

    This is not a cage the blessed man learned to tolerate. It is God’s gracious instruction, and he wants it. Torah is richer than English “law.” From yarah, to point the way.

  • הָגָה hagah · “meditate” · Strong’s H1897

    Originally of sound — the low murmuring of a dove or the rumbling of a lion over its prey — hagah came to mean the quiet, repetitive vocalization of someone absorbing a text or thought. Biblical meditation was fundamentally oral: you murmured the words until they soaked in, not a silent emptying but an active filling.

    Biblical meditation is oral filling, not silent emptying. Joshua 1:8 uses the same verb. Day and night is the same pair. A glance is not hagah.

  • שָׁתַל shathal · “planted” · Strong’s H8362

    The Hebrew verb shathal means to plant, especially to transplant — not a seed tossed and hoped for, but a tree set where water can reach it. KJV in Psalm 1:3 renders it planted.

    The blessed man is not a volunteer weed. Someone put him by the rivers. The fruit later is not luck. It is the issue of a root placed on purpose.

  • פֶּלֶג peleg · “rivers” · Strong’s H6388

    The Hebrew noun peleg names a stream that has been divided into channels — irrigation water, not a random flood. It is kin to the verb that means to split or divide.

    Streams, channels — water that keeps coming. The tree does not carry its own weather. It is stationed where the water already is.

  • צָלַח tsalach · “shall prosper” · Strong’s H6743

    A Hebrew verb meaning to prosper, succeed, be profitable, advance, rush (upon). In the Qal stem, it means to rush or advance forcefully.

    Joshua 1:8 promised prosperity after mouth, meditation, and doing. Psalm 1 says the same thing without a battlefield: fruit in season, leaf that does not wither, whatever he does prospers. Season still belongs to God. The tree does not fruit on demand. It fruits when it is time, because it was planted where it can last.

2. Where It Sits

Psalm 1 is the doorway of the Psalter. Two men, two ways, two ends. The ungodly are chaff. The LORD knows the way of the righteous. The whole book of prayers that follows assumes this fork.

Verse 2 is the hinge. Without delight and hagah, verse 3 becomes a prosperity slogan and verse 1 becomes mere separation. With them, the refusals of verse 1 make sense: a man whose mouth is full of the law has less room for the counsel of the ungodly.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed reading refuses two corruptions at once. First: that blessing is a technique for getting whatever you do to pay. Second: that meditation is an emptying or a mood. Hagah is a man murmuring a text until it soaks.

I am not hanging a verbatim Father-quote on this page this turn. The CCEL Henry fetch for adjacent pages returned a loading shell, not a clean public-domain paragraph I could check word for word. The psalm already interprets itself: chaff versus a transplanted tree. One has no root. The other was placed on purpose.

4. Confessional Anchor

The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience.

1689 LBCF, ch. 1 — Of the Holy Scriptures

Psalm 1:2 is that sentence as appetite. Sufficient — the blessed man does not need a second stream. Certain — he meditates this law, not a private substitute. Obedience — the tree fruits. A rule you never delight in will not hold you when the scornful take their seat.

5. For the Man Reading This

Stop asking first whether your leaf looks green. Ask whether you have been planted by the water, and whether anything in you actually delights in the law.

Hagah today, out loud, one psalm or one commanded text, morning and night, until it can come back without the screen. That is not a program. That is verse 2.

Then stay where the water is. A man who meditates on Sunday and takes counsel with the ungodly by Tuesday is not a tree having a dry week. He is walking back toward verse 1.

And do not use “prosper” as the test. Fruit comes in season. A transplanted tree can look slow. Chaff looks busy. Stay at the river.

Season still belongs to God. The tree does not fruit on demand. Stay at the river.
