  • Endyo · Put on · Strong's G1746

    The Greek verb endyo means to put on, to clothe oneself, or to be clothed with. It appears about 27 times in the New Testament in both literal and rich metaphorical senses.

  • θεός theos · of God · Strong's G2316

    The standard Greek word for God, over 1,300 times in the NT. In classical Greek, used broadly for any deity.

    [WRITE — what "of God" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • δύναμαι dynamai · may be able · Strong's G1410

    A deponent verb meaning to be able, to have power or capacity, to be capable of. It is the verbal root of dynamis (G1411, power, miracle) and the English words 'dynamic' and 'dynamite.' It encompasses both physical ability and moral/spiritual capacity — what one can do versus what one cannot do.

    [WRITE — what "may be able" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ἵστημι histēmi · to stand · Strong's G2476

    To cause to stand, to set in place, to establish — and in the intransitive, to stand firm, to take a stand. Histēmi conveys both physical positioning and spiritual resolve.

    [WRITE — what "to stand" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • διάβολος diabolos · of the devil · Strong's G1228

    The Greek diabolos combines dia (through/across) and ballo (to throw) to mean 'one who throws across' or 'one who accuses'. As a noun with the article (ho diabolos), it is the primary Greek title for Satan, the Devil.
