Verse Study · Matthew

Matthew 5:5

The earth is not promised to the man who seizes it. It is promised to the man whose strength has been broken to the bit.

Blessed are the gentle, for they shall inherit the earth.— World English Bible (public domain)

Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth.— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

This is the third beatitude. The already-written Matthew 5:3 draft locked the door: poor in spirit first, present-tense kingdom. Verse 4 (already a written draft on the live hub) names the grief. Verse 5 names the posture that follows: not the grab, the yielded strength.

WEB’s gentle is not wrong as a color. KJV meek keeps the older English that still remembers power under control. Do not flatten this into niceness. Niceness inherits applause. Meekness inherits what it refused to snatch.

  • μακάριος makarios · “Blessed” · Strong’s G3107

    The Greek word for blessedness — not emotional happiness dependent on circumstances, but the deep well-being of those who are right with God. The Beatitudes (Matt 5) open with makarios.

    Already locked on the Matthew 5:3 draft: not mood-happiness, but the settled well-being of a man right with God. If makarios meant cheerful, “blessed are those who mourn” would be nonsense. The same word stands here.

  • πραΰς praus · “meek” · Strong’s G4239

    Praus appears 4 times in the NT (Matthew 5:5; 11:29; 21:5; 1 Peter 3:4). The Greek word was used of a wild animal that had been tamed — a warhorse broken to the bit, powerful but controlled.

    Used of a wild animal tamed — a warhorse broken to the bit, powerful but controlled. Not spinelessness. Strength submitted to discipline. Jesus calls Himself praus and humble in heart (Matthew 11:29). A meek man can still swing. He just does not swing for his own name. The bit is the Father’s.

  • κληρονομέω kleronomeo · “inherit” · Strong’s G2816

    Kleronomeo appears 18 times in the New Testament, derived from kleros (lot/inheritance) + nemo (to distribute). It is the language of inheritance — receiving what has been promised, allotted, and secured through covenant relationship.

    Inherit is the opposite of seize. The meek do not take the earth. They are given it. That is why the sentence can stand in a world where the violent still look like they are winning.

    The earth in view is not a smaller backyard spirituality. It is the same creation Genesis 1:1 said God made, now promised to men who will not try to become lords of it by force. Psalm 37 stands behind the line: the meek shall inherit the earth. Jesus is not inventing a softer ethic. He is preaching the Psalter on the mountain.

2. Where It Sits

Matthew 5:3–10 reads as one portrait deepening. Bankrupt (v. 3), grieving (v. 4), no longer asserting himself (v. 5), craving a righteousness he does not have (v. 6). Meekness is not a separate hobby for mild personalities. It is what poverty of spirit looks like when it stops grabbing.

The mountain frame still holds from the 5:3 draft: a new Moses, disciples in front, crowds listening, first words blessing rather than commanding. Verse 5 is still blessing. The command-life of the sermon comes later. First He names who the kingdom people are.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

The Reformed instinct here is the same as on 5:3: this is not a temperament to perform. A man can fake quietness the way he fakes poverty of spirit. Genuine praus is strength that has been mastered, not weakness that never had anything to master.

I am not hanging a verbatim Father-quote on this page this turn. The CCEL Henry fetch for this stretch returned a loading shell or a chapter head, not a clean public-domain paragraph I could check word for word. The lexicon’s warhorse picture and Psalm 37 are enough to keep the verse from becoming a courtesy poster.

4. Confessional Anchor

They who are united to Christ, effectually called, and regenerated, having a new heart, and a new spirit created in them, through the virtue of Christ’s death and resurrection, are also farther sanctified, really and personally, through the same virtue, by his Word and Spirit dwelling in them; the dominion of the whole body of sin is destroyed, and the several lusts thereof are more and more weakened and mortified, and they more and more quickened and strengthened in all saving graces, to the practice of all true holiness, without which no man shall see the Lord.

1689 LBCF, ch. 13 — Of Sanctification

Meekness is that mortifying work in the will to seize. Strength is not erased. It is brought under the bit. The inheritance is promised; the grabbing is being put to death.

5. For the Man Reading This

If you lead a house, meekness is not letting the house drift. It is refusing to use your strength to make the house about you.

Ask where you are still seizing — the argument you have to win, the boy you have to crush, the reputation you will not lay down. That is not praus. That is an unbroken horse.

Take the bit in one concrete place this week. Yield the last word. Keep the standard. Those are not opposites. Christ was meek and He still cleared the temple.

And do not envy the men who look like they are inheriting the earth by force. The promise is future and it is not theirs. Your job is to stay under the bit until the allotment is given.
