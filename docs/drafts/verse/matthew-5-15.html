  • οὐδέ Oude · Neither · Strong's G3761

    The Greek word oude (οὐδέ) is a strong compound negative meaning 'neither,' 'nor,' or 'not even.' It intensifies negation beyond the simple ou (not). It appears about 143 times in the NT.

  • καίω Kaio · do men light · Strong's G2545

    The Greek verb kaio means to burn, to set on fire, to cause to shine by burning. It describes both literal fire and metaphorical burning — the burning of lamps, hearts, and judgment.

  • λύχνος lychnos · a candle · Strong's G3088

    A portable lamp, typically an oil lamp — the primary source of indoor illumination in the ancient world. Used metaphorically for spiritual illumination, divine guidance, and the believer's witness.

  • τίθημι Tithemi · put · Strong's G5087

    The Greek verb tithemi (τίθημι) means to put, place, set, lay, or appoint. It is one of the most common verbs in the NT, appearing about 100 times.

  • ὑπό Hupo · under · Strong's G5259

    Hupo (ὑπό) is one of the fundamental Greek prepositions, expressing: (1) position under — beneath, below, subject to; (2) agency — by/through (especially in passive constructions: 'raised by God'); (3) subjection — under the authority or power of another. It appears hundreds of times in the NT and is grammatically crucial for identifying the agent in passive verbs.
