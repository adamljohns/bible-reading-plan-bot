  • ἐκ / ἐξ ek / ex · cometh by · Strong's G1537

    Ek (ἐκ, used before consonants) / ex (ἐξ, used before vowels) is one of the most common Greek prepositions, occurring over 900 times in the NT. It always takes the genitive case and fundamentally expresses origin, source, or separation: "out of," "from," or "by means of." It answers the question: where did this come from?

4. Confessional Anchor

The grace of faith, whereby the elect are enabled to believe to the saving of their souls, is the work of the Spirit of Christ in their hearts; and is ordinarily wrought by the ministry of the Word; by which also, and by the administration of baptism and the Lord's Supper, prayer, and other means appointed of God, it is increased and strengthened.

1689 LBCF, ch. 14 — cites Rom 10:14,17

This promise of Christ, and salvation by him, is revealed only by the Word of God. Neither do the works of creation or providence, with the light of nature, reveal Christ or grace by him, so much as in a general or obscure way — much less that men destitute of the revelation of him by the promise or gospel should be enabled thereby to attain saving faith or repentance.
