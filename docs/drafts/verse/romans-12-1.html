1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • παρακαλέω parakaleo · I beseech · Strong's G3870

    Parakaleo (G3870) covers the full pastoral range: to comfort the grieving, to exhort the sluggish, to urge the wavering, to appeal to the hard-hearted. It shares its root with parakletos — the Comforter/Advocate, the Holy Spirit.

    [WRITE — what "I beseech" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • οὖν oun · therefore · Strong's G3767

    Oun is an inferential conjunction meaning "therefore," "then," "so," or "consequently." It draws logical conclusions from what has been stated. It appears about 499 times in the NT and is especially common in John and Paul, where careful argument is being built.

    [WRITE — what "therefore" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • ἀδελφός adelphos · brethren · Strong's G80

    Literally 'from the same womb' — a brother by blood. In the NT, it expands to include spiritual brothers and sisters, fellow members of the faith community.

    [WRITE — what "brethren" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • οἰκτιρμός Oiktirmos · the mercies · Strong's G3628

    The Greek noun oiktirmos (οἰκτιρμός) means mercy, compassion, or tender compassion. It typically appears in the plural (oiktirmoi) to express the fullness and intensity of compassionate feeling.

  • θεός theos · of God · Strong's G2316

    The standard Greek word for God, over 1,300 times in the NT. In classical Greek, used broadly for any deity.
