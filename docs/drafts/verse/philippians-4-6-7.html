1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • πᾶς pas · every thing · Strong's G3956

    All, every, each, the whole. Pas is the most common adjective in the NT, appearing over 1,200 times.

    [WRITE — what "every thing" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • προσευχή proseuche · by prayer · Strong's G4335

    Prayer — the specific word for directed prayer to God, as distinguished from general petition or intercession. Also used for a place of prayer (Acts 16:13).

    [WRITE — what "by prayer" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • Deesis · supplication · Strong's G1162

    From deomai ("to be in want, to beg"), deesis denotes a specific, urgent request — supplication arising from felt need. It is more focused than proseuche (G4335, general prayer) and emphasizes the petitioner's sense of need and dependence.

    [WRITE — what "supplication" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • εὐχαριστία eucharistia · thanksgiving · Strong's G2169

    Thanksgiving, the giving of thanks, gratitude. From eu (well) + charis (grace) — literally 'good grace' or 'gracious response.' Eucharistia is both the attitude of gratitude and the act of giving thanks, particularly in worship.

    [WRITE — what "thanksgiving" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • γνωρίζω gnōrizō · let · Strong's G1107

    The Greek verb gnōrizō (γνωρίζω) means to make known, reveal, or declare — to bring something from hidden to known. It is used of God making His will known, of the gospel being proclaimed, and of believers making known the mystery of Christ.
