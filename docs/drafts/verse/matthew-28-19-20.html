1. The Text and Its Words

  • πορεύομαι poreuomai · Go ye · Strong's G4198

    Poreuomai (πορεύομαι) is a deponent verb meaning to go, travel, proceed, or journey. It appears about 153 times in the NT and covers both literal travel and figurative life-conduct (to "walk" or live in a certain way).

    [WRITE — what "Go ye" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • οὖν oun · therefore · Strong's G3767

    Oun is an inferential conjunction meaning "therefore," "then," "so," or "consequently." It draws logical conclusions from what has been stated. It appears about 499 times in the NT and is especially common in John and Paul, where careful argument is being built.

  • μαθητεύω matheteuo · and teach · Strong's G3100

    Matheteuo (μαθητεύω) means to make a disciple, to become a disciple, or to teach. It appears 4 times in the New Testament.

    A Greek noun meaning nation, people, race. In the plural (ta ethnē), it specifically refers to the nations, the Gentiles — all peoples outside of Israel.
