1. The Text and Its Words

[WRITE — open the exposition — what is this verse actually claiming, and what does the sentence assume that the reader may not have noticed]

  • ὑποτάσσω Hupotasso · Submit yourselves · Strong's G5293

    The Greek verb hupotasso (ὑποτάσσω) means to arrange or place under, to submit, or to be subject to. It is a military term: to arrange under a commander.

    [WRITE — what "Submit yourselves" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • οὖν oun · therefore · Strong's G3767

    Oun is an inferential conjunction meaning "therefore," "then," "so," or "consequently." It draws logical conclusions from what has been stated. It appears about 499 times in the NT and is especially common in John and Paul, where careful argument is being built.

    [WRITE — what "therefore" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • θεός theos · to God · Strong's G2316

    The standard Greek word for God, over 1,300 times in the NT. In classical Greek, used broadly for any deity.

    [WRITE — what "to God" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • διάβολος diabolos · the devil · Strong's G1228

    The Greek diabolos combines dia (through/across) and ballo (to throw) to mean 'one who throws across' or 'one who accuses'. As a noun with the article (ho diabolos), it is the primary Greek title for Satan, the Devil.

  • φεύγω pheugo · he will flee · Strong's G5343

    Pheugo (G5343) is a strategy, not a weakness. Paul commands believers to pheugo from certain dangers — sexual immorality (1 Corinthians 6:18), idolatry (1 Corinthians 10:14), love of money (1 Timothy 6:11), youthful lusts (2 Timothy 2:22).
