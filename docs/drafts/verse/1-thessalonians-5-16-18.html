  • προσεύχομαι proseuchomai · Pray · Strong's G4336

    Proseuchomai is the most common NT verb for prayer, appearing 86 times. It comes from pros (toward) + euchomai (to wish, pray), and carries the idea of directed, intentional address to God.

    [WRITE — what "Pray" is doing in this verse specifically — not a dictionary entry]

  • Adialeiptōs · without ceasing · Strong's G89

    The Greek adverb adialeiptōs (ἀδιαλείπτως) means without ceasing, unceasingly, incessantly, or constantly. It derives from a- (not) + dialeipō (to leave off, cease, interrupt), thus "without interruption." It appears four times in the New Testament: Romans 1:9; 1 Thessalonians 1:3; 1 Thessalonians 2:13; and the famous 1 Thessalonians 5:17 — "Pray without ceasing (adialeiptōs)." Paul uses the same word to describe his own unceasing prayer for his congregations.

    All, every, each, the whole. Pas is the most common adjective in the NT, appearing over 1,200 times.

  • εὐχαριστέω eucharisteo · give thanks · Strong's G2168

    From eu (good) + charis (grace/favor), eucharisteo is to give thanks — literally to declare that good grace has been received. It is the verb from which the Church took 'Eucharist' as its name for the Lord's Supper, as Jesus gave thanks (eucharisteo) over the bread and cup.
