1. The Text and Its Words

  • ἑαυτοῦ heautou · your · Strong's G1438

    The reflexive pronoun heautou (and its forms heautō, heauton, heautēs, etc.) means of/to/by himself/herself/itself — the subject acting upon or referring back to itself. It appears ~319 times in the NT and is essential for expressing self-denial, self-giving, and mutual submission.

  • γυνή gynē · wives · Strong's G1135

    The primary Greek word for a woman or wife. Like the Hebrew ʾishshâ (H802), it covers both an adult female and a married woman, with context determining the sense.

  • καθώς kathos · even as · Strong's G2531

    From kata ('according to') and hos ('as'). Kathos is the Greek word for comparison — 'just as' or 'even as' — used to draw parallels, typically between divine action and human obligation.

  • Χριστός Christos · Christ · Strong's G5547

    The Anointed One — the Greek translation of Hebrew Mashiach (Messiah). In the OT, kings, priests, and prophets were anointed with oil as a sign of God's commissioning.

    From ek (out of) + kaleō (to call) — the 'called-out ones,' an assembly or gathering. In secular Greek, it was a political assembly of citizens.
