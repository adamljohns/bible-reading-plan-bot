Verse Study · Ephesians

Ephesians 5:25

The command to the husband is not “be nicer than the age.” It is: love her the way Christ loved the church — and Christ handed Himself over.

Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the assembly, and gave himself up for it;— World English Bible (public domain)

Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it;— King James Version (public domain)

1. The Text and Its Words

Paul has just named the wife’s submission and the husband’s headship (5:22–24). Then he turns to the man, and he does not say “rule well.” He says love, and he nails the word to the cross.

The measure is not her loveliness, his mood, or whether the week was fair. The measure is Christ. And the Christ in view is not a mascot of warmth. He is the Christ who gave Himself.

  • ἀνήρ anēr · “Husbands” · Strong’s G435

    The Greek noun anēr names an adult male, and often a husband in particular. It is more specific than anthrōpos (G444), which can mean a human being of either sex.

    This sentence is aimed at men in the house, not at a unisex audience that can trade places at will. Aner is an adult male, and often a husband in particular — more specific than anthropos.

  • ἀγαπάω agapaō · “love” · Strong’s G25

    The Greek verb agapaō means to love — not as a passing feeling, but as a settled will to seek another’s good. In the New Testament it names the Father’s giving of the Son, the Son’s self-giving for His people, and the commanded love of God, neighbor, and wife.

    The verb is imperative. A husband does not wait to feel this. He is ordered into it. Agapao names the Father’s giving of the Son, the Son’s self-giving, and the commanded love of God, neighbor, and wife.

  • ἐκκλησία ekklēsia · “the church” · Strong’s G1577

    From ek (out of) + kaleō (to call) — the 'called-out ones,' an assembly or gathering. In secular Greek, it was a political assembly of citizens.

    Christ did not give Himself for an institution’s brand. He gave Himself for a people He means to present holy.

  • παραδίδωμι Paradidōmi · “gave himself” · Strong’s G3860

    The Greek verb paradidōmi means to hand over, deliver up, entrust, or betray — to pass something from one person's hands to another's. The prefix para- (alongside/over) + didōmi (to give) creates the sense of a deliberate handoff.

    The same verb names Judas handing Jesus over, and Jesus handing Himself over. The husband’s pattern is not vague sacrifice. It is self-delivery. He puts himself in her place at cost.

    Verses 26–27 keep the aim: that He might sanctify her, cleanse her by the word, and present her glorious, without spot. Headship that will not move toward her holiness is not this verse. It is vanity with a title.

2. Where It Sits

Ephesians 5 opens with imitating God and walking in love as Christ gave Himself (5:1–2). Then it names the uncleanness that must not even be mentioned among saints (5:3–14). Then wise walking, Spirit-filling, and mutual submission in the fear of God (5:15–21). Then the household: wives, husbands, children, fathers, slaves, masters.

So 5:25 is not a standalone marriage poster. It is the cross applied to the man’s side of a structured house. Verse 23 has already said the husband is head of the wife as Christ is head of the church. Verse 25 tells him what that headship costs. Verse 28–29 tell him to nourish and cherish her as his own body. Verse 32 says the mystery is Christ and the church.

If you cut 5:22–24 away to make 5:25 safer, you have not protected the wife. You have removed the frame Paul used. If you keep 5:22–24 and ignore 5:25, you have not protected the husband’s authority. You have turned it into a throne with no cross.

3. What the Witnesses Saw

Matthew Henry’s chapter head on Ephesians 5, checked this turn at CCEL, at least names the movement: mutual love, then a war on uncleanness, then the conscientious discharge of relative duties from verse 21 on. That is the right map even without a longer verbatim paragraph. The verse sits inside relative duty, not inside romance as a private feeling.

The Reformed instinct is the same as the HELP1 / EGAL1 lock already live on this site: the house has a head, and the head’s love is cruciform, not menial and not co-head. Christ does not cease to be head by dying for the church. He proves the kind of head He is.

I am not hanging a longer Henry block-quote this turn; the CCEL page gave the chapter outline cleanly and not a verse-25 paragraph I could certify word for word.

4. Confessional Anchor

Marriage was ordained for the mutual help of husband and wife, for the increase of mankind with a legitimate issue, and the preventing of uncleanness.

1689 LBCF, ch. 25 — Of Marriage

Mutual help is not interchangeable office. Chapter 25 still names one man and one woman, and it will not let marriage become a stage for uncleanness. Ephesians 5:25 is how the husband keeps that ordinance: he helps her by giving himself, not by making her carry the house alone while he keeps his hobbies.

to be the Mediator between God and man; the prophet, priest, and king; head and savior of the church, the heir of all things, and judge of the world

1689 LBCF, ch. 8 — Of Christ the Mediator

That is the pattern Paul will not let the husband forget. Headship and self-giving are the same Christ.

5. For the Man Reading This

If you are a husband, this verse does not ask whether she has been easy. It asks whether you have handed yourself over.

Name the cost this week in one concrete place: your time, your body, your money, your attention, your repentance. Agapao that is only verbal is a man quoting Paul at a woman he will not die for in the small hours.

Do not use “head” as a shield for passivity or for tyranny. Christ the Head washed His people and aims at their holiness. A man who will not correct, will not nourish, will not confess, and will not stay has not loved as Christ loved the church. He has loved his own peace.

And do not hide in the crowd of “we all love each other.” The sentence has a subject. Husbands.
