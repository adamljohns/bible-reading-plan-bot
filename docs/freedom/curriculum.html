12-Week Program

The Curriculum

Each week is structured, purposeful, and builds on the last. This is a march — not a sprint.

Overview

How the Weeks Are Structured

The 12-week curriculum is divided into three phases: Foundation (Weeks 1–4), Formation (Weeks 5–9), and Commissioning (Weeks 10–12). Each session includes Scripture study, teaching, group discussion, and accountability reporting.

Session content below is drafted (in the Week 1–2 teaching voice) for Adam Johns to review and refine before teaching.

Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–4)

Getting honest, getting grounded
Week 1

Honesty & Assessment

Where are you, really? This week is about dropping the mask. Each man gives an honest account of where he is in the battle and what he's hoping to find in this group.
Key Scripture: Psalm 32:3–5 · James 5:16

Core Principle: The High Cost of Hiddenness

King David hid his sin with Bathsheba for nearly a year. He describes the experience in Psalm 32: "When I kept silent, my bones wasted away through my groaning all day long. For day and night your hand was heavy upon me; my strength was sapped as in the heat of summer." Hiding sin is exhausting. It isolates us, drains our spiritual vitality, and puts us in a prison of our own making. The first step to freedom is acknowledging the cost of that prison.

Key Action: Honest Assessment

This week is about dropping the mask. Before we can get to where we need to be, we must be brutally honest about where we are. This isn't about shame; it's about diagnosis. We will conduct a thorough, confidential self-assessment covering behavior, thought patterns, triggers, and the history of the struggle.

Discussion & Reflection

  • What has been the "cost of hiddenness" in your own life (spiritually, relationally, mentally)?
  • Read James 5:16. What is the connection between confession, prayer, and healing?
  • What are your biggest fears or hesitations about being completely honest in this group?
Week 2

The Biology of Brokenness

Understanding how pornography and sexual sin rewire the brain — and how neuroplasticity makes recovery possible. This is not an excuse. It is intelligence for the battle.
Key Scripture: Romans 12:2 · 2 Corinthians 10:5

Core Principle: Rewiring the Brain for Righteousness

Pornography and sexual sin are not just spiritual problems; they are neurological. Every time we indulge, we reinforce neural pathways that make the behavior more automatic and harder to resist. But the brain is not fixed. Neuroplasticity, the brain's ability to reorganize itself, means we can, through disciplined practice and the power of the Holy Spirit, form new pathways. This is the science behind what Paul commands in Romans 12:2: "Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind."

Key Action: Taking Thoughts Captive

Freedom is won or lost in the mind long before any physical action is taken. We fight by "taking every thought captive to obey Christ" (2 Corinthians 10:5). This week, we introduce practical, moment-by-moment strategies to identify and reject destructive thought patterns and replace them with truth from Scripture. This is not positive thinking; it is active spiritual warfare for the mind.

Discussion & Reflection

  • In what specific ways have you been "conformed to the pattern of this world" in your thought life?
  • What does it practically look like to "take a thought captive"? Share a recent example.
  • What are 1-2 key Scriptures you can use as weapons to replace common tempting thoughts?
Week 3

Biblical Foundation for Purity

What does God actually say about the body, sexuality, and purity? Building a robust theology — not a shame-based rulebook but a dignifying vision of what God intended.
Key Scripture: 1 Corinthians 6:18–20 · Matthew 5:27–30 · Hebrews 13:4

Core Principle: The Dignity of the Body

God’s vision of purity is not a shame-based rulebook — it is a dignifying vision of what the body is for. Paul says the body is "a temple of the Holy Spirit," bought at a price, so we glorify God with it (1 Corinthians 6:18–20). Jesus raises the standard by locating adultery in the heart and the eyes (Matthew 5:27–30), and Hebrews 13:4 declares the marriage bed honorable. God is not against your sexuality; He is for it, rightly ordered. Purity is stewardship of something holy, not deprivation.

Key Action: Trade the Rulebook for a Vision

This week, replace "I can’t" with "I belong to Another." Write out a short personal theology of the body: who owns it, what it is for, and why purity dignifies rather than diminishes a man. A vision you are fighting for sustains far longer than a rule you are fighting under.

Discussion & Reflection

  • Have you related to purity mostly as a rulebook or as a vision? What changes when the body is a temple rather than a battleground?
  • Matthew 5:27–30 raises the bar to the heart — does that strike you as bad news or good news, and why?
  • How does knowing God is FOR your sexuality (rightly ordered) reframe the way you fight?

Phase 2 — Formation (Weeks 5–9)

Building the structures that sustain freedom
Week 5

Accountability Structures

The Cord of Three — what real accountability looks like in practice. Setting up accountability partners, daily check-ins, and the tools that make it sustainable.
Key Scripture: Ecclesiastes 4:9–12 · Galatians 6:1–2

Core Principle: The Cord of Three Strands

Isolation is the enemy’s strategy; a man alone is a man exposed. "Two are better than one... a cord of three strands is not quickly broken" (Ecclesiastes 4:9–12) — and the third strand is Christ. Galatians 6:1–2 shows the posture: restore one another gently and bear one another’s burdens. Accountability is not surveillance; it is brotherhood with skin in the game.

Key Action: Build the Structure

Name a Battle Buddy this week and set a daily check-in — a simple, honest text ("eyes clean / walked in truth / one win, one struggle"). Agree in advance what a relapse report sounds like, so confession has a channel before it is ever needed. Structure beats willpower.

Discussion & Reflection

  • Where has isolation cost you in this battle?
  • What makes accountability feel safe rather than shaming — and how do we keep ours in the Galatians 6:1 lane of gentleness?
  • What specific daily check-in will you commit to this week, and with whom?
Week 6

Identity in Christ

You are not your sin. This week we go deep into who God says you are — and why that identity must precede any lasting behavioral change. The man who knows who he is fights differently.
Key Scripture: 2 Corinthians 5:17 · Ephesians 2:10 · Romans 8:1

Core Principle: You Are Not Your Sin

Behavior flows from identity, not the other way around. In Christ you are a new creation — "the old has passed away; behold, the new has come" (2 Corinthians 5:17). You are God’s workmanship, created for good works (Ephesians 2:10), and "there is therefore now no condemnation" (Romans 8:1). The man who knows who he is fights from victory, not merely for it.

Key Action: Rename Yourself in the Truth

List the labels your sin has tried to make your name (failure, pervert, hypocrite). Next to each, write the verse that overturns it. Then rehearse the true name daily — out loud if you can. The accuser works by labels; the Father works by adoption.

Discussion & Reflection

  • What labels has your sin tried to turn into your identity?
  • Romans 8:1 — do you actually live like there is "no condemnation," or do you still carry the sentence?
  • How would fighting from victory instead of for victory change this coming week?
Week 7

Wounds, Shame & the Root System

Most sexual sin is rooted in something deeper — early exposure, trauma, neglect, or shame. This week we go to the root with grace and Scripture.
Key Scripture: Isaiah 53:4–5 · Psalm 147:3 · Revelation 12:10–11

Core Principle: Go to the Root

Most sexual sin is a symptom watering a deeper root — an old wound, trauma, neglect, or shame. Christ "took up our pain and bore our suffering" (Isaiah 53:4–5); He "heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds" (Psalm 147:3). And the accuser is overcome "by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (Revelation 12:10–11). Shame dies in the light.

Key Action: Trace One Root

Identify a recurring trigger and ask honestly: what wound or unmet need does this behavior medicate? Bring that one root into the light with the group. Testimony spoken aloud strips the accuser of his leverage; secrecy is the only soil shame can grow in.

Discussion & Reflection

  • What wound or unmet need does your sin most often try to medicate?
  • Shame says "I am bad"; guilt says "I did bad" — which voice runs your inner dialogue, and what does it cost you?
  • Revelation 12:11 — what testimony, spoken aloud, would take power away from your shame?
Week 8

Marriage, Relationships & the Stakes

Sexual sin has a cost — and a reversal has a reward. Whether married or single, this week addresses the relational stakes and what purity makes possible.
Key Scripture: Ephesians 5:25–33 · Proverbs 5:15–19 · 1 Thessalonians 4:3–5

Core Principle: Purity Has a Reward, Not Just a Cost

Sexual sin steals from real intimacy; purity is what makes covenant love possible. Husbands are called to love "as Christ loved the church" (Ephesians 5:25–33). Proverbs 5:15–19 commands a man to "rejoice in the wife of your youth" — fidelity framed as joy, not mere duty. And sanctification means each man learning "to control his own body in holiness and honor" (1 Thessalonians 4:3–5). Married or single, purity protects the covenant you are in or the one you are being prepared for.

Key Action: Count Both Ledgers

Name what sin has already cost — trust, presence, desire, peace — and name what its reversal makes possible. Then take one concrete step: if married, a deliberate act of pursuit toward your wife this week; if single, one boundary that honors a future spouse you have not yet met.

Discussion & Reflection

  • What has sexual sin actually cost your closest relationships?
  • Proverbs 5 frames fidelity as delight, not just duty — do you truly believe purity leads to more joy, or less?
  • What is one concrete step toward covenant intimacy (if married) or covenant readiness (if single) this week?
Week 9

Practical Tools for Ongoing Battle

Covenant Eyes, device filters, accountability apps, prayer rhythms, Scripture memorization — the practical technology of freedom. Not crutches. Weapons.
Key Scripture: Ephesians 6:10–18 · Psalm 119:11

Core Principle: Weapons, Not Crutches

Freedom is sustained by "the full armor of God" — truth, righteousness, faith, and the sword of the Spirit, the Word of God (Ephesians 6:10–18) — and by Scripture hidden in the heart: "I have stored up your word in my heart, that I might not sin against you" (Psalm 119:11). Filters, Covenant Eyes, and prayer rhythms are not admissions of weakness. They are a soldier’s standard kit.

Key Action: Build Your Kit

Install or verify accountability software, set real device boundaries, choose three "battle verses" to memorize, and fix a daily prayer rhythm. Put the armor on before the attack, not during it. A weapon you reach for mid-ambush is a weapon you never trained with.

Discussion & Reflection

  • Which piece of the armor (Ephesians 6) is currently missing from your daily life?
  • Do you treat tools like filters as crutches (shameful) or as weapons (wise) — and where did that belief come from?
  • What one or two verses will you memorize as frontline weapons this week?

Phase 3 — Commissioning (Weeks 10–12)

From healed to equipped
Week 10

Confession, Forgiveness & Restoration

How does a man receive forgiveness he doesn't feel he deserves? And how does he extend it to himself? The mechanics of grace, applied specifically to sexual sin.
Key Scripture: 1 John 1:9 · Micah 7:19 · Psalm 103:12

Core Principle: Grace Deeper Than the Sin

God "is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). He "will tread our sins underfoot and hurl all our iniquities into the depths of the sea" (Micah 7:19), removing them "as far as the east is from the west" (Psalm 103:12). The question is rarely whether God forgives — it is whether a man will receive it and stop re-litigating a case the Judge has already closed.

Key Action: Receive and Release

Confess specifically, then practice accepted forgiveness: when the memory rises to accuse you, answer it with the verdict already rendered. Then extend to yourself the grace God has already extended. Self-condemnation is not humility; it is unbelief in the cross.

Discussion & Reflection

  • Is it harder for you to believe God forgives you, or to forgive yourself — and why?
  • Psalm 103:12 / Micah 7:19 — do you live as a man whose sin is in the depths of the sea, or do you keep diving for it?
  • Whose forgiveness — God’s, a spouse’s, or your own — do you most need to receive this week?
Week 11

Becoming a Man Who Leads

Sexual integrity is not the ceiling — it is the floor of masculine leadership. A man who has won this battle leads with different authority in his home, church, and community.
Key Scripture: Joshua 24:15 · 1 Timothy 3:1–5 · Proverbs 20:7

Core Principle: Integrity Is the Floor, Not the Ceiling

Sexual integrity is not the finish line of the Christian life — it is the foundation of a man’s leadership. "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15). The elder qualifications are about tested character, not flawless perfection (1 Timothy 3:1–5). And "the righteous who walks in his integrity — blessed are his children after him" (Proverbs 20:7). A battle won becomes a generational blessing.

Key Action: Lead Something This Week

Take one arena — your home, your marriage, a younger man — and exercise the authority your integrity has earned. Freedom is given for commissioning, not just for comfort. The point of being free is to lead others toward freedom.

Discussion & Reflection

  • Where has this battle held back your leadership at home, church, or work?
  • Proverbs 20:7 ties your integrity to your children’s blessing — how does that raise the stakes for you?
  • What is one place God is calling you to step up and lead, now that you are walking in freedom?
Week 12

Commissioning & Ongoing Brotherhood

The group ends. The brotherhood continues. Each man receives a charge and commits to ongoing accountability. Some men will go on to lead future groups.
Key Scripture: 2 Timothy 2:2 · Philippians 3:13–14

Core Principle: The Group Ends; the Brotherhood Continues

What you have received, you now entrust to others: "what you have heard from me... entrust to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2). And you keep pressing on — "forgetting what lies behind and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal" (Philippians 3:13–14). Freedom maintained is freedom multiplied.

Key Action: Commit and Commission

Define your ongoing accountability beyond this group — specifically, with names and rhythms. Write a personal charge for the next season, and prayerfully consider whether God is calling you to help lead a future cohort. Then sign the continuation covenant with the brothers.

Discussion & Reflection

  • What is your concrete plan for accountability after this group ends?
  • Philippians 3:13 — what is the "what lies behind" you must keep forgetting in order to press forward?
  • Who is one man you could walk with next, passing on what you have received (2 Timothy 2:2)?
