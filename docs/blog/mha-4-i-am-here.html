“I Am Here!”

Courage that calms a house, and the father who refuses to bring his fear home.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So Gideon and I were back on the couch, another episode of that hero show, and I watched the number one hero do the thing he always does. A disaster is unfolding, people are screaming, and he does not sprint in scowling and tense. He steps into the frame already smiling, plants his feet, and announces that he has arrived. And you can see the crowd change. Shoulders drop. Panic drains out of the street. He has not thrown a single punch yet, and the rescue has already started.

I paused it. You know by now that when I pause the show, something is about to become a thing. To your credit, you did not roll your eyes.

Because here is what got me, Son. His arrival is the rescue. Before the fight, before the strength, before any villain gets touched, the fear leaves the block simply because a strong man walked in calm. The writers built the whole hero around one idea: a man whose steady face is a public service. They call him the Symbol of Peace. People stop being afraid just by seeing him show up unafraid.

Read that twice, because that is a job description, and it has your last name on it.

Your Face Is Weather in Your Home

I have taught you the Family Captain stuff since you were small: the captain sets the weather on the ship. A crew reads the captain's face before they read the sky. If he is white-knuckled at the wheel, the whole deck tightens; if he is steady, they work. Nobody voted on it. It just travels down from the man in command.

Your house is the same. Your mother, your brothers, your little sister, they take the emotional temperature of the whole home off the man who walks in the door. If Dad comes home carrying the day like a live grenade, the house learns to flinch. If Dad comes home steady, the house learns to breathe. That is not a personality trait, Son; it is a stewardship. The anxious man makes an anxious house without saying a word.

The hero on the screen is a shadow of a real and better thing. He calms a street by showing up unafraid; a godly father calms a home the same way. But here is where the cartoon runs out of gas, and where Scripture keeps going: All Might can only fake it for a few hours before his real, broken body gives out. His calm is a performance on an empty tank. Yours cannot be. A Christian man's steadiness is not a mask he wears until he collapses; it is drawn from a well he did not dig.

“Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous! Do not tremble or be dismayed — for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go."” (Joshua 1:9)

Notice the LORD does not command Joshua to feel brave. He commands him to be brave, and He anchors the command in a fact: I am with you. Courage in Scripture is not a mood you summon; it is a response to a Person who is present. That is why a believing father can be steady when the numbers are bad, when the diagnosis is grim, when the checking account is thin. His calm is not denial. It is trust with its boots on.

The Non-Anxious Man

There is a phrase I keep coming back to for this: the non-anxious presence. It does not mean the man who feels nothing. It means the man who is not run by what he feels. The world is full of reactive men, men who chase every complaint, who match their wife's stress and raise it, who let the loudest emotion in the room drive the truck. That is not leadership. That is being a weathervane.

A lighthouse does not chase the storm; it holds its ground and gives light so everyone else can find theirs. I have told you the difference between a lighthouse and a laser: a laser burns whatever it is pointed at, a lighthouse steadies everyone in range. When the house is anxious, a laser goes looking for who to blame; a lighthouse just stands there and shines until the panic finds its bearings. Do not chase the complaint, Son. Anchor. Be the fixed point the family can steer by.

“Fear not, for I am with you; be not dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you, yes, I will help you, I will uphold you with My righteous right hand.’” (Isaiah 41:10)

Count the promises in that one verse. I am with you. I am your God. I will strengthen. I will help. I will uphold. That is not a pep talk; that is a supply line. The reason a Christian man can carry a calm face into a hard room is that the calm is not manufactured behind the face. It is delivered to him. He is upheld before he holds anyone else.

The captain sets the weather.

Where the Non-Anxious Heart Comes From

Now, let me be a careful pastor here and not just a hype man. You cannot white-knuckle your way to a steady heart. Anybody who has tried to decide to stop being anxious knows how well that works; it lasts about four minutes. So where does the settled heart actually come from?

It comes from what you believe about who is running the world. Look at the man in Psalm 112:

“He will not be afraid of evil tidings; his heart is steadfast, trusting in the LORD.” (Psalm 112:7)

Look at the order, because the order is everything. He is not first unafraid and then trusting. He is first trusting, and therefore unafraid. His heart is firm, fixed, established, because it is leaning its whole weight on the LORD. His steadiness is downstream of his theology. Bad news comes, the same bad news that would flatten another man, and he does not fall apart, not because he is tougher, but because he knows Who holds the news.

This is why I keep hammering the doctrine of providence with you, Son. Providence is not a dusty word; it is the ground you stand on when the floor drops. Nothing reaches your family that did not first pass through the hands of a Father who is good, and wise, and in complete control down to the smallest sparrow. The anxious man secretly believes the world is unsupervised. The steady man knows better. He is not afraid of bad news because he is not afraid of the One who governs it.

And here is the part that separates our house from the cartoon for good. The Symbol of Peace has to be the strong one; if he cracks, the city panics. You do not have to be the strongest one in your home. You just have to be tethered to Him. Your job is not to be the source of the peace; your job is to be the pipe that carries it. That is a lighter load than the world puts on men, and it is the only one that will hold under real weight.

“For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind.” (2 Timothy 1:7)

Fear is a spirit, and it is not the one God gave you. He gave you power, so you can act. He gave you love, so the action bends toward your people and not toward saving face. And He gave you a sound mind, a governed mind, so that when the room gets loud, the loudest thing is not your own panic. A father with a sound mind is a gift to everyone downwind of him.

Make Peace, Do Not Just Keep It

Let me draw a line I do not want you to miss. There is a coward's version of calm that looks like the real thing from a distance. It is the man who keeps peace by avoiding everything, who never raises the hard subject, who mistakes silence for steadiness. That is not a non-anxious man; that is a checked-out one. He is not calm; he is absent.

I do not merely keep peace in my home; I aim to make it. Keeping peace is passive; it just wants the noise to stop. Making peace is active; it walks toward the hard thing on purpose, the way a hero walks toward the fire, and it settles the room by being present in it, not by hiding from it. The blessed are the peacemakers, not the peace-avoiders (Matthew 5:9). The steady man engages the storm; he does not just wait for it to pass and call the waiting maturity.

So when your brother is melting down, when your mother is carrying something heavy, when the news is bad, you do not go quiet and disappear into your phone. You show up. You plant your feet. You bring the calm into the room instead of leaving to protect your own. That is the whole difference between a hireling and a son, and you already know which one I am raising you to be.

Hold the Line

The world will tell you a man's job is to be intense, reactive, always on edge, that his stress proves he cares. The screen gives you a hero whose calm runs on an empty tank and a few borrowed hours.

Scripture gives you something far better: a Father who is with you wherever you go, who upholds you with His own right hand, who has not given you a spirit of fear. Draw from that well, and then go be the fixed point your house steers by. Set the weather. Bring the calm. Show up unafraid because Someone unshakeable is standing behind you.

Stand your post. Hold the line. Be here.

Peace,
Dad

Father & Son Talk

Three questions for the two of us, on the couch, phones down:

  1. When I walk in the door at the end of a hard day, what does my face usually tell the house? What would you want it to tell the house?
  2. What is one piece of bad news that has knocked you off your feet lately, and where were you actually placing your trust when it hit?
  3. Can you tell the difference between a man who keeps peace by hiding and a man who makes peace by showing up? Where do you tend to hide?
⚔ Today's Quest

One action: The next time the house gets tense today, do not react to it, anchor it. Lower your voice, slow down, plant your feet, and be the calmest person in the room on purpose. Set the weather instead of catching it.

Read together: Psalm 112:7, and then the whole psalm. Notice how the steady man got that way.

What We Confess

Our steadiness is not self-generated; it rests on the God who governs everything. The Confession puts it plainly:

“God the good Creator of all things, in His infinite power and wisdom, does uphold, direct, dispose, and govern all creatures and things, from the greatest even to the least, by His most wise and holy providence.”

1689 London Baptist Confession, ch. 5 (Of Divine Providence), 5.1

And the catechism trains a boy to say it back:

Q. What are God's works of providence?
A. God's works of providence are His most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all His creatures, and all their actions.

This is why a believing man can carry a calm face into a hard room: God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble (Psalm 46:1). The steady heart is not brave in the dark; it simply knows Who is holding the room.

A note to Gideon

Son, you have seen me get this wrong. You have seen me bring the day home like a loaded pack and drop the weight of it on the whole house. I am still learning to be the man I am writing to you about. But do not miss the point because your dad is a work in progress: the goal was never for me to be strong enough on my own. It was never for you to be strong enough on your own either. The goal is to be tethered to the One who is, so that when the hard news comes, and it will, your heart is already firm because it was already trusting. Be the fixed point your house steers by. Not because you are unshakeable, but because He is. I love you. Now go.

— Dad
