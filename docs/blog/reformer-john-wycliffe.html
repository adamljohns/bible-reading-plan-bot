John Wycliffe: The Morning Star Was Not Yet the Dawn

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

John Wycliffe (c. 1330–1384) is the Morning Star, not the sunrise. Oxford scholar, Lollard Bible, bones burned in 1428. A forerunner. Not a member of a class of 1517.

Before there was a Reformation to join

A classroom that starts the story in 1517 starts late. John Wycliffe was an English theologian at Oxford, about 1330 to 1384. The MOOP Dictionary calls him the Morning Star of the Reformation because he held, more than a century early, lines the sixteenth century would have to recover: Scripture above popes and councils, the church as the body of the elect rather than the Roman ladder, rejection of transubstantiation, rejection of the pope’s temporal claims, and the conviction that a Christian ought to be able to read the Bible in his own tongue.

He and the Lollards produced the first complete English Bible in the 1380s, hand-copied from the Latin Vulgate, smuggled, illegal. That is not yet Tyndale’s Greek. It is still a man putting the Book where a plowman could reach it. Do not promote him into 1517 to make the set neat. He died in his bed in 1384. The fight was not finished.

They could burn the bones. They could not chain the Word.

The dictionary records what his enemies did next. The Council of Constance in 1415 declared him a heretic, ordered his books burned, and decreed that his bones be dug up. In 1428 the order was carried out at Lutterworth. The remains were burned and thrown into the River Swift. The dictionary quotes the chronicler Thomas Fuller: the Swift carried the ashes into the Avon, the Avon into the Severn, the Severn into the sea, and the sea into the ocean, so that the ashes became an emblem of a doctrine dispersed. I am not holding Fuller’s manuscript. I am quoting the dictionary’s record of him. The doctrine does not need the poetry to be true.

Isaiah 40:8: “The grass withers, the flower fades, But the word of our God stands forever.”

2 Timothy 2:9: “for which I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; but the word of God is not chained.”

The dictionary also records his last written words as “I believe in the end the truth will conquer.” Treat that as the dictionary’s report, not as a tape. The substance under it is older than Wycliffe. The grass withers. The Word does not.

A forerunner is not a founder. Honor him without stuffing him into a roll he never joined.

So what

Men like a closed team. Wycliffe will not sit on it. He prepared a road he did not live to walk. If you only honor the men who got the credit in the textbook, you will miss the brother who handed you the Book before your century began. Read him as a witness, not as a mascot. And do not confuse a later Bible-translation society that borrowed his name with the fourteenth-century priest. The dictionary mentions that society. This post does not audit it.

2 Timothy 3:16-17: “All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work.”

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

  1. John Wycliffe — forerunner
  2. Jan Hus — forerunner

  5. Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich
  6. Heinrich Bullinger

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
