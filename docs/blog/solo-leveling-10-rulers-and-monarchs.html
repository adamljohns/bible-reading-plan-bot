Rulers and Monarchs
The War Behind the War

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 2026

Last time we talked about the weight Jinwoo carries that nobody can see. This week the camera pulls way back, and we find out the weight was never just personal. There is a war on, and it is far bigger than he knew.

For a long time the show let us think the conflict was simple: gates open, monsters come out, hunters go in, people get paid, families get fed. That is the surface. But as the story widens, the curtain lifts on something underneath all of it, a cosmic war between two ancient sides, the Rulers and the Monarchs. The gates, the dungeons, the awakenings, even Jinwoo himself, none of it is random. It is all moves in a conflict that started long before any human ever picked up a weapon, and most of humanity is walking around with no idea the real battle is even happening over their heads.

Jinwoo thought he was leveling up to protect his family. He was. But he gets pulled into a battle he did not start, on a battlefield he could not see, with stakes he never signed up for.

Son, that is not just good worldbuilding. That is the truest thing the show has said yet, and it is describing the world you actually live in.

The War You Were Born Into

Here is what I most want you to hear at nineteen: you live in a world at war, and the deepest front of that war is invisible. Most men go their whole lives like the background characters in the show, arguing about money and status and which gate pays best, never once looking up to see that there are powers and a battle and a real enemy who hates them. The Bible refuses to let you live that blind.

"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places." (Ephesians 6:12)

Read that again. Rulers of the darkness. The same words the show borrows for its big reveal, Scripture used first, and meant literally. The man who cut you off in traffic is not your real enemy; the brother who hurt you is not your real enemy; even the version of yourself you are most ashamed of is not the root of it. There is a real enemy, a real dark kingdom, and it is far more interested in your soul than in your circumstances.

The Bible does not tell you to win the war; it tells you to stand. Most of the fight is refusing to be moved off the truth.

The Word

God does not reveal the war to scare you; He reveals it to arm you. Notice the very next thing Paul does after naming the powers, he hands you a kit and tells you to suit up.

"Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might. Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil." (Ephesians 6:10,11)

"Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God... Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness... taking the shield of faith... the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God: praying always." (Ephesians 6:13–18)

And do not miss how the war is actually fought. It is not mostly swords and dramatic poses. It is fought in the mind, over what you believe, by pulling down the lies that set themselves up against God.

"For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds; casting down imaginations... and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ." (2 Corinthians 10:3–5)

"Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: whom resist stedfast in the faith." (1 Peter 5:8,9)

The Monarch Who Already Lost

Now here is where the show is only a shadow, and where the gospel is so much better than the story. In Solo Leveling the outcome of the war is still in doubt; the sides are still grinding it out; nobody knows for certain how it ends. That is great for tension. It is terrible theology, and it would be a miserable way to actually live.

Because the real war is not undecided. The decisive battle has already been fought, and the enemy already lost, on a hill outside Jerusalem, where it looked for all the world like the darkness had won.

"And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it." (Colossians 2:15)

Look at the language. The same powers Paul told you to fight, Christ has already stripped, already disarmed, already paraded as a defeated enemy. The cross was not Jesus losing the war; it was the killing blow. You are not fighting to see who wins. You are a soldier in a kingdom whose King has already won, mopping up an enemy who knows he is finished and is furious about it. That changes everything about how a man stands. You do not fight for the victory; you fight from it.

It is called the armour of God for a reason. You do not forge it; He hands it to you. Your job is to put it on.

What We Confess

This is the part of Christ's work our forefathers called His kingly office, and it is exactly the comfort a young man in a war needs:

Q. How doth Christ execute the office of a king?
A. In subduing us to Himself, in ruling and defending us, and in restraining and conquering all His and our enemies. (Baptist Catechism)

Ruling you, defending you, conquering your enemies. That is not a hired bodyguard; that is your King taking personal responsibility for the war you are in. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 8, "Of Christ the Mediator") confesses that the Father gave Christ to be the Head and Saviour of His people and the conqueror of their enemies. So when the lion roars at you, you are not standing alone in the gate. The strongest Monarch who has ever existed, or ever will, has already claimed you and already won. Your standing firm is just trusting a victory that is older than your fear.

Father & Son Talk

  • Most people live like the background characters, never looking up to see the real war. Where in your own life have you been treating a "flesh and blood" enemy as the real problem, when the real fight is deeper?
  • Paul lists the armor piece by piece. Which piece do you think you go out the door without most days, the truth, faith, the word, prayer?
  • The show keeps the outcome in doubt; the gospel says the enemy already lost. How would your week look different if you really believed you fight from victory and not for it?

⚔ Today's Quest

A soldier does not wait for the war to feel real before he gets dressed. So today, name one real battlefield in your life, one lie you keep believing or one temptation that keeps winning. Then put on one piece of armor: out loud, speak the truth from Scripture that answers that exact lie. If you have a minute first, read Ephesians 6:14 and gird up with truth. That is the whole quest.

Gideon, you were born onto a battlefield, but you were born on the winning side. Stand up, put the armor on, and stop fighting like the outcome is still in question; your King settled it before you ever drew a breath.

"For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers..." (Ephesians 6:12)
