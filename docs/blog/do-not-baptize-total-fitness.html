Do Not Baptize Total Fitness

MCA is right about Marines. Meaning is not the Holy Spirit.

By Adam L. Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries

A brother sent me Marine Corps Total Fitness from the Gazette (Col Eric J. “Chewy” Keith, SgtMaj Emmanuel Hudson, SgtMaj Jacob Reiff, 26 August 2026). Another brother, a Marine general on this staff, looked at a slide in the same conversation and said the quiet part: the doctrine is human; the layout is machine-flavored. Four boxes. Three bullets. Gold on the last word. Officers write in paragraphs. Models tile a brief.

He would not give a percentage. Neither will I. Dash-count is not a court-martial. If the Gazette piece leans on long dashes and repeating cadences, that is a tell, not a verdict. Named Marines signed it. I will argue with what they said.

What they get right

Total fitness is not another rock in the pack. Physical is more than a PFT. Mental fitness is a warfighting requirement, not a wellness add-on. Kyle Carpenter: survival is refusing to give up, and there is strength in admitting weakness and seeking help. Dakota Meyer: be who you say you are, and live the standard you expect of everyone else. Connection is not softness. Marines who feel connected thrive. A force that knows why it fights will outlast a force that only knows how.

USMC Ministries will not sneer at that. Moral injury is real. Hypocrisy kills a unit. Rent is due every day.

What they must not be allowed to mean

Then the spiritual domain:

Spiritual fitness is not about religion alone; it is about meaning. … Leaders play a critical role in helping their Marines strengthen that centerline, whether through faith, family, nation, or legacy.

That sentence is the whole problem. It is chaplain-corps English for pick a why. Faith or family or nation or legacy. Four norths on one compass.

When they collide, and they will, “why” becomes whatever still feels honorable. Family can break. Nation can shame you. Legacy can be a statue. Faith, if it only means “whatever helps me endure,” is still self.

John 14:6:

Jesus said to him, “I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through Me.”

Acts 4:12:

Nor is there salvation in any other, for there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved.

You cannot baptize MCTF by pouring that water on “meaning.” Baptize, here, does not mean the ordinance. It means splashing Christian language on a good earthly thing until a man thinks he is spiritually alive because he has a strong why. He may only be a well-motivated pagan, or a believer who has swapped Christ for the Corps.

Semper Fi is a covenant with a service. It is not the blood of the covenant. Meyer’s return to the Ready Reserve is gratitude. It is not regeneration.

Sparta in the piece is a training metaphor. Fine. Do not let “polis, phalanx, ancestral line” become your god and call it spiritual fitness. The church is not an agoge.

Two views of the Marine

Limited view (MCA, as written): look out for welfare in four domains so the crisis-response force lasts another 250 years.

Kingdom view (this ministry): know your Marine and care for his eternity. Look out for his present. Do not stop at his present. Philippians 2:19–21 still holds, genuine concern for welfare, and then the man must meet Christ, not a purpose statement.

Gus was right about that hinge. The slide put it in a grid. The article leaves it as meaning. We will not pretend the grid is the gospel, and we will not pretend the article is a revival.

Charge

If you wear the uniform: take MCTF. Pay rent every day. Seek help. Be who you say you are.

If you name the Name: do not let “spiritual fitness” become a fourth PT. Your centerline is a Person. When faith, family, nation, and legacy disagree, Jesus does not share the throne.

MCA trains a spiritually fit force. We train men whose spirit is alive in Christ. Those can sit in the same fire team. They are not the same thing.

Watch Charge: Honor the Corps. Do not baptize the compass. Preach Christ.
