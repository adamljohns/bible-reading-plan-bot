Arise
The Counterfeit Resurrection and the Real One

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 5, 2026
Listen to this post · 24 min
This recording is of an earlier version of this post.

Well, son, here we are at the end of the season. Twelve episodes back a scrawny, mocked E-rank hunter got left for dead in a dungeon, and tonight the story closes with that same man standing over the ground while the dead climb up out of it at his command. We've walked this whole arc together, you and I, and I saved the biggest theme for last, because the finale hands us the deepest picture in the entire show, a picture so close to the gospel that it almost hurts to look at, and just wrong enough that it can teach you what the true thing is. Let me set the scene, and then let's go where the anime can't follow.

The Duel and the Word

The test comes before the power. Jinwoo is made to face the Blood-Red Knight Commander, Igris, a chivalrous, deadly warrior, and the fight goes hard. Then Jinwoo does a strange, honorable thing: he lays his weapon down to fight the knight hand-to-hand, man to man, with no advantage taken, and the knight, being what he is, drops his own sword in answer, so that honor meets honor. In that clean exchange Jinwoo wins, and because he has survived every trial the System declares his class upgraded to something new. From here on he is the Shadow Monarch, and no longer just a leveling hunter.

With the title comes a power called Shadow Extraction, and that power needs a command word, one spoken trigger. The word he chooses is "Arise."

And the ground obeys him. The dead come up: fallen enemies climb out of the earth as shadow soldiers, kneel before him, and fall in as his army, and he learns he can raise a whole host this way. He turns last to the knight he just defeated, Igris, and marks him for extraction. The first attempts fail, and then the last one takes, and the crimson knight rises up out of the dark to kneel before his new monarch. The screen goes black, and season one is over.

It's a stunning image, a man speaking a word to the dead and the dead getting up. If you've read your Bible at all, something in you should sit up straight, because that idea is stolen territory, and no one in a studio made it up. The devil owns no original material, son; he only counterfeits. So let's do the hard, careful thing and ask what that word made.

They come back up, yes, but they come up as shadows: bound, silent, still essentially dead, and now owned by a new master.

Look at What Rises

You have to slow down and look here, because the counterfeit is dazzling and the difference is everything. Jinwoo's "Arise" is real power; I'm not going to pretend it isn't. But watch what it produces. The dead do come up, but they come up as shadows. They aren't restored to their own lives, and they aren't given back their names, their families, their freedom, or their futures. They rise as soldiers, extensions of one man's will, conscripts bound to serve their monarch, and when they kneel it is ownership and not worship. They are, if you look at it squarely, still dead, only dead and now enslaved to a new master. He gave them a leash where they needed life.

That is reanimation with a chain on it, and it is no resurrection, and that, son, is the best a counterfeit can ever do. Every false power in this world, every idol, every ideology, every dark thing that promises to make you more than you are, works the same way: it can raise something, but it can't make anything alive. It pulls the dead up out of the ground only to press them back into service, as extensions of a will that isn't their own. Sin does this. Sin is a monarch too, and its word over you when you were in it was "Arise, my soldier," and never "Arise, my son," and it left you bound, owned, and marching.

And that is the word Scripture uses for what we were before Christ.

“And you were dead in your trespasses and sins —” (Ephesians 2:1, MBT)

The word is dead, which is a different thing from sick, or weak, or underleveled. We were dead, and worse off than any shadow soldier, because we were dead and in bondage to a master and thought we were free the whole time. If the story ended there, the best any of us could hope for is what Igris got, which was to be hauled back up to serve. But the story doesn't end there, because there is Another who speaks to the dead.

There Is Another Who Says Arise

The Shadow Monarch isn't the only one in the universe who talks to corpses. Long before any anime imagined it, the living God walked into rooms full of grief and spoke to the dead, and when His word landed, something happened that no shadow army has ever seen.

He stood outside a sealed tomb where a man had been dead four days, already stinking, and He said, in effect, come out. And Lazarus came out as himself, neither a shadow nor a slave, alive, unwrapped, and handed back to his sisters to go on living his own life.

“When He had said this, He cried out with a loud voice: "Lazarus, come out!"” (John 11:43, MBT)

He took a little dead girl by the hand, a father's whole world lying cold on a bed, and He said to her, in effect, little girl, arise. And she got up and walked, and He told them to get her something to eat, because she was a child and not a soldier, alive again and hungry for breakfast.

“And taking the child by the hand, He said to her, "Talitha kum!" — which translated means, "Little girl, I say to you, arise!"” (Mark 5:41, MBT)

Do you feel the difference, Gideon? When the Shadow Monarch says "Arise," the dead come up as shadows and kneel as property, and when the Lord Jesus says "Arise," the dead come up alive and go home as themselves. The one word makes soldiers and the other Word makes sons. This is the true and better resurrection that the anime is only a shadow of, and I mean "shadow" precisely, because a shadow is a real shape cast by a real body, always pointing back to the thing that is casting it. Jinwoo's shadow army is a shadow of the true Monarch's living family, thrown backward onto a screen, so look past the shape to the Body.

God said it through Ezekiel over a valley of dry bones, a whole nation of the long dead, and what He promised them was breath and life, with no word about reanimating them as a slave-host.

“Thus says the Lord GOD to these bones: 'Behold, I will cause breath to enter you that you may come to life.” (Ezekiel 37:5)

The True Monarch Adopts Sons

Now hear the gospel, because this is the point I've been driving towards for nine weeks. When God spoke over you, dead in your sins, He did not raise you to conscript you. Read the next breath after "dead in trespasses," and mind the two words at the front of it.

“But God, being rich in mercy — because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in our transgressions, made us alive together with Christ. By grace you have been saved.” (Ephesians 2:4–5, MBT)

It is by grace, and not by surviving a trial, or defeating every enemy, or earning an upgrade. Jinwoo becomes a monarch because he passed the test; you become a son because God is rich in mercy and loved you while you were still a corpse. That is the reverse of the anime's engine, and it is the difference between the whole religion of works and the true gospel, because the System rewards the strong and the cross raises the dead who could do nothing but die.

And what does He raise you into? He raises you into a family, and not into a shadow army.

“For you have not received a spirit of slavery, leading to fear again — but you have received a spirit of adoption as sons, by which we cry out, "Abba! Father!"” (Romans 8:15, MBT)

What you received is the Spirit of adoption, and it has nothing in common with a spirit of slavery or a leash. Igris kneels before Jinwoo as owned property; you kneel before Christ and He lifts your chin and calls you son. Shadow soldiers keep their silence and their chains, while adopted sons get a new heart, a new name, and the freedom to cry out "Abba, Father" to the One who raised them. The Shadow Monarch adds units to an army and the true Monarch adds children to a home, because He adopts sons and doesn't conscript shadows.

His word raises the dead all the way to life, and not as shadows: free, named, adopted, walking out of the tomb as sons.

And this next part is meant for you by name, Gideon. If you are in Christ, you have already heard that "Arise" once. The day God made you alive, when you who were dead in trespasses first drew a living breath towards Him, that was the true Monarch speaking the true word over your grave (Ephesians 2:5), and it has already happened. And you will hear it once more, on the last day, when the same voice that called Lazarus out calls every one of His own out of the earth, and this body of yours goes into the ground a corpse and comes up glorified and free.

“Do not be amazed at this, for an hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear His voice and will come out — those who have done good into a resurrection of life, and those who have practiced evil into a resurrection of judgment.” (John 5:28–29, MBT)

“In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet — for the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed.” (1 Corinthians 15:52, MBT)

What We Confess

Our fathers in the faith wrote this hope down carefully, so no son of ours would confuse a counterfeit raising with the true one. On the resurrection of the dead, the 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 31, "Of the State of Man after Death, and of the Resurrection of the Dead") confesses that "the bodies of men after death return to dust... but their souls, which neither die nor sleep, having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God," and that at the last day all the dead shall be raised with the selfsame bodies, "and none other, although with different qualities." That means the selfsame body, made new and incorruptible, and not a shadow of itself.

And on how that life first comes to a dead soul, chapter 10, "Of Effectual Calling," confesses that God calls His elect "out of that state of sin and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and salvation by Jesus Christ," and does it "not from any thing at all foreseen in man, nor from any power or agency in the creature," but by grace alone, quickening and renewing them by His Spirit so that they come "most freely, being made willing by His grace." They come freely, because they have been made willing, and that is a son answering a Father, Gideon, never a shadow dragged up to serve a monarch.

Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the resurrection?
A. At the resurrection, believers, being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged and acquitted in the day of judgment, and made perfectly blessed in the full enjoying of God to all eternity. (Baptist Catechism)

Father & Son Talk

  • Jinwoo's "Arise" raises the dead as shadows, bound to serve him; Christ's "Arise" raises the dead as free, adopted sons. In your own words, what is the real difference between the two, and why does it matter which monarch is speaking over your grave?
  • Every counterfeit power can raise something but can never make anything truly alive. Where in the world around you, or in your own heart, have you been tempted to trust a power that offers strength but hands you a chain?
  • Ephesians 2 says you were dead and God made you alive "by grace," not by passing a trial. How does that cut against the way your generation is told to earn its worth by leveling up? What does it feel like to be given life you could never earn?

Today's Quest

Read Ephesians 2:1–10 slowly, out loud, on your own. As you read, do one thing: circle or note the two words "But God" in verse 4, and thank Him plainly, in your own words, for speaking "Arise" over you when you were dead and could do nothing but lie there. That's the whole quest, son. Receive the life you did not earn.

So we end the season here, Gideon. The last shot is a man raising shadows to serve him, and it is a striking picture, but it is a shadow of the greater thing, and now you can see clean past it. There is a Monarch whose word over the dead makes sons and not soldiers. You have heard His "Arise" once already, in the quiet of your own regeneration, and you will hear it once more, over your own grave, on a morning with no night after it. Live like a son who knows both are true.

“But God, being rich in mercy — because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in our transgressions, made us alive together with Christ. By grace you have been saved.” (Ephesians 2:4–5, MBT)
