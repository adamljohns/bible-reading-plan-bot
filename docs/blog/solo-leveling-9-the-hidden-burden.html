Arise
The Counterfeit Resurrection and the Real One

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

Well, son, here we are at the end of the season. Twelve episodes back a scrawny, mocked E-rank hunter got left for dead in a dungeon, and tonight the story closes with that same man standing over the ground while the dead climb up out of it at his command. We've walked this whole arc together, you and I, and I saved the biggest theme for last, because the finale hands us the deepest picture in the entire show; a picture so close to the gospel that it almost hurts to look at, and just wrong enough that it can teach you exactly what the real thing is.

Let me set the scene, and then let's go where the anime cannot follow.

The Duel and the Word

Before the power comes the test. Jinwoo is made to face the Blood-Red Knight Commander, Igris; a chivalrous, deadly warrior. The fight goes hard. Then Jinwoo does a strange, honorable thing: he lays his weapon down to fight the knight hand-to-hand, man to man, no advantage taken. And the knight, being what he is, drops his own sword in answer. Honor meets honor. In that clean exchange Jinwoo wins, and by surviving every trial the System declares his class upgraded to something new. He is no longer just a leveling hunter. He is the Shadow Monarch.

With the title comes a power called Shadow Extraction, and that power needs a command word; a single spoken trigger. He chooses one word. He says, "Arise."

And the ground obeys him. The dead come up. Fallen enemies climb out of the earth as shadow soldiers, kneel before him, and fall in as his army. He learns he can raise a whole host this way. He turns last to the knight he just defeated, Igris, and marks him for extraction. The first attempts fail. Then the last one takes, and the crimson knight rises up out of the dark to kneel before his new monarch. The screen goes black. Season one is over.

It is a stunning image. A man speaks a word to the dead, and the dead get up. If you have read your Bible at all, something in you should sit up straight, because that is not a made-up idea. That is stolen territory. The devil owns no original material, son; he only counterfeits. So let's do the hard, careful thing and ask: what exactly did that word make?

They come back up, yes. But they come up as shadows: bound, silent, still essentially dead, now owned by a new master.

Look at What Rises

Here is where you have to slow down and look, because the counterfeit is dazzling and the difference is everything. Jinwoo's "Arise" is real power; I'm not going to pretend it isn't. But watch what it produces. The dead do come up, but they come up as shadows. They are not restored to their own lives. They are not given back their names, their families, their freedom, their futures. They rise as soldiers, extensions of one man's will, conscripts bound to serve their monarch. They kneel not in worship but in ownership. They are, if you're honest about it, still dead; just dead and now enslaved to a new master. He didn't give them life. He gave them a leash.

That is not resurrection. That is reanimation with a chain on it. And that, son, is the very best a counterfeit can ever do. Every false power in this world, every idol, every ideology, every dark thing that promises to make you more than you are, works exactly the same way. It can raise something. It cannot make anything alive. It pulls the dead up out of the ground only to press them back into service, extensions of a will that is not their own. Sin does this. Sin is a monarch too, and its word over you when you were in it was not "Arise, my son." It was "Arise, my soldier." Bound. Owned. Marching.

And that is exactly the word Scripture uses for what we were before Christ.

“And you were dead in your trespasses and sins —” (Ephesians 2:1)

Dead. Not sick, not weak, not underleveled. Dead, and worse than any shadow soldier, because we were dead and in bondage to a master and thought we were free the whole time. If the story ended there, the best any of us could hope for is what Igris got: hauled back up to serve. But the story does not end there, because there is Another who speaks to the dead.

There Is Another Who Says Arise

The Shadow Monarch is not the only one in the universe who talks to corpses. Long before any anime imagined it, the living God walked into rooms full of grief and spoke to the dead, and when His word landed, something happened that no shadow army has ever seen.

He stood outside a sealed tomb where a man had been dead four days, already stinking, and He said, in effect, come out. And Lazarus came out; not as a shadow, not as a slave, but as himself, alive, unwrapped, handed back to his sisters to go on living his own life.

“When He had said this, He cried out with a loud voice: "Lazarus, come out!"” (John 11:43)

He took a little dead girl by the hand, a father's whole world lying cold on a bed, and He said to her, in effect, little girl, arise. And she got up and walked, and He told them to get her something to eat, because she was not a soldier, she was a child, alive again and hungry for breakfast.

“And taking the child by the hand, He said to her, "Talitha kum!" — which translated means, "Little girl, I say to you, arise!"” (Mark 5:41)

Do you feel the difference, Gideon? When the Shadow Monarch says "Arise," the dead come up as shadows and kneel as property. When the Lord Jesus says "Arise," the dead come up alive and go home as themselves. One word makes soldiers. The other Word makes sons. This is the true and better resurrection that the anime is only a shadow of; and I mean "shadow" precisely, because a shadow is a real shape cast by a real body, always pointing back to the thing that is casting it. Jinwoo's shadow army is a shadow of the true Monarch's living family, thrown backward onto a screen. Look past the shape to the Body.

God said it through Ezekiel over a valley of dry bones, a whole nation of the long dead, and He did not promise to reanimate them as a slave-host. He promised breath. He promised life.

“Thus says the Lord GOD to these bones: 'Behold, I will cause breath to enter you that you may come to life.” (Ezekiel 37:5)

The True Monarch Adopts Sons

Now hear the gospel, because this is the whole point I've been driving toward for nine weeks. When God spoke over you, dead in your sins, He did not raise you to conscript you. Read the very next breath after "dead in trespasses," and mind the two words at the front of it.

“But God, being rich in mercy — because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in our transgressions, made us alive together with Christ. By grace you have been saved.” (Ephesians 2:4–5)

By grace. Not by surviving a trial. Not by defeating every enemy. Not by earning an upgrade. Jinwoo becomes a monarch because he passed the test; you become a son because God is rich in mercy and loved you while you were still a corpse. That is the exact reverse of the anime's engine, and it is the difference between the whole religion of works and the true gospel. The System rewards the strong. The cross raises the dead who could do nothing but die.

And what does He raise you into? Not a shadow army. A family.

“For you have not received a spirit of slavery, leading to fear again — but you have received a spirit of adoption as sons, by which we cry out, "Abba! Father!"” (Romans 8:15)

Not the spirit of bondage. Not a leash. The Spirit of adoption. Igris kneels before Jinwoo as owned property; you kneel before Christ and He lifts your chin and calls you son. Shadow soldiers keep their silence and their chains. Adopted sons get a new heart, a new name, and the freedom to cry out "Abba, Father" to the very One who raised them. The Shadow Monarch adds units to an army. The true Monarch adds children to a home. He does not conscript shadows. He adopts sons.

His word does not raise shadows. It raises the dead all the way to life; free, named, adopted, walking out of the tomb as sons.

And here is the part meant for you by name, Gideon. If you are in Christ, you have already heard that "Arise" once. The day God made you alive, when you who were dead in trespasses first drew a living breath toward Him, that was the true Monarch speaking the real word over your grave (Ephesians 2:5). That already happened. And you will hear it once more, on the last day, when the same voice that called Lazarus out calls every one of His own out of the earth, and this body of yours goes into the ground a corpse and comes up glorified and free.

“Do not be amazed at this, for an hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear His voice and will come out — those who have done good into a resurrection of life, and those who have practiced evil into a resurrection of judgment.” (John 5:28–29)

“In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet — for the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed.” (1 Corinthians 15:52)

What We Confess

Our fathers in the faith wrote this hope down carefully, so no son of ours would confuse a counterfeit raising with the real one. On the resurrection of the dead, the 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 31, "Of the State of Man after Death, and of the Resurrection of the Dead") confesses that "the bodies of men after death return to dust... but their souls, which neither die nor sleep, having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God," and that at the last day all the dead shall be raised with the selfsame bodies, "and none other, although with different qualities." Not shadows of themselves. The selfsame body, made new and incorruptible.

And on how that life first comes to a dead soul, chapter 10, "Of Effectual Calling," confesses that God calls His elect "out of that state of sin and death, in which they are by nature, to grace and salvation by Jesus Christ," and does it "not from any thing at all foreseen in man, nor from any power or agency in the creature," but by grace alone, quickening and renewing them by His Spirit so that they come "most freely, being made willing by His grace." Freely. Made willing. That is a son answering a Father, Gideon, never a shadow dragged up to serve a monarch.

Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the resurrection?
A. At the resurrection, believers, being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged and acquitted in the day of judgment, and made perfectly blessed in the full enjoying of God to all eternity. (Baptist Catechism)

Father & Son Talk

  • Jinwoo's "Arise" raises the dead as shadows, bound to serve him; Christ's "Arise" raises the dead as free, adopted sons. In your own words, what is the real difference between the two, and why does it matter which monarch is speaking over your grave?
  • Every counterfeit power can raise something but can never make anything truly alive. Where in the world around you, or in your own heart, have you been tempted to trust a power that offers strength but hands you a chain?
  • Ephesians 2 says you were dead and God made you alive "by grace," not by passing a trial. How does that cut against the way your generation is told to earn its worth by leveling up? What does it feel like to be given life you could never earn?

⚔ Today's Quest

Read Ephesians 2:1–10 slowly, out loud, on your own. As you read, do one thing: circle or note the two words "But God" in verse 4, and thank Him plainly, in your own words, for speaking "Arise" over you when you were dead and could do nothing but lie there. That's the whole quest, son. Receive the life you did not earn.

So we end the season here, Gideon. The last shot is a man raising shadows to serve him, and it is a genuinely striking picture; but it is a shadow of the greater thing, and now you can see clean past it. There is a Monarch whose word over the dead does not make soldiers, it makes sons. You have heard His "Arise" once already, in the quiet of your own regeneration. You will hear it once more, over your own grave, on a morning with no night after it. Live like a son who knows both are true.

“But God, being rich in mercy — because of His great love with which He loved us, even when we were dead in our transgressions, made us alive together with Christ. By grace you have been saved.” (Ephesians 2:4–5)
