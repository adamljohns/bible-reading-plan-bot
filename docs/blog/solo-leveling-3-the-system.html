The System
The Daily Quest Nobody Sees

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 29, 2026
Listen to this post · 8 min
This recording is of an earlier version of this post.

Last time, in the Double Dungeon of Episode 2, the weakest hunter laid himself down on that altar and you called him a sacrificial lamb. He walked out of the Cartenon Temple alive, and he walked out changed. This week, in Episode 3, "It's Like a Game," we find out how the change takes hold, and it isn't what most people expect.

Jinwoo wakes up in a hospital bed three days later, his ruined arm and leg somehow whole again, and only he can see it: a glowing blue window floating in the air, like a video-game menu. It's the System, and it is his alone, because his little sister Jinah is standing right there and can't see a thing. And the first thing this System hands him is a chore list, where you'd expect a sword or a power-up. It's a daily quest of 100 pushups, 100 situps, 100 squats, and a 10-kilometer run, every day, and under it, in cold letters, there's a warning: skip the quest, and you get sent to the Penalty Zone.

Jinwoo does what most of us would do the first time; he scoffs at it like a game glitch and ignores it, and that's a big mistake. The System rips him out of his room and drops him into the Penalty Zone, a blistering endless desert, and sends giant sand-worms boiling up out of the dunes to hunt him for hours until he barely survives. He doesn't skip the quest again, having learned the hard way that the daily discipline wasn't a suggestion.

So the weakest hunter alive starts doing pushups, alone in his room, with no one clapping and no one filming. He grinds, he aches, he hates it some days, and he does it again the next morning, with no glory and no crowd, just the reps. And slowly, off-camera, he gets stronger, and it comes from a thousand boring mornings no one saw and not from one heroic moment in a dungeon.

Son, I want you to notice that, because it's one of the truest things this show teaches by accident. The big moment got him chosen, but the daily reps are what made him into something.

The 10-kilometer run has no shortcut and no skip button, just one foot in front of the other on a road no one else is watching.

Growth Comes in Reps, Not Highlights

The lie the world sells you about becoming a man is that it happens in a moment: one big decision, one mountaintop experience, one viral highlight, and you arrive. We love the montage and we skip what the montage is hiding, which is months of unglamorous, repetitive work when no one is watching.

God changes a man in reps and not in a montage. The Bible has a word for the slow, daily, lifelong process of being made more like Christ, and the word is sanctification, and when Paul wants to teach Timothy how it works, he reaches for the language of the gym, the same language the System speaks.

“But have nothing to do with worldly fables fit only for old women. On the other hand, train yourself for the purpose of godliness. For bodily training is of little profit — but godliness is profitable for all things, since it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come.” (1 Timothy 4:7,8, MBT)

“Train yourself” is a gym word, a training word. You don't get spiritually strong by accident any more than you get physically strong by accident; you train, and you do the reps. Paul ran his own life that way, and he wasn't gentle about it.

“Do you not know that those who run in a race all run, but only one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may win. Everyone who competes in the games exercises self-control in all things. They do it to receive a perishable wreath — but we, an imperishable. Therefore I run in such a way as not without aim. I box in such a way as not beating the air. But I discipline my body — and make it my slave, so that, after I have preached to others, I myself will not be disqualified.” (1 Corinthians 9:24–27, MBT)

And what doesn't make the highlight reel is that it won't feel good while you're doing it. Jinwoo's quest hurt, and your reps will too, and Scripture doesn't pretend otherwise.

“All discipline for the moment seems not to be joyful, but sorrowful — yet to those who have been trained by it, afterwards it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness.” (Hebrews 12:11, MBT)

The word to hold onto is “afterwards,” because the fruit comes afterwards, and that's the deal: you plant in the dark and you reap in a season you can't see yet, which is why most men quit right before it pays off.

“Let us not lose heart in doing good. For in due time we will reap, if we do not grow weary.” (Galatians 6:9, MBT)

Reps Are Not How You Earn Him

Now I have to be careful here, because this is the spot where a lot of men go wrong, and where the show is only a shadow. In Solo Leveling, the grind is how Jinwoo earns his strength. The System is a ledger: he pays in pushups and it pays out in levels, and if he slacks, he gets penalized. It's pure performance, and it isn't the gospel.

You don't do the reps to earn God's love, because you already have it; Christ bought it for you outright, before you ever did one pushup of obedience. The Father isn't floating a Penalty Zone over your head, waiting for you to miss a quiet time. Jinwoo trained alone, by his own grit, to make himself into something, and you don't. Listen to how Paul frames the Christian's effort:

“Therefore, my beloved, just as you have always obeyed — not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence — work out your salvation with fear and trembling. For it is God who is at work in you — both to will and to work for His good pleasure.” (Philippians 2:12,13, MBT)

You work, and God is the one working in you the whole time; you do the reps, but it's His strength moving your arms. That's the difference between a slave grinding to keep his master happy and a son training in his father's gym, with his father's hand on his back the whole time. The sweat is the same and the heart is completely different.

So why bother with the reps at all, if you're already loved? Because the reps are how grace takes root in the life you really live. God has given us ordinary means, the Word, prayer, the Lord's Day rest, the company of His people, and through those plain, daily channels He grows what He has already planted. The quest is how His love builds a man, and it isn't the price of His love.

Your System is quieter than his, and far better: an open Bible, a few minutes of prayer, a Lord's Day kept, which is boring on any given morning and world-changing over a lifetime.

What We Confess

This is the slow, certain work our forefathers confessed God does in every man He saves:

“For bodily training is of little profit — but godliness is profitable for all things, since it holds promise for the present life and also for the life to come.” (1 Timothy 4:8, MBT)

Father & Son Talk

  • Jinwoo got strong from a thousand boring mornings nobody saw. What is one "rep" you know you should do daily but keep skipping because no one is watching?
  • The System punished Jinwoo the second he slacked; God is not holding a Penalty Zone over your head. How would it change the way you train if you truly believed you were already fully loved, not earning it?
  • Where are you waiting for a "montage moment" to suddenly become the man you want to be, instead of just starting the reps today?

Today's Quest

Pick one tiny spiritual rep and do it today, no montage, no audience: five slow minutes in Psalm 1, or ten honest sentences of prayer, or one chapter read out loud with me. Small enough that you can do it again tomorrow. That's the whole quest; we'll do the next one then.
