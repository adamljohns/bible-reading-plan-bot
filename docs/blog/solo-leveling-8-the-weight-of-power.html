The Weight of Power
Pride Is the Fall That Comes Before the Fall

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 2026

Last week we watched Jinwoo command the dead to rise, and we drew a hard line between the counterfeit and the real Risen One. This week the danger is quieter, and a lot closer to home. It isn't a monster in a dungeon. It's the thing that grows inside a man right alongside his strength.

By this point in the story, Sung Jinwoo is nearly unstoppable. He walks into rooms full of A-rank and S-rank hunters, the elite of the whole system, and he is simply on another level than all of them. He doesn't have to try anymore. People who used to look at him with contempt now look at him with fear. And somewhere in there a new temptation slides in, so soft you could miss it: the pull to look down. To enjoy being feared. To treat the weak the way the strong once treated him.

Watch him in those scenes and you can feel the show flirting with it, that cold little smile when an arrogant hunter realizes he's outmatched. It's satisfying. It's also a warning shaped exactly like a victory.

Son, this is the oldest trap there is, and it almost never announces itself. Strength doesn't usually fall to an enemy from the outside. It falls to pride on the inside, and pride always shows up disguised as something you earned.

Every fallen crown in history started as a real one; the dust comes later, after a man forgets who handed him the power in the first place.

The Fall Before the Fall

Here's the order God's word gives, and the order matters. Most men think the fall is the moment the empire collapses, the day the strong man gets knocked off the mountain. But Scripture says the real fall happened earlier, quietly, when his heart got high. The crash is just the part everyone can see.

"Pride goeth before destruction, and an haughty spirit before a fall." (Proverbs 16:18)

There's a king in the Bible who lived this out, and I want you to know his name. Uzziah became king at sixteen, sought God, and God made him strong; his fame spread, his army was mighty, his enemies feared him. And then the sentence turns on a single word.

"But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction." (2 Chronicles 26:16)

Read that line slowly. Not "when he was weak." When he was strong. The strength God gave him became the very thing he stopped giving God credit for. That's the danger of power; it's the most spiritually expensive thing a man can carry, and the bill comes due right at the top.

Power Has to Be Under Control

Now hear me, because I am not telling you strength is bad. I spent twenty-one years in the Marine Corps learning to build it, aim it, and discipline it. The Bible never calls a man to be weak. It calls him to be meek, and those are not the same word. Meekness is not the absence of power; it's power on a leash, strength that has chosen a master. A warhorse is meek when it answers the reins. So the question God puts to a strong man is never "are you strong," it's "who holds your reins."

"God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble." (James 4:6)

Sit with how serious that is. God doesn't merely dislike pride; He sets Himself against it. The proud man isn't fighting his rivals; he's fighting the One who handed him every ounce of strength he has. There is no version of that fight a man wins.

The Strongest One Knelt

And here's where the story can only be a shadow, because the real answer to pride isn't a stronger hero. It's the strongest Being who has ever existed choosing to go low on purpose. Jinwoo grows in power and the temptation is to lord it. Jesus held all power, the actual power that made galaxies, and did the opposite of lording it.

"Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves... Who, being in the form of God... made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant... he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross." (Philippians 2:3,7,8)

That is the whole thing, son. The most powerful person in existence picked up a towel and washed dirty feet, then stretched out His arms and let weak men nail Him down. He didn't look down on the weak; He came down to them. And notice what happens next in that same passage: because He humbled Himself, God exalted Him and gave Him the name above every name. The way up really is down. Jinwoo's strength lifts him over people. Christ's strength bent Him under them, and that is the only kind of power that doesn't rot the man holding it.

A King on His knees with a basin and a towel; this is what real power looks like when it finally stops needing to be seen.

What We Confess

Our forefathers were honest about where pride comes from, because they were honest about what sin actually is:

Q. What is sin?
A. Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God. (Baptist Catechism)

Pride isn't a personality quirk; it's a transgression. It's the heart refusing to bow to the One it owes everything to. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 6, "Of the Fall of Man, of Sin") traces every sin we commit back to that first one in the garden, where a man reached for a crown that wasn't his to take. Every prideful thought you've ever had is a tiny echo of that grab. The good news of this whole series is that the True and Better Man went the other direction, all the way down, to lift up grabbers like us.

Father & Son Talk

  • Uzziah fell apart "when he was strong," not when he was weak. Where are you strongest right now, and is that exactly where you're least dependent on God?
  • Meekness is power on a leash. Name one area where you've got real strength; who or what is actually holding your reins there?
  • Jesus had all power and washed feet. Be honest with me: who is someone you've quietly looked down on lately, and what would it cost you to serve him instead?

⚔ Today's Quest

Today, name one spot where pride is creeping in, the rush of winning, the urge to flex, the little flick of contempt for someone weaker, and then do one humble thing about it before the day ends: serve someone who can't pay you back, yield a point you could have won, or apologize for something you've been too proud to own. If you have a minute first, read Philippians 2:3–8 slowly. That's the whole quest.

Gideon, you're going to get strong; you already are. I'm not worried about your strength. I'm praying about your knees, that the stronger you grow, the lower they bend, because the only safe place for a powerful man to stand is on them.

"God resisteth the proud, but giveth grace unto the humble." (James 4:6)
