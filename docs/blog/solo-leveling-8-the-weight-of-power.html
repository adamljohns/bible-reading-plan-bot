The Empty Throne
Every Man Serves a Throne; The Only Question Is Whether the King Is Alive

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

Son, in this episode the System hands Jinwoo a Job Change Quest, a Trial of Courage, and he walks into a special gate alone. Inside, the waves never stop. Knights, assassins, archers, magicians, all of them coming at him without end. He remembers how raw strength once let him break a giant snake, and he tears an armored knight clean in two. Then the trial reveals its real face: the Blood-Red Knight Commander, Igris the Bloodred. The System reframes everything; this is a survival trial now. Last as long as you can. Endure, and you earn advancement and a promotion. And the episode ends with the floor dropping out; Jinwoo loses his teleportation stone, trapped in that dungeon as his health and mana bleed away and the enemies keep swarming.

All of that is worth talking about. But the title of the episode is where I want to plant our flag, because the title is doing theology whether the writers meant it to or not: "A Knight Who Defends an Empty Throne."

Igris is magnificent. Disciplined, loyal, lethal, everything a warrior is supposed to be. He guards a throne with all the fire in him. But the king who once sat that throne is long gone. Igris pours his whole existence, his discipline, his loyalty, his lethal skill, into defending a seat with no living king on it.

Sit with that picture, Son, because it is the truest thing in the whole show. That is not a monster. That is a man.

Every fallen crown in history started as a real one; the dust comes later, after a man forgets who handed him the power in the first place.

The Throne Is Never Empty of a Servant

Here is the thing I need you to see before anything else: Igris never once considers walking away. The throne is empty, but his loyalty is total. He does not serve halfway. He gives everything.

That is you. That is me. That is every man alive. We were built to kneel, built to guard a throne, built to pour ourselves out for a king. The question was never whether you'd serve a throne; you will, we all do. The only question is whether the King on that throne is alive.

Because a man will give his one and only life to something. Money. Status. Applause. A career. A hobby. Even a genuinely good thing that he quietly promotes into an ultimate thing. And every one of those thrones has the same fatal flaw as Igris's: it cannot save him, and it will never speak back. God said it about His own people with a grief you can almost hear out loud:

““For My people have committed two evils: They have forsaken Me, the fountain of living waters, And hewn themselves cisterns—broken cisterns that can hold no water.” (Jeremiah 2:13)

Two evils, not one. They walked away from a living fountain, and then they went and dug their own dry, cracked tank to stand guard over. That is Igris to the letter. That is the man who guards an empty throne. He didn't just fail to serve; he served the wrong thing with everything he had.

Idols Have a Throne but No King

The Scriptures are almost brutal about what these thrones actually are. God's people kept bowing to idols, and the psalmist just describes the thing they were kneeling to:

“Their idols are silver and gold, the work of human hands. They have mouths, but they do not speak; eyes they have, but they do not see; They have ears, but they do not hear; noses they have, but they do not smell; They have hands, but they do not handle; feet they have, but they do not walk; nor do they make a sound in their throat. Those who make them are like them; so is everyone who trusts in them.” (Psalm 115:4–8)

Read that last line slowly, Son. They that make them are like unto them. You become like what you worship. Guard an empty throne long enough and you go hollow too. Igris is beautiful and deadly and completely still on the inside, because the throne he serves has never once spoken to him. It never will. A dead king makes a dead servant.

And this is the part that catches good men, so hear it plainly: an idol is not always a wicked thing. It is very often a good thing that you have promoted past its rank. Jesus put it in terms of masters and money, one of the most respectable idols there is:

“No one can serve two masters. For either he will hate the one and love the other, or he will hold to one and despise the other. You cannot serve God and mammon.” (Matthew 6:24)

Not should not. Cannot. There is one throne in the center of your chest and it seats one king. Whatever you pour yourself out for, whatever gets your first and best loyalty, whatever you'd defend to the death, that is on the throne. Name it honestly and you'll know what you actually worship.

The One Throne That Is Not Empty

And here, Son, is where the shadow finally breaks and the light comes through, because the whole point of Igris is that his loyalty was wasted. He gave a king's worth of devotion to a corpse. The tragedy is not that he served; it's that there was no one home to serve.

There is exactly one throne in all of reality that is not empty. And the King who sits it is not gold, not carved, not gone. He is alive. He speaks. He made you, and He made the very thrones you're tempted to bow to instead of Him.

“for in Him all things were created — in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible — whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities. All things have been created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things hold together. And He is the head of the body — the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from among the dead — so that in everything He may have the preeminence.” (Colossians 1:16–18)

Do you see it? Money was created by Him and for Him. Your career, your strength, your applause, every good thing you'd ever be tempted to enthrone, He made them, and He made them to point back to Him, not to sit where He sits. To worship any of them is to bow to a thing He built and ignore the One who built it. And unlike Igris's silent throne, His throne answers, because the King on it is living and eternal:

“But of the Son He says, "Your throne, O God, is forever and ever — and the scepter of righteousness is the scepter of His kingdom.” (Hebrews 1:8)

That is the whole difference, Son. Igris guards a throne forever empty. The Christian guards a throne forever full, occupied by a King who died and rose and reigns and speaks and loves the men who kneel to Him. He is worth every ounce of the loyalty Igris poured out on a dead seat. He is the living fountain, not the broken cistern. Turn all that warrior devotion toward Him, and for the first time in your life you will be serving a throne that serves you back.

One throne in all of reality is not empty; the King on it made everything you were ever tempted to worship instead of Him.

What We Confess

Our forefathers named this danger with the first two commandments, because they knew the human heart is, in Calvin's phrase, a factory of idols:

“You shall have no other gods before Me.” (Exodus 20:3)

The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 2, "Of God and of the Holy Trinity") opens by confessing that "the Lord our God is but one only living and true God," and that to Him alone "is due from angels and men... whatsoever worship, service, or obedience, as creatures they owe unto the Creator." Read that against Igris. Worship, service, obedience, all of it is owed to the living Creator, and Igris rendered every bit of it to a throne that was neither living nor his creator. That is the definition of idolatry in one word: giving to a created thing the allegiance that belongs to the living God.

And the Baptist Catechism drives the first commandment straight into the chest: it requires us "to know and acknowledge God to be the only true God, and our God; and to worship and glorify him accordingly." Only true. Our God. Worship Him accordingly. Not a career. Not a crown. Not an empty throne. Him.

Father & Son Talk

  • Igris gave everything to a throne with no living king on it. If you're honest, what throne are you tempted to pour your one life into right now? Name it out loud to me.
  • An idol is often a good thing promoted to an ultimate thing. Is there something good in your life, a goal, a girl, a game, a grade, that has quietly climbed onto the throne where only Christ belongs?
  • Igris's tragedy wasn't that he served; it's that no one was home. What would it change about how you spend this week if you really believed the King on your throne is alive and listening?

⚔ Today's Quest

Today, name the empty throne you're most tempted to serve, the money, the status, the applause, the good thing you've made ultimate, and write it on paper. Then, out loud, hand that throne back to the living King it belongs to, and do one concrete thing that proves it: give away something you're clutching, quit defending a seat that can't save you, or simply spend the time you'd have spent guarding it in prayer instead. Read Jeremiah 2:13 and Psalm 115:4–8 slowly first. That's the whole quest.

Gideon, you were made to kneel; I've never doubted you'd give your life to something. What I'm praying is that you never waste a warrior's loyalty on a dead throne. Bow to the King who's alive, the One who made the very things you're tempted to worship, and you'll find you were built for exactly that all along.

“You shall have no other gods before Me.” (Exodus 20:3)
