It Was All Worth It
The Sleeper Wakes

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 8, 2026

Gideon, we have come to the end of the road together. Twelve weeks ago I sat down next to you to watch a story about the weakest hunter alive, and I promised you it would be about more than a boy getting strong. Now I get to keep that promise, because the whole series has been quietly walking us toward one hospital room, one bedside, and one woman opening her eyes.

Go back with me to Part 5. All the way back in the first season, Jinwoo won something that mattered more to him than any weapon or title: the recipe for the Holy Water of Life, the one and only cure for the Eternal Slumber, the strange magic sickness that had held his mother in a coma for years. He had the recipe. He did not yet have the strength to gather what it required. So he wrote a promise across his own back and went to work.

The Long Obedience of a Faithful Son

Everything we watched after that, everything, was aimed at that bedside. The doubled reps. The daily quests he could not skip. The burning Demon Castle and the beast at its gates. Cerberus. The years of grinding through gates most men would not have survived, taking wounds, dying to his old weakness, becoming someone new one painful level at a time. It looked, from the outside, like a young man chasing power. It was actually a son who would not stop until his mother could wake up.

That is the shape of real love, Son, and I want you to see it clearly before we go one inch further. Love that quits when it gets expensive was never love; it was convenience wearing love's coat. The love that means anything is the love that keeps its promise after the cost comes due. Jinwoo could have taken his new strength and built himself a comfortable life. Instead he spent it, all of it, gathering ingredients for a cure that would help exactly one person who could not thank him or even see what it cost. That is love that will not quit.

He crafted the Holy Water of Life at last. The whole climb had a name, and its name was her.

The Waking

Then comes the moment the entire series was built to earn. He finishes it. He carries the Holy Water of Life into the quiet hospital room, sits down beside the mother who has been silent for years, and gives it to her. And she wakes. After all that silence, her eyes open, and she knows her son. The family that had been broken by that long sleep is whole again in a single breath.

There is a reason that scene undoes grown men who would never admit it. It reaches down and touches the deepest ache we carry, the ache under every graveside and every hospital hallway: the longing that the ones we have lost to sleep might simply open their eyes and know us again. And here is what I most want you to catch, Gideon. The Bible does not treat that longing as a fantasy to be outgrown. It treats it as a promise to be kept.

Listen to how gently Scripture talks about the death of God's people. Jesus stood outside a tomb where His friend had been dead four days, and He did not call it what everyone else called it:

“Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep. But I am going so that I may wake him up."” (John 11:11)

He said the same thing in a grieving house where a little girl had just died, with the mourners already weeping. He looked at that ruined room and told them the truth they could not yet see:

“And entering in, He said to them, "Why make a commotion and weep? The child has not died — but is asleep."” (Mark 5:39)

They laughed at Him. Then He took that dead child by the hand and spoke to her the way you would speak to wake someone from a nap:

“And taking the child by the hand, He said to her, "Talitha kum!" — which translated means, "Little girl, I say to you, arise!"” (Mark 5:41)

And she got up. Son, for the one who belongs to Christ, death is not annihilation and it is not the end. The New Testament keeps calling it sleep on purpose, because sleep is a thing you wake up from, and there is Someone coming who has the authority and the love to do the waking.

The True and Better Cure

Now stand Jinwoo's story next to the real thing, because a shadow is only worth anything if it makes you turn and look at what casts it. Jinwoo spent years, and nearly his own life, to win a cure that woke one woman for a while. It was a beautiful, costly, faithful love. And it was still only a shadow, because the sleep he broke was temporary, and so was the waking. His mother woke to live more years, and those years, like all of ours, will one day end.

Jesus is the true and better Son. He did not spend a season of hard training to win the cure; He spent His whole sinless life and then laid it down, poured out His own blood, to win a Holy Water that no dungeon holds. He called Himself the living water, and He said what that water does:

“But whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again — ever. The water that I will give him will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life."” (John 4:14)

Hear the difference and let it land. Jinwoo's cure bought his mother a few more years of life. The cure Jesus won does not wake you up for a while; it raises you to a life that never ends. He said it to a grieving sister at her brother's grave, and He says it over every believer's coffin:

“Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in Me — even if he dies — will live.” (John 11:25)

And this is not a private trick for one bedside. Paul promises a day when the King Himself will stand over the whole company of His sleeping people and wake them all at once:

“For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so, through Jesus, God will bring with Him those who have fallen asleep.” (1 Thessalonians 4:14)

That is the morning every graveside is secretly waiting for. That is the room where the silence finally breaks for good, and no one ever falls asleep again:

“He will swallow up death forever, and the Lord GOD will wipe away tears from all faces; the rebuke of His people He will take away from all the earth; for the LORD has spoken.” (Isaiah 25:8)

He stood at a grave and called a sleeper out. One day He will speak, and everyone asleep in Him will hear it. The empty tomb is the proof He can.

What We Confess

This is not a mood I am hoping you will feel, Son. It is a truth our fathers in the faith staked their lives on and wrote down so we would not lose it. The 1689 London Baptist Confession, chapter 31, "Of the State of Man after Death and of the Resurrection of the Dead," puts our hope in plain and immovable words:

Of the Resurrection of the Dead.
"The bodies of men after death return to dust, and see corruption; but their souls, which neither die nor sleep, having an immortal subsistence, immediately return to God who gave them... At the last day, such of the saints as are found alive shall not sleep, but be changed; and all the dead shall be raised up with the selfsame bodies, and none other, although with different qualities, which shall be united again to their souls forever." (1689 LBCF 31.1–2)

The same selfsame body, raised, glorified, reunited, forever. That is the blessed hope. It is why a believing family can stand at a grave with real tears and real joy in the same moment; we are not saying goodbye, we are saying goodnight. And Scripture tells us how the story finally ends, in a room with no more hospital beds and no more slumber at all:

“And He will wipe away every tear from their eyes — and there will no longer be any death; nor mourning, nor crying, nor pain. The first things have passed away."” (Revelation 21:4)

Father & Son Talk

  • Jinwoo spent years keeping a promise no one could make him keep. Where in your life right now is love asking you to keep going after it has already gotten expensive?
  • Jesus keeps calling death "sleep" for His people. How does it change the way you would walk into a hospital room, or stand at a grave, if you really believed the sleeper is going to wake?
  • Of everyone you love, whose waking do you most long to see on that last morning? Have you told them about the One who won the cure?

Today's Quest

Sit down today and read John 11 slowly, the whole account of Lazarus, start to finish, and watch how Jesus handles a tomb. Then do one small, brave thing: think of one person you love who is asleep in Christ, or one you long to see wake, and thank God out loud for the resurrection by name. Speak the hope. It is real, and it is yours.

Gideon, this is the last page of our run together, so I am going to say the truest thing I know, straight, the way a father says a thing he would stake his life on. One day my body is going to lie still, and it will look to the world like the end of your dad. I want you to remember every word of this one when that day comes. It will not be the end. It will be sleep. And there is a King who spent everything to win the cure, who calls His own people by name, who has already walked out of His own tomb, and who is coming back to wake every last one of us who slept in Him. On that morning you will see your father get up, whole, changed, and forever. It was all worth it, Son, all of it, because of Him. So do not grieve as those who have no hope. Rest in the One who does the waking. I will see you in the morning.

“Jesus said to her, "I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in Me — even if he dies — will live.” (John 11:25)

That closes our run of Solo Leveling and biblical manhood, twelve weeks written for my son and for any man reading over our shoulder. But the story does not stop here, and neither does our walk together. Next: we lace the boots back up and keep going, into the next arc, the next book, the next fight, side by side. There is always another rep. If you just found us, start at the beginning and climb the whole trail with us.
