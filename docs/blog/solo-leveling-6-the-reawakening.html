The Reawakening
The Trial That Tells the Truth About You

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 2026

Last week we talked about what all that power is actually for, that a man spends his strength on the people he is given to protect. This week the show does something harder to watch. It throws Jinwoo into a trial built to kill him, and we find out that the strength itself has to be tested before it can be trusted.

Jinwoo walks into a reawakening test, a trial of strength meant to measure a hunter. What he gets is a chamber that pushes him past every limit he thought he had. He bleeds, he buckles, he is hit harder than he has ever been hit, and there is a long stretch where it genuinely looks like the test is going to finish him. But it doesn't. He grinds through it, and on the other side he is not the same man who walked in. The trial did not just measure him; it remade him.

Here is the thing I want you to see, son. The trial told the truth about him. It revealed what was really in there, and then it burned off what wasn't. A man can tell himself all kinds of stories about how strong he is. A real trial settles the argument.

A blade is not made strong in the rack. It is made strong in the fire and on the anvil. So is a man.

Don't Waste the Trial

Most men do one of two things with a hard season. They run from it, or they just endure it with their teeth clenched, waiting for it to be over. The Bible tells you to do a third thing that sounds almost crazy the first time you hear it: count it joy.

"My brethren, count it all joy when ye fall into divers temptations; knowing this, that the trying of your faith worketh patience. But let patience have her perfect work, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing." (James 1:2-4)

Read that slowly. The joy is not because the trial feels good; it doesn't. The joy is because of what the trial is doing. It is "working" something in you, the way fire works iron. Paul says the exact same chain of cause and effect, and he is not being poetic; he is telling you how God grows men.

"...we glory in tribulations also: knowing that tribulation worketh patience; and patience, experience; and experience, hope: and hope maketh not ashamed." (Romans 5:3-5)

Tribulation, then patience, then proven character, then hope. There is no shortcut around that chain. You cannot skip to the hope without going through the fire, any more than Jinwoo could skip to the strength without going through the trial. Peter tells you why the heat is worth it, and what it is actually testing.

"...though now for a season, if need be, ye are in heaviness through manifold temptations: that the trial of your faith, being much more precious than of gold that perisheth, though it be tried with fire, might be found unto praise and honour and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ." (1 Peter 1:6-7)

Your faith is worth more than gold, so God is willing to put it through what gold goes through. And Solomon tells you who is running the furnace; it is not random, and it is not the enemy.

"The fining pot is for silver, and the furnace for gold: but the LORD trieth the hearts." (Proverbs 17:3)

The Refiner Sits Over the Gold

This is where the show is only a shadow, and a thin one. In Solo Leveling the trial is a cold mechanism. The System sets the test, the chamber does its damage, and Jinwoo is on his own to survive it or die. There is no one in the room who loves him. The fire has no face.

Your trials are not like that. There is a face over your fire, and it is the face of a Father who is for you. Nothing reaches you that did not first pass through His hands, and He is not letting you be tested to break you; He is doing it to make you, and He has already promised what it adds up to.

"And we know that all things work together for good to them that love God, to them who are the called according to his purpose." (Romans 8:28)

"All things." Not most things, not the easy things, the all things, including the trial you are in right now that you would give anything to be out of. A refiner does not walk away from the crucible and leave the gold to chance. He sits over it; he watches the heat; he skims the dross as it rises; and he knows the metal is finished when he can see his own face in it. That is what your Father is doing with you. He is not absent in the fire. He is the closest He has ever been.

And here is the part Jinwoo's story can never give you. The deepest trial in all of history was endured by a Man who did not need to be refined at all, because there was no dross in Him. Jesus walked into a furnace He did not deserve, the cross, and He came through it on the third morning, so that the fire you walk through can refine you instead of consume you. He took the trial that would have destroyed you and turned every trial you will ever face into a forge.

The dross rises so the refiner can lift it off. What feels like loss in the fire is the very thing He is removing because He wants you pure, not because He wants you gone.

What We Confess

Our forefathers had a word for the face over the fire. They called it providence, and they confessed that nothing in your life is loose or random:

Q. What are God's works of providence?
A. God's works of providence are His most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all His creatures, and all their actions. (Baptist Catechism)

"All their actions." Every blow that lands in your trial is preserved and governed by a holy, wise, and powerful hand. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 5, "Of Divine Providence") goes further and says that God ordains the trials of His own children on purpose, "for sundry other just and holy ends," including to humble them, to expose what is hidden in their hearts, and to make them lean harder on Him. The chamber that nearly killed Jinwoo had no purpose for him beyond the test. Your furnace has a Father, and your Father has a purpose, and the purpose is you.

Father & Son Talk

  • The trial revealed what was really in Jinwoo. If a hard season hit you today, what do you think it would reveal is actually in you right now, the good and the dross?
  • James says count it joy. That is not the same as pretending it doesn't hurt. What is the difference between fake cheerfulness and real joy in a trial?
  • Jinwoo's fire had no one in it who loved him. How does it change a hard thing you are facing to know your Father is sitting over the crucible, watching for His own reflection in you?

⚔ Today's Quest

Name the trial you are in right now, out loud, to God; say it plainly, no softening it. Then thank Him for one specific thing the fire is forging in you, even if you can only half see it yet. If you have a minute, read James 1:2-4 first. That's the whole quest.

Gideon, you are going to walk into chambers that feel built to finish you. Don't waste them, and don't face them like they have no face. The fire is real, but so is the Refiner, and He has already promised you will come out as gold.

"Blessed is the man that endureth temptation: for when he is tried, he shall receive the crown of life, which the Lord hath promised to them that love him." (James 1:12)
