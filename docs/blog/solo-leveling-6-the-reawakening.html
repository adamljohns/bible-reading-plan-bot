Before It's Too Late
The Apology Waited Too Long, and the Real Monster Wore a Badge

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

Last week we talked about what all that strength is actually for, that a man spends himself on the people he is given to protect. This week the show hands us two deaths in a single dungeon, and both of them are the kind of thing you want to look away from. But don't look away, son. There is a hard lesson buried in each one, and I want you to carry both of them out of here.

Here is the ground. Jinwoo has been keeping vigil over his mother, who lies in a long, wasting sleep from a strange disease. He takes up a young man's plan to run raids and one day build a guild, and answering a raid request he ends up back at a low-rank gate with the survivors of that first cursed double dungeon; a healer, a couple of veteran hunters, men who bled beside him once. It is a bittersweet reunion, old comrades who thought they might never see each other again. And into that reunion walk three handcuffed convicts, criminals turned loose to raid dungeons in exchange for shorter sentences, watched over by a higher-ranked Association hunter named Kang Taeshik, a man with rank, a badge, and every appearance of authority.

Then the appearance shatters. Taeshik, the lawful one, the overseer, is the real killer in the room. He murders the convicts, and he murders one of Jinwoo's old comrades, Kim Sangshik, planning to blame every corpse on a goblin swarm and walk out clean past the inspectors. Two threads run out of this, son, and I want to pull on them both.

Kim Sangshik had been cold to Jinwoo, dismissive, a little cruel. He meant to fix it later. He ran out of later.

The Apology He Waited Too Long to Make

Earlier in the story, Kim Sangshik had not been kind to Jinwoo. He was dismissive, a little contemptuous, the way strong men are sometimes cruel to the ones they have written off as weak. Nothing dramatic; just a low, ongoing coldness. And I would bet everything I own that somewhere in the back of his mind he figured he would smooth it over eventually. Later. When it mattered less. When it was easier.

He runs out of later. Bleeding out on that dungeon floor, the thing that comes up out of Kim Sangshik is not fear of death; it is regret. He wishes he could have made it right with Jinwoo. He wishes he could have said the words. And now he cannot, because the sun has gone down for good and the door is shut. That is one of the saddest things a man can carry to his death, an apology he owed and kept putting off.

Son, hear the Lord Jesus on this, because He does not treat a broken relationship as a small thing you can leave lying around:

“Therefore, if you are presenting your offering at the altar, and there remember that your brother has something against you, leave your offering there before the altar — and go your way. First be reconciled to your brother — and then come and present your offering.” (Matthew 5:23-24)

Notice how urgent He makes it. You are standing at the altar, in the middle of worship, and you remember somebody has something against you. Jesus says stop; put the offering down; go fix it first. Reconciliation is not something you fit in when it is convenient. It cuts to the front of the line, ahead of even your worship. Paul says the same thing about the clock on your anger:

“Be angry, and yet do not sin. Do not let the sun go down on your anger, and do not give the devil an opportunity.” (Ephesians 4:26-27)

Do not let the sun go down on it. Why the sun? Because you do not own tomorrow's sunrise. Kim Sangshik assumed he had more days than he had, and the assumption killed the apology before the goblin ever did. Do not make his mistake. If there are hard words you owe your brother, your friend, your father, do not save them for a better day that may never come. Now is the time; the Scripture will not let you push it off:

“for He says, "At the acceptable time I listened to you, and on the day of salvation I helped you." Behold — now is the acceptable time! Behold — now is the day of salvation!” (2 Corinthians 6:2)

The Real Monster Wore a Badge

Now the second thread, and it is uglier. Everybody in that dungeon knew who the criminals were; they were the ones in handcuffs. Everybody knew who the authority was; he had the rank and the badge and the Association behind him. And every single one of them had it exactly backwards. The men in chains were not the predator. The predator was the one holding the keys.

This is one of the oldest and hardest truths in the world, son, and you had better learn it young. A badge is not a heart. A title is not a soul. A reputation is a coat a man puts on, and some of the worst men who ever lived wore very clean coats. The Lord said this to the prophet Samuel when even Samuel was being fooled by outward appearance:

“But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not look at his appearance or at his physical stature, because I have refused him. For the LORD does not see as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart."” (1 Samuel 16:7)

Man looks at the badge. God looks at the heart under it. Kang Taeshik built his whole plan on the fact that men judge by appearances; he was going to hide a massacre behind a rank and a cover story and stroll past the inspectors. And that is exactly the plan that can never work, because there is One inspector who does not read badges, and nothing is hidden from Him:

“For nothing is hidden that shall not become evident, nor anything secret that shall not be known and come to light.” (Luke 8:17)

In the show, the cover-up unravels because Jinwoo's senses are sharp enough to catch the assassin's move, and the killer's murderous intent trips the very power that undoes him. But notice, son, it still took a stronger man happening to be in the room. Out in the real world, monsters in clean coats get away with it all the time; the inspectors do get fooled; the badge does hold. Which is exactly why you must never make your peace with the idea that getting away with it here is the same as getting away with it. It is not. There is a final inspection, and it is not run by men.

The One Who Reconciled Us Before Our Too-Late, and the Judge No Badge Can Fool

Here is where the show is only a shadow, and both threads run straight to the same Man. Take the first one. Kim Sangshik died with his apology unspoken; he reached for reconciliation and the door closed in his face. That is the natural end of every man who waits, and left to ourselves, that is all of us, because the relationship we most need to make right is not with a comrade in a dungeon. It is with God, whom we have wronged, and we are the ones in the wrong. And we could never say the words in time. We could never reach across that gap before our own too-late.

So He reached across it first. While we were still His enemies, still hostile, still dismissive of Him the way Kim was dismissive of Jinwoo, Christ went to a cross and made the peace we could not make.

“For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of His Son — much more, having been reconciled, we shall be saved by His life.” (Romans 5:10)

Do you feel the weight of that, son? The apology you could never get out in time, the reconciliation you would have carried to your grave undone, Jesus accomplished it for you, from His side, before you ever asked. That is the gospel. He did not wait for a better day. He came in the fullness of time and made it right.

And take the second thread. Kang Taeshik banked everything on fooling the inspectors, and the Christ who reconciled us is also the Judge who cannot be fooled by any badge on any chest. Every hidden heart is open before Him, and there is a fixed appointment no cover story escapes:

“And inasmuch as it is appointed for men to die once — and after this comes judgment —” (Hebrews 9:27)

So the same Jesus is both halves of your hope. He is the One who reconciled you before your too-late, and He is the Judge no clean coat will deceive. That is terrifying if you are Taeshik and comforting if you are hiding in Christ, and which one you are is the only question that finally matters.

The badge burns off in the fire. What is left is the heart, and the heart is the only thing the Judge was ever reading.

What We Confess

Our forefathers confessed that God sees the whole of a man, the outside and the hidden inside, and that nothing escapes His inspection. The Baptist Catechism puts His knowledge like this:

Q. What is God?
A. God is a Spirit, infinite, eternal, and unchangeable in His being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness, and truth. (Baptist Catechism)

Infinite in wisdom, and unchangeable in justice. A God like that does not squint at a badge and get confused about who the criminal was. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 2, "Of God and of the Holy Trinity") says His knowledge is infinite and that "all things are open and manifest in His sight," so that "nothing is to Him contingent or uncertain." Kang Taeshik's cover story was written for men with limited eyes. It was never going to survive the eyes of the One who sees all things open and manifest. And that same all-seeing God is the one who, in Christ, reconciled us before we ever cleaned ourselves up; He saw the whole ugly heart and made peace with us anyway. That is not a God to be gamed. That is a God to be trusted, and feared, and run to.

Father & Son Talk

  • Kim Sangshik died wishing he had said he was sorry. Is there anyone right now you owe hard words to, an apology, a confession, a making-right, that you have been telling yourself you will get to "later"? What is the "later" really costing you?
  • Everyone in that dungeon trusted the badge and missed the monster. Where in real life do people give a pass to someone just because of their title or reputation? How do you keep a right respect for authority without ever handing a man your conscience?
  • Christ reconciled us to God before we ever asked, from His side of the gap. How does it change the way you go make peace with someone, knowing that God made peace with you first, when you were still His enemy?

Today's Quest

Before the sun goes down tonight, make one thing right. Pick the person you have been putting off, and send the message, make the call, or say it to their face: the apology, the confession, the hard word you owe. Do not save it for a better day; you are not promised one. Read Matthew 5:23-24 first, then go. That's the whole quest.

Gideon, two men die in that dungeon, and I want both of them lodged in your chest. Do not be the man with the unspoken apology; be reconciled now, today, while you still hold the day. And do not be fooled by a badge, and never think for a second that a clean coat will get anyone past the final inspection. The One who reconciled you before your too-late is the same One who sees every hidden heart, and He will not be fooled, and He will not be late.

“for He says, "At the acceptable time I listened to you, and on the day of salvation I helped you." Behold — now is the acceptable time! Behold — now is the day of salvation!” (2 Corinthians 6:2)
