Before You Decide

Prayer in the middle of the loop — OOPDA and the ALERT that keeps a man open

By Adam “MOOP” Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries

I am putting prayer before the hypothesis. Observe. Orient. Pray. Decide. Act.

The Corps taught me a loop.

If you have worn khaki, you have seen it on a whiteboard: Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. Four boxes. Cycle faster than the enemy. The schoolhouse likes that version because it fits on a slide and it sounds like winning.

A brother who teaches Command and Staff sent me back to the real sketch — Boyd, 1995, The Essence of Winning and Losing. Orientation sits in the middle. Decision is a hypothesis. Action is a test. A man who is rightly oriented often never holds a formal Decide at all. Implicit guidance already has him moving. Feedback is everywhere. It is not a race.

That is the tool. It is not a saint. I do not need you to love the man who drew it. I need you to stop treating a cartoon as doctrine.

Then I want a harder baptism than fighter warfare.

The fight I actually live is the world, the flesh, and the devil. A man who will not pray will still decide. He will decide from a closed system — refining yesterday’s take until it cannot see the room he is standing in — and he will call that wisdom. Boyd named the entropy of a closed system in Destruction and Creation. Scripture named the man a long time before that: he leans on his own understanding and wonders why the peace never comes.

So I am not adding a fifth box so we can cycle faster.

I am putting prayer before the hypothesis.

Observe. Orient. Pray. Decide. Act.

OOPDA.

Two moves in. Two moves out. Prayer in the middle, where Orientation either stays open to God or collapses into self-talk with better vocabulary.

The sketch, short

Observation takes what is unfolding — circumstances, outside word, the environment you are actually in, not the one you briefed last week.

Orientation is the center of gravity. Heritage, culture, previous experience, new information, analysis and synthesis. That is memory. That is also the place a lie gets comfortable. If Orientation is wrong, faster Decide only gets you to the wreck sooner.

Decision is a hypothesis. Action is a test. Feedback comes home and Orientation either learns or hardens.

The Marine four-box race is pedagogy. Useful for a lieutenant who has never thought in a loop. Fatal if you preach it as the whole doctrine.

I will not put “cycle faster” on this page. Tempo is not holiness. A praying man is not trying to outrun God. He is trying not to decide before he has heard Him.

The baptism

A Christian does not orient toward the enemy first.

He orients toward God.

Prayer is not a bolt-on piety module. It is how Orientation stays open. Folded hands are not a pause in the fight. They are the fight at the point that matters — before you sign, talk, or say “I got this.”

I call the prayer ALERT.

Five letters. Two hands. Before Decide.

When the hands fold, some letters hit twice. That is on purpose.

A — Acknowledgement

Thumbs. Two hits when the hands fold: God, then you.

Know that the LORD, He is God; It is He who has made us, and not we ourselves; We are His people and the sheep of His pasture. — Psalm 100:3 (NKJV)

If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. — 1 John 1:9 (NKJV)

This is light, not self-loathing. Shame hides. Productive guilt names the dirt and takes it to the throne. You are not groveling to earn a hearing. You are telling the truth: He is God, and you are not, and the blood of Jesus is enough for what you already know you did. Acknowledgement is repentance with a Father, not rumination. Confession that turns into contempt for the image-bearer is not holiness. It is another closed system.

L — Listen

Index.

My sheep hear My voice, and I know them, and they follow Me. — John 10:27 (NKJV)

And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth. — John 1:14 (NKJV)

Open the Word. Shut your mouth. Wait.

You listen to the inspired Word. You listen to the Word made flesh. You listen for the Spirit to the heart. Faith comes by hearing — that line supports this letter; it is not a third stamp. If you skip Listen, Exaltation becomes noise and Request becomes a shopping list.

E — Exaltation

Middle.

But You are holy, Enthroned in the praises of Israel. — Psalm 22:3 (NKJV)

Let us therefore come boldly to the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy and find grace to help in time of need. — Hebrews 4:16 (NKJV)

Praise is not fluff before the “real” prayer. He is enthroned in the praises of His people. That is the NKJV. Do not smuggle a different rendering in because it sings prettier.

Exaltation emboldens a scared man to walk to the throne instead of to the first human hatch that will tell him he is fine. Encouragement is a cousin of this letter, not a sixth one.

R — Request

Ring. You named this one yourself.

Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. — Philippians 4:6–7 (NKJV)

Ask for what God must do in you and for you. Ask for others. Then stop talking.

Philippians 4:6 already names thanksgiving. That does not collapse T into R. Request still has to ask. Thanksgiving still has to thank. Do not let one verse eat a letter.

Request without Acknowledgement is entitlement. Request without Listen is guesswork. Request without Thanksgiving is panic with a Bible cover.

T — Thanksgiving

Pinky. Two hits when the hands fold: what He has done, and what He will do.

Rejoice always, pray without ceasing, in everything give thanks; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you. — 1 Thessalonians 5:16–18 (NKJV)

Thanksgiving is not the polite ending. It is how a man refuses to treat the last observation as the whole story. He has a past mercy and a promised future. Both are facts. Both keep Orientation open.

What has to happen

Mac — deckplate.

Listen, Lieutenant. You don’t need Boyd’s sketch to fold your hands. You need five letters and two hands before you sign, talk, or say “I got this.”

Thumbs — A. God is God. You are not. Confess what you already know you did. Two hits when the hands fold.

Index — L. Open the Word. Shut your mouth. Wait.

Middle — E. Say who He is out loud. That’s not fluff. That’s how a scared butterbar walks to the throne instead of the CO’s hatch first.

Ring — R. Ask for you. Ask for your Marines. Then stop talking.

Pinky — T. Thank Him for what He already did and what He said He will do. Two hits again.

Then you decide. Not before.

One slow A-L-E-R-T before the hypothesis — FRAGO, liberty chit, or “I got this.” Two hits on A. Two hits on T. Then he decides.

What it means

Gus — officer sidebar.

The schoolhouse cartoon treats Decide as the prize. The 1995 sketch treats Orientation as the prize. A Christian already knew that in other words: keep your heart, for out of it are the issues of life.

Prayer before the hypothesis is not delay. It is how a man refuses a closed system. Implicit guidance — the rightly oriented man who never holds a formal Decide — is not impulsiveness. It is a heart already pointed. The man who will not pray has to manufacture every decision from scratch, and he will call the grind “ownership.”

I will not make Boyd a household saint. The loop is a tool. Christ is Lord. Fold the hands.

Alert to the dirt — and then home

We return to the dirt because that is where men actually live and where Christ meets them — not because dirt is the last word. Man is dust; the Last Adam walked out of the grave. Stay alert to the dirt so you do not live like a man who thinks he is permanent. Stay alert to the resurrection so you do not pray like a man who thinks he is finished.

If the Lord gives me one more ride, I want it headed home. That is homecoming, not a eulogy. Gideon is twenty. The countdown that matters was never the retirement date.

The Corps is a billet. The household is a covenant. The grave is a doorway. None of those sentences work if you decide first and pray after the wreck.

Afterthought — for a khaki second lieutenant only

A khaki 2ndLt already knows oops, then duh. That is the life after the wreck: the signature, the talk, the “I got this,” and then the late prayer.

That line is a warning label. It is not the name of the prayer. It does not go on the title, the plate, or the mnemonic. If you need it for a mentee who is living oops-then-duh, park it after ALERT is taught. If it fights the prayer for airtime, cut it.

Do not let the joke eat the prayer.

The two plates

First, the 1995 sketch we started with — Orientation as the Schwerpunkt, analysis and synthesis as the engine, implicit guidance that can skip Decide. Then the baptism: the same well, with ALERT folded in before the hypothesis.
