Arise
A Counterfeit of the Only Power That Beats Death

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 2026

Last week we watched the trial forge Jinwoo into something new. This week we get the payoff the whole show has been building toward, and it's the moment I most wanted to slow down and talk to you about.

Jinwoo becomes the Shadow Monarch. He stands over the fallen, the monsters he's beaten, even mighty enemies, and he speaks one word: "Arise." And they rise. Death lets go of them at his command, and they stand back up as shadow soldiers, an army that obeys him alone. It is one of the most visually stunning beats in modern anime, and I get why it makes the hair on your arms stand up. A man speaks, and the dead obey.

I want to enjoy that scene with you. I also want to teach you something that matters more than any scene, which is how to watch with your eyes open. Because what looks like the show's greatest power is, measured against reality, its biggest lie.

Naming the Counterfeit

Let's say the hard thing plainly, then I'll show you why it's good news. What Jinwoo does is necromancy: a man raising the dead by his own strength and bending them to his will. The Bible does not treat that as neutral. God put it on the forbidden list, not because He's stingy with power, but because it's a grab at the one thing that was never ours to take.

"There shall not be found among you any one that... useth divination, or an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch... or a necromancer. For all that do these things are an abomination unto the LORD." (Deuteronomy 18:10–12)

Now hear me, son, I am not telling you the anime is evil or that you should feel guilty for liking it. A counterfeit twenty is only worth talking about because there's a real twenty somewhere. The reason "Arise" thrills you is that it's reaching for something true and deep in you: the longing for death to not get the last word. That longing is from God. The show just hands you a fake version of the thing you actually want. Good discernment isn't refusing to enjoy a story; it's enjoying it while knowing exactly where it stops being true.

Here is the real "Arise." No army was raised to serve a monarch's pride; one Man walked out of His own grave, and now offers His life to His enemies.

The Real Arise

The Shadow Monarch raises the dead to grow his power. Jesus raises the dead to give His life away. That's the whole difference, and it's everything. Jinwoo commands corpses from the outside; Christ went down into death Himself, on purpose, and came back up with the keys.

"Jesus said unto her, I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live." (John 11:25)

Look at how He says it. Not "I have a technique that raises the dead," but "I am the resurrection." It's not a power He picked up by leveling. It's who He is. And His resurrection isn't a one-off magic trick; it's the first one of a harvest, the promise that everyone who is His will rise too.

"But now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept... For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive." (1 Corinthians 15:20–22)

And here's the part that should land on you personally, because there's a kind of death worse than the one in the dungeon. Before Christ, every one of us is already a kind of walking dead man, spiritually flat-lined, going through the motions, unable to make ourselves alive. We don't need a monarch to recruit us. We need a Savior to raise us.

"And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins... But God, who is rich in mercy... even when we were dead in sins, hath quickened us together with Christ." (Ephesians 2:1,4–5)

The Shadow Monarch wins by his own strength. Christ won by dying, not by command-from-strength; He made a public spectacle of death itself.

Notice the other contrast too. Jinwoo conquers death by being strong enough to dominate it. Jesus conquered death by letting it take Him, then walking out the other side. He didn't out-muscle the grave; He went through it and came back, and on His way He disarmed every dark power that ever held a man.

"And having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it." (Colossians 2:15)

What We Confess

This is what our forefathers held to be true about death and what comes after it; it's the rock under everything I'm telling you:

Q. What benefits do believers receive from Christ at the resurrection?
A. At the resurrection, believers being raised up in glory, shall be openly acknowledged and acquitted in the day of judgment, and made perfectly blessed in the full enjoying of God to all eternity. (Baptist Catechism)

"Raised up in glory." Not raised as someone's shadow soldier, but raised as a beloved son, glorified, acquitted, home for good. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 31, "Of the State of Man after Death, and of the Resurrection of the Dead") confesses it plainly: the bodies of the dead will be raised by the power of Christ, the unjust to judgment, the just to honor. There is exactly one Person in heaven or earth who holds that authority, and His name isn't Monarch. It's Jesus.

Father & Son Talk

  • The "Arise" scene gives you a real feeling, that death shouldn't win. Where do you think that longing actually comes from, and why can't a story finally satisfy it?
  • What's the difference between a man who raises the dead to serve him, and a Savior who dies to raise the dead and serve them? What does each one tell you about its god?
  • Discernment isn't refusing to watch good stories; it's knowing where they stop being true. Name one other show or game you love, and where it's only a shadow of the real thing.

⚔ Today's Quest

Find the one area of your life where you've been spiritually playing dead, the thing you've gone numb on, quit fighting, or stopped praying about. Today, name it out loud and ask the real life-giver, not your own strength, to make it alive again. Then read Ephesians 2:1–5 slowly. That's the whole quest.

Gideon, enjoy the Shadow Monarch for what he is, a great character in a great story. But never confuse the counterfeit with the King. Only One ever beat death from the inside, and He says the word over dead men still: Arise. Make sure it's His voice you're listening for.

"I am the resurrection, and the life: he that believeth in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live." (John 11:25)
