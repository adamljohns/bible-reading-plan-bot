Carry the Ones Who Can't
What Strength Is Actually For

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

Son, this episode is called "What Is This, a Picnic?" and I want you to sit with why that title is a joke, and then why it stops being funny once you see what's really happening.

Here's the setup. To legally enter a C-rank gate, the law says you need eight hunters. Jinwoo doesn't need eight; he could clear the whole dungeon himself. So his young partner, Jinho, goes and rounds up six hunters nobody else wanted: men who are injured, green, struggling, one of them fighting a bottle. They come to the gate expecting to risk their lives. Instead Jinwoo tells them the truth: only he and Jinho are going in. The other six are paid three million won apiece just to stand outside and stay alive. When the White Tiger Guild's men show up and find seasoned-looking hunters lounging around in the grass while one man clears a dungeon, it looks ridiculous to them. Like a picnic.

But look closer, because there's a whole theology of manhood buried in that "ridiculous" scene. A strong man just used every ounce of his strength to make sure the weak ones went home breathing. That is not weakness dressed up as mercy. That is exactly what God made your strength for.

Strength Is a Shield, Not a Scoreboard

Think about what Jinwoo could have done. He could have driven those six into the dungeon as bodies, as a legal formality, and let the weak ones die for the headcount. Plenty of guilds in that world would have. He could have stood over them and made them feel small for being injured, green, or broken. He did neither. He took the danger onto himself and let them stand down with their dignity and their paycheck intact.

That instinct, whether the show knows it or not, is lifted straight out of Scripture. The whole New Testament turns strength on its head. In the world's math, strong means you get served; in God's math, strong means you carry.

“Now we who are strong ought to bear the weaknesses of those without strength — and not just please ourselves.” (Romans 15:1)

Read that again slowly. Ought to. It's not a nice bonus for the strong; it's an obligation that comes attached to the strength. If God has made you capable, competent, tougher than the man next to you, then part of what that capability is for is bearing what he can't carry. The strong man who uses his strength only to win, only to prove he's above the weak, has misunderstood the whole gift.

“Bear one another's burdens — and so fulfill the law of Christ.” (Galatians 6:2)

Here is the trap I want you to see, son, because it's the one strong men fall into constantly. When you are gifted, it is so easy to treat the weak as a scoreboard: proof of how far ahead you are. The world trains you to measure yourself against the man who's struggling and feel taller. But a real man does the opposite. He measures his strength by how much weight he can take off someone else's back, not by how far he's outrun them.

Nobody Is Disposable Muscle

The six weak hunters were the "throwaway" ones every other guild sidelined. Jinwoo treated them as men to be protected, not headcount to be spent. God does the same with the parts the body is tempted to write off.

The reason the White Tiger hunters laughed is that in their world, weaker hunters are consumables. You bring them to fill a slot; if they die, that's the cost of doing business. Jinwoo refused that math. And in refusing it, he stumbled onto something the church has confessed for two thousand years: the weak are not spare parts. They are indispensable.

“On the contrary, it is much truer that the members of the body which seem to be weaker are necessary. And those members of the body which we deem less honorable, on these we bestow more abundant honor — and our less presentable members become much more presentable.” (1 Corinthians 12:22–23)

Paul is describing the body of Christ, but the principle exposes the lie underneath the "picnic" joke. The weaker members get more honor, not less. The kingdom of God is not a hunter's guild where the low ranks are expendable. It's a body where the man who's limping gets carried, and the one strong enough to carry him counts it a privilege, not a burden.

There's one more mark of a real man in this episode, and it's quieter. Lee Joohee, the healer who has walked beside Jinwoo since the beginning, has been through too much. The dungeons have wounded something in her that no potion heals, and she decides to retire and go home. Jinwoo doesn't guilt her. He doesn't call her a quitter or make her feel like she failed the team. He lets her lay the burden down with honor, and they part warmly, each wishing the other well.

Learn that, son. Part of leading the weak is knowing when a faithful person has carried their share and needs to rest. A worn-out soldier who has served well is not a coward. Sometimes the most honoring thing a strong man does is release a tired one, with a blessing and not a barb.

“He will feed His flock like a shepherd; He will gather the lambs with His arm, and carry them in His bosom, and gently lead those who are with young.” (Isaiah 40:11)

The Gate We Could Never Clear

We are the six who could never clear the gate. Christ went in alone, cleared what we never could, and came back to carry the weak all the way home.

Now let me lift your eyes past the shadow to the real thing it's pointing at, because this whole episode is a picture of the gospel and I don't want you to miss it.

You and I are the six hunters. That's the honest truth of it. We are the injured, the inexperienced, the ones with a bottle or a besetting sin we can't beat. There is a gate in front of every one of us, the wrath our sin has earned, and we could not clear it. Not with our best effort. Not with more training. We would die inside it. We are, every one of us, the ones who cannot carry themselves.

And Jesus did exactly what Jinwoo did at that gate, only infinitely truer. He looked at the ones who could never survive what was inside, and He said: stand down. I'll go in alone. He took the danger, the whole of it, onto Himself. He walked into the wrath we had coming and cleared it, and He walked out the other side.

“For while we were still helpless, at the right time Christ died for the ungodly.” (Romans 5:6)

"Without strength." That's us. That's the six. Christ didn't wait for us to level up and pull our weight; He went in because we couldn't. And this is where the gospel breaks the anime's frame entirely. Jinwoo protected the weak from the outside; he was strong the whole time. But Christ, to reach us, made Himself weak. He became low to win the low.

“To the weak I became weak, that I might win the weak. I have become all things to all men — so that I may by all means save some.” (1 Corinthians 9:22)

And once He's cleared the gate, He doesn't leave the weak stranded outside. He carries them home. He gathers the limping, the traumatized, the ones like Joohee who have nothing left, and He gives them rest. That's the invitation the whole Bible has been building toward.

“Come to Me, all who are weary and heavy-laden — and I will give you rest. Take My yoke upon you, and learn from Me — for I am gentle and humble in heart. And you shall find rest for your souls. For My yoke is easy, and My load is light."” (Matthew 11:28–30)

What We Confess

This is not a new idea I dreamed up from a cartoon. It's what our forefathers confessed about how the saints are bound to carry one another; it's the rock under everything I'm telling you:

"All saints...being united to one another in love, they have communion in each other's gifts and graces, and are obliged to the performance of such duties, public and private, in an orderly way, as do conduce to their mutual good, both in the inward and outward man." (1689 London Baptist Confession, ch. 27, "Of the Communion of Saints")

Hear those words: obliged, mutual good, inward and outward man. The 1689 Confession says the strong Christian owes his gifts to the weaker one, not as charity he can take or leave, but as a duty of love. And the Baptist Catechism drills the same duty right down into the commandments: to love your neighbor is to actively guard his life and his good as if it were your own. A man who hoards his strength for himself, or who despises the brother struggling beside him, has broken the very law he thinks his strength makes him better than. Strength was never a scoreboard. It was always a stewardship.

Father & Son Talk

  • The White Tiger hunters saw six strong-looking men resting and called it a picnic. When have you looked down on someone for being sidelined, without knowing the real story? What did you assume?
  • Where in your life are you the strong one right now, physically, socially, spiritually? Name one specific person whose burden you're actually in a position to carry, and one honest reason it's tempting to just outrun them instead.
  • Jinwoo let Joohee retire with honor instead of shame. Is there anyone in your life who's tired and needs permission to rest, not a guilt trip to keep grinding? How could you be the one to bless them out of the fight instead of shaming them in it?

⚔ Today's Quest

Find one person this week who is weaker than you in some real way, tired, behind, struggling, and take a piece of their load off their back without making a show of it or keeping score. Carry a bag, cover a shift, sit with the one everyone else wrote off. Do it quietly, the way Jinwoo paid the six and said nothing. Then read Galatians 6:1–2 slowly and ask God to make bearing burdens your reflex, not your afterthought. That's the whole quest.

Gideon, the world will tell you strength is for winning, for standing on top of the pile of everyone you outran. Don't believe it. Your strength is a gift on loan, and it was given to you the way a shield is given to a soldier: to put your body between the danger and the ones who can't defend themselves. You are one of the six who could never clear the gate on his own. Never forget that; it will keep you humble. And a King went in alone for you. Now go be a small shadow of Him for somebody weaker than you.

“Now we who are strong ought to bear the weaknesses of those without strength — and not just please ourselves.” (Romans 15:1)
