Martin Luther: The Hammer, Not the Whole Church

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Martin Luther (1483–1546) posted the Ninety-five Theses in 1517, translated the German Bible, and left a corpus the whole Protestant house still lives in. Receive the doctrine. Repudiate the late anti-Jewish pamphlets. He is not the whole Reformation.

One city is not the map

Martin Luther is the man a classroom puts first, and for cause. German Reformer, 1483 to 1546, born at Eisleben, Augustinian, priest, doctor and professor of Bible at Wittenberg. The dictionary walks the anguish: Rome in 1510, the tower work on Romans, the protest against Tetzel’s indulgence trade, the theses on the church door at Wittenberg on 31 October 1517. That date is the traditional mark. It is not the birthday of every man who loved the gospel before or after it.

Excommunication followed. The Diet of Worms followed. The dictionary records the traditional line, here I stand, I can do no other. Treat it as the traditional report, which the dictionary prints, not as a stenographer’s tape. What is solid is the sequence: hidden at the Wartburg by Frederick, New Testament in German in 1522, full Bible in 1534, marriage to Katharina von Bora in 1525, six children, a pastor’s death in 1546. The Small Catechism, the Large Catechism, the Smalcald Articles, the Galatians and Romans work. That is a life. It is not a census.

The just shall live by faith

The dictionary hangs his discovery on Romans 1:17. Here is the line as the Bible Translation Engine prints it, not as a slogan on a shirt.

Romans 1:16-17: “For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, for it is the power of God to salvation for everyone who believes, for the Jew first and also for the Greek. For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith to faith; as it is written, “The just shall live by faith.””

Habakkuk 2:4: “Behold the proud, His soul is not upright in him; But the just shall live by his faith.”

Galatians 3:11: “But that no one is justified by the law in the sight of God is evident, for “the just shall live by faith.””

Ephesians 2:8-9: “For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, lest anyone should boast.”

The dictionary’s other Luther page names five recoveries under his influence: Scripture alone as final authority, justification by faith alone, grace alone, Christ alone as Mediator, glory to God alone. Five recoveries. Not five men. A man can hold a sola and still be a sinner with a pen. Luther was.

Receive the hammer. Repudiate the pamphlet.

The dictionary’s own warning is the one this house will keep. Do not turn him into a plaster saint, and do not let the late anti-Jewish writing, including On the Jews and Their Lies (1543), define the whole corpus or be shrugged off as a footnote. It is a foul part of a large life. Repudiate it by name. Keep the catechism, the German Bible, and the doctrine of justification. A man who cannot do both is either a fan or a cynic. Be a Christian.

Wittenberg carried a recovery. It did not own the church.

So what

If you are a husband and a father, take the catechism seriously and the celebrity lightly. Teach your house that a man is justified by faith apart from the deeds of the law, and that a hero’s late sins are still sins. Then go read Zurich and Geneva before you tell a boy there were three reformers and the story is closed.

Romans 3:28: “Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith apart from the deeds of the law.”

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

  1. John Wycliffe — forerunner
  2. Jan Hus — forerunner
  3. Martin Luther — Wittenberg
  4. Philip Melanchthon
  5. Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich
  6. Heinrich Bullinger
  7. Martin Bucer
  8. William Tyndale
  9. Thomas Cranmer
  10. Hugh Latimer
  11. Nicholas Ridley
  12. John Calvin — Geneva
  13. John Knox
  14. Theodore Beza
  15. There is no headcount

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
