The Power That Takes, and the Power That Gives

All For One vs One For All: The Two Kingdoms in Every Man

Two powers, two kings, and the war for the throne of your chest.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

Son, of everything this show has handed us, this is the frame I most wanted to sit you down for. Two powers with almost the same name. One is called All For One. One is called One For All. Say them slow and you can hear the whole Bible in the word order.

Here is what the story does, in my own words, so we keep it honest. There is a villain who has spent lifetimes taking. He can reach into another person, pull the gift right out of them, and stack it onto his own pile. Every power he owns used to belong to somebody else. He is strong the way a thief is rich; everything on him was carried in from another man's house. His whole kingdom is built on the same verb, over and over: take.

And across from him there is a power that works the other direction. It cannot be stolen. It cannot be seized. It only ever moves one way, hand to hand, an older bearer pouring himself into a younger one, and the strange thing about it is that it gets stronger as it is passed, because every man who carries it for a while adds his own portion before he hands it down. One power hoards and grows heavy. One power gives and grows deep. The screen calls them All For One and One For All. I paused it right there, because I have read that contrast before, and it was not in a comic book.

Two Names for the Same Word

We have done this move before in these letters, you and I. We take one word and hold it up to the light and find there are two men living inside it. We did it with meddling, back in Part 3; the world calls him a busybody, Scripture calls him a watchman. Do it now with the word power.

To the flesh, power is a thing you get. You accumulate it, you defend it, you make sure the pile with your name on it is bigger than the pile next to you. To the Spirit, power is a thing you spend. You receive it only so you have something worth pouring out, and you measure a man not by what he has gathered but by what he has given away. Same word. Two kingdoms. And your own Lord settled the argument in one sentence, quoting Himself through Paul:

“In everything I showed you that by working hard in this manner you must help the weak — and remember the words of the Lord Jesus, that He Himself said: 'It is more blessed to give than to receive.'"” (Acts 20:35)

Read that twice, Son. Not "it is nobler." Not "it is more polite." More blessed. The giving man is the happier man; the account runs uphill. The whole logic of All For One, take so you will have, is not just wickeder than the gospel, it is dumber than the gospel. The thief spends his whole life getting rich and stays poor in the only ledger that clears.

The Flesh Is All For One

Now I have to tell you the part that is not on the screen. You do not have to go looking for a supervillain to find the taking-power. You were born carrying a small quiet version of it, and so was I. Paul draws the two kingdoms right through the middle of a single Christian man:

“But I say, walk by the Spirit, and you will not carry out the desire of the flesh. For the flesh sets its desire against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh — for these are in opposition to one another, so that you may not do the things you please.” (Galatians 5:16–17)

That is the fight. Not out there between a hero and a monster; in here, between two kings who both want the throne of your chest. The flesh is All For One. Its native grammar is mine: my comfort, my name, my appetite, my being right, my getting the last word, my pile bigger than his. It does not announce itself as a villain. It shows up as a preference, a mood, a very reasonable-sounding reason you deserve the thing you are about to take. James follows the taking-power all the way down to where it lives and what it breeds:

“But if you have bitter envy and selfish ambition in your heart, do not be arrogant and so lie against the truth. This wisdom is not the kind that comes down from above — but is earthly, natural, demonic. For where envy and selfish ambition are, there is disorder and every evil thing.” (James 3:14–16)

Trace that acronym for me, because I have watched it run in my own chest and in the chest of every man I have ever counseled. The autopsy of the taking-power reads the same every time: envy looks at what another man has and wants it pulled onto his own pile; ambition makes his name the point of every room; strife is what happens when two piles want the same square foot; and the coroner writes the same cause of death at the bottom, confusion and every evil work. That is All For One in a man who has no cartoon powers at all. He does not steal quirks. He steals credit, steals the last word, steals his wife's peace, steals his kids' patience, and calls it standing up for himself.

The Spirit Is One For All

So there is the autopsy. Here is the antidote, and it runs in the exact opposite direction, because the Spirit is One For All. Where the flesh reaches in to take and keep, the Spirit reaches down to give and be spent. Paul writes the whole ethic in two verses, and if you memorize nothing else from your old man this month, memorize these:

“Do nothing from selfish ambition or empty conceit. Rather, in humility consider others as more important than yourselves. Do not look out only for your own interests, but also for the interests of others.” (Philippians 2:3–4)

That is the giving-power, written as a command. And notice the show accidentally preached it: the power that could only be handed down, never seized, is the one that grew stronger every generation. That is not a writer's gimmick, Son; that is a law of the kingdom. The kernel of wheat that will not fall and die stays a single kernel, alone; the one that gives itself into the ground bears much. All For One ends heavy and alone with a mountain of stolen gifts and no son to give them to. One For All ends light, poured out, and multiplied, because it was built to be handed off.

Here is where the anime, honest as it is, runs out of runway, and I have to point past the screen. Even the giving-power in the story is a shadow, a good shadow, of the real thing. Because the truest One For All in the universe is not a torch passed between heroes. It is a King who had all the power there is, and who did not clutch it like the villain would. He did not count His equality with God a thing to be grabbed and stacked on His pile. He emptied Himself. He was spent to the last drop, given away on a cross, so that the power He earned could be poured into men who could never have seized it. All For One takes life to keep it and loses it. The Lord Jesus gave His life away and God handed Him a name above every name. That is the pattern under the pattern. Every real hand-off you will ever make is a small echo of His.

Which King Are You Feeding?

Practically, Son, the two kingdoms do not fight once and settle it. They fight at breakfast. They fight over the controller, over whose turn it is, over the tone your brother used, over the credit you did not get. And you are always feeding one of the two kings. The flesh grows on every mine you indulge; the Spirit grows on every his you choose. You will never accidentally drift into self-giving. Water runs downhill; the flesh is downhill; taking is the default. Giving is the thing a man has to choose on purpose, in the Spirit, before he feels like it.

This is not you white-knuckling your way into being a nicer person. Hear me on the order, because I will die on this hill: you do not give in order to become a son. You give because you already are one. The Father has already accepted you in Christ, already secured you, already handed you His own Spirit as the down payment. You are not taking your way toward His approval; you are spending out of a fullness He already gave you for free. The man who is trying to earn it will always, underneath, be All For One, because an empty man can only take. Only a loved man can truly give. Get the identity first, and the giving comes up out of you like water out of a spring.

So when the taking-power flares up in you tomorrow, and it will, you do not negotiate with it and you do not hate yourself over it. You name it. That is the flesh; that is All For One; I reckon it crucified with Christ, and I am not its servant. Then you turn around and do the opposite on purpose. You hand the thing over. You take the smaller piece. You let him have the last word. You spend your oil and your wine and your minute on someone who cannot pay you back. Put on your war face and give something away. That is not weakness, Son. That is the strongest thing a man ever does, because it is the only power that was ever able to save anybody.

Stand your post. Hold the line. Be the man who opens his hand.

One For All. Never all for one.

Peace,
Dad

A note to Gideon

Bud, you are nineteen, and you are already stronger than you know, and that is exactly why I needed you to see this one. Strength is not the question. The question is which direction your strength points. A strong man who takes is just a bigger thief; a strong man who gives is a father, a friend, a watchman, a Christ-shaped man. I have spent years learning this the slow way, falling into the taking-power and getting back up, over and over, because that has been the theme of my whole life, getting back up. I would rather hand it to you clean. Do not build a pile. Build a hand-off. Everything real I have, I am spending on you on purpose, and one day you will spend it on a son of your own. That is how the giving-power works. I love you. Now go.

— Dad

Father & Son Talk

Three questions for the couch

  1. Name one thing you caught yourself taking this week, the last word, the credit, the bigger piece, the mood everyone had to work around. What king were you feeding when you took it?
  2. Acts 20:35 says giving is more blessed, more genuinely happy, than getting. Where in your own life have you actually felt that true, and where do you still, if you are honest, only half believe it?
  3. The truest One For All is Jesus, who had all the power and spent Himself instead of hoarding it (Philippians 2). If you really believed you were already fully accepted and secure in Him, what is one thing you would stop grabbing for?
⚔ Today's Quest

Make one hand-off

Before the sun goes down, give one thing away on purpose that the flesh wanted to keep. Hand your brother the bigger portion. Let someone else have the win. Spend fifteen minutes, or a few dollars, on someone who cannot pay you back and will not even know to thank you. Do not announce it. All For One needs an audience; One For All does not. Do it quiet, and notice which king felt fed at the end of the day.

What We Confess

The taking-power has a name

Scripture does not call the flesh a personality type or a bad habit. It calls it sin, and our fathers in the faith taught us to name it plainly. The Baptist Catechism (Keach) asks the question and answers it in one clean line:

"What is sin? Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God."
Baptist Catechism, Q. 17

Any want of conformity, that is the taking-power even before it moves a muscle, the crooked bent of a heart that wants mine where God commanded his. And the 1689 Confession traces that bent back to its root: in the Fall, our first parents reached out to take what was not theirs to take, and by that first act "the guilt of the sin was imputed, and corrupted nature conveyed, to all their posterity," so that "we are utterly indisposed, disabled, and made opposite to all good, and wholly inclined to all evil" (1689 London Baptist Confession, ch. 6, Of the Fall of Man, of Sin, and of the Punishment Thereof). That is why self-giving never comes naturally and taking always does. All For One is not a villain we watch; it is the nature we were born with, and only the Spirit of the true One For All can crucify it and give us a heart that opens its hand.
