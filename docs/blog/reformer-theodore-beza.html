Theodore Beza: The Second Man at Geneva

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Theodore Beza (1519–1605) followed Calvin at Geneva, rectored the Academy, and carried the Huguenot case. The Tabula is a diagram, not a new gospel. The long post is the lesson.

A poet who got sick and left France

Theodore Beza is the reminder that a recovery has to survive its genius. Born in 1519 at Vézelay to a noble house, died in 1605. Classical letters at Orléans under Melchior Wolmar, who had also taught Calvin. Law in 1539. A praised poet, the Iuvenilia of 1548. Then a serious illness, a conversion, flight to Geneva, and a public marriage to Claudine Denoss. Greek at Lausanne from 1549 to 1558. Calvin brought him to the new Academy as rector in 1559, and he held that rectorship until 1599.

When Calvin died in 1564, Beza took the moderatorship of the Company of Pastors through 1580 and the practical lead of Geneva until his own death. Four decades of a second man. The churches that despise second men do not survive first funerals.

What he added, and what he diagrammed

The dictionary lists the contributions without romance. The Tabula Praedestinationis of 1555, a diagram of a supralapsarian scheme of election and reprobation. The Confessio Christianae Fidei of 1560. The Annotationes on the New Testament, Greek text, Latin translation, notes. The Codex Bezae, a fifth-century Greek-and-Latin manuscript of the Gospels and Acts, which he presented and which now sits at Cambridge. Diplomacy for the Huguenots at the Colloquy of Poissy in 1561, through the French wars of religion.

A diagram is not a verse. Beza’s table has been used ever since as a short cut around Romans. Do not take the short cut. Read the apostle.

Romans 9:15-16: “For He says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whomever I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whomever I will have compassion.” So then it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy.”

Romans 8:29-30: “For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom He predestined, these He also called; whom He called, these He also justified; and whom He justified, these He also glorified.”

John 6:37: “All that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.”

The second man’s job is to keep the gospel, not to draw a chart that replaces it.

So what

If you are the man after the founder, in a church, a ministry, or a family business, Beza is your patron not because of the Tabula but because he stayed. Rector, moderator, correspondent, a Greek text in the next generation’s hands. Do that. And when you are tempted to settle an argument with a diagram, put the diagram down and read John 6:37 out loud. The Father gives. The Son receives. The one who comes is not cast out. Any chart that cannot live with that sentence is too clever for the church.
