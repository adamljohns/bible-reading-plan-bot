You Can Be a Hero

How the God who sees past the scoreboard calls the last man in the room.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So Gideon and I were on the couch again the other night, a bowl of something salty between us, starting a Japanese cartoon about a boy who wanted to be a hero and had every reason in the world to believe he never could. You need the setup, Son, because the setup is the whole point.

In the world of this show, almost everybody is born with a special power; they call it a "Quirk." Roughly four out of five people have one, so having a power is normal and having none is the freak case. The main character, a kid named Izuku, is the freak case. The doctors run the test and confirm it: he was born with nothing. No power, no edge, no chance, in a world where power is the whole currency of respect.

And the people around him make sure he feels it. His classmates mock him. His closest childhood friend, a gifted kid with a loud, explosive Quirk, treats Izuku's dream like a personal insult and gives him a cruel nickname that basically means "useless." A teacher reads the boy's career goal out loud to the class, and the room laughs. When Izuku finally gets to ask the greatest hero alive, straight up, whether a powerless kid could ever really become a hero, even his idol gives him the honest, crushing answer: running into danger with no power is not how you become a hero, it is how you get yourself killed. Go be a doctor. Go be a cop. Just not this.

So the boy walks home having heard it from everyone who matters to him, in stereo: give it up.

And Son, sitting there next to you in the dark, here is the thing I could not shake. The world had already scored him, and the score was zero.

Then the show turns. A villain grabs that same childhood friend, the one who tormented him for years, and every trained, Quirk-blessed hero on the scene freezes, doing the math, waiting for backup. And the powerless kid's legs start moving before his brain signs off on it. He sprints straight at the monster to save the one person on earth who never once was kind to him, with nothing in his hands and no plan that makes any sense. He does the thing all the powerful people were too calculated to do.

And it is that, not a power, not a test score, not a ranking, that makes the greatest hero change his verdict about the boy. He looks at the kid and tells him the thing the kid had waited his whole life to hear:

"You too can become a hero." — All Might

Read that twice. The word did not come after the boy finally measured up. The word came while he was still, by every number anyone had ever run on him, the weakest one in the room.

Pause the cartoon right there, because God wrote that story first, and He has been writing it into real men for three thousand years.

God Does Not Read the Scoreboard You Read

When God sent the prophet Samuel to a man named Jesse to anoint the next king of Israel, Samuel walked in and did exactly what you and I would do. He looked at the oldest son, the tall one, the one who looked the part, and he thought, that is obviously the guy. God stopped him cold. Not that one. Samuel went down the line, seven impressive sons, and God said no to every one of them. The kid God had actually chosen was not even invited to the lineup; he was out in the field with the sheep because nobody thought it was worth calling him in. That was David.

Here is what God told the prophet when he was still staring at the tall, obvious brother:

“But the LORD said to Samuel, "Do not look at his appearance or at his physical stature, because I have refused him. For the LORD does not see as man sees; for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart."” (1 Samuel 16:7)

The world runs your worth off the outward numbers; your height, your Quirk, your follower count, your bench, your grades, who said yes and who said no. God is running a completely different instrument panel, and it is reading something the world cannot even see. So the men the world benches, God drafts, and He does it on purpose. Listen to Paul say the quiet part out loud:

“But God has chosen the foolish things of the world to shame the wise. And God has chosen the weak things of the world to shame the things which are strong.” (1 Corinthians 1:27)

God does not recruit the strongest hunter. He recruits the weakest one deliberately, so that when the weak man becomes mighty, nobody in the room can mistake whose strength did it. Your weakness is not the disqualifier you have been told it is, Son. In the economy of God, it is closer to the job requirement.

He Called You Before You Could Do Anything

Now look at how far back the call reaches. God grabbed a young man named Jeremiah and told him what had been true before Jeremiah ever drew a breath:

“"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you — and before you were born I consecrated you. I have appointed you a prophet to the nations."” (Jeremiah 1:5)

And Jeremiah answered God with the exact objection the boy in the cartoon threw at his idol. He did not say, "I have no Quirk." He said something close enough to be its twin: I cannot do this, I do not even know how to talk, I am only a kid. And God overruled the objection on the spot:

“Then I said, "Alas, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak — because I am a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am a youth' — because everywhere I send you, you shall go, and all that I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them — for I am with you to deliver you," declares the LORD.” (Jeremiah 1:6–8)

Do not say it. That is God cutting off the "I am too young, too weak, too overlooked" speech before it can get up a head of steam. He knew the boy before He formed him; He set the call in place before the boy could do a single impressive thing to earn it. The call was never a reward for a performance. The call came first, and everything else was meant to grow out of it.

The same God said the same thing straight to a young man through Paul, and Son, I want you to hear it as if it has your name on the envelope:

“Let no one look down on your youthfulness — but rather, in speech, conduct, love, faith, and purity, show yourself an example of those who believe.” (1 Timothy 4:12)

Notice God does not answer the young man's smallness by telling him to go get big first and come back when he is impressive. He tells him to go be an example now, at his age, from exactly where he stands. The call does not wait on the resume.

Identity Comes Before the Achievement

Here is the lie the world hands every young man, and it is the same lie that sits on the powerless boy in that first episode: you are your ranking. Your worth is your output, and until the output shows up, you are a zero.

Scripture flips the order completely. God assigns the identity first, and the achievement flows out of it, never the other way around. He called David "king" while David was still smelling like sheep. He called Jeremiah "prophet to the nations" while Jeremiah was still stammering. If you are in Christ, God calls you "son" before you have won a single thing, and that name is not a participation trophy; it is the opposite of one. It means your worth was settled by Someone who cannot be impressed and cannot be fooled, which means no man with a bigger number than yours can ever take it from you.

I have a way of saying it: God names a man by what He is going to make him, not by what He finds him doing. He found David in a field and called him king. He found Gideon hiding in a winepress and called him a mighty man of valor. He is not describing the boy He sees; He is declaring the man He intends to build.

One honest word about the cartoon, so we read it like men and not like fanboys. The show can name the ache in you; that hunger to matter, to be chosen, to be told you are more than your worst rankings. It names it well. But it cannot fill it, and the hero on the screen is only a shadow of the real thing. All Might hands the boy a borrowed power and it nearly breaks him. The true Hero is Christ, and He does not just tell you that you can be a hero; He actually calls the weak, He actually gives the strength, and He lays down His own life to do it. Take the ache the show names, Son, and carry it past the screen to the only One who can answer it.

What We Confess

This is not a new idea I cooked up on the couch. Our fathers in the faith built it right into the confession and the catechism, so a boy could carry it in his pocket. The 1689 London Baptist Confession, chapter 10, "Of Effectual Calling," teaches that when God calls a man to Himself, He does it out of pure grace, by His Word and His Spirit, drawing a man who was dead in sin and could not lift a finger toward God on his own. The call rests on nothing at all foreseen in the man; not his strength, not his rank, not his usefulness, only the free and special mercy of God. In other words: the call was never something you earned by being impressive. It was something God did because He is gracious. That is the best news a weak man ever heard.

And the very first question our forefathers taught a child to answer settles what a man is even for:

Q. What is the chief end of man?
A. Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy Him forever. (Baptist Catechism, Q.1)

Read that as a hero's manual, Son. Your "end," your purpose, your win condition, was never to out-rank the other men in the room. It is to glorify God and to enjoy Him. A man who truly knows that can be the weakest one on the raid and still be the freest man there, because he is not playing the game that everyone else is losing their soul to win.

Father & Son Talk

  • Where in your life do you feel like the powerless kid; the last one picked, the least likely, the one told to sit down? Name it out loud; do not carry it in silence.
  • The boy in the show believed his worth was his power. Where are you quietly believing that your worth is your output, your grades, your body, or somebody's yes or no?
  • If you really believed God named you before you earned anything, what is one thing you would stop doing to prove yourself, and one thing you would finally have the courage to start?

Today's Quest

The powerless boy became a hero by running toward the thing everyone else backed away from. Yours is smaller and just as real: read Jeremiah 1:4–10, then say one honest sentence out loud, "God, You named me before I earned anything; help me believe it, and help me answer the call in front of me today." That is the whole quest. We will do the next one tomorrow.

Son, you are not behind. You are standing right where every man who ever amounted to anything started; at the bottom, unproven, with the world's verdict already written and God's verdict louder still. The difference between the man who rises and the one who stays a zero forever is not a power he was born with. It is whose voice he decides to believe about who he is, and then whether he will get up and answer the call in front of him.

You can be a hero, bud. Not because you are strong, but because the One who calls you is, and He called you first. Let's go. I've got your six.

“Do not be afraid of them — for I am with you to deliver you," declares the LORD.” (Jeremiah 1:8)

I love you. Now go.
— Dad
