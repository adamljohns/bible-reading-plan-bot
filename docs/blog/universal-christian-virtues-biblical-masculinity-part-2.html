What We All Owe God: The Christian Virtues That Are Not Masculine

By Adam "MOOP" Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries · June 30, 2026 · Series: Biblical Masculinity, Part 2 of 5

Before we can answer what makes biblical masculinity distinct, we have to be honest about what it is not. There is a long list of virtues, commands, and disciplines that Scripture lays on every believer regardless of sex, and any honest discussion of masculinity has to start by acknowledging that ground. If we do not, we end up calling generic Christian discipleship "manhood," which is exactly the failure mode I named in Part 1.

So here is the list, plainly. Every Christian, man or woman, is commanded to:

  • Believe the gospel and rest in Christ alone for salvation (Romans 3:23–24, Ephesians 2:8–9)
  • Repent of sin and turn from it daily (Acts 3:19, 1 John 1:9)
  • Pray without ceasing (1 Thessalonians 5:17, Matthew 6:9–13)
  • Read and obey Scripture (Joshua 1:8, 2 Timothy 3:16)
  • Pursue holiness (1 Peter 1:15–16, Hebrews 12:14)
  • Love God and love neighbor (Matthew 22:37–40)
  • Forgive others as Christ has forgiven (Ephesians 4:32, Colossians 3:13)
  • Bear the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23)
  • Make disciples (Matthew 28:18–20)
  • Endure suffering (Romans 5:3–5, James 1:2–4)
  • Worship in spirit and truth (John 4:23–24)
  • Serve in the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 12:7, 1 Peter 4:10)

These are not negotiable, they are not optional, and they are not masculine. They are Christian. They are owed to God by every redeemed soul on the planet, and any man who tries to call himself a believer while neglecting them is fooling himself.

Here is where many pastors stop, and that is the problem I named in Part 1. They preach this universal list with skill and conviction, the men in the pews nod along, and then the men go home without an answer to the question they showed up with: what is God calling me to as a man, specifically? The pastor has not given them anything they could not have gotten from a sermon written for a women's conference, and over time, the men quietly drift away or, worse, stay and become passive, because they have come to believe that being a Christian and being a man are essentially the same job done by different people.

That conclusion is wrong, and it is also unbiblical. Scripture clearly assumes that the same God who created humanity in two sexes also assigned distinct callings, responsibilities, and offices along that line. The shared list above does not exhaust what God says to men, because God says more, and the next three posts in this series exist precisely to surface what He says.

But before we get there, hear this part well: a man who is failing the universal Christian list cannot recover his masculinity by adding male-specific responsibilities on top of an unrepentant heart. The order matters. Before a man can lead his household, he must first submit to Christ as Lord of his own life. Before he can teach his children to pray, he must first be a man who prays. Before he can call other men to discipline, he must first be disciplined himself.

So the order for the rest of this series is: shared virtues first, male-specific stewardships second, and the foundation under both is union with Christ. We will not separate them again.
