Character, Desire, Responsibility: The Three Legs of Biblical Manhood

By Adam "MOOP" Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries · July 2, 2026 · Series: Biblical Masculinity, Part 4 of 5

A man can hold every responsibility Part 3 listed and still fail, because responsibility without character produces a tyrant, character without desire produces a quietist, and desire without responsibility produces a fool. Biblical manhood requires all three legs, and Scripture builds them in a specific order, on a specific foundation.

The foundation, before anything else, is union with Christ. A real man is first a man who joyfully submits to the lordship of Jesus Christ; everything that follows grows from that foundation. Without that, the rest is moralism, and Scripture has nothing kind to say about moralism dressed up as godliness.

The first leg is character. Gene Getz, in his classic work The Measure of a Man, walked through the elder qualifications in 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1 and found that almost none of them describe competencies. They describe character: above reproach, sober-minded, self-controlled, hospitable, gentle, faithful, disciplined. God appears far more interested in the kind of man you are becoming than the size of your platform, the volume of your output, or the reach of your influence. If a man is gifted but lacking in character, Scripture warns the church not to entrust him with leadership, no matter how impressive his résumé.

The second leg is desire. Emerson Eggerichs in Love and Respect describes six common masculine desires under the acronym CHAIRS: Conquest, Hierarchy, Authority, Insight, Relationship, Sexuality. None of these are commands, and Eggerichs is careful to say they describe common patterns rather than universal mandates, but they map something real. Men, on the whole, are wired for productive conquest, for ordered authority, for analytical insight, for shoulder-to-shoulder relationship, and for sexual fidelity inside covenant. These desires were given for a reason, and discipling young men means helping them aim those desires at biblical targets rather than apologizing for the desires themselves.

The third leg is responsibility. This is where the REAL MAN acrostic I have been teaching for years sits, but I am refining it. The original list was:

  • Rejects passivity
  • Engages intentionally
  • Accepts responsibility
  • Leads courageously
  • Manages faithfully
  • Accounts accurately
  • Never gives up

Every line of that is still true, and I will keep teaching it, but I am adding the foundation explicitly going forward. Before any of those seven behaviors, the real man is the one who has joyfully submitted to Christ as Lord. Without that, a disciplined unbeliever can imitate every external trait on the list, and history is full of men who did. What distinguishes Christian masculinity from worldly masculinity is not merely responsible action, but Spirit-formed character flowing from union with Christ. Paul summarizes the Christian life with words like walk, abide, and imitate Christ, and the goal is not merely to produce men who accomplish great things, but to produce men who increasingly resemble the Son, and from that transformed life, accomplish the works God prepared beforehand for them (Ephesians 2:10).

So when you teach a young man what biblical masculinity is, hand him all three legs in the right order. First, submit to Christ. Second, build character that will hold weight. Third, recognize the desires God built into you and aim them at His targets. Fourth, accept the responsibilities He has assigned, and execute them faithfully whether anyone notices or not. That is the order, and that is the shape.
