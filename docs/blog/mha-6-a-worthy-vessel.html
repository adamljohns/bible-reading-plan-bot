A Worthy Vessel

The gift is given by grace. The vessel is built by discipline.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So Gideon and I were back on the couch working through My Hero Academia, and I was bracing for the good part. Deku had just been chosen; the greatest power in the world was going to be handed to a scrawny, quirkless kid. I figured the next scene was him throwing a truck across a parking lot. Instead, All Might handed him a training schedule and pointed him at a beach buried in other people's garbage. I paused the show. You know by now that when I pause the show, we are about to make it into a thing.

Here is what stopped me. The power was real. The promise was real. The boy had already been chosen, that part was settled. But his body could not hold what was coming. Pour a power like that into an untrained frame and it does not make a hero; it snaps bones and tears muscle off the bone. So before the gift could be poured in, the vessel had to be prepared: about ten months of dragging junk across sand at dawn, of a body being rebuilt rep by rep until it was finally strong enough to carry the thing it had been promised. The gift came first, by grace. The grind came next, so the gift would not destroy the one who received it.

Read that twice. Because that is not a superhero gimmick, Son. That is the difference between being chosen and being useful, and it has a name in Scripture:

Sanctification.

I sent the shape of this out to some of my Mighty Men the next morning, then figured I would flesh it out here for the rest of y'all, and mostly for you.

The Gift Needs a Vessel

Last time we talked about One For All as a power that can only be given, never earned, the gospel with the volume turned up (that was Part 5). So hear me clearly before we go one inch further: you do not discipline your body to become a son. You already are one. Christ was handed to you; you did not earn Him and you cannot lose Him. Identity comes first, always. But the God who saved you is not content to leave you a museum piece on a shelf. He means to use you.

“Therefore, if anyone cleanses himself from these things, he will be a vessel for honor — sanctified, useful to the Master, prepared for every good work.” (2 Timothy 2:21)

Prepared. A vessel does not prepare itself into existence; the Potter makes it. But a vessel does get cleaned, emptied, set apart, and made ready for the Master's hand. The word there is useful. You can be genuinely saved and still, right now, be a cluttered jar the Master cannot easily reach for. Sanctification is the LORD taking a chosen man and making him usable, the ten months on the beach after the gift is already yours.

I Discipline My Body

I spent twenty-one years in the Marine Corps and I have a NASM certification hanging on the wall, so let me tell you what I know in my bones: nobody drifts into strength. You do not accidentally get disciplined. The body is the most honest ledger there is; it keeps a record of every workout you skipped and every one you didn't. Paul knew this, and he reached for the same picture I would reach for at the gym:

“But I discipline my body — and make it my slave, so that, after I have preached to others, I myself will not be disqualified.” (1 Corinthians 9:27)

The older translations say Paul buffets his body, he beats it black and blue, he drives it under like a fighter driving an opponent to the mat. Notice the direction of the leash, though. The body is not the master; it is the slave. The appetite does not set the schedule; the man does. This is not hating the body; it is the opposite. You discipline what you love and intend to use. A soldier maintains his rifle because he needs it in the fight, not because he despises it.

Here is a line I keep coming back to with the men I sit with: meekness is power on a leash. A man with no strength who does nothing is not meek; he is just weak. Meekness is a trained fighter who has learned to hold the punch. Deku disciplined his body not so he could be gentle in the sense of harmless, but so that when the power finally landed, it would move a mountain instead of shattering him. Discipline is not the enemy of power. Discipline is what makes power safe to hand you.

A Living Sacrifice

Now, why do it? Not to earn anything; we already settled that. We discipline the body as an act of worship, because the body is the altar we actually have.

“Therefore, I urge you, brothers, by the mercies of God — to present your bodies as a living and holy sacrifice, acceptable to God, which is your spiritual service of worship.” (Romans 12:1)

Note the order Paul builds it on: by the mercies of God. Twelve chapters of grace come first, and only then, therefore, the altar. The offering is a response, never a down payment. But look at what the offering actually is: your body. Not a warm feeling. Not a Sunday sentiment. The muscle and blood and hours you are made of, laid down on the altar and kept there. And here is the hard part of a living sacrifice, Son: it can crawl back off the altar. Every morning it has to climb back on. That is what makes it a daily thing and not a one-time thing. Worship you can measure by what your body did on Tuesday.

Bought With a Price

There is one more reason the body matters, and it is the one the world hates most: your body is not your own.

“Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you — whom you have from God? And you are not your own, for you have been bought with a price. Therefore glorify God in your body.” (1 Corinthians 6:19–20)

You are a temple with a resident. That reframes everything, especially purity. The world says your body is a playground and the only rule is consent. Scripture says your body is a sanctuary and the owner is God. Those are two entire universes, and you cannot live in both.

Here is a warning I give every man I mentor, because it is how the fall actually happens, and it never announces itself: Opportunity, Concealment, and Ambiguity add up to Permission. When you are alone (opportunity), when no one will know (concealment), and when the line feels blurry enough to argue about later (ambiguity), your flesh will read all three together as a green light. That is the moment. So you decide the thing before the moment, not during it. A disciplined body is not built in the crisis; it is spent in the crisis out of what you banked in the quiet. Deku did not decide to be strong the day the power arrived. He decided it every gray morning on the beach when nobody was watching and quitting was always available.

Sanctification Is a Training Log

Here is the honest word about all of this, and it is the least glamorous sentence in the post: it is slow. Sanctification is not a highlight reel; it is a training log. It is the same handful of unremarkable, repeated obediences, showing up in the Word before the phone, telling the appetite no, laying the body back on the altar, over and over, for years, while it feels like nothing is happening. And then one day you are handed a weight you could not have carried a decade ago, and you carry it, and you realize the beach was the whole point.

You are not being disqualified, Son. You are being prepared. So do not despise the grind.

Build the vessel. Carry the weight. Glorify God in your body.

Peace,
Adam

Father & Son Talk
  1. Deku was chosen before he ever trained. Where in your own life are you tempted to flip that, to train and strive as if the discipline is what makes God accept you, instead of what flows from His already accepting you?
  2. Paul says he makes his body his slave. Name one appetite that is currently the master of your schedule instead of the servant of it. What would putting it on a leash actually look like this week?
  3. Opportunity, concealment, ambiguity. Think about where those three line up for you. What decision could you make right now, in the calm, so that your body is not deciding it alone in the moment?
⚔ Today's Quest

Do one hard physical thing today that no one will clap for. A cold shower, a real workout, twenty minutes of prayer with the phone in another room, one meal you say no to. Small, unseen, and on purpose. Then log it. You are not earning anything; you are hauling one piece of trash off the beach.

What We Confess

1689 London Baptist Confession, Chapter 13: Of Sanctification

The Confession teaches that those united to Christ are "further sanctified, really and personally," by His Word and Spirit dwelling in them; yet this work is imperfect in this life, so that "there abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part, whence ariseth a continual and irreconcilable war, the flesh lusting against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh." (1689 LBCF 13.1–2)

Baptist Catechism (Keach's), on Sanctification

"Sanctification is the work of God's free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness." (Keach's Catechism, Q. 38)

Note both words, Son: God's free grace and more and more. Grace does the work; and the work is gradual, a lifelong war, not a single victory. That is the beach in confessional language.

A Note to Gideon

Bud, I have watched you grow into that frame of yours, and I want to tell you something a father has to say out loud so it sticks. The strength you are building is not for the mirror and it is not for the highlight clip. It is a vessel. God is going to hand you weight one day, a wife, sons of your own, men to lead, seasons that would flatten a soft man, and He is preparing you for it right now in a hundred boring reps you think nobody sees. I see them. He sees them.

Do not wait for the power to arrive before you build the body. Build it now, in the gray mornings, when quitting is right there and no one would blame you. That is where sons are forged. You were chosen before you ever trained; now train like a man who knows it.

I love you. Now go. Love, Dad.
