John Knox: The Galley, Then the Pulpit, Then a Blast You Do Not Have to Keep

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

John Knox (c. 1514–1572) came through Wishart’s martyrdom, a French galley, and Geneva, then led Scotland to the Scots Confession in 1560. Keep the confession. Do not baptize the First Blast.

A notary who buried a preacher and would not stay a notary

John Knox is what the recovery looks like when it has to cross a border and a prison. About 1514 to 24 November 1572. Haddington. Trained at St Andrews, or possibly Glasgow. A priest, a notary, a tutor. Then George Wishart. Knox was with him on the last preaching tour, present at the arrest at Ormiston in December 1545, marked by the martyrdom at St Andrews in March 1546. Some men are converted by a book. Knox was steadied by a dead friend’s courage.

He joined the Protestants in St Andrews Castle, was called to preach in 1547, and when the French took the castle he went to the galleys until 1549. England under Edward gave him a royal chaplaincy. He declined Rochester. He had a hand in the 1552 prayer book. Mary Tudor drove him to the Continent. Frankfurt, then Geneva, 1556 to 1559, under Calvin. That is the making of the man. Galley, then Genevan discipline, then home.

What to keep, and what to leave in 1558

In 1559 he returned and led the revolt against the Catholic regency. The Reformation Parliament of 1560 established the Reformed faith. The Scots Confession and the First Book of Discipline, both 1560, are the doctrinal and governmental foundation. He was principal minister of St Giles in Edinburgh until he died. Confrontations with Mary, Queen of Scots, included. Thunder in the pulpit, included. A nation’s worship pried out of Rome’s hand, included.

Also included, and not to be baptized: the First Blast of the Trumpet Against the Monstrous Regiment of Women, 1558, aimed at the female monarchs of England and Scotland. The dictionary calls it controversial. It is. This house does not take a pamphlet against women rulers as the doctrine of the church. Headship in a marriage is a text. A political program against a queen is not that text. Knox can be the architect of the Scottish Reformation and still have written a blast this ministry will not own.

Acts 17:11: “These were more fair-minded than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness, and searched the Scriptures daily to find out whether these things were so.”

2 Timothy 3:16-17: “All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work.”

Take the confession. Leave the blast. A reformer is not a permission slip.

So what

If you have been through a galley, a bad command, a season that tried to make you an oar instead of a man, Knox is kin. Get to a church that will discipline you, then go home and build something that can outlast your temper. Write the confession. Do not write the blast. Your anger at a corrupt throne is not yet a theology of women, and your scars are not yet a license.

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
