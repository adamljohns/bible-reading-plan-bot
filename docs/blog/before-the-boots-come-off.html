Before the Boots Come Off

Total Fitness that stops at the next PFT is inventory. The honest question only came when the boots were already off.

By Adam “MOOP” Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries

Companion (already live): Know Your Marines. Look Out for Their Welfare.

Watch: My hypocrisy goes only so far · I’m dying. How are you?

A few days ago I wrote that welfare which stops at the next PFT, the next inspection, the next brief, is not welfare. It is inventory. Know your Marines still stands. This is the same charge from another room.

I watched two clips from Tombstone (1993). Doc Holliday after he kills Johnny Ringo. Then Doc in the sanitarium, boots off, lungs gone.

Interesting how a man’s perspective can change from when he takes a life to when he’s losing his own.

Fifteen years ago I wrote Hypocrisy. I used those same lines and put them in the wrong rooms. I had Doc saying “my hypocrisy only goes so far” on his deathbed. He doesn’t. He says that over a dead man, with a badge he will not wear home. On the deathbed he says something plainer.

That mix-up is worth owning. When you still have a gun in your hand, you get to be clever. When you don’t, you get to be honest. Total Fitness that only scores the first three domains is the same mix-up. Physical, mental, social — still a man performing. The last column is the one we hollow out until the boots come off.

Two sentences, not one conversion

The first clip is the oak tree. Ringo came to finish a game. Doc was not as sick as he made out. He used dying as camouflage so he could kill. Wyatt finds the deputy badge on the body. Doc says, “My hypocrisy goes only so far.”

He will borrow the law to finish a private fight. He will not keep the costume. There is a remaining scruple in that. There is vanity in it too. He still wants to be the author of the scene.

The other line people remember is earlier, with a priest: “It appears my hypocrisy knows no bounds.” Same man. Latin with Ringo. A crucifix with a Father. A star with Wyatt. Style is the last weapon a dying gambler has.

Then the sanitarium. No Latin. No huckleberry. Wyatt asks how he is. Doc answers. Wyatt says, “Pretty much the same.”

One man is losing his lungs. The other has already lost the life he thought he was owed. Doc’s last useful order is not about the Cowboys. It is “Live, Wyatt. Live for me.”

That is social fitness with the score sheet gone. That is spiritual fitness without a poster. Family programs can teach four domains and still leave a man alone at 0200. Wyatt did not leave.

What actually changed

I do not think Doc got religion in the last reel. I think he ran out of room to pretend.

Taking a life still lets a man believe he is the exception. Losing his own takes that story away.

Wyatt said the line I keep coming back to, and Doc never said it: one shooting, a man lost his life, and I took it. You don’t know how that feels, and you don’t want to. Doc kills and quotes. Until he cannot.

He named the hole in Ringo. A man who can never kill enough to fill it. Revenge for being born. Doc saw himself in the eyes and hated him for it. Putting Ringo down does not fill Doc. The sanitarium proves it.

Bare feet. He wanted to die with his boots on. He does not. “I’ll be damned. This is funny.” That is closer to Ecclesiastes than to a tract.

If you lead Marines, or men who used to be, this is the spiritual column the Corps named and then often emptied. Identity. Purpose. An eternal horizon. Not a PFT. Not a brief. A man who can still answer “how are you” before the room where that is the only sentence left.

Do not make Kilmer into a Sunday school flannelgraph

I like the guy. I always have. Transparency is why people like him. Understanding that you don’t always do what you say you want to do is still an important first step.

He is not a type of Christ.

The one Man whose mind did not reverse between giving death and receiving it is not in Tombstone. He is in Gethsemane and on the Cross. He did not take the other thief’s place with a borrowed badge.

“And as it is appointed for men to die once, but after this the judgment.” — Hebrews 9:27 (NKJV)

In 2011 I reached for Romans 7 and James 1:22, and I still will. We say one thing and do another. Paul named it. The answer is still, “I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord.” Transformation is possible. Hearers have to become doers.

What I would add now is this: most of us only tell the truth about ourselves when we can no longer pull the trigger. That is late. Psalm 90:12 asks for a heart of wisdom before the sanitarium.

I am a bigger hypocrite because I know so clearly what I am supposed to do. The first step is still to admit it. The next step is not another quote. It is obedience.

The test I named on the Total Fitness post still holds: know the story, watch the growth, speak the Gospel when you have earned a hearing, stay present, pray by name. Do that while the man can still wear his boots. Command is not the cure of souls. Inventory is not welfare.

The question I cannot dodge

So whether your hypocrisy knows no bounds, or only goes so far, the Lord still sees the actual distance.

If you still have strength, do not wait for the boots to come off. Confess now. Make it right now. Live now, and not as a line you saw in a movie. Look out for another man’s welfare now — all the way to the horizon God named.

If you are already in a room like that, the honest report is enough. Ask the question. Stay in the room.

Peace.

Adam
