Biblical Manhood · Solo Leveling · Part 13

When the Strong Ones Fall
Jeju Island and the Trust That Fails

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

Son, last time we closed the first season on a hospital room and a mother opening her eyes; the long climb finally landed on that one bedside, and we said together that for the believer death is only sleep, and One is coming to wake us. Now the story turns a hard corner, and I want to walk this new arc with you carefully, because it teaches a lesson every man has to learn before life forces it on him the hard way.

Here is where the story picks up. There is an island, Jeju, that has been overrun by a monster swarm so bad that the hunters had to abandon it entirely. Now the strongest guilds in Korea gather to take it back, and they invite Japan's elite team to fight alongside them. These are the mightiest people alive, the top-ranked hunters on earth, walking onto that beach with confidence to spare. And the man who has quietly become the strongest of them all, Jinwoo, is not there. He is home, because he just finished spending everything to save his mother. The raid launches without him.

The Beach Full of Champions

Watch how the whole thing is framed at the start, Son, because it is meant to make you feel safe. The two nations even hold a friendly contest of strength before the real fight, S-rank against S-rank, showing off. The mood is swagger. Everyone on that sand is a proven monster-slayer, a national hero, a name people whisper with awe. If raw human power could carry the day, this is the roster you would want. You look at that beach and you think, surely these are enough.

That feeling, that surely these are enough, is exactly the thing the Bible warns a man about. Not because strong men are bad; God makes strong men and calls them to hard work. The warning is about where you set the full weight of your trust. Listen to how David says it after a lifetime of watching armies and heroes rise and fall:

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses; but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.” (Psalm 20:7)

Chariots were the tanks of the ancient world, and horses were the muscle; that beach at Jeju is a beach full of chariots and horses. David is not saying they are worthless. He is saying they are not where a wise man rests his soul. The strong thing you can see is always tempting to lean on, precisely because you can see it. And the Scripture is even more blunt when it names the trap:

“Do not put your trust in princes, nor in a son of man, in whom there is no salvation.” (Psalm 146:3)

The son of man in whom there is no help. Not because he is evil, but because he is mortal; his breath goes out, and he goes back to the ground like everyone else. Hold that verse in one hand, and keep watching the beach.

When the Threat Lands

Then the real threat arrives, and it is nothing like what they prepared for. A new kind of monster rises out of that swarm, the Ant King, and he is not another beast to be swarmed and cut down; he is faster, smarter, and stronger than anything on that beach. Japan's elite team, the ones brought in as the finishing hammer, are torn apart. The plan collapses. The proven heroes, the ranked strongest, the swaggering champions of two nations, are overwhelmed, and men who have never lost in their lives are dying on that sand.

I do not tell you that to be grim, Son. I tell you because it is true to life, and the sooner you know it the better you will stand. Every human hero has a ceiling. Every strong man meets a stronger thing eventually, whether it is another power, or sickness, or age, or the grave that takes us all. The men on that beach were not weak. They were simply human, and human strength runs out. Hear how honestly God's word admits this, even about the young and the fast:

“Even the youths shall faint and be weary, and the young men shall utterly fall; but those who wait on the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles, they shall run and not be weary, they shall walk and not faint.” (Isaiah 40:30-31)

Notice the turn in the middle of that verse. Even the youths faint; even the strong young men fall utterly. That is the beach at Jeju in one line. But then, they that wait upon the LORD renew their strength. The strength that does not run out is never the strength you carry in your own arms; it is the strength you receive by leaning on the One who never tires. That is the whole difference between a man who trusts in chariots and a man who trusts in God.

And there is a personal warning tucked in here for men who are, in fact, strong. The ones most likely to be humbled on that beach were the ones most sure they could not be. The apostle Paul saw it and said it plainly:

“Therefore let him who thinks he stands take heed that he does not fall.” (1 Corinthians 10:12)

The moment you are most certain you cannot fall is the moment you are standing closest to the edge. Confidence in yourself is not the same as trust in God, and Jeju is what it looks like when a man confuses the two.

The One Champion Who Never Falls

Now here is where the shadow turns and points past itself, Son, and I do not want you to miss it. The whole ache of this arc is that we long for a champion who cannot be overwhelmed, a strong man who will not go down, someone we could trust all the way to the bottom and never hit a floor that gives way. Jeju says no man on the beach is that champion. The gospel says there is exactly One who is.

Jesus Christ is the strong man who met the strongest enemy there is, and did not fall. He walked straight into death, the thing that overwhelms every hero on every beach, the Ant King none of us can beat, and He came out the other side alive forever. He is the only man who ever went down into the grave under His own power and got back up under His own power, on purpose, having won.

“and the Living One. I was dead — and behold, I am alive forevermore. And I have the keys of Death and of Hades.” (Revelation 1:18)

Do you see why that changes everything about trust? Every human champion you can name will one day faint, fall, and return to his earth; his breath will go out and his thoughts will perish. But this Champion holds the keys to the very thing that beats everyone else. He does not have a ceiling. He does not have a stronger enemy waiting to overwhelm Him. When you set the full weight of your soul on Him, you are leaning on the one Man in all of history who has already proven He will not give way. He is the same yesterday, today, and forever (Hebrews 13:8); the rock that does not crack when you finally put your whole life on it.

What We Confess

This is not just a feeling I want you to have, Son; it is something the church has confessed for centuries, staked on Scripture. The Baptist Catechism asks who the Redeemer of God's elect is, and answers in words worth memorizing:

"The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ, who, being the eternal Son of God, became man, and so was, and continueth to be, God and man, in two distinct natures and one person, forever." (Baptist Catechism, Q. 25)

Read that last word again: forever. The heroes on the beach are men only, and men run out. Our Champion is God and man in one person, and He continues so forever; His strength has no bottom to hit. And the confession our fathers wrote guards this same hope, that He alone is the sure foundation a man can build his whole soul upon:

"It pleased God, in his eternal purpose, to choose and ordain the Lord Jesus... to be the mediator between God and man; the prophet, priest, and king; the head and saviour of the church, the heir of all things, and judge of the world." (1689 LBCF 8.1)

The whole point of naming Him prophet, priest, and king, head and saviour, heir and judge, is that He fills every office no human champion can hold without eventually failing. Put not your trust in princes; put it in the King who never dies.

Father & Son Talk

  • The men on that beach were the strongest alive, and it still was not enough. Can you name a time you were sure something, or someone, would come through for you, and it just did not? What did that teach you about where to put your final trust?
  • Psalm 146 says even good men return to the earth and their thoughts perish. How is trusting a strong person different from trusting God? Where might you be quietly leaning on a person the way you should only lean on the Lord?
  • Jesus holds the keys of death and never falls. What would actually change in how you face a hard week if you truly believed your Champion cannot be overwhelmed?

⚔ Today's Quest

Name out loud, or write down, the one human hero you have quietly been leaning on to hold your world up: a parent, a friend, a coach, a leader, maybe your own strength. Thank God for that person, then say plainly that they are a gift and not your foundation, and move that weight onto Christ. Then read Psalm 20 slowly, and let the last verses settle in: some trust in chariots, but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.

Gideon, one day the strong men in your life, including your dad, are going to reach the edge of what we can do for you. I am not the champion who never falls, and I would be lying to tell you I am. But I can point you to the One who is, over and over, until it is the first place your heart runs. When the strong ones fall, and they will, there is a King standing over the whole beach who has already beaten the thing that beats us all. Lean the full weight of your soul on Him, Son. He will not give way.

“Some trust in chariots, and some in horses; but we will remember the name of the LORD our God.” (Psalm 20:7)
