Self-Care Is the Wrong Word. Stewardship Is the Right One.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries · October 18, 2026

The man who actually protects, provides, and presides is usually too busy to fight about manhood online. He is at work. He is home. He is tired in the right direction. That man does not need a softer word for discipline. He needs a true one.

Ryan Michler said most of this plainly on the Order of Man episode “What Self-Care for Men Actually Looks Like.” I am not reprinting his script. I am keeping the orders and correcting the two places the talk slips. Credit where it is due. Scripture where it binds.

Ten orders, not ten tips

  1. Wake up on purpose. What you care about gets the first attention. A list is not spirituality. A drifted day is not humility.
  2. Train mind, body, and soul. Read something that can correct you. Strength-train. Open the Bible, pray to God through Jesus Christ, and get to church.
  3. Read to obey. A stack of unfinished books is a badge, not a life. Fewer books. Notes. Application. James 1:22.
  4. Get brothers before the crisis. Divorce, job loss, and grief are late to go looking for men who will tell you the truth. Proverbs 27:17. Hebrews 3:12–13.
  5. Track the money. Every dollar in. Every dollar out. Cut the leak. Stewardship buys time with your wife and children. It is not the prize.
  6. Be home when you are home. Phone down. Wife and children get the man, not the remainder.
  7. Own the miss. No excuse. Admit it. Correct it. Move.
  8. Examine the heart. Anger, sorrow, and guilt are not sins by themselves. Destroying people with them is. Sit still long enough to see whom you hurt.
  9. Write the code before the temptation. Men do not ruin a marriage in one moment. They walk there by small compromises. Job made a covenant with his eyes (Job 31:1). Keep the heart, because from it flow the issues of life (Proverbs 4:23).
  10. Do hard things for a named purpose. Comfort dressed up as calling is a lie. Difficulty that is not tied to a duty is just stubbornness.

That list is stewardship. Ephesians 5:15–16: walk carefully, not as fools but as wise, redeeming the time, because the days are evil. 1 Timothy 5:8: a man who will not provide for his own has denied the faith. None of that is self-care. Self-care asks what soothes you. Stewardship asks what you owe.

Two slips

First: the soul is not trained by “whatever your book is” or “whoever your god is.” Two contrary claims cannot both be true. Jesus Christ is the way, the truth, and the life. No man comes to the Father except through Him (John 14:6). Sanctify them by Your truth. Your word is truth (John 17:17). A men’s podcast can be useful and still be wrong the moment it treats the living God as one option on a shelf.

Second: examining your sin is not Carl Jung’s shadow work. Honest self-examination matters. It does not cleanse you. Staring at the dark either puffs a man up or sinks him. Godly sorrow produces repentance leading to salvation, not to be regretted; the sorrow of the world produces death (2 Corinthians 7:10). Put off the old man. Put on the new. That power is Christ’s, not a technique (Ephesians 4:22–24).

Do one thing tomorrow

Do not collect ten new habits and do none of them. Pick the order you are already failing.

  • Isolated: get one godly man on the calendar this week. Do not wait for the wreck.
  • Home and gone: phone in another room from supper until the children are down.
  • Calling sin “a moment”: write the code tonight. One line you will not cross. Tell your wife.

The culture is loud. The man who leads his own house is the answer that does not need a comment section.
