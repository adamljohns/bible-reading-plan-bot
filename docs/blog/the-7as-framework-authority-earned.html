The 7 A's: What Every Man Needs Before He'll Let You Lead Him

March 23, 2026 · Men's Ministry

By Adam "Moop" Johns | U.S.M.C. Ministries

Framework credit: Jordan Northrup, updated 2023

There's a reason men don't stay in discipleship programs.

It's not that they don't want to grow. It's not that they don't believe in accountability. It's that somewhere between the first conversation and the third session, something breaks — and they disappear. Ghost. Drift. Make excuses. Stop showing up.

I've watched it happen too many times. For years, I thought the problem was them.

Then a mentor showed me a framework that flipped everything. He called it the 7 A's and the 6 F's — and once you see it, you can't unsee it.

The 7 A's: What a Man Must Experience Before He'll Let You In

These aren't nice-to-haves. They are prerequisites for real discipleship. Skip one and you're building on sand. Each one is a door. You walk through all seven before the real work begins.

1. Acceptance

Before a man can change, he has to believe he's welcome as he is. Not his best self. Not his cleaned-up Sunday version. Him. The guy who yells at his kids. The guy who hasn't opened his Bible in three weeks. The guy carrying shame he hasn't told anyone.

If he senses your love is conditional — contingent on performance — he will never let you past the surface.

Jesus didn't wait for people to get it together before He sat with them. The tax collectors, the woman at the well, the man at the pool for 38 years — He accepted them first. Change came after.

"Accept one another, just as Christ also accepted us, to the glory of God." — Romans 15:7 read in BTE

2. Admiration

Men need to be genuinely respected — not flattered, not managed. Respected. A man can smell fake admiration from a mile away and it repels him. But real admiration — seeing his strength, his effort, his heart — unlocks something.

This is why great coaches know their players' strengths before they ever call out their weaknesses. You can't lead a man you secretly look down on.

Find something real to admire. If you can't find it yet, you haven't looked hard enough.

3. Affirmation

Admiration is what you see. Affirmation is what you say. Men are starved for it — not because they're weak, but because most grew up with fathers who withheld it, coaches who used silence as motivation, and a culture that mocks male vulnerability.

Specific, earned affirmation changes men. Not "you're doing great, bro" — but "I watched you choose your family over that opportunity last week. That was a hard call. That was a strong man's move." Say it. Mean it. Watch what happens.

4. Approachability

Can he come to you when he fails? Or does he already know that failure means a lecture?

Approachability isn't about being soft — it's about being safe. A man won't bring you his real problems if he's already rehearsing your disappointment in his head. The most dangerous thing in discipleship is when a man starts performing for his mentor instead of being honest with him.

If he can't come to you at his worst, you'll only ever see his best — and his best doesn't need you.

5. Affection

This one makes men uncomfortable. But it's real. Men need to know the relationship is more than transactional — that you actually care about him, not just his attendance or what he can do for the ministry.

Affection in discipleship looks like: remembering the small things, checking in when there's nothing to check in about, showing up when he's struggling with no agenda. It's love with skin on it.

"We were well-pleased to impart to you not only the gospel of God but also our own lives, because you had become very dear to us." — 1 Thessalonians 2:8 read in BTE

Paul didn't just give men information. He gave them himself.

6. Accountability

Now we're in the harder territory. After the first five A's are established — after a man feels accepted, admired, affirmed, sees you as approachable, and knows you genuinely care — then accountability works.

Accountability without the first five is just surveillance. Men hate being surveilled. They'll comply on the surface and route around you. But accountability inside a real relationship with the first five A's? That's iron sharpening iron.

True accountability means calling him what he is — not just cataloging what he's done. You hold him to his own stated values. And because he trusts you, he doesn't just tolerate it. He wants it.

"Iron sharpens iron, and one man sharpens another." — Proverbs 27:17 read in BTE

7. Authority

This is the whole game.

Authority isn't taken — it's given. A man grants you the right to speak into his life after he's experienced the first six A's. It's the final door. You cannot kick it open. You can only earn the key.

This is why Jesus taught with an authority unlike the scribes and Pharisees — He wasn't quoting other people's opinions. He spoke from relationship, from truth, from demonstrated love. The people gave Him authority willingly because they experienced something real.

A man who grants you authority over his life is the most coachable, transformable human being on earth. He's not agreeing because he has to. He's agreeing because he trusts you — with his growth, his failure, his future.

Get to #7 and nothing is off the table.

The 6 F's: What Happens When the A's Aren't There

Without the 7 A's — especially if #7 is demanded rather than earned — a man doesn't just stay the same. He actively retreats. And it follows a predictable pattern.

1. Fight / Flight / Freeze

The amygdala fires. Something in the relationship feels like a threat — judgment, comparison, exposure. He either attacks (deflects, argues, pushes back), disappears (misses sessions, ghosts), or freezes — shows up but shuts completely down, giving you nothing real.

This is biology. Don't take it personally. Diagnose it.

2. Frustration

The relationship starts to feel like work with no return. He came expecting growth and got guilt. He came for brotherhood and got homework. Frustration is the gap between expectation and experience — and it signals that one or more of the A's is missing.

3. Fatigue

Sustained frustration becomes exhaustion. He's tired of performing. Tired of the gap between who he actually is and who the relationship seems to demand he become. Fatigue is what kills slow.

4. Faking / Facade

A fatigued man stops trying to actually grow and starts trying to look like he's growing. He knows what to say. He knows how to answer the questions. The relationship continues — but nothing real is happening. You're discipling a performance.

5. Forgetfulness of Anything Good

By this stage, the negative weight has buried the positive. He can't remember why he started. The good moments are gone. All that remains is the weight of expectations he can't meet. This is dangerous — not because he's rebellious, but because he's lost.

6. Forced Separation

The relationship ends — by his choice or by the structure collapsing. He sets "healthy boundaries" or simply stops showing up. He leaves more guarded than when he arrived.

This is the outcome you're trying to prevent.

Buy-In Before Build-Up

The first 3–4 sessions of any discipleship or coaching relationship aren't about content. They're about building the A's.

Before you hand a man a reading plan, a growth chart, or a list of habits — establish:

  • Does he feel accepted as he is right now?
  • Can he tell you something real that he's ashamed of?
  • Has he seen genuine admiration from you — not flattery?
  • Does he believe he can come to you when he fails?

Run intake not just as data collection — but as A-building. Every question is an invitation to trust.

Only when you've walked through all 7 does real discipleship begin. And then the 6 F's lose their power — because a man who is fully known and fully loved has nothing to hide and nothing to fear.

The Authority Question

Someone once asked: "What is your authority?"

Right question. The answer isn't your credentials. It's not your ordination. It's not your podcast following.

Your authority is the sum of the A's a man has experienced with you. The degree to which he has been accepted, admired, affirmed, found you approachable, felt genuine affection, and been held accountable in love — that is your authority.

Jesus didn't say "I have authority because I went to Rabbinical school." He had authority because He loved people better than anyone had ever loved them. And they gave Him everything.

"And they were astonished at his teaching, for he taught them as one who had authority, and not as the scribes." — Mark 1:22 read in BTE

Go and do likewise.

Your next step: Pick one man in your life and ask yourself honestly — which of the 7 A's is weak in your relationship with him? Start there this week. Don't add another session or pile on more content — just add one A, and watch what happens.
