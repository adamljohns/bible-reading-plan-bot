The Hero Notebook

Faithful in the small, ready in the wait.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So we were a few episodes deep into that hero show of yours the other night, Son, the one about the boy born with no power in a world where everybody has one. And something snagged in me and would not let go. Long before that kid ever gets his shot, long before anyone hands him a single thing, he is filling notebooks. Page after page. He studies every hero he can find, watches how they move, writes down what makes them win and what makes them break. He has no power, no prospects, and no reason to believe his chance will ever come. And he does the work anyway.

You gave me the look you give me now, the one that says "Dad's about to make this into a thing." Bud, I was already three verses deep. Here is the beat that stopped me:

He prepared for a door that had not opened, in case it ever did.

Read that twice. Because most men do the exact opposite. Most men wait for the door to open, and only then start looking for the key. They want the platform before the practice, the rank before the reps, the calling before the character. That is not how God builds a man. He builds him in the small, in the quiet, in the notebook nobody claps for.

The Boy With the Notebook

Here is the thing the show gets right, and it gets it more right than it knows. The power in that story cannot be earned; it can only be given. It is handed down, one worthy bearer to the next. The boy cannot train his way into it. So on paper, all his study is pointless. Why fill a hundred notebooks for a power you can never manufacture?

And yet the diligence is exactly what marks him out as ready to receive. When the chance finally comes, he is not fumbling around asking what a hero even does. He already knows. He has been in the film room for years. The gift is grace; the notebook is not the price of the gift, it is the shape of a boy who was ready to steward it. Son, hold those two together and do not let go: the power is given, and the faithful man is the one caught preparing when it comes.

That is the gospel wearing a cartoon costume. You did not earn your standing before God; Christ handed it down to those who could never buy it (Eph 2:8-9). And the mark of a man who has actually received grace is not that he now coasts, it is that he gets to work, faithful in the small, because he belongs to the One who gave it. Identity first. The labor is the fruit, never the root.

Faithful in the Small

The Lord does not audition men on the big stage. He watches the small one.

“He who is faithful in a very little thing is faithful also in much — and he who is unrighteous in a very little thing is unrighteous also in much.” (Luke 16:10).

Notice the LORD does not say the little thing does not matter. He says the little thing is the test. The made bed, the finished chore, the homework nobody checked, the workout you did when no one was counting, the notebook you filled with no audience. Faithfulness is not a switch you flip when the cameras arrive; it is a habit you have already worn into your hands. The man who is sloppy in the small will be sloppy in the large, only with more people watching him fail. Boys wait to be great. Men are faithful in the small until greatness has no choice but to find them prepared.

Diligence Has a Destination

Do not hear me preaching busywork. Diligence is not the same as being busy; plenty of anxious men are busy going nowhere. Diligence is skill sharpened on purpose, and Scripture says it has a landing place.

“Have you noticed a man quick and practiced at his craft? It is before kings he will take his stand — not before men of no account.” (Proverbs 22:29).

Solomon is not promising you fame, Son; he is telling you that quiet, compounding skill puts a man in rooms his complaining never could. The boy with the notebook ends up standing next to the very hero he studied, not because he begged for a spot, but because he had made himself the kind of man worth standing next to. Skill is patient. It does not announce itself. It just keeps showing up, one honest rep at a time, until the day it opens a door that talk could never open.

Do the Work of Study

Now here is where a father has to press past the cartoon, because the notebook that matters most is not a study of heroes. It is the study of the Word.

“Be diligent to present yourself approved to God — a worker who has no need to be ashamed, rightly handling the word of truth.” (2 Timothy 2:15).

I love that the old King James keeps the word workman. Handling Scripture is labor, Son; it is a craft you build, not a feeling you wait on. The word behind "rightly dividing" carries the picture of a man cutting a straight furrow or laying a road true, no wandering, no bluffing. You do not get to be a man who rightly handles the truth under pressure if you never opened the book in the calm. Fill the notebook now. Memorize the verse now. Wrestle the hard chapter now, while it costs you nothing but discipline, so that when the storm comes it costs you nothing but a page you already turned. That is what the means of grace are for: the Word, prayer, the ordinances, the gathered church. God has appointed ordinary tools to shape you in the ordinary week. Use them.

Prepare the Horse; the Battle Is the LORD's

And then, right when a driven man is tempted to think his preparation is the whole story, Scripture drops the anchor that keeps diligence from curdling into pride.

“Though the war-horse stands harnessed and waiting for the hour of battle, it is the LORD alone who grants deliverance.” (Proverbs 21:31).

Read that twice, because it holds two things most men split apart. You prepare the horse. Every strap, every drill, every notebook, all of it is on you, and there is no excuse for a lazy stable. And the outcome is not on you at all. The victory belongs to the LORD. So you work like it depends on you and you rest like it depends on Him, because it does. The boy filling notebooks he might never use is living this verse without knowing the reference. He readies the horse for a battle he cannot guarantee, and he leaves the giving of the chance in hands stronger than his own. That, Son, is the shape of a faithful man in the wait: fully diligent, fully dependent, never anxious.

So do not despise the notebook years. The wait is not wasted time; the wait is the workshop. Ready the horse. Trust the LORD with the door.

Father & Son Talk
  1. Where in your life right now are you waiting for a "door" to open before you start doing the work? What is one small, faithful thing you could begin today whether the door ever opens or not?
  2. Luke 16:10 says the little things are the test. Name one small area where you have been sloppy because you figured it did not matter. What would faithful look like there this week?
  3. Proverbs 21:31 says we ready the horse but the victory belongs to the LORD. How do you tell the difference in your own heart between healthy diligence and anxious control? Which one is driving you lately, and how would you know?
⚔ Today's Quest

Start your own hero notebook, the real one. Get a physical notebook or open a fresh note, and today write down one verse you want to know by heart, in your own hand, along with two sentences on what it means and why it matters. Do it again tomorrow. That is the beginning of a workman's habit; small, daily, unwatched, and forming you.

Read together: 2 Timothy 2:15 and Proverbs 21:31.

What We Confess

Good works are the fruit, never the root

Our confession teaches that good works are only those God has commanded in His Word, and that these works, done in obedience, "are the fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith." It is careful to add that our ability to do any good "is not at all of themselves, but wholly from the Spirit of Christ." So the notebook, the diligence, the discipline: none of it earns God's favor. It flows from favor already given. Identity before performance, always.

1689 London Baptist Confession, Chapter 16 (Of Good Works), paragraphs 1–3

And how does the Spirit form this faithfulness in an ordinary week? Through appointed means. Keach's Baptist Catechism asks, "What are the outward means whereby the Holy Spirit communicateth to us the benefits of Christ's redemption?" Answer: "The outward and ordinary means whereby the Holy Spirit communicateth to us the benefits of Christ's redemption, are His ordinances, especially the Word, Baptism, the Lord's Supper, and prayer; all which are made effectual to the elect for salvation." Son, that is your real notebook: the Word, prayer, the table, the gathered church. Ordinary tools God has promised to bless.

The Baptist Catechism (Keach), Question 94

The world will tell you to wait until you feel ready, until you are noticed, until the big moment arrives. The workman knows better. The workman prepares in the dark for a light that has not come on yet, because the readying is his job and the timing is God's. Ready the horse. Fill the notebook. Be faithful in the small.

Especially while no one is watching.

Semper Fi,
Dad

A note to you, Son

Gideon, I have watched you get discouraged in the waiting, and I get it; the door feels bolted and the work feels pointless. Hear me: the notebook years are not wasted years. God is not idle while you prepare, and neither should you be. Do the small thing well today. Read the Word today. Ready the horse today. The victory was never yours to manufacture anyway; it belongs to the LORD, and He has never once been late.

I love you. Now go fill a page.
