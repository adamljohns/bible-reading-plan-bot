THDB: Try Harder, Do Better

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published August 25, 2026  ·  Aim the old sermon instead of throwing it away
TLDR

Most men in conflict get one sermon: THDB — Try Harder, Do Better. I’m not throwing it away. I’m aiming it. Aim it at communicating. Aim it at dwelling with your wife in understanding. Aim it at the half of Ephesians 5 we keep quoting and the half we keep omitting. Culture built a permission structure where her want becomes his assignment and his want becomes his problem to kill. That is not peace. Peace is both of you under the same Lord. And the old line — “there are some things we’ll never understand” — was an incomplete sentence. Add unless. Unless the Spirit gives a greater understanding. Surrender can lead to revelation. Revelation can lead to breakthrough.

The yellow light

I hit the brakes this morning. Could have run it. I wasn’t going that fast. When a red light shows up, you stop.

That’s a decent picture of a closed door. Last week a door I thought was going to stay open… didn’t. Closed door from the Lord. We should be thankful for those. NGL — it still leaves you confused. You thought that one was yours. You look around and you don’t see the next one yet, open or shut.

I’m on my way to sit with a mentor. I’m trying to figure out what I’m going to do when I grow up. I think we all are. I want to mature sooner, not later.

But that is not why I hit record.

THDB

Everything’s got an acronym now. The kids throw NGL around like a comma. Fine. Here’s mine.

THDB. Try Harder, Do Better.

That’s the advice most men get when the marriage is tight, the kids are hard, or work won’t yield. No diagnosis. No Scripture with both sides on the table. Just grind. Be nicer. Lead better. Try harder. Do better.

I’m going to embrace it. Not as a beating. As a direction.

Men can take a hit. We don’t need constant affirmation. We do need to be held accountable. Remember the 7 A’s of authentic relationships. How you do it matters. Disparity matters. Don’t tell me you’re not going to beat up on men and then, fifteen minutes later, you’re doing it and you don’t even know it. Mother’s Day: she can do no wrong. Father’s Day: traditional beat-up. It’s easy to punch a man in the pulpit and call it preaching. We need the truth. We don’t need that.

Give both. Tell it like it is. Then affirm some. Then hold the line.

Permission structures

The main thing I wanted to put in front of the brothers is a marital dynamic — and how to make peace inside it — when the culture has built permission structures so a woman does not have to admit when she sins.

A brother and I wrote a point paper on this before I was doing the blog thing. It still needs to become its own post: the church does not really preach against feminine sins. They exist. They are often the same sins. Pride is pride. In a man it looks one way. In a woman it looks another. I’m not going to catalog it for you. You can think of the examples.

If the only sin the room knows how to name is his, you will never get peace. You will get a managed husband.

The yield that only runs one direction

Watch the counseling script.

Wife wants something. Husband doesn’t. They work it out. The counselor admonishes him: compromise. Yield. Do what she wants.

Husband brings a grievance. He would really like her to do this. She doesn’t want to. Now he has to yield again: she just doesn’t want to, so you need to stop wanting that.

Wait a second.

We point to Ephesians 5 — submit one to another, husbands lay your lives down for your wives — and we act like that’s the whole chapter. Totally omitted: wife, submit to your husband as to the Lord… in everything. Even the small things he wants that she doesn’t feel like doing. Yes. Because God wants you to do some things you may not want to do from time to time as well.

This is not a demand for a ten-course meal and a fantasy morning. That’s not the request. A husband who is beginning with the end in mind will ask for ordinary good — health, order, a household that can actually sleep — and he should not have to apologize for wanting it.

A wife is not a child. A husband is not a tyrant for having a will. If “love” always means he yields, and “peace” always means he kills his want, you have not interpreted Ephesians 5. You have edited it.

THDB, then, is not “try harder to disappear.” It is try harder to tell the truth without making her the enemy. Do better at asking like a husband, not a prosecutor. Do better at receiving a no without baptizing bitterness, and at refusing a culture that calls her sin “her truth” and his desire “control.”

The incomplete sentence

I used to say: as husbands, there are some things we have to understand that we will never understand. Application of dwell with your wives with understanding (1 Peter 3:7) — because they are different.

That was an incomplete sentence.

I needed unless after the second understand.

There are some things we may never understand — unless the Holy Spirit supernaturally reveals a greater understanding of those things.

When we surrender, that can lead to a greater revelation. That revelation can lead to breakthrough.

So yes. THDB. Try harder. Do better. At communicating. At dwelling with her in understanding. At not calling the sentence finished before the Spirit has spoken.

Closed doors still confuse. Yellow lights still ask you to brake. Grow up anyway.

I love you brothers. I’ll be in touch.
