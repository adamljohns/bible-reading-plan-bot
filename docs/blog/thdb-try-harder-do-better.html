THDB: Try Harder; Do Better

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published August 25, 2026  ·  A drive-to-the-mentor memo, cleaned up
Listen to this post · 6 min · Voice: Pops
TLDR

Most men in a tight marriage get one sermon: try harder; do better. I am not throwing that away. I want it aimed at how we talk, at dwelling with our wives in understanding, and at the half of Ephesians 5 we keep quoting while we skip the other half. If her want becomes his assignment and his want becomes a sin he has to kill, that is not peace. Peace is both of you under the same Lord. I used to say there are some things we will never understand, and I left a word off that sentence. The word is unless: unless the Spirit gives more light. Surrender can open that, and then you see something you could not see before.

The yellow light

I hit the brakes this morning. I could have run the yellow, but I was not going that fast, and when a red light shows up you stop.

That is a decent picture of a closed door. Last week a door I thought would stay open did not, and I take that as a closed door from the Lord. We should be thankful for those. Not gonna lie (NGL), it still leaves you confused. You thought you had that one, but you look around and you do not see the next one yet, open or shut.

I am on my way to sit with a mentor, still trying to figure out what I am going to do when I grow up. I think we all are. I want to mature sooner, not later.

That is not why I hit record on my phone to the Content, Kind, Long-suffering Peacemakers group on Signal, the same group linked on the homepage. I share thoughts there in long form pretty regularly, and this time I decided to turn one of those memos into a blog post.

THDB

Everything has an acronym now, and the kids throw NGL around like a comma, so here is mine: THDB. Try harder; do better.

That is the advice most men get when the marriage is tight, the kids are hard, or work will not yield. There is no diagnosis and no Scripture with both sides on the table, just grind, be nicer, lead better, try harder, do better.

I am going to keep it, not as a beating but as a direction.

Men can take a hit. We do not need constant affirmation, but we do need to be held accountable, and the way you do that matters. Remember the 7 A’s of authentic relationships, because disparity matters. Do not tell me you are not going to beat up on men and then, fifteen minutes later, you are doing it and you do not even know it. On Mother’s Day she can do no wrong, and on Father’s Day it is the traditional beat-up. It is easy to punch a man in the pulpit and call it preaching. We need the truth (alētheia), and we do not need that. Give both: tell it like it is, then affirm some, then hold the line.

Permission structures

The main thing I wanted to put in front of the brothers is a marriage dynamic, and how to make peace inside it, when the culture has built permission structures so a woman does not have to admit when she sins.

A brother and I wrote a point paper on this before I was doing the blog thing, and it still needs to become its own post. The church does not really preach against feminine sins, but they exist, and they are often the same sins. Pride is still pride, even if it looks one way in a man and another in a woman. I am not going to catalog it for you. You can think of the examples.

If the only sin the room knows how to name is his, you will never get peace. You will get a managed husband.

Peace is not him disappearing. Peace is both of you under the same Lord.

The yield that only runs one direction

Watch the counseling script. Wife wants something the husband does not, they work it out, and the counselor admonishes him to compromise, yield, and do what she wants. Then the husband brings a grievance because he would really like her to do something, she does not want to, and now he has to yield again: she just doesn’t want to, so you need to stop wanting that. Wait a second.

We point to Ephesians 5 (submit one to another, husbands lay your lives down for your wives) and we act like that is the whole chapter. What gets totally omitted is wife, submit (hupotassō) to your husband as to the Lord, in everything (Ephesians 5:22–24), even the small things he wants that she does not feel like doing. Yes, because God wants you to do some things you may not want to do from time to time as well.

This is not a demand for a ten-course meal and a fantasy morning. That is not the request. A husband thinking ahead will ask for ordinary good: health, order, a household that can actually sleep, and he should not have to apologize for wanting it.

A wife is not a child, and a husband is not a tyrant for having a will. If “love” (agapaō) always means he yields, and “peace” always means he kills his want, you have not interpreted Ephesians 5. You have edited it.

THDB is not “try harder to disappear.” It is try harder to tell the truth without making her the enemy, do better at asking like a husband instead of a prosecutor, and do better at receiving a no without baptizing bitterness. Refuse a culture that calls her sin “her truth” and his desire “control.”

The incomplete sentence

I used to say, as husbands, there are some things we have to understand that we will never understand. That was me applying dwell with your wives with understanding (gnōsis, 1 Peter 3:7, chapter), because they are different. That was an incomplete sentence. I needed unless after the second understand.

There are some things we may never understand, unless the Holy Spirit shows us more than we had. When we surrender, that can lead to a greater revelation, and that can lead to breakthrough.

So that is THDB: try harder; do better at talking, at dwelling with her in understanding, and at not calling the sentence finished before the Spirit has spoken. Closed doors still confuse and yellow lights still ask you to brake, but grow up anyway.

I love you brothers. I will be in touch.
