Nicholas Ridley: The Supper Was Worth the Fire

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Nicholas Ridley (c. 1500–1555), Bishop of London, was a principal hand on the 1552 prayer book’s Communion service and was burned with Latimer on 16 October 1555. He also preached for Jane Grey. Name both.

A bishop converted by a ninth-century book

Nicholas Ridley is the sacrament man in the English trio. About 1500 to 16 October 1555. Willimoteswick in Northumberland. Pembroke Hall, Cambridge. The Sorbonne and Louvain. Master of Pembroke in 1540. The dictionary says the turn came in the early 1540s through Bertram of Corbie, a ninth-century treatise against a bodily-presence reading of the Supper. A reformer converted by an old book is a rebuke to men who will only read what was published after their favorite podcast.

Under Edward he was Bishop of Rochester, then London in 1550. With Cranmer he was a principal architect of the 1552 Book of Common Prayer. The revised Communion service carries his care: not Rome’s transubstantiation, not Luther’s consubstantiation. The dictionary says his prison farewell and the Oxford disputation papers still hold that Reformed sacramentology. I have not re-edited those papers here. The death that followed them is not in doubt.

A bad political bet, and a true confession

When Edward died, Ridley preached at Paul’s Cross for Lady Jane Grey. The dictionary calls it a fatal political miscalculation. It was. Mary took the throne. Ridley was arrested, imprisoned at Oxford with Cranmer and Latimer, tried in 1554, and burned with Latimer on 16 October 1555 in that same ditch outside the Bocardo. A man can be wrong about a succession and right about the body and blood. Do not let the first error eat the second truth. Do not let the second truth excuse the first error.

1 Corinthians 11:24-25: “Take, eat; this is My body which is broken for you; do this in remembrance of Me. … This cup is the new covenant in My blood. This do, as often as you drink it, in remembrance of Me.”

John 6:63: “It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh profits nothing. The words that I speak to you are spirit, and they are life.”

The cup proclaims the Lord’s death until He comes. It does not become the king’s hostage.

So what

Know what you believe about the Table before a committee asks you to be flexible. Ridley did the reading early, in an old book, and when the fire came he was not inventing a position in the smoke. Do the reading now. And if you have made a Jane Grey bet in public, a cause you dressed up as providence, own the miscalculation without dropping the gospel to pay for it.

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

  1. John Wycliffe — forerunner
  2. Jan Hus — forerunner
  3. Martin Luther — Wittenberg
  4. Philip Melanchthon
  5. Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich
  6. Heinrich Bullinger
  7. Martin Bucer
  8. William Tyndale
  9. Thomas Cranmer
  10. Hugh Latimer
  11. Nicholas Ridley
  12. John Calvin — Geneva
  13. John Knox
  14. Theodore Beza
  15. There is no headcount

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
