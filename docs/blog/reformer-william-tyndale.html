William Tyndale: The Bible in the Bale of Cloth

By U.S.M.C. Ministries · drafted with AI assistance, reviewed by Adam Johns  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

William Tyndale (c. 1494–1536) translated the New Testament from Greek and much of the Old from Hebrew, was betrayed at Antwerp, and was killed at Vilvoorde. His point was the Word in a man’s own tongue, and the percentage quoted below is the dictionary’s estimate and not a new audit.

The bishop said no, and the press said yes

William Tyndale is the English-speaking church’s debt, whether the church likes the man or not. He lived from about 1494 to 6 October 1536, and his road ran through Gloucestershire and Magdalen Hall, Oxford, with Greek likely at Cambridge, and he was a priest. He wanted the Scriptures in English for every believer, and when Cuthbert Tunstall, the Bishop of London, would not authorize it, Tyndale left in 1524. The New Testament from the Greek was begun at Cologne in 1525 and finished at Worms in 1526, and it came home in bales of cloth. They banned it, and men read it anyway.

From Antwerp he kept going: the Pentateuch from the Hebrew in 1530, Jonah in 1531, the historical books in manuscript, revisions of the New Testament, and The Obedience of a Christian Man in 1528. Then Henry Phillips betrayed him, and what followed was Vilvoorde Castle, a charge of heresy, and on 6 October 1536 he was strangled and burned. The dictionary records the traditional last words as “Lord, open the King of England’s eyes.” Within a year, Coverdale and Matthew were carrying his English under Henry’s own nose. Tyndale prayed and then did the work the prayer requires, and we should do both as well.

Do not worship a percentage

The dictionary says studies estimate that 75 to 83 percent of the King James New Testament stands substantially on Tyndale. That is the dictionary’s estimate, and since we haven’t audited the count ourselves we won’t preach the percentage. What the pages do show is that the Great Bible, the Geneva Bible, the Bishops’ Bible, and the King James all sit downstream of a man they killed for translating, and that’s enough.

Psalm 119:105 (NKJV): “Your word is a lamp to my feet And a light to my path.”

Romans 10:17 (NKJV): “So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

1 Peter 1:24-25 (NKJV): “All flesh is as grass, And all the glory of man as the flower of the grass. The grass withers, And its flower falls away, But the word of the LORD endures forever.”

Faith comes by hearing and hearing comes by the Word, so a locked Bible is a locked church.

So what

You have in your pocket what Tyndale died to put in a cloth bale, so don’t be casual with it. Read it in the morning before you read the feed, and teach your children the sentences themselves instead of a summary from somebody with a microphone. And if you’re tempted to trust a bishop, a brand, or a study Bible more than the text, remember Tunstall, because authorization is not inspiration.

2 Timothy 3:16-17 (NKJV): “All Scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness, that the man of God may be complete, thoroughly equipped for every good work.”
