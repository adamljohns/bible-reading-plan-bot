When Your AI Reads the Bible Better Than You: The Ethics of AI in Christian Ministry

March 12, 2026 · General

By Adam "MOOP" Johns | USMC Ministries

I recently watched a conversation between C.R. Wiley and the team at ABWE’s The Missions Show about the theology and ethics of artificial intelligence, and I could not watch it as a bystander, since I am one of the people actively building AI-powered Bible study tools for men’s discipleship. Wiley’s core argument is one I think every believer needs to hear. Underneath the technological revolution, he says, AI is a competing eschatology (that’s seminary-speak for “it offers a rival vision of the future”). Transhumanism promises transcendence through technology and Christianity promises resurrection through Christ, so the two are built on the same frame while preaching very different gospels.

Where Wiley really convicted me was in arguing that these technologies, especially once people begin treating them as sentient or divine, function as modern idols: the prompt takes the place of prayer, an algorithm’s approval takes the place of a human being’s, and chatbot wisdom takes the place of divine guidance. As a Marine veteran who now builds AI tools for ministry, I have to face that honestly. I use large language models every single day. I’ve built a Bible translation engine that blends nine English translations with interlinear Hebrew and Greek roots, and I have AI delivering daily Scripture readings formatted with theological commentary. So I had to ask myself: am I building a tool, or am I building an idol?

Wiley also points out something most people don’t think about, which is that LLMs (large language models) are black boxes. Even the engineers who build them can’t fully explain why they produce certain outputs, because what the model does is predict the next word based on statistical patterns across billions of documents, which is a different thing from reasoning. That matters for ministry. When I use AI to surface the places where translations diverge, or to identify a Strong’s concordance entry, the tool is serving the Word. But if I ever start treating the AI’s theological commentary as authoritative, as if the machine understands Scripture, I’ve crossed a line, because the Bible is living and active (Hebrews 4:12), and the algorithm is not.

So here is my conviction, shaped by a literary-grammatical and cultural-historical hermeneutic and anchored in the 1689 London Baptist Confession: AI is a tool, Christ is the Teacher, and the Holy Spirit is the Counselor. I build these tools so that men (husbands, fathers, veterans, leaders) can engage Scripture more deeply. The MOOP Bible Translation Engine doesn’t replace a man sitting with his Bible at 5 AM; it enriches that time by surfacing what he might miss in one translation. In the same way, the daily reading plan doesn’t replace the discipline of the Word but structures it, with Wisdom at dawn, a Husband’s Post at midday, a Father’s Charge in the afternoon, a Citizen’s Stand for civic engagement, and Evening Peace before rest. The AI serves the man and the man serves Christ, in that order.

Wiley calls the church to prepare ethically and spiritually, with discernment and not only with rules, and I agree with him. From down here in the trenches I would add a few things. Use AI, but don’t worship it; if your morning devotional starts with “Hey ChatGPT” instead of “Lord, open my eyes,” you’ve got a problem. Know its limitations, too. It can fetch, organize and cross-reference, but it cannot repent, it cannot worship, and it cannot love your wife for you. Build for the Kingdom and not for the algorithm: every tool I build has to answer one question, which is whether it helps a man lead his family closer to Christ, and if it doesn’t, it gets deleted. And teach your men, because most of the guys in your church are already using AI and just don’t have a theological framework for it, so somebody ought to give them one.

In the Corps we had a saying: “Improvise, adapt, overcome.” AI is the most powerful tool I’ve ever held, more capable than any weapon system I operated in uniform, so what matters isn’t whether you use it but who you are using it for. If the answer is yourself, you’ll build an idol, and if the answer is Christ and His people, you’ll build a ministry. I choose ministry, every time.

Peace,
Adam
