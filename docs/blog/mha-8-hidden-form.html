All Might's Hidden Form

Strength made perfect in weakness, and the treasure God keeps in jars of clay.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So Gideon and I were on the couch again, deep into another stretch of My Hero Academia, when the show pulled back the curtain on its number one hero. For episodes on end he is a mountain of a man, all shoulders and smile, the one everybody runs toward when the sky falls. Then the camera stays a beat too long after the fight, and you see it: the giant deflates into a gaunt, hollowed-out frame, half a body carrying an old catastrophic wound, coughing where the muscle used to be. Son, I paused it. You know by now that when I pause the anime I mean it.

Here is what got me. The strongest man in that world is not actually strong. Not the way the crowd thinks. He can only hold the towering hero-form for a few hours a day, and every year that window shrinks. Underneath, he is spent, scarred, running on fumes. And he grins the biggest right when it costs him the most, because if he lets the fear show, the whole city drinks the fear with him. So he hides the wreckage, not to deceive anyone, but to carry the fear himself so no one else has to. He pours out the little he has left, on purpose, for people who will never see the cost.

The world has one name for that. It calls him invincible. Scripture, watching the same thing, reaches for a different name.

A jar of clay.

The Muscle Is Not the Treasure

Here is the line I want to hand you, Son, before anything else:

“But we have this treasure in earthen vessels — so that the surpassing greatness of the power will be of God, and not from ourselves.” (2 Corinthians 4:7)

Read that twice. The treasure is real; the vessel is common. God puts the surpassing power inside the cracked clay pot precisely so that when the power shows up, nobody mistakes it for the pot. The show gets an echo of this exactly right, and then it stops short, and I want you to see both halves.

What it gets right: the strength everyone in that world leans on is not sitting in the muscle. The muscle is the jar. The muscle is fading. The strength that saves is carried inside a broken man, and it comes out of him only because he keeps deciding to spend it. Where the show stops short is here: its hero is running down toward empty with nothing behind him. The tank drains and does not refill. But the Christian is a jar of clay with a well underneath. When our strength gives out, we are not at the end; we are finally at the beginning of His.

“And He has said to me, "My grace is sufficient for you — for power is perfected in weakness." Most gladly therefore, I will rather boast about my weaknesses — so that the power of Christ may dwell in me. Therefore, I am well content with weaknesses, with insults, with distresses, with persecutions, with difficulties — for Christ's sake. For when I am weak, then I am strong.” (2 Corinthians 12:9-10)

Paul asked three times for the thorn to be pulled out. Three times God said no, and told him why: the weakness was not the obstacle to the power, it was the doorway for it. When you are weak, then you are strong, because the strength is no longer yours to boast in. That is not a slogan, Son; that is the load-bearing wall of the whole Christian life. The world says hide the weakness so the strength looks bigger. God says name the weakness so the strength looks like His.

Two Names for the Same Man

Watch how the world reads that hero, and how heaven reads him, because you will get read both ways your whole life.

The world calls the man behind the smile a fraud waiting to be exposed. Fading. Past his prime. Living on borrowed time, and everyone politely not saying it. The world has no category for a strength that grows through a body breaking down; it only knows the strength that peaks and then betrays you.

Scripture calls that same man something else entirely:

“Therefore we do not lose heart. But though our outer man is decaying, yet our inner man is being renewed day by day.” (2 Corinthians 4:16)

Two men, one body. Outer man wasting, inner man renewed, and both true at once, on the same Tuesday. This is the thing your generation has almost no words for. You have been handed a thousand images of the peak and none of the perishing, so you think the perishing means the end of you. It does not. It is the outer jar cracking so the treasure inside can finally show. The faint get power. The used-up get strength. Hear it straight from Isaiah:

“He gives power to the weak, and to those who have no might He increases strength.” (Isaiah 40:29)

Not to the mighty. To the man who has no might left. That is the address God delivers to. I have said this to you before and I will keep saying it: the theme of my life is getting back up, because I have fallen so much. Twenty-one years in the Marines, three combat tours, a hundred percent disabled body that reminds me every cold morning what it cost, and the truest thing I know about strength is that mine ran out early and often, and His never once did. When we are weak, He is strong; Him in us is the whole game.

The Cross Behind the Curtain

Now here is where the anime becomes a shadow, and I want you to feel the difference, because a shadow is real, and a shadow is also not the thing.

A mighty one who veils his true strength, who takes a body that can be broken, who smiles through agony and spends the last of himself so the ones he loves will not have to be afraid. Son, that is a rumor of the gospel written into a cartoon. It is a shadow cast backward by the only Man who ever fully lived it. The Symbol of Peace hides a broken body to hold off fear for a few more years. Jesus took a body that could be broken, and let it be broken, to hold off the wrath we had earned, forever.

"For though he was crucified through weakness, yet he liveth by the power of God." (2 Corinthians 13:4)

Crucified through weakness. The strongest act in the history of the universe looked, from the outside, like total defeat: a beaten man, a jar of clay smashed on a Roman hill, no muscle, no smile, no rescue coming. The crowd read it exactly like the world reads every weakness, as the end of Him. And it was, from the outside, the emptiest jar there ever was. But the treasure inside was the power of God to save the world, and three days later the well underneath came up and the tomb could not hold the pressure. The hero of the show spends himself and shrinks. Christ spent Himself and rose. One is a shadow. One is the light that casts it.

And this is the pin, Son: your strength does not start with you toughening up the jar. It starts with what is already inside a man who is in Christ. This is not a strength you perform your way into on your best day; it is a strength that is put in you and then most clearly seen on your worst one. Identity before performance, every time. You are not strong because you finally stopped being weak. You are strong because the strength living in you was never yours to begin with, and weakness is just the place where everyone finally gets to see it.

The Autopsy and the Antidote

So let me give you the couplet, the way I give it to my Mighty Men. Here is the autopsy, the four lies your age preaches about weakness, and then the antidote, the four things God says instead. Learn them both.

The autopsy (the lie): Hide it, so you look whole. Fake it, so you look strong. Carry it alone, so no one sees the crack. Fear the day the jar finally breaks.

The antidote (the truth): Name it, and grace comes rushing to the honest man. Own it, and His power rests on you like it rested on Paul. Bring it to the brothers, because a jar of clay was never meant to stand on a shelf by itself. And do not fear the breaking, because for the man in Christ, the breaking is exactly how the treasure gets out. No blessing without breaking. Bless me, God; break me, God; and keep giving me away.

Do not hear one wrong word in that, Son. I did not say "forgive yourself," and I never will; that is not in the Book and it is not on my lips. The weak man does not sit alone and make peace with himself. He brings the weakness to the God whose grace is already sufficient, and to the brothers who will help him carry the jar, and there he finds a strength he could never manufacture. Meekness is power on a leash. The strongest men I have ever known were the ones who stopped pretending the jar was iron.

Father & Son Talk

Three questions to sit with

  1. Where are you spending real energy right now hiding a weakness so people think the jar is stronger than it is? What would it cost to name it out loud to one trustworthy man this week?
  2. The show's hero drains toward empty with nothing behind him; the Christian is a jar with a well underneath. When your own strength runs out, do you actually believe there is more of His waiting, or do you live like the tank is all there is?
  3. Jesus was "crucified through weakness, yet liveth by the power of God." How does it change the way you carry a hard day to know that the emptiest, most broken moment was where the greatest power in history was hiding?
⚔ Today's Quest

Before your head hits the pillow tonight, name one real weakness out loud to one man who loves God, and ask him to pray it over you. Not a vague "pray for me," but the actual crack in the jar. That single honest sentence is where grace gets in.

Hold the Line

The world will tell you to armor up the jar and never let anyone hear it crack. It will sell you the peak and hide the perishing. It will call the smiling, spent, honest man a fraud.

Heaven knows better. The treasure was always in the clay. The power was always made perfect in weakness. The strongest thing you will ever do is stop pretending you are the source, and let the One inside you be strong on the day you are not.

Stand your post. Hold the line. And when the jar cracks, and it will, do not panic. That is just the light getting out.

When you are weak, then you are strong.

A note to Gideon

Bud, you watched the mighty man deflate into that broken frame, and I saw something flicker across your face, like it made you a little sad. Hold onto that. But do not read it the way the show wants you to, as a hero running out of time. Read it the way the Book reads it. You are going to be weak, in ways that scare you, in ways you will want to hide from me and from everyone. Do not hide them. The crack is not your disqualification; it is your address, the exact place God has promised to deliver His strength. I have been the empty jar more times than you know, and He filled it every time I finally stopped pretending it was full. You do not have to be the biggest man in the room. You have to be the honest one with the treasure inside. I love you. Now go.

Semper Fi. Dad.

What We Confess

1689 London Baptist Confession, ch. 8, "Of Christ the Mediator" (8.4): This office the Lord Jesus most willingly undertook; to discharge it, He was made under the law and perfectly fulfilled it, and underwent the punishment due to us, which we should have borne, being made sin and a curse for us, enduring most grievous sorrows in His soul and most painful sufferings in His body. Here is our strength in weakness, purchased by His: the mighty Mediator laid down His might, and was crucified through weakness, that we might live by the power of God.

The Baptist Catechism (Keach's), Q. 28, on Christ's humiliation: "Wherein did Christ's humiliation consist?" Answer: Christ's humiliation consisted in His being born, and that in a low condition, made under the law, undergoing the miseries of this life, the wrath of God, and the cursed death of the cross; in being buried, and continuing under the power of death for a time. The One who had all power emptied Himself of its display, took the jar of clay, and let it be broken; the treasure that came out is our salvation.
