Know Your Marines. Look Out for Their Welfare.

By Adam “MOOP” Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries

We don’t just lead Marines. We shepherd souls. Rank is not the cure of souls. A Marine is not a readiness widget.

The Corps already wrote the line. Know your Marines and look out for their welfare.

I keep seeing that principle laid next to Total Fitness — physical, mental, social, spiritual — and then next to Philippians 2. That is the right move if you mean it. It is a cheap poster if you do not.

Paul is not decorating a leadership brief. He is sending Timothy because Timothy will sincerely care for their state, and he names the usual alternative: everyone seeks his own, not the things which are of Christ Jesus. Philippians 2:19–21 (NKJV). “Genuine concern for your welfare” is the hinge. The English maps onto the Marine principle on purpose.

The four domains are not a church invention laid on alien ground. The institution already named them. Then it often hollowed out the last column. Filling spiritual fitness with identity in Christ, purpose, and an eternal horizon is honest. Earthly peace is real and required — the Marine must be ready for the mission. It is also penultimate. A commander who only asks whether the man is ready for the next event has done part of his duty. He has not done all of it if he is a Christian.

Here is the line I will not let slide: We don’t just lead Marines. We shepherd souls.

Command and the cure of souls are different offices. The CO leads. The chaplain shepherds. A Christian officer may do both in a man — know the story, pray by name, point to Christ — but he may not use the rank structure as a pulpit. If “care for their eternity” becomes coercion with an EGA on it, you have not expanded welfare. You have confused the two cities. If it means you cannot treat a Marine as a readiness widget and still sleep, that is costly grace.

The test is not gold type on a slide. The test is five ordinary actions: know the story, watch the growth, speak the Gospel when you have earned a hearing, stay present, pray by name. Without those, the poster is cheap.

There is a second sequence I like better than the slogan. Professional excellence first. Earn a hearing. Then speak. Then name the Prince of Peace. That is Colossians 3:23 without pretending competence is optional. Character never becomes irrelevant. Character without excellence often never gets a hearing. Both are true.

Jesus is Semper Fidelis will either land or make a good man wince. Theologically it is true — He keeps faith when men and institutions do not. Institutionally it can sound like a hijack of an oath Marines made to one another. I would rather say Christ is the One who remains faithful when the institution drifts. I already know that sentence in my own last year.

Family programs can teach Total Fitness and still leave a man alone at 0200. Welfare that stops at the next PFT, the next inspection, the next brief, is not welfare. It is inventory.

If you lead Marines, or men who used to be, look out for their welfare all the way to the horizon God actually named. Do it without stealing the chaplain’s office. Do it without treating the man as a score.

Peace.

Adam

Adam “MOOP” Johns is a Christ-following husband and father, retired US Marine, military aviator, and combat veteran (21 years of service), and founder of U.S.M.C. Ministries (Uniting, Serving, Mentoring & Counseling) in Fredericksburg, VA.
