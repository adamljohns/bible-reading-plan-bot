A New President, and an Amendment Worth Watching

By Adam “MOOP” Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published June 9, 2026  ·  Updated June 10 with the amendment result

I'm Southern Baptist; I go to an SBC church; so when the messengers gather every June, what happens on that floor is family business to me, not spectator sport. On Tuesday in Orlando the family chose a new president. Willy Rice, senior pastor of Calvary Church in Clearwater, Florida, took 5,217 of the 9,063 ballots cast, a 57.56 percent majority over Josh Powell, pastor of Taylors First Baptist Church in Taylors, South Carolina. He succeeds Clint Pressley of Hickory Grove Baptist in Charlotte, who finished the customary two terms and handed off the gavel.

Rice is no stranger to convention life. He came up through Samford University and took both his M.Div. and his D.Min. from New Orleans Baptist Theological Seminary; he served as president of the Florida Baptist Convention from 2006 to 2008, led the SBC Pastors' Conference in 2015, and put in a term as a North American Mission Board trustee. His nomination leaned on what he calls the Seven Pillars of Renewal, and the line that carried the room was about facing forward: faithfulness to the Word of God in the years ahead, rather than merely protecting the past. Time will tell what that means in practice; the words themselves are the right ones.

The quieter story is the bigger one

The presidential vote made the headlines, but the motion that matters most for the long run came earlier in the first business session, when Albert Mohler stood to formally propose his constitutional amendment on women serving in pastoral roles. The operative language says a cooperating Southern Baptist church “does not act to affirm, appoint, or endorse a woman serving in the office or function of a pastor/elder/overseer, specifically preaching to the assembled congregation.” In plain terms: the convention would write into its constitution what its own confession of faith, the Baptist Faith and Message 2000, already states about the office of pastor.

The process is the part to watch. Standing Rule 6 would ordinarily have sent the amendment off to the Executive Committee to wait a year, but messengers voted Tuesday to suspend the rule, clearing the amendment to come to the floor Wednesday morning at 8:45 for the vote that counts. A constitutional amendment requires a two-thirds vote at two consecutive annual meetings, so even a win this week means a confirming vote in 2027; a similar effort last year drew 60.74 percent of the ballots, a clear majority that still fell short of the bar.

Update, Wednesday June 10: it passed, and not narrowly. Messengers approved the Truth and Unity Amendment 6,028 to 2,026, a hair under 75 percent and comfortably past the two-thirds threshold that sank last year's attempt by a few points. The amendment now needs a second approval at the 2027 annual meeting in Indianapolis before it enters the constitution, so the question isn't fully settled; but a three-to-one margin a year after the same idea fell short is the clearest signal the convention has given on this, and it points only one direction. The language that carried writes the office and the function together: a cooperating church does not affirm, appoint, or endorse a woman serving as pastor, elder, or overseer, including the function of preaching to the assembled congregation.

Why this ministry cares

Our Church Directory now holds more than 28,500 congregations, and over 14,000 of them are Southern Baptist. Every record is scored against the same ten-category rubric, and one of those categories is Gender, because Scripture is not ambiguous about the office in question; 1 Timothy 3 and Titus 1 describe the overseer as the husband of one wife, and we already rate a church that places a woman in the pastoral office RED at minimum. We didn't wait for a constitutional amendment to say so, and we won't need one afterward; the standard was settled long before Orlando. But it matters when the largest Protestant denomination in the country moves to align its governing documents with its confession, and it matters to the thousands of SBC records in our directory whose ratings turn on exactly this question.

We put the news to work the same evening. Rice's church record (Calvary Church Clearwater) now carries the election note and his credentials; Powell's record (Taylors First Baptist) finally has its pastor field verified; and Pressley's church (Hickory Grove Baptist, Charlotte) got a cleanup we owed it anyway, with a stale address corrected to the Harris Campus and a duplicate record merged away. Small things, but a directory earns trust by sweating small things on the day the news breaks, not a month later.

For the reporting itself, credit where due: Baptist Press on the election, The Alabama Baptist on the business sessions, Baptist Standard on the Mohler motion's path to the floor, the Biblical Recorder on the amendment's final form, and The Christian Post and Religion News Service on Wednesday's vote count.
