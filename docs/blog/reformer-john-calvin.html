John Calvin: More Than a Five-Letter Flower

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

John Calvin (1509–1564) wrote the Institutes, pastored Geneva, and commented through Scripture. TULIP is a later summary, not his table of contents. His consent to Servetus’s execution is a real failure. Do not hide it behind the Academy.

Noyon, then the Institutes, then a city

John Calvin is the third city on the classroom map, and the map is already too small for him. French Reformer, 1509 to 1564, born at Noyon. Paris for letters, Orléans and Bourges for law. Reformation convictions around 1533. Flight after the affair of the placards in 1534. The first Institutes of the Christian Religion at Basel in 1536. William Farel would not let him pass through Geneva. Two pastorates there, 1536 to 1538 and 1541 to 1564, with Strasbourg in between, where he pastored French refugees and where Bucer had a hand on him. The Institutes grew through five editions to the 1559 Latin form. That is a life of exposition, not a meme.

Do not reduce him to five letters

The dictionary is blunt, and it is right. TULIP was not Calvin’s coinage. It is a later summary of Dort-era distinctives: total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, perseverance of the saints. Useful as a handle. Useless as a portrait. What he actually gave was commentaries on nearly the whole Bible, grammatical and Christ-centered; an Institutes ordered by the knowledge of God and the knowledge of ourselves; plural eldership; worship under the Word; the Academy of Geneva; a political theology of lesser magistrates. The pastoral letters are warmer than the cartoon.

Ephesians 1:4-5: “just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before Him in love, having predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, according to the good pleasure of His will”

Romans 9:15-16: “For He says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whomever I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whomever I will have compassion.” So then it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy.”

John 6:37: “All that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.”

Romans 8:29-30: “For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom He predestined, these He also called; whom He called, these He also justified; and whom He justified, these He also glorified.”

Read those sentences in the letters Paul wrote, not in a comment thread. Election is not a whip for a proud man. It is a mercy for a dead one. The one who comes to Christ, He will not cast out. If your “Calvinism” cannot say that last line in John’s wording, you have a system, not a Savior.

Servetus

The dictionary names the infamous failure and I will not step around it. In 1553 Calvin consented to the execution of Servetus. The dictionary says it reflects the era more than a distinctive doctrine, and that it remains a warning about the church tangled with the sword. Both clauses can stand. An era explains a sin. It does not justify it. Do not defend the stake to protect the Institutes. The Word did not need that fire.

Geneva was a school and a church. It was not a license to burn a man for a bad christology.

So what

If you want Calvin, want the commentaries and the eldership and a man who knew himself as well as he knew God. Do not want a five-point badge and a short temper. And if you hold a little authority, in a house or a board, remember Servetus before you confuse your office with the judgment seat. Mercy is not an elective in Romans 9. It is the point of the quotation from Moses.

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

  1. John Wycliffe — forerunner
  2. Jan Hus — forerunner
  3. Martin Luther — Wittenberg
  4. Philip Melanchthon
  5. Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich
  6. Heinrich Bullinger
  7. Martin Bucer
  8. William Tyndale
  9. Thomas Cranmer
  10. Hugh Latimer
  11. Nicholas Ridley
  12. John Calvin — Geneva
  13. John Knox
  14. Theodore Beza
  15. There is no headcount

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
