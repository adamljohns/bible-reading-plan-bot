John Calvin: More Than a Five-Letter Flower

By U.S.M.C. Ministries · drafted with AI assistance, reviewed by Adam Johns  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

John Calvin (1509–1564) wrote the Institutes, pastored Geneva, and commented through Scripture. TULIP is a later summary and not his table of contents, and his consent to Servetus’s execution is a failure that should not be hidden behind the Academy.

Noyon, then the Institutes, then a city

John Calvin is the third city on the classroom map, and the map is already too small for him. He was a French Reformer, born at Noyon, who lived from 1509 to 1564. Paris gave him letters, and Orléans and Bourges gave him law. His Reformation convictions date from around 1533, his flight from 1534, after the affair of the placards, and the first Institutes of the Christian Religion from Basel in 1536. William Farel would not let him pass through Geneva, and he held two pastorates there, from 1536 to 1538 and from 1541 to 1564, with Strasbourg in between, where he pastored French refugees and where Bucer had a hand on him. The Institutes grew through five editions to the 1559 Latin form. A life like that is a life of exposition, and it doesn’t fit on a meme.

Do not reduce him to five letters

The dictionary is blunt, and it’s right: TULIP was not Calvin’s coinage. It is a later summary of Dort-era distinctives (total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, perseverance of the saints), useful as a handle and useless as a portrait. What he gave the church was commentaries on nearly the whole Bible, grammatical and Christ-centered; an Institutes ordered by the knowledge of God and the knowledge of ourselves; plural eldership; worship under the Word; the Academy of Geneva; and a political theology of lesser magistrates. The pastoral letters are warmer than the cartoon.

Ephesians 1:4-5 (NKJV): “just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and without blame before Him in love, having predestined us to adoption as sons by Jesus Christ to Himself, according to the good pleasure of His will”

Romans 9:15-16 (NKJV): “For He says to Moses, “I will have mercy on whomever I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whomever I will have compassion.” So then it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy.”

John 6:37 (NKJV): “All that the Father gives Me will come to Me, and the one who comes to Me I will by no means cast out.”

Romans 8:29-30 (NKJV): “For whom He foreknew, He also predestined to be conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brethren. Moreover whom He predestined, these He also called; whom He called, these He also justified; and whom He justified, these He also glorified.”

Read those sentences in the letters Paul wrote, and not in a comment thread. Election is a mercy for a dead man and no whip for a proud one, and the one who comes to Christ, He will not cast out. If your “Calvinism” can’t say that last line in John’s wording, you have a system and not a Savior.

Servetus

The dictionary names the infamous failure and we won’t step around it. In 1553 Calvin consented to the execution of Servetus. The dictionary says it reflects the era more than a distinctive doctrine, and that it remains a warning about the church tangled with the sword, and both clauses can stand, because an era explains a sin without justifying it. Don’t defend the stake to protect the Institutes. The Word did not need that fire.

Geneva was a school and a church, and it gave no one a license to burn a man for a bad christology.

So what

If you want Calvin, want the commentaries and the eldership and the man himself, who knew himself as well as he knew God, and leave the five-point badge and the short temper alone. And if you hold a little authority, in a house or on a board, remember Servetus before you confuse your office with the judgment seat. Mercy is the point of the quotation from Moses in Romans 9, and it isn’t an elective.
