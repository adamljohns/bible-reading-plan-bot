The True King
Every Monarch Bows to One Throne

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 2026

Last week we pulled the curtain back on the war behind the war, the cosmic fight between Rulers and Monarchs that was always bigger than gates and money. This week the story gives that fight a crown.

The boy who started as the weakest hunter alive is now a monarch. Jinwoo stands as the Shadow Monarch, a king with a throne and an army of shadows that answer to his voice. After all the grinding, all the trials, all the leveling, he finally has the one thing the whole story has been driving toward: a kingdom of his own.

And here is the thing the story is honest about, the thing I want you to notice, son. Even monarchs are not the top of the ladder. In this world, a king with an army still answers to something greater; there is always a higher throne, a power that crowned the powers. Nobody in that story actually sits at the very top. The crown always sits under a crown.

That instinct, that even the highest king must bow to a higher One, is one of the truest things this anime stumbles into. Because there is a throne above every throne, and there is a King who has no King above Him.

Every Crown Is a Borrowed Crown

Watch how the story handles power, because it teaches a lesson it may not even mean to. Every ruler in it got his authority from somewhere; it was granted, inherited, forged, or seized. No one in the story made himself out of nothing. Even the strongest monarch is a created thing wearing a borrowed crown.

That is exactly true of every king who has ever lived on this earth, too. Caesar, Pharaoh, the president, the CEO, your future boss, every one of them holds an authority that was loaned to him and will be taken back. There is only One who never borrowed His crown, because every other crown was made through Him and for Him.

"For by him were all things created, that are in heaven, and that are in earth, visible and invisible, whether they be thrones, or dominions, or principalities, or powers: all things were created by him, and for him... and he is the head of the body, the church... that in all things he might have the preeminence." (Colossians 1:16–18)

Read that slowly. Thrones, dominions, principalities, powers; every monarch in heaven and earth, every shadow army and every human throne, was made by Him and for Him. He is not the strongest king in a long line of kings. He is the King the kings were made for.

Sooner or later every crown gets laid down. The only question is whether you set yours down willingly, or have it taken.

The Word

The Bible does not whisper about Christ's kingship; it shouts it. When John is given a vision of the risen Jesus riding out in glory, he sees a name written where every eye will read it:

"And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS." (Revelation 19:16)

King of kings. Not a king among kings; the King over all of them. Every monarch the world has ever feared is a king He is King of. And here is how He won that title, which is the part that breaks the world's whole idea of power:

"Wherefore God also hath highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name: that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow... and that every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord." (Philippians 2:9–11)

Notice the word "wherefore." He was exalted to the highest throne because He first went the lowest, because He emptied Himself, obeyed, and died on a cross. The crown came after the cross. And His throne is not something He is anxious about, not something He had to fight to keep, because the Father set Him there.

"Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion. I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee." (Psalm 2:6–7)

Where the Shadow Ends

So let's be honest about where the story is only a shadow, because that is how we keep a good story from becoming a small god. Jinwoo's crown was earned by climbing; he grew strong, beat the right enemies, and rose to the top of a brutal ladder. His kingdom is held by his power, and the moment a greater power shows up, his throne is in question. That is how every earthly throne works, and the show is honest about it: there is always a bigger fish.

Jesus is the True and Better King, and He is better at the exact point where every other king fails. He did not climb to His throne; He descended to it. He won His crown not by killing His enemies but by dying for them, and not by surviving the battle but by going into the grave and walking back out. There is no greater power that can come and unseat Him, because He already faced the last enemy, death itself, and beat it. His throne is not at risk. It is the one fixed point in the universe.

And this is the part I most want you to hear, son. Every man bows to something. You will spend your strength serving some throne; the only choice you get is which one. You can crown your appetites, your reputation, your fear, your phone, the approval of people who will forget your name in a week. Or you can bow your knee now, gladly, to the one King who laid down His life to win you, and who will never lose His grip on you.

"...he shall reign for ever and ever." (Revelation 11:15)

One day every knee bends here, the proud and the humble alike. Blessed is the man who bends his now, while it is still a gift and not a sentence.

What We Confess

This is what our forefathers confessed about the throne Christ holds and the rule He exercises over us:

Q. How doth Christ execute the office of a king?
A. In subduing us to Himself, in ruling and defending us, and in restraining and conquering all His and our enemies. (Baptist Catechism)

Look at what kind of King He is. He subdues you, yes, but then He rules and defends you; He restrains and conquers your enemies so you do not have to fight them alone. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 8, "Of Christ the Mediator") puts the kingly office right alongside His work as Prophet and Priest, because the same Jesus who died for you now reigns for you. Jinwoo's army serves his glory; Christ's throne is bent toward your good. That is a King worth bowing to.

Father & Son Talk

  • Every man serves some throne. If we watched a normal week of your life, what would it say you have actually crowned king, where does your time, money, and worry really go?
  • Jinwoo climbed to his crown; Jesus descended to His. Why does the world find a King who wins by dying so hard to believe, and do you?
  • Christ's kingship means He subdues us, but also rules and defends us. Where do you most need Him to fight an enemy for you that you cannot beat on your own?

⚔ Today's Quest

Before your phone, before the noise, before anything else gets to be king today, give the first five minutes to the real King. Sit down, read Philippians 2:9–11 slowly, and out loud tell Jesus He is Lord of this day. Crown Him first, on purpose, before anything else can. That's the whole quest.

Son, you are going to bow to something every day of your life; that is not in question. The only question is whether your King is one who will use you up, or one who already laid Himself down for you. Crown the right One, and crown Him first.

"...KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS." (Revelation 19:16)
