A Philosopher Diagnosed the Problem. Here's What He's Missing.

March 21, 2026 · Culture & News

By Adam "MOOP" Johns | U.S.M.C. Ministries | March 2026

A response to Jared Henderson's "I don't like what the internet is doing to me"

Watch the video

Jared Henderson is one of the sharpest philosophy YouTubers on the platform. 738,000 subscribers. Millions of views. The man can THINK. And in his most recent video — a raw, personal confession — he admits something that 147,000 people watched in four days and collectively said: "Me too."

"I don't like what the internet is doing to me."

He's not wrong. He's just not done.

What Jared Got Right

Henderson draws on three books to frame his struggle:

  1. "Non-Things" by Byung-Chul Han — We're drowning in information that has no substance. Digital "things" aren't things at all. They're ghosts. You can't hold a like. You can't eat a subscriber count.
  2. "You and Your Profile" by Moeller & D'Ambrosio — The internet doesn't just show who you are. It replaces who you are. Your profile becomes your identity. And when the profile underperforms, YOU feel like a failure — even when nothing in your actual life has changed.
  3. "The Score" by C. Thi Nguyen — This is the knockout punch. Nguyen argues that metrics — views, likes, engagement scores — are designed to replace the values they were supposed to measure. You start creating because you love philosophy. You end up creating because the number went up. The score ate the soul.

Henderson's confession: the joy of teaching philosophy has been replaced by the dopamine of checking analytics. He's decided to drop all sponsorships and fund through Patreon and Substack instead.

That takes guts. Most creators won't even admit the problem, let alone sacrifice income over it.

What Jared Is Missing

Here's where a Marine minister has to respectfully push back on a secular philosopher.

Jared correctly identified the disease. But his prescription is incomplete.

"Step away from the internet" is the equivalent of telling an alcoholic to "just drink less." It addresses behavior without addressing the void the behavior fills.

The Real Diagnosis: A Worship Disorder

Augustine of Hippo said it 1,600 years ago: "Our hearts are restless until they rest in You."

What Henderson describes — the compulsive checking, the identity displacement, the metric-driven anxiety — is not just a technology problem. It's a worship problem. Every human being was designed to orient their attention, affection, and identity toward something ultimate. When that something is God, it's called worship. When it's anything else — views, likes, subscriber counts, career success — it's called idolatry.

The algorithm didn't steal Henderson's joy. He gave his worship to something that can't sustain it.

The metrics are golden calves. They shine. They promise validation. And they leave you empty every single time. (Exodus 32 has entered the chat.)

The Solution Jared Can't Offer

Henderson's audience — 147,000 people who watched this video in four days — is starving for what he can't give them:

  • Identity rooted in something unmovable. Not a profile. Not a platform. "I am a child of God" doesn't fluctuate with the algorithm. (1 John 3:1)
  • Community that doesn't require engagement metrics. Real brotherhood. Men who show up at your door, not your comment section. (Hebrews 10:24-25)
  • Purpose that transcends the score. If your calling comes from God, no analytics dashboard can validate or invalidate it. (Colossians 3:23-24)
  • Disciplines that rebuild the soul. Cold showers don't care about your subscriber count. Prayer doesn't have a like button. Fasting can't be optimized. These are the ancient practices that reconnect a man to reality — and to God.

What You Can Do Right Now

If Jared's video resonated with you, here are five action steps that go beyond "step away":

1. Define your identity BEFORE you open the app.
Write down who you are — not what you do. "I am a husband. A father. A man under God's authority." Tape it to your monitor. Read it before you create, post, or scroll.

2. Replace metric-checking with prayer.
Every time you reach for the analytics, pray instead. Not a long prayer. Just: "Lord, the work is Yours. The results are Yours. I am Yours." Watch how fast the anxiety dissolves.

3. Fast from screens one day a week.
A digital Sabbath. Sundown Friday to sundown Saturday (or whatever works). No phone, no laptop, no scrolling. Read a physical book. Talk to your family. Be bored. Boredom is where God often speaks loudest.

4. Find three men who will hold you accountable.
Not online. In person. Men who will ask you hard questions about your screen time, your thought life, your prayer life. The kind of men who will call you out AND call you up.

5. Read Exodus.
Seriously. The whole book. Israel was enslaved to a system that valued their productivity over their humanity. God delivered them — not into comfort, but into covenant. The parallels to our digital enslavement are staggering.

Jared Henderson is brilliant. His diagnosis is accurate. But a diagnosis without a cure is just a death sentence delivered politely.

The cure isn't less internet. It's more God.

"You will keep in perfect peace those whose minds are steadfast, because they trust in you."

— Isaiah 26:3
