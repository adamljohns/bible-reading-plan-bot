Martin Bucer: The Man in the Hallway Between the Houses

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Martin Bucer (1491–1551) reformed Strasbourg, chased peace between Lutheran and Reformed, hosted Calvin, refused the Augsburg Interim, and died as Cambridge's divinity professor. Second-tier is an insult. The hallway was the work.

Converted in the room where Luther debated

Martin Bucer is what happens when a recovery has more than one room and somebody has to walk between them without selling either. Born at Sélestat in 1491, died in 1551. A Dominican teenager, schooled at Heidelberg, converted when he heard Luther at the Heidelberg Disputation in 1518. Released from his vows. Married Elisabeth Silbereisen in 1522, one of the early clerical marriages on the Reformed side. Strasbourg called him in 1523. He stayed twenty-five years.

The dictionary marks four labors. A reformed church and school. Mediating papers: the Tetrapolitan Confession in 1530, the Wittenberg Concord in 1536, an attempt to get Lutheran and Reformed to say the same thing about the Supper. A house for refugees, including Calvin in the Strasbourg years, 1538 to 1541. And books: Romans, Ephesians, the Gospels, and De Regno Christi, his unfinished word to King Edward on a Christian commonwealth.

He would not sign the Interim

After the Schmalkaldic defeat, Strasbourg took the Augsburg Interim in 1548. Bucer would not conform. Cranmer brought him to Cambridge as Regius Professor of Divinity. He died there in 1551, having put his hand to the 1552 prayer book. The dictionary calls him second-tier. That is a historian’s seating chart. Calvin’s exile and England’s second prayer book both run through his study. Sit him where the work was.

The dictionary sets Romans 14:17 beside him: the kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Spirit. I have not fetched that verse from the BTE in this pass, so I will not print a wording. The point of the citation, as the dictionary uses it, is that a mediator still has a kingdom. Peace is not mush.

The man in the hallway is still under the Word. Agreement is not surrender.

So what

If you are the brother both sides will talk to, do not waste it on flattery. Bucer tried to get estranged churches to say the true thing together, and he left when the Interim asked him to say a false thing. That is the order. Seek peace. Refuse the lie. Host the refugee. Then die at your post if the post is Cambridge and the king is a boy who needs a prayer book that tells the truth.
