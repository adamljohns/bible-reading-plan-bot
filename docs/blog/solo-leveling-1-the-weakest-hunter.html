Solo Leveling & Manhood · Part 1

The Weakest Hunter
Why God Starts With the Last Man in the Room

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  June 2026

Gideon, this one's for you, and for any man reading over our shoulder.

You and I have been watching Solo Leveling together. If you've never seen it, it follows Sung Jinwoo, a hunter who clears monster-filled "gates" for a living. There are S-rank hunters who level cities; there are A and B ranks who get rich and famous; and then there's Jinwoo, an E-rank, the lowest there is. The show literally calls him "the weakest hunter of all mankind." He gets hurt on every raid; he nearly dies for pocket change; his own teammates see him as a liability they tolerate.

The very first episode is titled "I'm used to it." That's his whole life in four words. Used to being last, used to being overlooked, used to being the one nobody picks.

Here's why I wanted to start a whole series with this scene, because before Solo Leveling was ever a show, God wrote this exact story, and He gave the main character your name.

"The LORD is with thee, thou mighty man of valour... Go in this thy might... have not I sent thee?" (Judges 6:12,14)

The lowest rank there is, used to being overlooked. The world had already decided what he was; God had not.

The Original Weakest Hunter

When the angel of the LORD shows up to call Gideon, He doesn't find a warrior on a battlefield. He finds a scared young man hiding in a winepress, secretly threshing wheat so the enemy won't steal it. That's the equivalent of an E-rank hunter ducking the real gates. And the first words out of God's mouth are "mighty man of valour," spoken over a guy who is, at that exact moment, the opposite of mighty.

Gideon argues with Him. Listen to how he sees himself:

"Oh my Lord, wherewith shall I save Israel? behold, my family is poor in Manasseh, and I am the least in my father's house." (Judges 6:15)

The weakest clan; the least in the house. Son, that's an E-rank ranking if I've ever heard one. Gideon ranked himself dead last, and he wasn't even wrong about the math. He really was the smallest, from the smallest family, in a beaten-down nation. What he was wrong about was the conclusion. He thought small meant disqualified; God called him anyway, and then made the army smaller (Judges 7) so there'd be no confusion about Whose strength won the battle.

Gideon, called while hiding in the winepress. God names a man by what He's going to make him, not by what He found him doing.

Identity Comes Before Achievement

Here's the lie the world hands every young man, and it's the same lie that haunts Jinwoo in episode one: you are your ranking. Your stats, your strength, your follower count, your bench press, your GPA, the girl who said yes or no. The world measures the man by the output, and then tells him that the output is the man.

The Bible flips the order completely. God assigns the identity first, and the achievement flows out of it. Gideon is "mighty man of valour" before he's swung a sword. You were made in the image of God, and if you're in Christ, a son of God, before you've done a single impressive thing. That's not a participation trophy; it's the opposite. It means your worth was settled by Someone who cannot be impressed and cannot be fooled, which means it can never be taken by a man with a bigger number than yours.

"...the LORD looketh not as man looketh; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart." (1 Samuel 16:7)

And listen to why God works this way; Paul says it out loud:

"God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty... that no flesh should glory in his presence." (1 Corinthians 1:27,29)

God doesn't recruit the strongest hunter. He recruits the weakest one on purpose, so that when the weak man becomes mighty, everyone knows exactly Who did it. Your weakness isn't the disqualifier you think it is; in the economy of God, it's the job requirement.

What We Confess

This is older than the show, older than you and me. Our forefathers in the faith built it right into the catechism, the very first question a child was taught:

Q. What is the chief end of man?
A. Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy Him forever. (Baptist Catechism, Q.1)

Read that as a hunter's manual. Your "end," your purpose, your win condition, is not to out-rank other men; it's to glorify God and enjoy Him. A man who knows that can be the weakest in the room and still be the freest man in the room, because he's not playing the game everyone else is losing their souls to win. The 1689 London Baptist Confession (ch. 4) grounds it deeper still: you were made in God's image. The image came first; the leveling-up is just you growing into what you already are.

Father & Son Talk

  • Where in your life do you feel like the "E-rank," the last picked, the least likely? Be honest; name it out loud.
  • Gideon ranked himself "the least." Was his math wrong, or just his conclusion? What's the difference for you?
  • If your identity were truly settled before any achievement, if you had nothing left to prove, what's one thing you'd stop doing, and one thing you'd finally start?

⚔ This Week's Quest

Jinwoo only levels up by doing the daily quest, even when no one's watching; so do we. Here's Week 1:

  • Daily (7 days): Before any screen in the morning, read Judges 6:11–16 and pray one honest sentence: "God, You named me before I earned anything; help me believe it today."
  • Once this week: Write down the area where you feel weakest. Under it, write 1 Corinthians 1:27; keep the paper.
  • Log it. One line a day in your Quest Log: did you do it, yes or no. Miss a day, the count restarts. No penalty zone, just honesty.

Son, you are not behind. You're at the mouth of the gate, the same place every man who ever amounted to anything started. The difference between the hunter who rises and the one who stays an E-rank forever is not talent, and it never was; it's who he listens to about who he is, and then whether he's willing to do the next daily quest.

Let's go in. I've got your six.

"Have not I sent thee?" (Judges 6:14)
