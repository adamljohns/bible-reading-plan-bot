Hugh Latimer: Play the Man

By U.S.M.C. Ministries · drafted with AI assistance, reviewed by Adam Johns  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Hugh Latimer (c. 1485–1555) preached England towards the gospel, resigned Worcester over the Six Articles, and was burned with Ridley on 16 October 1555. The candle sentence is Foxe’s report, and the death is the fact.

A bishop who gave the see back

Hugh Latimer is the preacher in this English cluster, as distinct from the liturgist and the sacrament man. He lived from about 1485 to 16 October 1555, and his road ran through Thurcaston and Christ’s College, Cambridge, and he was converted around 1524 through Thomas Bilney. Henry liked him when the sermons were against Catholic abuses, and made him Bishop of Worcester in 1535, but in 1539 the Act of the Six Articles tried to nail transubstantiation and the old religion back onto the English church, and Latimer resigned. A see is not a soul, and he would not keep the office at the price of the article.

Henry imprisoned him, and Edward released him and made him the court preacher of the new reign, often at Paul’s Cross. The dictionary says the printed sermons are among the great English Reformation pulpit works. None of them is quoted here, because we did not have the sermons open and won’t fake a line from them, and the office is enough to name: he was chief preacher, and then a prisoner again when Mary took the crown in 1553.

The ditch outside the gate

He was tried at Oxford in 1554, with Cranmer and Ridley, and condemned. On 16 October 1555 he was chained with Ridley in the ditch outside the Bocardo Gate. The dictionary gives the line as Foxe preserved it in the Acts and Monuments of 1563: “Be of good comfort, Master Ridley, and play the man; we shall this day light such a candle, by God’s grace, in England, as I trust shall never be put out.” That is Foxe’s report, which the dictionary records, and a martyr’s last sentence can be remembered in a friend’s book and still be a true charge. The charge is to play the man, which is a different thing from playing the brand.

2 Timothy 2:9 (NKJV): “for which I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; but the word of God is not chained.”

Romans 10:17 (NKJV): “So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Play the man, because the candle is the gospel you would not trade for a see, and it has nothing to do with your personality.

So what

You may never see a stake, but you will see a Six Articles moment: a job, a church, or a family peace, offered to you if you’ll say the table is what you know it isn’t, or if you’ll be quiet while somebody else says it. When it comes, resign the see, and preach in the open while you can. And if you’re the one chained beside a brother, don’t spend your last breath on your reputation. Tell him to play the man.
