Hugh Latimer: Play the Man

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Hugh Latimer (c. 1485–1555) preached England toward the gospel, resigned Worcester over the Six Articles, and was burned with Ridley on 16 October 1555. The candle sentence is Foxe’s report. The death is the fact.

A bishop who gave the see back

Hugh Latimer is the preacher in this English cluster, not the liturgist and not the sacrament man. About 1485 to 16 October 1555. Thurcaston, Christ’s College, Cambridge. Converted around 1524 through Thomas Bilney. Henry liked him when the sermons were against Catholic abuses and made him Bishop of Worcester in 1535. In 1539 the Act of the Six Articles tried to nail transubstantiation and the old religion back onto the English church. Latimer resigned. A see is not a soul. He would not keep the office at the price of the article.

Henry imprisoned him. Edward released him and made him the court preacher of the new reign, often at Paul’s Cross. The dictionary says the printed sermons are among the great English Reformation pulpit works. I have not re-read the folio this morning. I will not fake a quotation from a sermon I do not have open. The office is enough to name: chief preacher, then prisoner again when Mary took the crown in 1553.

The ditch outside the gate

Oxford, 1554, the trial, with Cranmer and Ridley. Condemned. On 16 October 1555 he was chained with Ridley in the ditch outside the Bocardo Gate. The dictionary gives the line as Foxe preserved it in the Acts and Monuments of 1563: Be of good comfort, Master Ridley, and play the man; we shall this day light such a candle, by God’s grace, in England, as I trust shall never be put out. That is Foxe’s report, which the dictionary records. A martyr’s last sentence can be remembered in a friend’s book and still be a true charge. Play the man. Not play the brand.

2 Timothy 2:9: “for which I suffer trouble as an evildoer, even to the point of chains; but the word of God is not chained.”

Romans 10:17: “So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

Play the man. The candle is not your personality. It is the gospel you would not trade for a see.

So what

You may never see a stake. You will see a Six Articles moment: a job, a church, a family peace, offered if you will say the table is what you know it is not, or if you will be quiet while another man says it. Resign the see. Preach in the open while you can. And if you are the one chained beside a brother, do not spend your last breath on your reputation. Tell him to play the man.

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

  1. John Wycliffe — forerunner
  2. Jan Hus — forerunner
  3. Martin Luther — Wittenberg
  4. Philip Melanchthon
  5. Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich
  6. Heinrich Bullinger
  7. Martin Bucer
  8. William Tyndale
  9. Thomas Cranmer
  10. Hugh Latimer
  11. Nicholas Ridley
  12. John Calvin — Geneva
  13. John Knox
  14. Theodore Beza
  15. There is no headcount

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
