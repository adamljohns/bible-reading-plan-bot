The Conversation We're Not Having: What This Costs the Next Generation

By Adam "MOOP" Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries · July 3, 2026 · Series: Biblical Masculinity, Part 5 of 5

I have spent four posts walking the framework, and now I want to land where it actually matters: the young man sitting across from you, the husband you are discipling, the father who came to your men's group last week and has not been back since. The cost of getting biblical masculinity wrong is not abstract, and it is not academic. It is generational, and it shows up in the men we are losing, the homes that no longer have a steady head, and the churches that have quietly drifted from one of their primary functions for sixty years.

So here is what I would tell each of them, in the order they sit at my desk.

To the single young man: Stop trying to find yourself, and stop trying to optimize yourself. Start submitting yourself. Become the kind of man whose word is reliable, whose appetites are governed, and whose vision is clearer than the woman you are pursuing. You are not entitled to a wife, but you are responsible to become a man worth marrying, and that work begins long before any specific woman shows up. Read your Bible daily, build a real fellowship of two or three brothers who know everything about you, get a job that does not embarrass you, and stop waiting for permission to lead the small piece of life God has already given you.

To the husband: Your wife does not need you to be a project manager, and she does not need you to be her therapist. She needs a husband who has a vision for the family, who articulates it clearly, who leads in that direction with both warmth and resolve, and who takes responsibility for outcomes regardless of how they land. Stop apologizing for the calling God placed on you, and start carrying it. Set a weekly family meeting, lead family worship, plan the year intentionally, and pursue your wife on purpose, not on accident.

To the father: Your children are not consumers of your time, they are inheritors of your soul. Whatever you do not pass down on purpose, they will pick up by accident, and the culture is already lined up to disciple them in the absence of your voice. Read the Bible to them, pray over them, name what is true and what is false in clear language, and discipline them in love with the long view in mind. Generations are made or unmade by ordinary fathers who decide ordinary Tuesdays matter.

To the pastor: Preach the universal Christian virtues, yes, and never stop. But also preach the male-specific stewardships. Tell the men in your congregation what Scripture actually says God expects from them as men, not just as believers, and call them to it without flinching. Disciple them in small bands, hold them accountable to real standards, and stop treating their masculine instincts as problems to be managed. The church needs them activated, not domesticated.

To the military officer, business owner, and civic leader: Your sphere of influence is not separate from your discipleship; it is the field on which your discipleship is tested. Lead like a man who fears God more than he fears Q4, who serves the people under his authority rather than extracts from them, and who measures success by faithfulness rather than by metrics that nobody will remember in twenty years.

The Church does not need men who imitate worldly masculinity, and she does not need men who confuse generic discipleship with biblical manhood. She needs men whose hearts have been transformed by Christ and whose lives increasingly reflect the masculine responsibilities God Himself assigned from creation, exercised with Christlike humility and sacrificial love. That is the man the next generation is waiting for. That is the man Scripture is forming. That is the man this ministry exists to build.
