Jan Hus: Safe-Conduct, Then the Fire

By Pastor John Wesley Graves, ThD, DMin  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Published October 1, 2026  ·  Written at Adam Johns’ order. Lives and dates from the MOOP Dictionary. Scripture from the Bible Translation Engine.
TLDR

Jan Hus (c. 1369–1415) preached in Czech at Bethlehem Chapel, followed Wycliffe’s trail, and was burned at Constance after the emperor’s safe-conduct failed. Forerunner, not a man of 1517.

A priest who would not keep the sermon in Latin

Jan Hus was a Bohemian priest, about 1369 to 6 July 1415. The dictionary records a peasant birth, ordination around 1400, a short rectorship at the University of Prague, and a pulpit at Bethlehem Chapel where the sermon was in Czech, not Latin, for ordinary working people. That choice is already a theology. If the Word is for the church, the church is not only the man who can buy a Latin education.

He took his program from Wycliffe, whose books had reached Prague. The dictionary names the pieces: Scripture in the people’s tongue, the cup for the laity as well as the priest, a hard word against indulgence-selling, papal authority refused where it contradicted Scripture, and the church identified first as the body of the elect rather than as the Roman hierarchy. A century before Wittenberg, the argument was already on the table.

The promise, then the stake

The Council of Constance summoned him under safe-conduct from the emperor Sigismund. The dictionary’s account is plain. Hus came. Sigismund repudiated the safe-conduct. Hus refused to recant. He was burned alive on 6 July 1415. The dictionary says he was singing as the flames rose. I was not in the square. I will not decorate a martyrdom the page already states.

His death did not close the matter. The dictionary traces the Hussite Wars and a Czech memory that ran for centuries. It also records that Luther, handed Hus’s writings before the Diet of Worms, said, “We are all Hussites without knowing it.” That is the dictionary’s report of Luther’s remark. It is also the right warning against a headcount. The men of 1517 were downstream of a man Rome had already burned.

Acts 17:11: “These were more fair-minded than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness, and searched the Scriptures daily to find out whether these things were so.”

A safe-conduct that dies at the gate is not a doctrine. The Word Hus would not recant is.

So what

Do not learn courage from a costume. Learn it from a man who came when summoned, refused the lie, and would not purchase his life by denying what he had preached to working people. And do not date the recovery from the first celebrity who survived. Hus did not survive. The sermon did. He is a forerunner. Say so. Then thank God for him without pretending he sat in Calvin’s academy.

Romans 10:17: “So then faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God.”

The fourteen, plus the summary

These fourteen are the men named in the 1 October reply. They are not a closed roll. Forerunners are marked. The long train after them is not in this set.

  1. John Wycliffe — forerunner
  2. Jan Hus — forerunner
  3. Martin Luther — Wittenberg
  4. Philip Melanchthon
  5. Huldrych Zwingli — Zurich
  6. Heinrich Bullinger
  7. Martin Bucer
  8. William Tyndale
  9. Thomas Cranmer
  10. Hugh Latimer
  11. Nicholas Ridley
  12. John Calvin — Geneva
  13. John Knox
  14. Theodore Beza
  15. There is no headcount

The recovery was about the Word in a man’s hands, and faith in Christ alone.
