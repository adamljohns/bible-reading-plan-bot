The DL: Five D's That Kill a Dream (and Five L's That Bring It Back)

March 23, 2026 · Men's Ministry

By Adam "Moop" Johns | U.S.M.C. Ministries

Every man I've watched fall didn't get taken out by a single bullet. It was a slow bleed. Five cuts, each one deeper than the last — and by the time he noticed, the dream was already on life support. His marriage. His calling. His fire. Gone quiet.

A pastor recently mentioned three D's that mark a man's descent. I heard it and thought: No, there are five. And once you see the pattern, you can't unsee it. You'll spot it in your own mirror. In your closest friend. In the guy sitting next to you at church who used to burn bright and now just occupies a seat.

Here's the DL — the real down low — on how a man falls. And how he gets back up.

The Five D's: How a Man Falls

1. Distraction

It never starts with a grenade. It starts with a glance. A man's eyes drift from what matters — his calling, his family, his God — toward something shinier, easier, more immediately satisfying. He's not chasing evil. He just stopped guarding what's precious.

"As a dog returns to its vomit, so a fool repeats his folly." — Proverbs 26:11 read in BTE

Harsh? Yeah. Solomon wasn't here to coddle you.

2. Discouragement

Once distracted, a man starts measuring himself against the wrong scoreboard. Everyone else seems to have it together while he's spinning his wheels. The gap between where he is and where he thought he'd be becomes a weight on his chest. The enemy doesn't need to attack your faith — he just needs to make you feel like your effort doesn't matter.

3. Depression

Left unchecked, discouragement deepens. The man who once carried fire starts going through the motions. He stops dreaming. His soul goes gray. Even David knew this pit: "Why, my soul, are you downcast? Why so disturbed within me?" — Psalm 42:11 read in BTE

The fact that David — warrior king, giant-killer — wrote those words should comfort every man in the gray. It should also challenge him: David didn't stay there.

4. Defeat

Depression, left to fester, hardens into a worldview. The man starts believing the lie: This is just who I am. Things don't change for people like me. He stops fighting — not because he lost the battle, but because he quit before it started. That's not a setback. That's a surrender.

5. Death

Not always the casket kind. Often it's the death of a dream, a marriage, a calling, a legacy. The man who once carried purpose now carries nothing. Something God breathed into existence — killed by a thousand small surrenders.

The Five L's: How a Man Rises

Every D has an L that breaks its grip. The DL isn't just the spiral down — it's the ladder back up.

1. Laughter

Distraction loses its grip when a man is genuinely rooted — confident enough to be lighthearted without being careless. Not laughing at what's sacred. But having the kind of holy humor that refuses to take himself too seriously. A man who can laugh in the middle of chaos while staying locked in on what matters? Hard to knock off course.

"A joyful heart is good medicine, but a crushed spirit dries up the bones." — Proverbs 17:22 read in BTE

2. Love

Discouragement can't survive when a man knows — bone-deep knows — that he is loved. Not for his performance. Not for his output. "While we were still sinners, Christ died for us." — Romans 5:8 read in BTE

When your identity is rooted in the love of God rather than the applause of men, discouragement can't find a landing pad.

3. Liberation

Depression lifts when a man walks in truth — out loud. "You will know the truth, and the truth will set you free." — John 8:32 read in BTE

Liberation isn't a feeling. It's a man who speaks the truth about his situation, drags it into the light, confesses it to a brother. That's how you break the gray. You don't wait until you feel free — you speak until you are.

4. Legacy

Defeat dies when a man remembers why he's fighting. It was never about him. It's about what he leaves behind — for his kids, his community, his King. A man building a legacy doesn't have the luxury of a defeatist mindset. He's constructing something that outlasts him.

5. Life

Every dead dream points toward the one dream that cannot die. The ultimate L isn't winning this life — it's being found faithful by the One who conquered death itself. "I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live." — John 11:25 read in BTE

The Pattern

D → D → D → D → D: Distraction → Discouragement → Depression → Defeat → Death

L → L → L → L → L: Laughter → Love → Liberation → Legacy → Life

Every man has walked the D side. The question isn't whether you've been there. The question is which direction you're headed right now.

Your move: Pick one L. Just one. Start there today. Then tell a brother — because this road was never meant to be walked alone.
