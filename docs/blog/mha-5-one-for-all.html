One For All

Inheritance is not what a man leaves you when he dies. It is what he pours into you while he lives.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So we were back on the couch, Son, another late one, another episode running, and this time the thing that grabbed me wasn't a line of dialogue at all. It was the mechanics of the power itself. I sat there with the remote in my hand thinking, that's it; that's the whole thing; somebody who has never read a page of Paul just drew you a picture of 2 Timothy 2:2 and set it to a soundtrack.

Here is the shape of it, in my own words, no spoilers you can't survive. In this world the central power is called One For All. It has one rule that makes it different from every other ability in the show: it cannot be seized. It cannot be stolen, cannot be bought, cannot be taken by force from a man who does not want to give it. It only moves one way, by a willing hand into a chosen heart. And here is the part that stopped me cold; every man who has carried it added his own strength to it before he let it go, so the power arriving in the youngest hands is heavier, brighter, and more dangerous than it was in the first. It is a lineage you can feel. Generations of men are literally standing behind the boy who holds it now.

Read that twice, because it is a sermon.

The villain of the story runs the exact opposite economy. His power is built on taking; he strips abilities off of other people, hoards them, collects them like scalps, and grows strong by making everyone around him poorer. The writers even named the two forces as mirror images of each other so you could not miss it. One is given for others. One is taken for self. That is not a superhero gimmick, Son. That is the Spirit and the flesh, standing on opposite sides of the same street.

Four Generations in One Verse

Paul, an old man in a cold cell with his own execution on the calendar, writes to a younger man he has poured himself into, and he gives him the handoff order:

“And the things you have heard from me through many witnesses — entrust these to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also.” (2 Timothy 2:2)

Count the generations in that one sentence. Paul is the first. Timothy is the second, the faithful man receiving it. The faithful men Timothy will find are the third. And the "others also," the men those men will teach, are the fourth. Four generations of the same fire, named in a single breath, none of them stealing it, all of them handing it down and feeding it as it goes. That is One For All, five decades before the writers of a Japanese cartoon were born to draw it.

And notice the word Paul chooses. He does not say publish it, or broadcast it, or go viral with it. He says entrust it. That is a deposit word, a handoff word, a word for something valuable you are placing carefully into a set of hands you have watched and tested. The Greek there carries the weight of a treasure left with a trusted friend for safekeeping. You do not entrust your treasure to the loudest man in the room. You entrust it to the faithful one.

You Are the Point of the Whole Chain

Here is where this stops being a Bible study and becomes a father talking to his son on a couch. This is not new; this is old, older than the Marine Corps, older than the church, as old as fathers and sons. The LORD built it into the family before He ever built it into the ministry:

“We will not hide them from their children, Telling to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, And His strength and His wonderful works that He has done.” (Psalm 78:4)

The psalm goes on to say why; so that the next generation would set their hope in God, and teach it to their children, and on and on, a fire handed down until the LORD comes back. And He gave the marching orders for it at the kitchen table, not the pulpit:

“You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up.” (Deuteronomy 6:7)

Sitting in the house. Walking by the way. Lying down. Rising. There is no ceremony in that list, Son. That is a Tuesday. That is a couch and an anime and a dad who won't let a good thing go by without saying, do you see it? The handoff does not happen at a funeral. It happens in ten thousand ordinary evenings, and it is happening to you right now whether either of us calls it that.

My Father's Hands

My dad was a Recon Marine. Married your grandmother forty-three years. Hard man, quiet man, not a man who used a lot of words on feelings. And when he died I went looking for the inheritance, the way you do, and I realized the real inheritance had already come. It came in a thousand small transfers I never noticed while they were happening; how he stood, how he kept his word, how he showed up, how he treated a woman for forty-three years without quitting. He entrusted it to me by the way he lived in front of me. I did not get a lump sum at the end. I got a deposit, over decades, in the presence of many witnesses.

That is what I am doing to you. That is what these letters are. I am your wingman on this, and I am in the chair when you're ready, but understand what is actually being handed across this couch. It is not a hobby we share. It is a fire, and one day it is going to be heavier and brighter in your hands than it ever was in mine, because that is how the LORD designed it to move. The handoff must be made.

Where the Cartoon Runs Out of Road

Now, honesty. I read the anime for what is true in it and I tell you where it falls short of Christ, every time, because a shadow is only useful if you know it's a shadow. Here is where One For All runs out of road.

In the story, the power has to go into a worthy vessel. The boy has to prove he can hold it; there is a whole arc coming about the ten brutal months of training just so his body won't be torn apart by the gift. The power waits for someone strong enough to carry it. That is the noble, exhausting, works-based version of inheritance, and it is exactly backwards from the gospel.

Because the power the LORD hands down does not wait for a worthy vessel. It creates one. He gives His Spirit to the weak, the mocked, the powerless, the boy everyone counted out, and He does it before the boy has earned a thing, and then He builds the vessel around the gift He already gave. The cartoon says, become strong and then you will be given power. The gospel says, you are given power you could never earn, and He will make you strong. That is not a footnote, Son. That is the whole difference between the flesh and the Spirit, between the villain who takes and the Savior who gives Himself away. Identity first. Then the strength. Never the other way around. And the whole aim of the fire is not that you become impressive:

“By this My Father is glorified — that you bear much fruit, and show yourselves to be My disciples.” (John 15:8)

The fruit is not for the tree. The fire is not for the man who carries it. It is handed to you so that it can be handed through you, and the whole chain glorifies the Father who lit it. That is the ninth trait of a FULFILLED father, the last one, the one all the others build toward: disciple-making. A man who receives the gift and hoards it has misunderstood the gift. One For All was never one man's trophy. It was always everyone's inheritance, moving.

Father & Son Talk

Three questions for the couch

  1. Name one man who deposited something into you that you did not earn and might not have noticed at the time. What did he hand you, and how did he hand it, with words or with his life?
  2. The villain grows strong by taking; the hero grows strong by giving it away. Where in your own week are you playing the taker, quietly collecting for yourself instead of pouring out? Be specific.
  3. 2 Timothy 2:2 names four generations. You are one of them. Who is the "faithful man" younger or greener than you, a brother, a friend, a kid at church, that the LORD might be asking you to entrust something to this year?
⚔ Today's Quest

Find one person younger or greener than you and hand them one thing you have been given; a verse that carried you, a skill, a warning you wish someone had spoken to you sooner. Do it on purpose, out loud, today. Then tell me who it was. You cannot pass down a fire you are only keeping to yourself.

What We Confess

The 1689 London Baptist Confession, Chapter 26 (Of the Church)

The Confession teaches that the Lord Jesus calls His people out of the world to Himself through the ministry of His Word, and gives to His church all the officers, gifts, and means it needs to gather the saints and build them up, generation after generation, until He comes. The church is not a building you inherit; it is a body being handed down, kept alive by faithful men entrusting the deposit to faithful men. The mission is the inheritance, and the inheritance is on the move.

Keach's Catechism, on the duty God requires

Q. What is the duty which God requires of man? A. The duty which God requires of man is obedience to His revealed will. And His revealed will, spoken by the risen Christ, was a handoff order: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations" (Matthew 28:19). The Great Commission is not a suggestion for the gifted; it is the standing order for every man who has received the fire. You were given so that you would give.

A note to Gideon

Son, you are holding something right now that is heavier and brighter than you know, because men you never met poured into the men who poured into me, and I have been pouring into you since before you can remember. That is not pressure. That is a privilege, and it is grace; you did nothing to earn the front of this line, you were simply born into it and loved into it.

Don't be the villain who hoards. Don't be the vessel who thinks he has to be worthy first. Take the fire that was given to you free, let the LORD make you strong enough to carry it, and then start looking, early, for the younger man you will hand it to. The handoff must be made. It always must be made.

I love you. Now go.

— Dad

Peace,
Adam
