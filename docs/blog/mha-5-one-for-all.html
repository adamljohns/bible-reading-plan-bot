One For All

An inheritance is what a man pours into you while he lives, long before it is anything he leaves you when he dies.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So we were back on the couch, Son, another late one, another episode running, and this time the thing that grabbed me wasn’t a line of dialogue at all but the mechanics of the power itself. I sat there with the remote in my hand thinking, that’s it; that’s all of it; somebody who has never read a page of Paul just drew you a picture of 2 Timothy 2:2 and set it to a soundtrack.

Here’s how it works, in my own words, with no spoilers you can’t survive. In this world the central power is called One For All, and it has one rule that sets it apart from every other ability in the show, which is that it cannot be seized. No one can steal it or buy it or take it by force from someone who does not want to give it; it only moves one way, by a willing hand into a chosen heart. And what stopped me cold is that each one who has held it added his own strength to it before he let it go, so that the power arriving in the youngest hands is heavier, brighter and more dangerous than it was in the first. It is a lineage you can feel, with generations of men literally standing behind the boy who holds it now. Go back over that paragraph once more, because there is a sermon in it.

The villain of the story runs the opposite economy. His power is built on taking: he strips abilities off of other people, hoards them, collects them like scalps, and grows strong by making everyone around him poorer. The writers even named the two forces as mirror images of each other so you could not miss it, the one given for others and the other taken for self. Call that a superhero gimmick if you want to, Son, but what I see in it is the Spirit and the flesh, standing on opposite sides of the same street.

Four Generations in One Verse

Paul, an old man in a cold cell with his own execution on the calendar, writes to a younger man he has poured himself into, and he gives him the handoff order:

“And the things you have heard from me through many witnesses — entrust these to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also.” (2 Timothy 2:2, MBT)

Count the generations in that one sentence. Paul is the first, and Timothy, the faithful man receiving it, is the second; the faithful men Timothy will find are the third, and the “others also,” the men those men will teach, are the fourth. That is four generations of the same fire named in one breath, none of them stealing it and all of them handing it down and feeding it as it goes, which is One For All written down five decades before the writers of a Japanese cartoon were born to draw it.

And notice the word Paul chooses. He does not tell Timothy to publish it, or broadcast it, or go viral with it; he tells him to entrust it, which is a deposit word and a handoff word, a word for something valuable you are placing carefully into a set of hands you have watched and tested. The Greek there has the weight of a treasure left with a trusted friend for safekeeping, and a man with a treasure to leave does not go looking for the loudest man in the room. He goes looking for the faithful one.

You Are the Point of the Whole Chain

Now this stops being a Bible study and becomes a father talking to his son on a couch, because none of this is new. It is older than the Marine Corps and older than the church, as old as fathers and sons, and the LORD built it into the family before He ever built it into the ministry:

“We will not hide them from their children, Telling to the generation to come the praises of the LORD, And His strength and His wonderful works that He has done.” (Psalm 78:4, NKJV)

The psalm goes on to say why; so that the next generation would set their hope in God, and teach it to their children, and on and on, a fire handed down until the LORD comes back. And He gave the marching orders for it at the kitchen table, not the pulpit:

“You shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up.” (Deuteronomy 6:7, NKJV)

Sitting in the house, walking by the way, lying down, rising up: there is no ceremony anywhere in that list, Son. That is a Tuesday. It is a couch and an anime and a dad who won’t let a good thing go by without asking whether you see it. The handoff does not wait for a funeral. It happens over ten thousand ordinary evenings, and it is happening to you right now whether either of us calls it that.

My Father’s Hands

My dad was a Recon Marine, and he was married to your grandmother for forty-three years. He was a hard man and a quiet one, not somebody who used a lot of words on feelings. When he died I went looking for the inheritance, the way you do, and what I realized was that the true inheritance had already come. It had come in a thousand small transfers I never noticed while they were happening: how he stood, how he kept his word, how he showed up, how he treated a woman for forty-three years without quitting. He entrusted it to me by the way he lived in front of me, so that instead of a lump sum at the end I got a deposit, made over decades, in the presence of many witnesses.

That is what I am doing to you, and it is what these letters are. I am your wingman on this, and I am in the chair when you’re ready, but I want you to understand what is really being handed across this couch. It is a good deal more than a hobby we share. It is a fire, and one day it is going to be heavier and brighter in your hands than it ever was in mine, because that is how the LORD designed it to move. The handoff must be made.

Where the Cartoon Runs Out of Road

Now, honesty. I read the anime for what is true in it and I tell you where it falls short of Christ, every time, because a shadow is only useful if you know it’s a shadow, and this is the place where One For All runs out of road. In the story the power has to go into a worthy vessel. The boy has to prove he can hold it, and there is a whole arc coming about the ten brutal months of training he goes through just so his body won’t be torn apart by the gift. The power waits for someone strong enough to carry it, which is the noble, exhausting, works-based version of inheritance, and it is backwards from the gospel.

The power the LORD hands down does not wait for a worthy vessel; it creates one. He gives His Spirit to the weak, the mocked, the powerless, the boy everyone counted out, and He does it before the boy has earned a thing, and then He builds the vessel around the gift He has already given. Where the cartoon says, become strong and then you will be given power, the gospel says, you are given power you could never earn, and He will make you strong. I don’t want you to file that away as a footnote, Son, because it is the whole difference between the flesh and the Spirit, between the villain who takes and the Savior who gives Himself away. The identity comes first and the strength follows it, and it never runs the other way around. And the aim of the fire, when it comes, isn’t that you would become impressive:

“By this My Father is glorified — that you bear much fruit, and show yourselves to be My disciples.” (John 15:8, MBT)

The fruit is not there for the tree’s sake, and the fire is not there for the one holding it. It is handed to you so that it can be handed through you, and the whole chain glorifies the Father who lit it. That is the ninth trait of a FULFILLED father, the last one and the one all the others build towards, which is disciple-making. Anyone who receives the gift and hoards it has misunderstood the gift, because One For All isn’t one man’s trophy; it has always been everyone’s inheritance, on the move.

Father & Son Talk

Three questions for the couch

  1. Name one man who deposited something into you that you did not earn and might not have noticed at the time. What did he hand you, and how did he hand it, with words or with his life?
  2. The villain grows strong by taking, and the hero grows strong by giving it away. Where in your own week are you playing the taker, quietly collecting for yourself when you could be pouring out? Be specific.
  3. 2 Timothy 2:2 names four generations, and you are one of them. Who is the “faithful man” younger or greener than you (a brother, a friend, a kid at church) that the LORD might be asking you to entrust something to this year?
⚔ Today's Quest

Find one person younger or greener than you and hand them one thing you have been given: a verse that carried you, a skill, a warning you wish someone had spoken to you sooner. Do it on purpose, out loud, today, and then tell me who it was. You cannot pass down a fire you are only keeping to yourself.

What We Confess

The 1689 London Baptist Confession, Chapter 26 (Of the Church)

The Confession teaches that the Lord Jesus calls His people out of the world to Himself through the ministry of His Word, and gives to His church all the officers, gifts, and means it needs to gather the saints and build them up, generation after generation, until He comes. So the church is no building you inherit but a body being handed down, kept alive by faithful men entrusting the deposit to faithful men, which means the mission is itself the inheritance, and the inheritance is on the move.

Keach's Catechism, on the duty God requires

Q. What is the duty which God requires of man?
A. The duty which God requires of man is obedience to his revealed will.

And His revealed will, as the risen Christ spoke it, was a handoff order: “Go therefore and make disciples of all nations” (Matthew 28:19). The Great Commission is the standing order for everyone who has received the fire, not a suggestion offered to the gifted, and you were given to so that you would give.

A note to Gideon

Son, you are holding something right now that is heavier and brighter than you know, because men you never met poured into the men who poured into me, and I have been pouring into you since before you can remember. I want you to hear that as a privilege and as grace, and not as pressure; you did nothing to earn the front of this line, you were simply born into it and loved into it.

So don’t be the villain who hoards, and don’t be the vessel who thinks he has to be worthy first. Take the fire that was given to you free, let the LORD make you strong enough to carry it, and then start looking, early, for the younger man you will hand it to. The handoff must be made, Son; it always must be.

I love you. Now go.

— Dad
