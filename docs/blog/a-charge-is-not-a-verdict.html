A Charge Is Not a Verdict

By Adam “MOOP” Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Desk: Rush Sullivan AP + Sheriff Roy Caldwell  ·  Published by Max Pemberton
Response to Jon Harris, When Truth Becomes a Team Sport (15 Aug 2026). Original essay, original X post, and the Utah County case page are linked below. This is not a reprint and not a verdict.

A charge is not a verdict. Team Truth is not a third jersey.

Original essay: Jon Harris, When Truth Becomes a Team Sport Original X post: 15 Aug 2026

This is a response to that essay and the X post that pointed at it — not a reprint, not a debate recap, not a verdict on a living defendant.

Jon Harris is right about the jersey.

His piece is not mainly about who “won” a spectacle. It is about a Right that is losing the ability to tell truth from team loyalty. He watched Candace Owens and Andrew Wilson and saw the predictable loop: attack the man, lose on the merits, fans claim victory anyway. Then the replies arrive to punish anyone who agreed with a position as if they had baptized the man.

Harris is careful there, and we should be too. He says he wants no association with some of Wilson’s rhetoric. Cheering a claim is not the same as joining a franchise. That distinction is the whole essay. Credit where it is due.

The problem sits one layer deeper.

Truth is not a fact, and a fact is not a future

Some truths do not wait on a courtroom. Who God is. What a man is. What murder is. Those do not expire. They were true yesterday. They will be true after the last clip.

Facts are different. A fact has already happened and been established. You do not get future facts.

Picture a loop-the-loop. Unbuckle and it can be true that nothing is holding you, and true that a fall from that height can kill a man. It is not a fact that you fell until you fall.

That split is not word games. It is how you keep from baptizing a charging document.

What is on the record — and what is not

Charlie Kirk was killed at Utah Valley University on September 10, 2025. That is a fact.

Tyler Robinson was arrested and charged. Utah County filed aggravated murder, among other counts. That is a fact. The charging paper is public.

Prosecutors put on a week of preliminary-hearing evidence in July 2026 and say it is enough for trial. The defense says the state has not proven the elements and should not be bound over. Those are also facts — about what each side is arguing.

Utah County’s own notice: the court hears oral argument on probable cause — whether to bind the case over — on September 1, 2026. He has not been convicted. A preliminary hearing does not decide guilt. A charge is not a verdict.

A man can speak as if “Robinson killed Kirk” is already true — settled, jersey on, case closed. That is a truth-claim wearing a uniform. There is evidence the state is pinning on him. There are also questions that have not been adjudicated. Saying “the FBI said it, so it’s true” is not discernment. It is picking a franchise.

Jesus has a team. It matters to be on it. That team is not “believe the information.” It is: do not bear false witness, and do not call a thing finished because your side needs it finished (Ex 20:16; Prov 18:17).

What Harris named, and what still matters

Harris named the spectacle: personality over proof, celebrity affiliation over substance, a podcast class that sells privileged information and punishes anyone who questions the baron. Fine. Read him.

The deeper pastoral issue is simpler. Men swallow an official narrative whole hog and parrot it while the court is still doing its job. Other men swallow the counter-narrative whole hog and treat every open question as a completed conspiracy. Both are teams. Both skip the hard work.

Team Candace and Team Andrew are still teams. Team Truth is not a third jersey you put on by yelling louder. It is slow speech, a cited claim, and the humility to leave a thing unsettled until it is actually settled.

This desk will not invent a conviction. It will not invent a plot. It will point you at the original.
