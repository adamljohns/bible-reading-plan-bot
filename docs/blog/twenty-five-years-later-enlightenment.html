Twenty-Five Years Later: What a Midshipman Got Right (and Wrong) About the Enlightenment

By Adam “MOOP” Johns · U.S.M.C. Ministries · 2001 paper + 2026 reflection · Research assist: Prof X
This is not a reprint of the academy paper. It is a grown man’s reread of his own homework — baseline from MIDN Adam L. Johns, 1/c, Kings Point (H-253, Jan 2001). Original term paper available on request; not dumped into the CMS.

Companion narration of this 25-year reflection (not a read-aloud of the 2001 paper).

Why dig this up?

In January 2001, a midshipman at Kings Point turned in a twelve-page argument with twenty-odd footnotes and a stubborn conclusion: God is real, the Bible is true, and we are frail and fallible — and he hoped the reader had done his homework too.

Twenty-five years later that same man is a husband, father, Marine veteran, and ministry builder. The Enlightenment did not get quieter. It got into our phones, our schools, our churches’ embarrassment about miracles, and our men’s allergy to sin-nature talk. So the old paper is not nostalgia. It is a baseline.

What did the twenty-one-year-old see that still maps the battlefield? Where was he generous to a fault? Where does a Reformed, postmillennial Family Captain now tighten the bolts?

The 2001 thesis in one breath

Pros he named cleanly

  • Scientific method, critical thinking, and a hunger for human betterment
  • Break from mere traditionalism — creativity in learning, art, and government
  • Locke’s natural-rights stream into Jefferson and the Declaration
  • Kant’s insistence that public reason be scholarly, not a free-for-all of unformed opinion
  • Monumental tools like Diderot’s Encyclopedia and the broader republic of letters

Cons he refused to soft-pedal

  • “Nothing by faith” as a spiritual acid
  • Bible credibility damaged when the real culprit was often bad interpretation (e.g. geocentrism taught as dogma though Scripture never required it)
  • Science and theology talking past each other for lack of humility
  • Progress that fattens the pocketbook while the soul thins (he heard Rousseau’s warning)
  • Locke’s blank slate / no inherent evil as naive once you have met a prodigal raised in love

Close he already owned

Newton, Kepler, and Galileo — at their best — pointed glory past themselves to the Creator. Scripture, read carefully, is not the enemy of honest observation. The Enlightenment may have stabbed apathy (Hamlet’s pale cast of thought) but often substituted man’s reason for biblical explanation. Only Christ is infallible. Pick a side with your eyes open.

That is a midshipman’s paper. It is also, in outline, a ministry problem set.

What still holds — and why it matters more now

1. Faith is not a religious glitch; it is human hardware

The 2001 line still cuts: everyone has faith; the question is where it lies.

Secular modernity pretends neutrality. It is not neutral. It trusts method, market, self, or the next expert consensus. Romans 1 does not describe atheists as empty of worship — it describes worship pointed at the creature. Family Captain translation: when a son says “I only believe science,” he has not escaped faith. He has relocated it.

Keep: the paper’s refusal to let “faith” be a slur.

Add now: faith’s object must be the God who speaks in Scripture and who raised Jesus bodily — not a vague spiritual mood that baptizes whatever feels empowering.

2. Bad exegesis, not the Bible, lost unnecessary battles

Young Adam saw that “earth at the center” was a church interpretation problem, not a verse problem. That instinct was gold. Galileo’s conflict was tangled with power, Aristotelian physics, and institutional pride as much as with telescopes.

Keep: humility about tradition; courage to re-open the text.

Tighten now: the answer is not “Bible vs science” as two equal magisteria fighting for scraps. The answer is the God who made heaven and earth is not threatened by careful measurement of what He made. Where Scripture speaks, it speaks truly. Where the church strapped Aristotle to the pulpit, the church needed repentance, not a new pope of the lab coat.

This is why verse-grab apologetics that treat Job or Isaiah like a modern physics textbook still need pastoral care — the 2001 catalogs of “Bible consistent with astronomy/meteorology” were earnest and often suggestive, but a mature hermeneutic distinguishes what the text asserts from what a clever modern can reverse-engineer. The impulse (Scripture is not anti-knowledge) was right. The method can grow up without surrendering inerrancy.

3. Progress without holiness is a nicer waiting room for judgment

The paper asked the convicting question: pocketbook or soul?

Twenty-five years of Western “progress” have given us medicine, bandwidth, and loneliness; rights language and fatherlessness; data and despair. Rousseau’s barb — knowledge can polish the mind while corroding morals — no longer sounds like a museum quote. It sounds like a youth-group testimony.

Postmillennial hope does not deny that. Postmillennial hope says the leaven of the kingdom works through history by the Word and Spirit, not by autonomous reason congratulating itself. Optimistic eschatology without repentance is just Enlightenment with better branding.

4. Blank-slate anthropology cannot carry a household

Locke’s “no innate corruption, only experience” appealed then because it flatters control. It collapses the first time a well-loved child chooses folly — or the first time you watch your own heart invent new ways to justify itself.

Scripture’s account is harder and more hopeful: we are image-bearers and fallen; environment matters and the heart must be made new (Ezek 36; John 3; Eph 2). Parenting, men’s discipleship, and civic trust all break if we catechize Locke by accident.

Family Captain link: you do not raise sons by optimizing only inputs. You preach, correct, love, and plead with God for new hearts — while still building ordered homes (the TACC instinct: character under real load).

5. Action over endless self-reference — with a guardrail

The Hamlet close was pure Kings Point: better decisive obedience than infinite internal debate. That still preaches to men who research their lives instead of living them.

Guardrail the midshipman under-weighted: action is not self-justifying. Enlightenment “agency” without the fear of the Lord becomes the restless empire of the self. Biblical action is faith working through love (Gal 5:6) under Christ’s lordship — the semi under the right freight, not motion for motion’s sake. (See also this week’s elder-vocation thread: neither rot in leisure nor break under misassigned grind.)

None of that scolds the A/B+ paper. It is what twenty-five years of scars and study are for.

The Enlightenment as a discipleship problem

Here is the ministry turn.

The Enlightenment did not only change labs. It trained a type of man:

  • suspicious of inherited authority
  • confident in technique
  • thin on original sin
  • hungry for progress metrics
  • unsure what to do with worship

That man still walks into Spotswood, Freedom Group, Proven Men, and your living room. If you market hard-edged discipleship as if everyone is already an early adopter of repentance, you will dilute the edge and lose the ready (Diffusion lock: Preach broadly. Disciple selectively.). If you only curse “the Enlightenment” without offering a thicker Christendom of household and church, you hand the practical field back to the very rationality you critique.

Operational synthesis

  1. 1. Pulpit: universal gospel — reason is a gift; reason is not God.
  2. 2. Intensive: men who will admit sin nature, receive Scripture as final, and take a load.
  3. 3. Household first: if ordered love does not cross the front door, no essay about Locke will save the republic.
  4. 4. Civic memory: Washington’s character under pressure (another 2026 thread) is closer to biblical wisdom than blank-slate therapy politics — liberty needs virtue, not only procedures.
  5. 5. Elder vocation: the gray head’s job is not to win 2001’s term-paper argument again; it is to hand the argument down with humility, footnotes, and a living example.

A letter across twenty-five years

To MIDN Johns, 1/c —

You were right that intellect can be a battlefield and that faith is unavoidable. You were right to refuse a forced choice between telescopes and the God of heaven. You were right that progress can polish a culture while hollowing its soul. You were right that only Christ is infallible.

You did not yet know how expensive those truths become when you lead a wife, raise sons, bury illusions about your own virtue, and try to restore a town instead of only a paragraph. That is fine. The Lord was not grading you on 2026 in 2001.

Keep the stubborn close. Lose the need to win every side-skirmish of science rhetoric. Trade some survey breadth for confessional depth. And remember: the point was never to be the smartest man in H-253. The point was — and is — to fear God, love the truth, and act.

From the man you became.
