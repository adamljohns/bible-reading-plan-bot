Go Beyond: You Are the Next Symbol

Eleven posts, one couch, one show, and one boy I have watched become a man. This one is for you.

By Adam "MOOP" Johns  ·  U.S.M.C. Ministries  ·  Written for my son Gideon  ·  July 2026

So Gideon, we did it. We finished it. Twelve of these now, from the couch where a Quirkless kid nobody believed in first caught your attention, all the way to tonight, and the credits are rolling on the last episode I am going to write about. You did the polite teenage nod one more time. I am going to take it.

Here is the shape of the whole thing, in case you ever forget it. A boy is born with nothing the world respects. He gets mocked, told to quit, told to be realistic and stay in his lane. Then a broken old hero, a man dying behind a smile, looks at that empty-handed kid and decides to give him everything he has left. Not a loan; a gift. The power in that story was never something you could steal or earn or seize; the villain spent a lifetime trying to take power, and it hollowed him out. The hero's power moved the only way that kind of power ever moves: hand to hand, given, poured out, one bearer strengthening it and handing it to the next.

And now, at the end, the kid who was saved is the man who saves. More than that, he is the man who will train the ones coming up behind him. The story does not end with him being the strongest. It ends with him becoming a symbol, a light other people can steer by, and a teacher for kids who have not been found yet.

Read that twice, Son, because that is not a cartoon ending. That is the Great Commission.

“And Jesus came up and spoke to them, saying, "All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth. Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit — teaching them to observe all that I commanded you. And lo, I am with you always — even to the end of the age."” (Matthew 28:18-20)

Notice the order. Authority first, then go. The power is His, all of it, given; and out of that given power He sends. You do not go make disciples because you are strong enough. You go because He is, and He handed it to you. That is One For All with the volume turned all the way up and the counterfeit stripped out: a power that can only be given, never taken, passed down until the last generation, and the One who gives it never leaves the field.

Be the Light, Not the Spotlight

The show calls its greatest hero the Symbol of Peace. People stopped being afraid because he showed up smiling. That is a good picture, but it is a shadow of a better command, and I want you to see the difference so the show does not do your theology for you.

“You are the light of the world. A city set on a hill cannot be hidden. Nor do men light a lamp, and put it under the peck-measure — but on the lampstand. And it gives light to all who are in the house. Let your light shine before men in such a way that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven.” (Matthew 5:14-16)

Read where the light lands. Not on you. The whole point of shining is that people look up and see the Father. A hero wants the crowd to see the hero; a son of God wants the crowd to see God. The world's symbols are spotlights; they pull the eye to the man. You were made to be a lighthouse, not a laser. The lighthouse never chases the ship and never asks to be admired; it just stands its post, holds its light, and lets the whole harbor steer home safe. Be the light. Refuse the spotlight. Those are two different jobs, and only one of them is yours.

The Handoff Never Stops

Here is the verse I have been aiming this entire series at, the one under the whole show, the one I want carved into you.

“And the things you have heard from me through many witnesses — entrust these to faithful men, who will be able to teach others also.” (2 Timothy 2:2)

Count the generations in that one sentence. Paul, to Timothy, to faithful men, to others also. Four generations, one verse. That is not a suggestion for keen believers; that is the engine of the whole thing. The gift you have received is not terminal in you. It runs through you. A man who receives and does not hand off is a dead-end, and a captain who hoards the playbook is not a captain, he is a hobbyist.

Your great-grandfather, my dad, was a Recon Marine, married forty-three years, and he handed me more than a name. I have spent your whole life trying to hand it to you, on that couch, in the truck, over these twelve posts. Now the verse turns and points past me, past you, to men and boys neither of us has met yet. That is the beautiful, relentless math of the kingdom: I disciple you so that you will disciple them so that they will disciple people I will never see this side of glory. The handoff never stops. You are not the finish line. You are the next runner.

Why You Were Forged

Let me tell you where this whole ladder is going, because it is easy to make discipleship about you and miss the top of the pyramid. Salvation holds you; sanctification shapes you; stewardship trains you; serving spends you; and it all climbs to one apex, one reason for the entire climb, the chief end of everything: the glory of God. You were not forged for yourself, Son. You were saved to be sent, and you were sent for His glory, that He would be seen and treasured and steered-toward through a life that shines and a line of disciples who shine after you (Matthew 5:16).

And so I close this series where I have wanted to close it all along, on my banner, the verse I would put over your whole life if I could only pick one. Keep it in the King James, because it carries the weight I mean.

"Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, quit you like men, be strong. Let all your things be done with charity." (1 Corinthians 16:13-14)

Watch. Stand. Quit you like men, be strong. Then the hinge the world always forgets and the flesh always skips: let it all be done in love. Strength without love is All For One, a man taking and calling it greatness. Strength poured out in love is One For All, the real one, the kingdom one, the cross. Be strong, Son. Be strong in love. Go beyond, and take somebody with you.

Father & Son Talk
  1. When your light shines, where do you catch yourself wanting the eyes to land, on the Father or on you? Be honest with me; that is the whole diagnostic.
  2. Name one man or boy right now who is a generation behind you, someone you could actually hand something to. Who is your Timothy?
  3. Where does "quit you like men, be strong" feel true for you already, and where is love the part you keep skipping?
⚔ Today's Quest

Send yourself

Pick one person younger in the faith than you, a brother, a friend, a kid at church, and this week take him one rung up on purpose. Open the Word with him, teach him one thing you have actually been given, and ask him nothing in return.

That is the whole assignment: receive, then hand off. Do not wait to feel strong enough. You already have authority you did not earn, given to you; go use it on someone else's behalf.

What We Confess

The 1689 London Baptist Confession, Chapter 26, Of the Church

The confession teaches that the whole number of the elect, gathered under Christ the Head, is His body and bride; and that the members of His churches are saints by calling, who give themselves up to the Lord and to one another, walking together and being sent out to gather still more. The handoff, Son, is not freelance. It is the ordinary work of the ordinary church, Christ building His people through His people, generation after generation, until He has gathered them all.

Keach's (Baptist) Catechism, the concluding question

Q. What doth the conclusion of the Lord's Prayer teach us?

A. That we take our encouragement in prayer from God alone, and in our prayers praise Him, ascribing kingdom, and power, and glory to Him forever; and in testimony of our desire and assurance to be heard, we say, Amen. The kingdom is His, the power is His, the glory is His. That is where the whole ladder ends, and where you begin.

A note to Gideon

Bud, we started this show when you were sixteen, and I was not sure you were listening. You were. I watched a boy on that couch become a man on that couch, and I got to be the one holding the remote when it happened. That is the honor of my life.

The screen wanted to do your formation for you; it never got the chance, because the LORD had you. You do not carry a quirk. You carry the Spirit of the living God, given, not earned, and He is not leaving. So here is the last thing this old pilot will say from the chair before you take the controls: you were not forged for yourself. You were saved to be sent. Be the light. Raise the next. Stand your post, hold the line, and when it costs you, love anyway.

I love you. Now go.

— Dad
