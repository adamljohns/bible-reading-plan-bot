📖 Scripture: Proverbs 5

My son, attend unto my wisdom, and bow thine ear to my understanding: that thou mayest regard discretion, and that thy lips may keep knowledge. For the lips of a strange woman drop as an honeycomb, and her mouth is smoother than oil: but her end is bitter as wormwood, sharp as a two-edged sword. Her feet go down to death; her steps take hold on hell. Remove thy way far from her, and come not nigh the door of her house. Lest thou give thine honour unto others, and thy years unto the cruel. Let thy fountain be blessed: and rejoice with the wife of thy youth. Let her be as the loving hind and pleasant roe; let her breasts satisfy thee at all times; and be thou ravished always with her love. And why wilt thou, my son, be ravished with a strange woman? For the ways of man are before the eyes of the LORD, and he pondereth all his goings. His own iniquities shall take the wicked himself, and he shall be holden with the cords of his sins. He shall die without instruction; and in the greatness of his folly he shall go astray.

---

🧭 Context

Proverbs 5 is Solomon's most urgent fatherly warning — and his most intimate commission. The chapter moves in three stages: danger named, consequence traced, and covenant celebrated. Solomon is not embarrassed to speak plainly to his son about the most powerful force in a young man's life. He doesn't hedge it. He doesn't spiritualize it into abstraction. He looks his son in the eye and says: this road leads down. The Hebrew word for "strange woman" here (zarah) carries the idea of something foreign, something outside the covenant — a path that feels good but is alien to the life God designed. By the end of the chapter, Solomon pivots beautifully: the antidote to the foreign pull is the covenant close. Be ravished — his word — with your wife. This is not cold duty but warm delight. This is the father's message: guard the gate, love your covenant, and remember — God sees everything.

---

👨‍👧‍👦 F.U.L.F.I.L.L.E.D. Father — Teaching Your Children to Guard the Covenant

Faithfulness — "Her end is bitter as wormwood..."
The father who guards his own covenant teaches volumes before he speaks a word. Your sons are watching whether your eyes wander. Your daughters are learning what faithfulness looks like — from you. Faithfulness to your wife is the first and loudest lesson in Proverbs 5. Be the man whose covenant holds.

Unity — "Let thy fountain be blessed..."
When a father and mother are genuinely bound to each other — delight visible, not just duty performed — the home is a fortress. Your children are not blind. They see whether you cherish your wife. Unity in the covenant models the truth this chapter is building toward: that the covenant relationship is worth protecting.

Legacy — "Lest thou give thine honour unto others..."
Solomon's warning is about squandered legacy. The man who walks away from his covenant doesn't just lose his marriage — he loses his years, his honour, his name. What you protect now will be what your sons and daughters inherit. Faithfulness is the estate you leave them.

Focus — "Remove thy way far from her... come not nigh the door."
Proverbs 5 doesn't say "resist at the door." It says don't approach the door. Teach your children early that the battle is won or lost long before the moment of temptation arrives. Focus means choosing your route. Curate the path. The man who never walks close to danger rarely falls into it.

Integrity — "For the ways of man are before the eyes of the LORD..."
This is the chapter's spine. God is watching — not to punish but because He is the ground of all accountability. Integrity means living the same in private as in public, because the only audience that ultimately matters never stops watching. Teach your children: God sees.

Love — "Rejoice with the wife of thy youth... be thou ravished always with her love."
God designed delight within the covenant. Love your wife openly, warmly, visibly — and you will preach Proverbs 5 to your children without uttering a single verse. The best defence against the foreign path is a covenant so alive that there is no appetite for the counterfeit.

Lordship — "He shall die without instruction..."
The man who rejects instruction — from father, from Scripture, from the Spirit — dies in his folly. Lordship means submitting to the Father above so that you can speak as a father below. When Christ is Lord of your sexual life, your emotional life, your private life — you have something real to pass to your children.

Endurance — "He shall be holden with the cords of his sins."
Sin builds its own chains. The man who endures in faithfulness builds a different kind of cord — bonds of trust, honour, and peace that hold the household together through every storm. Endurance in the covenant is protection for your entire family.

Direction — "He pondereth all his goings."
God ponders your path. Are you pondering it too? A father who thinks carefully about where his eyes go, where his feet take him, what his hands reach for — gives his children a map. You are not just choosing for yourself. You are drawing the road your sons and daughters will want to walk.

⛏️ Application

This week, have the hard conversation — age-appropriately — with your children about purity and covenant. Solomon did it. God inspired it into Holy Scripture because fathers are supposed to speak to this.

Ask your sons: "What does it mean to be a man who keeps his word — to God and to his wife?"

Ask your daughters: "What does faithfulness look like in the men you respect most?"

Don't wait for the culture to explain it first. The father who is silent cedes the classroom to the world. Speak now. Speak plainly. Speak with love — like Solomon, like a man who knows both the danger and the delight, and wants his children to choose well.

🙏 Prayer

Father, You designed the covenant. You called it good. You guarded it with instruction and crowned it with delight. Forgive us for the ways we have drifted — in our eyes, in our thoughts, in our private choices. Forgive us for the silences that left our sons and daughters unguarded. Restore us to faithfulness — not because our wives deserve it, though they do — but because You are Lord, and because the children watching us deserve a father whose covenant holds. Fill our homes with the kind of love that makes the counterfeit look thin and hollow. Let our sons grow to be covenant men. Let our daughters recognize faithfulness when they see it, because they saw it first in us. And where we have failed, let Your mercy rebuild what folly broke. In the name of Jesus, who loved the Church and gave Himself for her, Amen.
