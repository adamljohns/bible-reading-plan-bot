Morning Wisdom

Solomon closes his second collection with a charge to courage — the wise man is strong, the faithful man rises, and the man with eyes open is not allowed to walk past a brother being dragged toward death.

🎙️ ElevenLabs voiceover — Morning Wisdom
📖 Proverbs 24

Do not envy evil men,
nor desire to be with them,
for their hearts plot violence,
and their lips talk trouble.

By wisdom a house is built,

🧭 Context Summary

Solomon ends this collection where every man's day actually starts — at the question of courage. He pictures a house being built, a war being waged, a brother being dragged toward the gallows, a vineyard rotting under a sleeping man's eye. The wise man is the man who refuses to look away, refuses to faint, and refuses to let his neighbor's life or his own field collapse for lack of action. The lamp of the wicked goes out; the lamp of the courageous keeps burning, because his strength comes from a fear of the LORD that does not flinch.

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Leads Courageously

A REAL MAN who leads courageously is not loud — he is awake. Solomon stacks his picture of courage out of plain images: a builder laying out a house, a general calling in counselors, a man refusing to walk past a brother in chains, a farmer who refuses to nap his vineyard into ruin. Courage is not a feeling; it is the daily, deliberate work of a man who knows that the LORD weighs hearts and will repay each man according to his work.

Notice the line that ought to cut every brother awake: "If you faint in the day of adversity, your strength is small." Solomon does not say your strength is gone — he says it was always smaller than you thought. The day that exposes you is not the day you became weak; it is the day you found out how much you had been borrowing on courage you never built. So build it now. The strength a man does not deposit in calm seasons is the strength he will not be able to withdraw when the alarm sounds.

And then there is verse 11 — "Rescue those being led away to death, hold back those staggering to slaughter." Brother, this is not metaphor. A leading man stands in the gap for the brother whose marriage is being dragged off, for the son whose phone is dragging him toward shame, for the friend whose drinking is staggering him toward a grave. Do not say afterward, "I did not know." The One who weighs hearts already weighed your knowing.

The leading man is also the man with eyes for his own vineyard. Solomon's last picture — the broken wall and the nettle-choked field — is what happens to a household when the man at its center hits "snooze" on his own life. A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands — and then the robber arrives. Lead, brother. Lead your own field first, then go pull the staggering man out of the road. The lamp of the wicked goes out; the lamp of a courageous man burns long enough to light another brother home.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Identify one brother who is staggering toward slaughter — marriage, finances, addiction, faith — and do one specific thing today to hold him back. A call, a meeting, a hard truth.
  • Walk your own vineyard. Pick one fence stone Solomon would say is broken in your life and reset it before sundown.
  • Build courage on a calm day: pray boldly, fast a meal, memorize a verse — make a deposit before the storm withdraws it.
  • When you feel the day of adversity coming, name it out loud: "My strength is small — Father, increase it by Your Spirit." Then take the next step anyway.

First Watch — The Husband's Post

The first watch carries us north into Israel — Omri's dynasty, Ahab's marriage to Jezebel, the rebuilding of Jericho under a curse, and a hungry prophet sent to a foreign widow who has only a handful of meal left. Down south, Jehoshaphat foolishly entangles his house with Ahab's.

🎙️ ElevenLabs voiceover — First Watch — The Husband's Post
📖 1 Kings 16:25–34; 17:1–16 & 2 Chronicles 18

Omri did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD; he did worse than all who came before him. He walked in all the way of Jeroboam son of Nebat, in his sins which he made Israel sin, to provoke the LORD God of Israel to anger with their worthless gods. Omri slept with his fathers and was buried in Samaria; his son Ahab reigned in his place.

In the thirty-eighth year of Asa king of Judah, Ahab son of Omri began to reign over Israel, and he reigned over Israel in Samaria twenty-two years. Ahab did what was evil in the eyes of the LORD more than all who were before him. As if it were a small thing for him to walk in the sins of Jeroboam, he took Jezebel daughter of Ethbaal king of the Sidonians as his wife. He went and served Baal and worshiped him. He set up an altar for Baal in the house of Baal that he built in Samaria, and he made an Asherah pole. Ahab provoked the LORD God of Israel to anger more than all the kings of Israel who were before him.

In his days, Hiel of Bethel rebuilt Jericho. He laid its foundation at the cost of Abiram his firstborn, and he set up its gates at the cost of Segub his youngest, according to the word of the LORD spoken by Joshua son of Nun.

⸻

Now Elijah the Tishbite, of the settlers of Gilead, said to Ahab: "As the LORD God of Israel lives, before whom I stand — there will be no dew or rain these years except by my word."

The word of the LORD came to him: "Get away from here; turn eastward and hide yourself by the brook Cherith, east of the Jordan. You will drink from the brook, and I have commanded the ravens to feed you there."

He went and did according to the word of the LORD; he lived by the brook Cherith. The ravens brought him bread and meat in the morning and bread and meat in the evening, and he drank from the brook. After a while the brook dried up, because there had been no rain in the land.

Then the word of the LORD came to him: "Get up, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and live there. I have commanded a widow there to feed you."

So he got up and went to Zarephath. When he came to the gate of the city, there was a widow gathering sticks. He called to her: "Bring me a little water in a jar, that I may drink." As she was going to get it, he called after her: "Bring me, please, a piece of bread in your hand."

Elijah said to her: "Do not be afraid; go and do as you have said. But first make me a little cake from it and bring it out to me; afterward make some for yourself and your son. For thus says the LORD God of Israel: the jar of meal will not be spent, and the jug of oil will not run dry, until the day the LORD sends rain on the face of the earth."

She went and did according to Elijah's word. She and he and her household ate for many days. The jar of meal was not spent and the jug of oil did not run dry, according to the word of the LORD which He spoke by Elijah.

⸻

Down in Judah, Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor, and he allied himself by marriage with Ahab. After some years he went down to Ahab in Samaria. Ahab killed sheep and cattle in abundance for him and the people who were with him, and persuaded him to go up against Ramoth-gilead.

Ahab king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat king of Judah: "Will you go with me against Ramoth-gilead?"

Jehoshaphat said: "I am as you are, my people as your people. We will be with you in the war." But Jehoshaphat said to the king of Israel: "Please inquire first of the word of the LORD."

So the king of Israel gathered four hundred prophets and said to them: "Shall we go to battle against Ramoth-gilead, or shall I refrain?"

They said: "Go up, for God will give it into the king's hand."

Jehoshaphat said: "Is there not still a prophet of the LORD here that we may inquire of him?"

Ahab said: "There is yet one man, Micaiah son of Imlah, by whom we may inquire of the LORD — but I hate him, because he never prophesies good for me but always evil."

Micaiah was brought up, and at first he played the chorus: "Go up and succeed; the LORD will give it into the king's hand." But under pressure he told the truth: "I saw all Israel scattered on the mountains as sheep without a shepherd. The LORD said, 'These have no master — let each return home in peace.'"

Then Micaiah told of a deceiving spirit sent into the mouths of Ahab's prophets to lure him to his death. Zedekiah son of Chenaanah struck Micaiah on the cheek; Ahab ordered Micaiah thrown in prison on bread and water until he came home in peace. Micaiah said: "If you ever return in peace, the LORD has not spoken by me."

Ahab disguised himself for battle; Jehoshaphat went in his royal robes. The Syrians pursued Jehoshaphat — until he cried out and the LORD turned them away. Then a random Syrian drew his bow and struck the king of Israel between the joints of his armor. Ahab was held up in his chariot until evening and died at sunset, exactly as the LORD by Micaiah had spoken. Jehoshaphat returned to his own house in Jerusalem in peace.

🗺️ Briefing Summary

Two houses, two ways of providing. Up in Israel, Ahab marries Jezebel and provides his household with Baal, Asherah, and a rebuilt cursed city — and even the foundations cost him a man's sons. Out by the brook and over the border in Zarephath, the LORD provides for His prophet first with ravens, then with a widow who is one meal from her grave. Down in Judah, Jehoshaphat almost throws his providence away by alliance with Ahab — and is rescued only because he cried out and because the LORD had told the truth through one despised prophet. A faithful husband learns from all three: don't marry into Baal's house, don't provide your family with what is cursed, and don't be too proud to be fed by a handful of meal and a jar of oil that you did not earn.

❤️ Reflection for Your Wife — H.A.P.P.Y. Husband — Providing

As a HAPPY husband called to provide, Elijah at the gate of Zarephath should rearrange your idea of providing for the rest of your life. The widow is gathering two sticks. She has a handful of meal in a jar and a thin film of oil in a jug. She is preparing what she believes is her last meal — for herself and her son. And the prophet of God shows up and asks her to feed him first.

Read that again, brother. The man of God did not deliver flour. He delivered the word of God — and the word made the flour. "The jar of meal will not be spent, and the jug of oil will not run dry, until the LORD sends rain on the face of the earth." Providing in the Christian sense has never primarily been about quantity. It has been about trusting that the God who keeps His promises has put His own word at the bottom of your wife's pantry. The handful does not run out when the word holds it up.

So what does that mean for your house this week? It means your job is not to convince Maria that you can earn enough. Your job is to keep speaking the word that holds the jar up. Ahab tried to provide for Jezebel out of his own treasury, and the heavens shut for three years. Hiel tried to rebuild a cursed city and paid with his firstborn and his youngest. A husband who provides without the word of the LORD provides his family a curse with the rent paid on time. A husband who provides under the word of the LORD provides his family rest under His promise — even when the jar looks like one more meal.

Don't be too proud to be fed by a widow's hand, either. Sometimes the LORD provides for your house through unexpected channels — your wife's salary, a neighbor's casserole, an unexpected check, your mother-in-law's freezer. Receive it like Elijah received it: with thanks, without shame, knowing the LORD is keeping His word through whatever vessel He chooses. Providing is the LORD's work; your role is the trusting word that hands the cake out the door first.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Open the Bible at the dinner table tonight before the food is opened. Speak one verse over the meal. Let your wife and children hear that the jar is held up by the word.
  • Take an honest look at your "Baal" — anywhere in your provision you have leaned on something cursed, exploitative, or shady. Cut it loose this week even if it costs you.
  • If your provision came in part this month through someone else's hand, thank them out loud — and thank the LORD out loud in front of your wife.
  • Pray over your bank account, your job, your investments, your storehouse — not as ornament, but as confession: "Father, this is held by Your word, not by my work."

Second Watch — The Father's Charge

The second watch shows us a prophet who refuses to leave a household until the LORD has restored its son — and then a king who refuses to let his nation drift after his own foolish alliance. Two pictures of fatherly discipline: one in a back room over a child's body, the other in the public square setting up judges who will fear the LORD.

🎙️ ElevenLabs voiceover — Second Watch — The Father's Charge
📖 1 Kings 17:17–24; 18:1–15 & 2 Chronicles 19

Some time later the son of the woman, the mistress of the house, became sick. His sickness was so severe that there was no breath left in him. She said to Elijah: "What do I have to do with you, O man of God? Have you come to me to bring my sin to mind and to kill my son?"

Elijah said: "Give me your son." He took him from her arms, carried him up to the upper room where he was staying, and laid him on his own bed. He cried out to the LORD: "O LORD my God, have You also brought disaster on the widow with whom I am staying, by killing her son?"

He stretched himself out on the boy three times and cried out: "O LORD my God, let this child's life return to him." The LORD listened to Elijah's voice; the life of the child returned to him, and he lived. Elijah took the child, brought him down from the upper room into the house, and gave him to his mother. He said: "See, your son lives."

He appointed judges in the land throughout all the fortified cities of Judah, city by city. Whenever a dispute comes to you from your brothers who live in their cities — whether between blood and blood, between law and commandment, between statutes and judgments — you shall warn them not to be guilty before the LORD, so that wrath does not come on you and your brothers. Do this, and you will not incur guilt. Look — Amariah the chief priest is over you in every matter of the LORD; Zebadiah son of Ishmael, the ruler of the house of Judah, in every matter of the king; and the Levites will serve before you. Deal courageously, and may the LORD be with the upright."

🗺️ Field Notes

Two scenes of discipline. In the back room, Elijah disciplines a household by refusing to let the boy's death stand — he carries the child to his own bed, cries out three times, and brings him back into the arms of his mother. Discipline here is not punishment; it is fatherly intercession over a son whom the household had given up for dead. Down in Judah, Jehoshaphat disciplines a kingdom by going from city to city setting up judges who fear the LORD. He doesn't shout, he doesn't bribe — he charges them: take heed, no partiality, no bribes, deal courageously. A faithful father disciplines like that: he stretches himself out over the son in the upper room, and he sets up clean judgment under his own roof.

👨‍👦 Reflection for Your Children — F.U.L.F.I.L.L.E.D. Father — Disciplining

As a FULFILLED father called to discipline, you have a temptation: to confuse discipline with loud correction. Elijah's upper-room scene rebukes that confusion. The widow blames the prophet for her son's death. Elijah does not argue with her. He says, "Give me your son" — and he carries the boy upstairs to his own bed. Then he prays over him, and prays again, and prays a third time. Discipline that bears fruit in your sons and daughter looks more like that upper room than like a raised voice in the kitchen.

Gideon is nineteen — a man, with man-size choices in front of him. Your discipline of Gideon is mostly intercession now. You do not "hand him over" to the world or to his own future just because he is old enough to make his own bed. You carry him upstairs in prayer. You stretch yourself out over him before the LORD until you can come back down and say, "See, your son lives." Don't underestimate that, brother. Three years from now Gideon will remember a father who would not stop praying for him more than he remembers any lecture you gave at fourteen.

Boaz is fourteen — the age when a son becomes a young man under your roof. Look at Jehoshaphat's charge to the judges: "Take heed what you do — there is no injustice with the LORD, no partiality, no taking of bribes." That charge belongs over Boaz's life. Your job with Boaz is to install the judges inside him now: an inner judge that fears the LORD, an inner judge that refuses partiality, an inner judge that refuses bribes (from peers, from screens, from his own appetites). Discipline a fourteen-year-old by setting his internal court — and then, when he stumbles, hear his case yourself with no partiality and no bribery.

Shiloh is five — and discipline at five looks like Elijah carrying the boy to his bed before anything is wrong. The discipline of a young daughter is mostly the framing of a safe room: clean rhythms, simple consequences, kind firm boundaries, the same daddy showing up the same way every night to pray over her. Five-year-olds learn what justice and tenderness are from the same father, in the same room, at the same bedtime — and a daddy who fails to keep that room consistent has not been "easygoing"; he has been undisciplined.

Then notice the public face of Jehoshaphat's reform: he goes city by city. He does not delegate the courts to whoever is willing. He installs men. Discipline at your house looks like that on the family scale: you do not subcontract correction to a screen, a teacher, a coach, a pastor, or your wife. You go room by room. You appoint judges in your own heart — and then you charge yourself: "Take heed what you do, for you are not judging for yourself but for the LORD, who is with you in this matter."

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Carry one child upstairs in prayer this week — quietly, three times if needed — for a thing they don't even know you are interceding about.
  • Sit with Boaz and walk through "no partiality, no bribery" as a frame for one specific friendship, one game, or one online decision he is making.
  • Hold the five-year-old's bedtime like a sacred liturgy this week. Same room, same prayer, same daddy, same firmness with Shiloh.
  • Examine your own internal courts. Is there a place where you have taken a bribe from comfort, from approval, from fatigue — and let injustice slide under your own roof? Repent and reset.

Third Watch — The Citizen's Stand

The third watch is the day's hinge — Elijah on Mount Carmel forcing a divided nation to choose, and Jehoshaphat at the temple forcing a frightened nation to wait. Two pictures of loyalty: the public confession, and the public dependence.

🎙️ ElevenLabs voiceover — Third Watch — The Citizen's Stand
📖 1 Kings 18:16–40 & 2 Chronicles 20:1–30

When Ahab saw Elijah, Ahab said to him: "Is it you, you troubler of Israel?"

Elijah said: "I have not troubled Israel — but you and your father's house have, because you have forsaken the commandments of the LORD and have walked after the Baals. Now then — send and gather all Israel to me on Mount Carmel, with the four hundred fifty prophets of Baal and the four hundred prophets of Asherah who eat at Jezebel's table."

So Ahab sent through all Israel and gathered the prophets at Mount Carmel. Elijah came near to all the people and said: "How long will you halt between two opinions? If the LORD is God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him."

The people did not answer him a word.

Then Elijah said to the people: "I, even I only, am left a prophet of the LORD, and Baal's prophets are four hundred fifty. Give us two bulls. Let them choose one bull for themselves and cut it in pieces, lay it on the wood, and put no fire under it. I will prepare the other bull and lay it on the wood and put no fire under it. You call on the name of your god, and I will call on the name of the LORD — and the God who answers by fire, He is God."

All the people answered: "It is well spoken."

Elijah said to the prophets of Baal: "Choose one bull for yourselves and prepare it first, since you are many. Call on the name of your god, but put no fire under it."

They took the bull and prepared it and called on the name of Baal from morning until noon, saying: "O Baal, hear us!" But there was no voice and no one answered. They limped around the altar they had made.

At noon, Elijah mocked them: "Cry louder, for he is a god — perhaps he is in conversation or wandered off or is on a journey, or perhaps he is sleeping and must be awakened." They cried louder and cut themselves with knives and lances, as was their custom, until the blood gushed out on them. The middle of the day passed, and they raved on until the time of the evening offering — but no voice, no answer, no one to pay attention.

Elijah said to all the people: "Come near to me." All the people came near to him. He repaired the broken-down altar of the LORD with twelve stones for the twelve tribes of the sons of Jacob, to whom the word of the LORD had come, saying: "Israel shall be your name." With the stones he built an altar in the name of the LORD. He made a trench around the altar, arranged the wood, cut the bull, laid it on the wood, and said: "Fill four jars with water and pour it on the burnt offering and on the wood." Then he said: "Do it a second time," and they did it a second time. Then he said: "Do it a third time," and they did it a third time. The water ran around the altar, and he filled the trench also with water.

At the time of the evening offering, Elijah the prophet came near and said: "O LORD, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel — let it be known today that You are God in Israel and I am Your servant, and that I have done all these things at Your word. Hear me, O LORD, hear me, that this people may know that You, O LORD, are God, and that You have turned their hearts back."

Then the fire of the LORD fell — it consumed the burnt offering, the wood, the stones, and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench. When all the people saw it, they fell on their faces and said: "The LORD, He is God! The LORD, He is God!"

Elijah said to them: "Seize the prophets of Baal — let none of them escape." They seized them; Elijah brought them down to the brook Kishon and slaughtered them there.

⸻

After this the sons of Moab and the sons of Ammon, along with some of the Meunites, came against Jehoshaphat to make war. Word came to Jehoshaphat: "A great multitude is coming against you from beyond the Sea, from Edom — they are at Hazazon-tamar (that is, En-gedi)."

Jehoshaphat was afraid; he set himself to seek the LORD and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah. Judah gathered to seek help from the LORD; out of all the cities of Judah they came to seek the LORD.

Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly of Judah and Jerusalem at the house of the LORD, in front of the new court, and said:

"O LORD God of our fathers, are You not God in heaven? You rule over all the kingdoms of the nations; in Your hand are power and might, and no one can stand against You. Did not You, our God, drive out the inhabitants of this land before Your people Israel and give it forever to the descendants of Abraham Your friend? They lived in it and built You a sanctuary in it for Your name, saying, 'If evil comes upon us — sword, judgment, plague, or famine — we will stand before this house and before You, for Your name is in this house, and we will cry to You in our affliction, and You will hear and save.'

Now look — the sons of Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom You would not let Israel invade when they came from Egypt — they turned away from them and did not destroy them. Look how they reward us — coming to drive us from Your possession that You have given us to inherit. O our God, will You not execute judgment on them? For we have no power against this great multitude that is coming against us. We do not know what to do — but our eyes are upon You."

All Judah stood before the LORD, with their little ones, their wives, and their children. Then the Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel son of Zechariah, a Levite of the sons of Asaph, in the middle of the assembly. He said:

"Listen, all Judah and you inhabitants of Jerusalem and King Jehoshaphat. Thus says the LORD to you: do not be afraid or dismayed because of this great multitude, for the battle is not yours but God's. Go down against them tomorrow. Look — they will be coming up by the ascent of Ziz, and you will find them at the end of the valley before the wilderness of Jeruel. You will not need to fight in this battle; take your position, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD on your behalf, O Judah and Jerusalem. Do not be afraid or dismayed; tomorrow go out against them, for the LORD is with you."

Jehoshaphat bowed his head with his face to the ground; all Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell down before the LORD, worshiping Him. The Levites of the sons of the Kohathites and of the sons of the Korahites stood up to praise the LORD God of Israel with a very loud voice.

They rose early in the morning and went out into the wilderness of Tekoa. As they went out, Jehoshaphat stood and said: "Hear me, Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem: believe in the LORD your God, and you will be established; believe His prophets, and you will succeed."

He took counsel with the people and appointed those who sang to the LORD and praised Him in holy attire as they went out before the army, saying:

"Give thanks to the LORD, for His steadfast love endures forever."

When they began to sing and praise, the LORD set ambushes against the sons of Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir who had come against Judah, and they were struck down. The sons of Ammon and Moab rose against the inhabitants of Mount Seir to destroy and annihilate them; when they had finished with the men of Mount Seir, they helped to destroy one another.

When Judah came to the lookout in the wilderness, they looked toward the multitude — and there they were, corpses lying on the ground, none had escaped. Jehoshaphat and his people came to seize the spoil; they found among them an abundance of valuables, clothing, and precious things, more than they could carry away. They were three days seizing the spoil, because there was so much. On the fourth day they assembled in the Valley of Beracah, because there they blessed the LORD. The kingdom of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest all around.

🛰️ Situation Report

Two showdowns, one verdict. On Mount Carmel, Elijah ends Israel's three-year hesitation in a single sentence: "How long will you halt between two opinions?" The people stand silent until the fire falls. Down in Judah, Jehoshaphat faces three nations marching at once and answers them with a fast, a prayer, and a verse: "We do not know what to do, but our eyes are upon You." The singers march in front of the army, and the LORD ambushes the enemy with their own swords. In both stories, loyalty to the LORD is not a feeling — it is a public act, taken in front of the whole nation, that refuses to keep two altars in the same field.

🛡️ Reflection for a R.E.S.O.L.U.T.E. Citizen — Loyal

Fredericksburg (local)
As a RESOLUTE citizen called to be loyal, your local block is your Mount Carmel. Elijah did not fly to Jerusalem and write a column about Israel's split loyalty — he stood on a hill in his own region, in front of the people who knew his name, and forced the question: "If the LORD is God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him." In Fredericksburg that question still has to be asked. The half-loyal man at the city council meeting, the church board, the school board, the neighborhood association — that is the man who is troubling our city. Be the one who steps to the front and says, plainly and without theatrics, "The LORD is God." The people will be silent first. The fire is the LORD's business; the confession is yours.

Virginia (state)
Across Virginia, loyalty looks like Jehoshaphat at the temple. He does not march to the border first; he gathers his people — little ones, wives, children, all of Judah — and prays a long, specific prayer in the new court of the temple. "We do not know what to do — but our eyes are upon You." Brother, the public square of our Commonwealth is full of men telling us they know exactly what to do. A loyal citizen is the man who, in the assembly, has the courage to say in front of his wife and his children, "We do not know — but our eyes are upon Him." Then he gets his Bible open and his fast on the calendar and his choir in front of the army. Loyalty is a public posture before it is a private comfort.

United States (nation)
At the national level, Mount Carmel and Jehoshaphat's valley are the same warning to the same audience. America has spent generations halting between two opinions. We have built altars to consumer comfort, to the algorithm, to economic gods we cannot name. We have sent four hundred fifty prophets in front of them, and the chorus is loud, and the cutting of ourselves with knives goes on day and night, and no fire falls. The loyal citizen does not lobby Baal to behave better. He repairs the broken altar of the LORD with twelve stones for the twelve tribes. He pours water on the offering until it is impossible. He prays a short, direct prayer at the time of the evening offering: "Hear me, O LORD — that this people may know that You are God." That is what loyalty looks like in a divided nation. Repair what is broken, pour water on every excuse, pray short and pray for fire, and let God answer.

🦅 This Day in American History — May 24

• 1844 — Samuel F. B. Morse sends the first official telegraph message from the U.S. Capitol to Baltimore: "What hath God wrought" — taken straight from the mouth of Balaam in Numbers 23:23. A loyal citizen who steps into a brand new medium with the LORD's name on his lips sets the public square's loyalty straight from the first transmission.
• 1883 — The Brooklyn Bridge opens to the public after fourteen years of construction. The bridge cost designer John Roebling his life, paralyzed his son Washington, and was finished by Washington's wife Emily — a quiet picture of generational loyalty: one family standing fast through accidents, illness, and ridicule until the span was open and the public could cross on the first day.

🌾 Reflection for a Man at Home and in Community

Tonight Solomon's pilgrim song wraps the whole day in a single picture: a man who fears the LORD, his wife flourishing inside his house, his children gathered around his table, his city blessed because his table is right. Three roles — one room.

As a HAPPY husband, Providing tonight means you sit down at the table and eat what the labor of your hands has brought, and you say out loud that the bread is the LORD's blessing on a man who fears Him. Maria does not flourish like a fruitful vine because you "worked hard"; she flourishes because the LORD blesses the husband who feared Him through the work. Tonight tell her, plainly, that you trust the LORD to keep her flourishing — and then receive the meal with thanks instead of strain.

As a FULFILLED father, Disciplining tonight means you look at Gideon, Boaz, and Shiloh as olive shoots around your table — not as projects, not as problems, not as performance.
