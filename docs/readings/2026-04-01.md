🧭 **Context**

After yesterday's victory over Sisera and the Canaanite army, Deborah and Barak break into song. This isn't a dry military report — it's poetry, passion, praise. The Song of Deborah is one of the oldest passages in Scripture, celebrating God's deliverance and the willing hearts who answered the call.

Notice the contrast: some tribes rushed to battle (Zebulun, Naphtali), while others stayed behind (Reuben, Dan, Asher). Deborah's song names names — praising courage, questioning hesitation. And at the center: Jael, a woman with a tent peg and the courage to act when it mattered most.

This is a song of **faithfulness rewarded** and **cowardice exposed**. God fought from heaven; the people fought from the field. Victory came when both met.

Enter Gideon — hiding in a winepress, threshing wheat like a fugitive in his own land. God called this fearful man "mighty warrior" and commissioned him to deliver Israel. But first, Gideon had to confront the idolatry in his own home. He tore down his father's altar to Baal by night, risking death from his own neighbors. Only after cleansing his household did God use him to rally the tribes and confront the enemy.

This is a story about civic renewal beginning with personal courage. God chooses the weak and obscure to accomplish His purposes — if they will obey.

🏗️ **R.E.S.O.L.U.T.E. Citizen — Fredericksburg, Virginia, America**

**Righteousness — Fredericksburg:** A city can only be as healthy as its households. Gideon's mission began *at home* — tearing down false gods before confronting national enemies. Fredericksburg's renewal depends on citizens who bring biblical integrity into their own families, businesses, and neighborhoods. Civic virtue flows from personal holiness. If we tolerate corruption in our own lives — compromise, greed, apathy — we forfeit the moral authority to call our city to higher standards. Righteous citizenship starts at the family altar.

**Excellence — Virginia:** Gideon was an unlikely leader — youngest son, weakest clan. Yet God called him "mighty warrior" before he'd proven anything. Virginia's strength has never been in elite pedigree but in ordinary men and women rising to extraordinary challenges. Excellence is not perfection; it's faithful obedience with the resources at hand. The Commonwealth needs citizens who stop hiding in winepresses of fear and step into the calling God has placed on their lives — whether in education, business, local government, or ministry. Virginia's legacy is built by those who refuse to shrink back.

**Transformation — United States of America:** Israel was "brought very low" — economically ruined, spiritually adrift, politically impotent. Transformation didn't come through a better strategy or a charismatic politician. It came when the people *cried to Yahweh* and one man obeyed in the dark. America's hope is not in the next election cycle but in the remnant who will tear down the idols of materialism, comfort, and compromise. National renewal begins with local obedience. God is still looking for ordinary citizens willing to do hard things — to stand when it's unpopular, to speak truth when it's costly, to build altars when the culture builds shrines to self.
