Morning Wisdom

A disciplined mind and a purified heart are the only defensible positions for a man who leads his household.

📖 Proverbs 25:15-28

By long forbearance a ruler is persuaded, And a gentle tongue breaks a bone.
Have you found honey? Eat only as much as you need, Lest you be filled with it and vomit.
Seldom set foot in your neighbor’s house, Lest he become weary of you and hate you.
A man who bears false witness against his neighbor Is like a club, a sword, and a sharp arrow.
Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble Is like a bad tooth and a foot out of joint.
Like one who takes away a garment in cold weather, And like vinegar on soda, Is one who sings songs to a heavy heart.
If your enemy is hungry, give him bread to eat; And if he is thirsty, give him water to drink;
For so you will heap coals of fire on his head, And the LORD will reward you.
The north wind brings forth rain, And a backbiting tongue an angry countenance.
It is better to dwell in a corner of a housetop, Than in a house shared with a contentious woman.
As cold water to a weary soul, So is good news from a far country.
A righteous man who falters before the wicked Is like a murky spring and a polluted well.
It is not good to eat much honey; So to seek one’s own glory is not glory.
Whoever has no rule over his own spirit Is like a city broken down, without walls.

🧭 Context Summary

Solomon’s scribes have compiled these maxims to instruct the king and the people in the art of godly governance and personal conduct. The passage contrasts the power of patience and gentleness with the destructive force of pride and uncontrolled temper. It emphasizes that true strength lies not in military might but in the internal mastery of one’s own spirit. The imagery of a city with broken walls serves as a stark warning about the vulnerability of a man who lacks self-discipline.

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Rules His Spirit

Listen closely, brother. In the field and in the home, the greatest threat to your covenant responsibility is often not the enemy outside, but the enemy within. The text declares that he who rules his own spirit is greater than he who takes a city. This is a sobering truth for any man who has held a rifle or held the reins of his family. Physical strength and tactical prowess are fleeting gifts, but self-governance is a fruit of the Spirit that endures. When you lose your temper, you do not merely have an emotional outburst; you breach the walls of your own soul, leaving your wife and children exposed to the chaos you have unleashed.

The call to rule your spirit is a call to daily repentance and reliance on Grace. It requires you to pause when the flesh demands reaction. It demands that you speak with a gentle tongue even when provoked, recognizing that your words carry the weight of authority. As a father and a husband, your peace is not a luxury; it is a provision. A home ruled by a tyrant or a fool is a city under siege. But a home ruled by a man who yields to the LORD through patience is a fortress of stability. You must cultivate a spirit that is slow to anger, for anger without wisdom is merely brute force masquerading as justice.

Consider the imagery of the honeycomb. Your words should bring healing and sweetness to those under your care, not bitterness and strife. If your tongue is a weapon of destruction, you have failed in your duty to protect and provide. The LORD desires men who are gentle yet firm, strong yet submissive to His will. This is the mark of true masculinity: the power to destroy held in check by the wisdom to build. Do not be like the fool who quarrels at every turn. Be like the wise man who rules his spirit, thereby honoring the LORD and securing the peace of his household.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Pause for ten seconds of silent prayer before responding to any provocation from your family today.
  • Identify one area where you have been quick to anger and confess it to the LORD in private repentance.
  • Speak only words that build up and encourage your wife and children, avoiding any sharp or critical tones.
  • Review your daily schedule to ensure you are prioritizing time for Scripture and prayer over mere activity.

📖 Joshua 7

But the children of Israel committed a trespass regarding the accursed things, for Achan the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, took of the accursed things; so the anger of the LORD burned against the children of Israel.
Now Joshua sent men from Jericho to Ai, which is beside Beth Aven, on the east side of Bethel, and spoke to them, saying, “Go up and spy out the country.” So the men went up and spied out Ai.
And they returned to Joshua and said to him, “Do not let all the people go up, but let about two or three thousand men go up and attack Ai. Do not weary all the people there, for the people of Ai are few.”
So about three thousand men went up there from the people, but they fled before the men of Ai.
And the men of Ai struck down about thirty-six men, for they chased them from before the gate as far as Shebarim, and struck them down on the descent; therefore the hearts of the people melted and became like water.
Then Joshua tore his clothes, and fell to the earth on his face before the ark of the LORD until evening, he and the elders of Israel; and they put dust on their heads.
And Joshua said, “Alas, Lord GOD, why have You brought this people over the Jordan at all—to deliver us into the hand of the Amorites, to destroy us? Oh, that we had been content, and dwelt on the other side of the Jordan!

Get up, sanctify the people, and say, ‘Sanctify yourselves for tomorrow, because thus says the LORD God of Israel: “There is an accursed thing in your midst, O Israel; you cannot stand before your enemies until you take away the accursed thing from among you.”

So Joshua rose early in the morning and brought Israel by their tribes, and the tribe of Judah was taken.
He brought the clan of Judah, and he took the family of the Zarhites; and he brought the family of the Zarhites man by man, and Zabdi was taken.
Then he brought his household man by man, and Achan the son of Carmi, the son of Zabdi, the son of Zerah, of the tribe of Judah, was taken.
Now Joshua said to Achan, “My son, I beg you, give glory to the LORD God of Israel, and make confession to Him, and tell me now what you have done; do not hide it from me.”
And Achan answered Joshua and said, “Indeed I have sinned against the LORD God of Israel, and this is what I have done:
When I saw among the spoils a beautiful Babylonian garment, two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold weighing fifty shekels, I coveted them and took them. And there they are, hidden in the earth in the midst of my tent, with the silver under it.”
So Joshua sent messengers, and they ran to the tent; and there it was, hidden in his tent, with the silver under it.
And they took them from the midst of the tent, brought them to Joshua and to all the children of Israel, and laid them out before the LORD.
Then Joshua, and all Israel with him, took Achan the son of Zerah, the silver, the garment, the wedge of gold, his sons, his daughters, his oxen, his donkeys, his sheep, his tent, and all that he had, and they brought them to the Valley of Achor.
And Joshua said, “Why have you troubled us? The LORD will trouble you this day.” So all Israel stoned him with stones; and they burned them with fire after they had stoned them with stones.
Then they raised over him a great heap of stones, still there to this day. So the LORD turned from the fierceness of His anger. Therefore the name of that place has been called the Valley of Achor to this day.

🗺️ Briefing Summary

Joshua 7 details the catastrophic defeat at Ai caused not by enemy strength, but by hidden sin within the camp. The LORD withdrew His favor because Achan had violated the ban on devoted things, stealing from what was holy. This passage serves as a stark warning that corporate blessing is contingent upon individual and communal holiness before God.

🗺️ Field Notes

Joshua 8 records the second campaign where Israel executes the Lord’s tactical plan to destroy Ai, reversing the shame of the first failure. The chapter details the strategic placement of the ambush and the precise obedience required to secure victory. It demonstrates that success in the spiritual battle depends entirely on adhering to the Commander’s orders rather than human intuition. The total destruction of the city serves as a sobering reminder of the seriousness of covenant obedience.

🦅 This Day in American History — March 25

On March 25, 1634, the weary passengers of the Ark and the Dove came ashore at St. Clement's Island, where Father Andrew White raised a rough-hewn cross and offered the first Catholic Mass in the English colonies, planting Maryland as a refuge for conscience. On the same day in 1776, the Continental Congress resolved to strike its first gold medal in thanksgiving for Washington's deliverance of Boston. An Obedient citizen honors both acts of grateful obedience, the colonists who began their settlement at the altar and the Congress that paused to give honor where honor was due, ordering all his beginnings and victories alike under God.

🌾 Reflection for a Man at Home and in Community

As a HAPPY husband, you must recognize that the peace of your home is not secured by your own strength but by the same God who shattered the heads of Leviathan in the deep. The psalmist cries out from the ashes of Jerusalem, yet he anchors his hope in the Lord’s covenant love. You are called to be the priest of your household, standing in the gap when chaos threatens your wife and your union, reminding her that the Lord is the Holy One who dwells in the praises of His people. Your authority is not a tyrant’s decree but a shepherd’s care, mirroring Christ’s love for the church by protecting your wife from the wolves of this world and leading her into the green pastures of spiritual safety.

As a FULFILLED father, you see in Psalm 74 the terrifying reality of what happens when leadership fails and the sanctuary is profaned. You are the designated guardian of your children’s souls, tasked with preserving the faith once delivered to the saints within your walls. When the culture screams its foolish blasphemies against biblical order, you must not retreat into silence. Instead, you must teach your sons to stand firm in the covenant, showing them that their Father in heaven remembers His congregation and will not forget the poor forever. Your discipline and instruction are the axes you wield to cut down the strongholds of sin in their young hearts, preparing them to be men who fear the LORD and keep His commandments.

As a RESOLUTE citizen, you understand that the church suffers when the civil magistrate abandons God, yet your ultimate allegiance remains to the King of Kings. The ruins of Zion remind you that earthly institutions are temporary, but the covenant of grace is eternal. You serve in the public square with a sober courage, knowing that the same God who dried up the seas and set the borders of the earth is still on the throne. Do not be shaken by the roar of your foes or the temporary triumph of the wicked, for the Lord will arise and plead His own cause. Your duty is to pray for the peace of the city, to uphold justice, and to model a masculinity that is rooted in the rock of ages, not in the shifting sands of popular opinion.

🙏 Prayer from the Stateroom

Father, I come before You as a soldier of Your cross, weary from the day’s watch but strengthened by Your word. You are the Holy One who dwells in the praises of Israel, and I thank You that You have purchased my soul by Your mighty hand. Forgive me for the times I have doubted Your care or failed to lead my family with the courage You have commanded. By the power of Your Holy Spirit, fill my home with the reverence of Your presence. Guard my wife as the apple of Your eye, and raise my sons to be men after Your own heart, who will not be moved by the roaring of the enemy. Let my life be a testament to Your sovereignty, that when I stand before You, I may hear, "Well done, good and faithful servant." In the name of Jesus Christ, my Lord and Commander, I pray. Amen.
