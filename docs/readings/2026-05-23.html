Morning Wisdom

We sit again at the table of Solomon — and he doesn't flinch. He aims at every appetite of a man: food, money, words, sons, women, and the cup.

🎙️ Audio — Morning Wisdom
📖 Proverbs 23

Do not withhold correction from a child, For if you beat him with a rod, he will not die.
You shall beat him with a rod, And deliver his soul from hell.
My son, if your heart is wise, My heart will rejoice—indeed, I myself;
Yes, my inmost being will rejoice When your lips speak right things.

For surely there is a hereafter, And your hope will not be cut off.
Hear, my son, and be wise; And guide your heart in the way.
Do not mix with winebibbers, Or with gluttonous eaters of meat;
For the drunkard and the glutton will come to poverty, And drowsiness will clothe a man with rags.
Listen to your father who begot you, And do not despise your mother when she is old.
Buy the truth, and do not sell it, Also wisdom and instruction and understanding.
The father of the righteous will greatly rejoice, And he who begets a wise child will delight in him.
Let your father and your mother be glad, And let her who bore you rejoice.
My son, give me your heart, And let your eyes observe my ways.
For a harlot is a deep pit, And a seductress is a narrow well.
She also lies in wait as for a victim, And increases the unfaithful among men.
Who has woe? Who has sorrow? Who has contentions? Who has complaints? Who has wounds without cause? Who has redness of eyes?
Those who linger long at the wine, Those who go in search of mixed wine.
Do not look on the wine when it is red, When it sparkles in the cup, When it swirls around smoothly;
At the last it bites like a serpent, And stings like a viper.
Your eyes will see strange things, And your heart will utter perverse things.
Yes, you will be like one who lies down in the midst of the sea, Or like one who lies at the top of the mast, saying:
“They have struck me, but I was not hurt; They have beaten me, but I did not feel it. When shall I awake, that I may seek another drink?”

🧭 Context Summary

Solomon turns the lamp of wisdom into every dark corner of a man's life — the table, the wallet, the rod of discipline, the marriage bed, the wine cup. He doesn't moralize from a distance; he talks like a father at the dinner table to a son who is about to leave the house. Watch your appetite. Watch your money. Watch your words. Watch your sons. Watch your women. Watch your drink. The man who refuses to manage these things will be managed by them.

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Manages Faithfully

A REAL MAN who manages faithfully is not a tyrant and he is not a miser — he is a steward. Solomon paints stewardship that runs from the dinner table to the bank account to the rod of correction to the wine cup. The faithful manager understands that everything he touches is on loan from God: his appetite, his hours, his children, his wallet, his bed. He does not let any of them go wild, because anything ungoverned ends up governing him.

A passive man tells himself, "I work hard, I deserve this." And the deceitful bread, the slow-poured cup, and the second look at the woman slide under the gate while he isn't watching. A faithful manager keeps a knife at his own throat at the ruler's table. He doesn't run himself ragged chasing wealth. He doesn't crave a stingy man's favor or eat the bread of a deceitful host. He buys truth — and once he owns it, he never sells it.

Notice how Solomon handles the rod. The man who manages faithfully isn't afraid to correct his sons, because faithful management of a child sometimes costs the child a little pain now to save him from Sheol later. He handles the cup the same way: he refuses to stare into the red sparkle. He knows what bites at the end of the night.

Manage your house under God today, brother. The boundary stones do not move themselves.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Audit one appetite — food, drink, screen, scroll — and put a knife to it today. Don't bargain.
  • Open the spreadsheet. Open the bank app. Don't pretend the numbers don't exist; faithful management starts with knowing.
  • Pick one son whose heart you've been "managing around" instead of managing — speak to him today, eye to eye, with both correction and tenderness.
  • Buy truth before you buy convenience. If a conversation costs you comfort but earns you wisdom, pay full price.

🗺️ Briefing Summary

Abijah, the son of Rehoboam, is no shining king — but he says one true sentence loud enough to bend a battle: "The LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him." That confession turns the trap of an ambush into a routing of Israel. Then his son Asa takes the throne and starts where any faithful husband should start: he removes idols, gathers his people back under the law, and uses the season of rest God gives him to build walls — not just for the kingdom, but for his sons.

❤️ Reflection for Your Wife — H.A.P.P.Y. Husband — Protecting

As a HAPPY husband called to protect, this passage hits you square in the chest. Asa does not protect Judah primarily by stockpiling weapons — although he does that in season. He protects Judah first by removing what God hates from the land. Foreign altars come down. Standing stones are broken. Asherah poles are chopped to splinters. The sun-pillars in every city of Judah are torn out by their roots.

Brother, that is the work of a real protector at home. The man who guards his wife is not first the one who locks the front door — he is the one who removes the idols already inside the house. Foreign altars in your home look like the slow normalization of bitter speech, the carefully framed pictures of women on a screen, the spending pattern that exalts comfort over covenant, the silence in the evening that you've learned to call "tired." Asa did not say, "We'll deal with the high places later." He removed them — first.

And then, only then, did Asa build walls. Because peace under God comes before fortification — not the other way around. A man who builds walls around an idolatrous house has only fortified the wrong thing. But a man who cleans his house first, then strengthens it, makes a real refuge for his wife.

Your wife is not asking you to be ferocious; she is asking you to be reliable. She needs to know the same God who held Judah's line through Abijah's confession still holds the perimeter of your home. She needs to know that when you say, "The LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him," you mean it on a Tuesday morning, not just in church.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Walk your house — physically — and identify one "high place" that quietly sits there. Take it down today. Don't argue with yourself.
  • Speak the confession of Abijah out loud, plainly, to your wife: "The LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him in this house."
  • Then build one wall — pick a real, practical safeguard (a budget rule, a screen rule, a bedtime rhythm, a Sunday liturgy) and put it in place this weekend.
  • Make sure your wife knows where the trumpet is. If trouble surrounds the house, she should know you will sound the alarm, not freeze.

🗺️ Field Notes

This passage is a father's heritage moment. Asa stares down a million-man army with a sentence: "Help us, O LORD our God, for we rely on You." God strikes the army, and the chase continues until the fear of the LORD has fallen on entire cities. Then a prophet rises up out of nowhere and lays a charge on Asa's whole generation: "Be strong. Do not let your hands hang down." Asa hears it, takes courage, and leads the largest covenant renewal his nation has seen since the days of Joshua. A faithful father is not merely brave in battle — he leads his household into a sworn covenant with the LORD, even when it costs him his grandmother's throne.

👨‍👦 Reflection for Your Children — F.U.L.F.I.L.L.E.D. Father — Leading

As a FULFILLED father called to lead, you are looking at the gold standard in Asa. He led his kingdom three times in one day: he led his men into battle, he led them to call on the LORD before they swung a sword, and he led them in covenant after the battle was over. Gideon, Boaz, and Shiloh are watching you do all three of those things in a smaller scale — every week.

Notice the order. Asa does not say to Judah, "Be brave." He says to the LORD, "We rely on You." A father who leads from his own strength teaches his children to lean on their own — and they will collapse the first time they meet a million-man army. A father who leads by calling on God in front of his children teaches them where strength actually comes from. Gideon needs that lesson written into his bones — purpose without prayer is just ambition. Boaz needs it because strength without humility curdles into self-trust. Shiloh needs it because she is growing up in a world that wants her to live by feelings, not by faith.

Then notice Azariah's word: "Be strong. Do not let your hands hang down." Brother, your hands hang down when you let your children disciple themselves through screens and friends. Your hands hang down when you skip the evening reading because everyone is "tired." Your hands hang down when you stop correcting because correction makes the room uncomfortable. A leading father lifts his hands again. He does not shout — he gathers. He does not shame — he covenants.

And note the courage Asa shows with his own grandmother. He does not say, "She's family — I'll let it slide." He cuts down her image and grinds it to powder. Leading fatherhood loves the people in your home enough to break the idols even the family elders have set up. Sometimes the hardest idol in your house came from someone you love.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Pray out loud over one child today before you correct, instruct, or send him into something hard. Let them hear Asa's prayer in your own voice.
  • Pick one "Asherah" your household has tolerated because the source was family — and break it cleanly today.
  • Lift your hands: re-start one evening practice you have let drop (Word, prayer, songs, family table, ordered bedtime).
  • Renew a small covenant out loud — even one sentence: "In this house we serve the LORD."

🛰️ Situation Report

Three kingdoms, three lessons. In Israel, four kings in a generation — Baasha, Elah, Zimri, Omri — and three of them die violently because they walked in the sin of Jeroboam. Down in Judah, the once-great Asa squanders his legacy at the end of his life: he bribes a pagan king instead of seeking the LORD, imprisons a prophet for telling him the truth, and dies refusing the help of God even on his deathbed. Then Jehoshaphat rises — and instead of inheriting his father's last decade of foolishness, he goes back to the earlier ways of David, sends teachers throughout the land, and the fear of the LORD goes out across the region. A nation lives or dies on whether its citizens — and especially its leaders — will steadily walk with God year after year.

🛡️ Reflection for a R.E.S.O.L.U.T.E. Citizen — Steadfast

Fredericksburg (local)
As a RESOLUTE citizen called to be steadfast, this passage is a hard mirror. The kings of Israel run through their thrones like a man burning through credit cards — each one starts strong, claims to know what he's doing, and ends as a cautionary tale. Steadfastness is the opposite of that. A steadfast Fredericksburg citizen shows up year after year at the same church, the same job, the same family table, the same voting booth, the same neighbor's porch — because faithfulness in your post is what actually changes a city. Be the man on your block who didn't drift.

Virginia (state)
Across Virginia, steadfastness looks like Jehoshaphat. He did not try to build a national following on a hot take — he sent teachers into every city of Judah with the Book of the Law of the LORD. Brother, in a state that careens between cultural fashions, the steadfast citizen is the one who keeps quietly investing in catechesis: at home, at church, in the homeschool group, in the men's huddle, on the long phone call with a younger man who's wobbling. You are not called to win the news cycle. You are called to teach the Book.

United States (nation)
And then there's Asa. He is the warning at the national level. A man who spent forty years walking with the LORD — strong, brave, reforming — turned at the end of his life and trusted Damascus over Jerusalem. He bribed a pagan king with the silver of God's own house. He imprisoned the prophet who told him the truth. He oppressed his own people. And when his feet rotted under him, he still would not seek the LORD. For our nation, the lesson is bare: you can spend decades blessed by God and then squander it in one foolish treaty. Steadfastness for an American citizen means refusing to outsource our trust to silver, to Damascus, to chariots, or to clever men. The eyes of the LORD still range to and fro throughout the whole earth to strengthen the heart of those who are wholly His. He has not become smaller because our nation has.

🦅 This Day in American History — May 23

On May 23, 1785, Benjamin Franklin set down in a letter his clever remedy for failing eyes, the bifocal spectacles that let a man see both his food and the faces across the table, a small fruit of a lifetime of inventive industry. On the same day in 1788, the delegates at Charleston ratified the Constitution, making South Carolina the eighth pillar of the new federal union. Whether bending his mind to a humble invention or his judgment to the framing of a lasting government, a Steadfast citizen gives himself to careful, enduring work, knowing that the God of order honors both the patient craftsman and the faithful builder of a free people.

🌾 Reflection for a Man at Home and in Community

As a HAPPY husband, Honest tonight means you stop pretending that the security of your home rests on your hustle. Maria is not safe because you stay up late or because you wake up early — she is safe because the LORD watches over the house. Honest means you say out loud, "Lord, You built this — and unless You keep it, my arms are not enough." That confession is rest to a wife.

As a FULFILLED father, Faithful tonight means you put your arrows into the quiver of the LORD before you carve any plan for their lives. Gideon, Boaz, and Shiloh are not your projects — they are heritage from the LORD. Tonight, that means you stop trying to "fix" them and start praying over them. For your sons, Gideon and Boaz, this means raising them as godly men. For your daughter, Shiloh, this means nurturing her gentle and modest spirit, guiding her toward godly womanhood and her feminine flourishing. The arrows will fly straight when the warrior — God Himself — pulls the string.

As a RESOLUTE citizen, Steadfast tonight means you refuse the modern lie that the city is held together by the watchmen. Watchmen matter; you serve as one in your sphere. But the LORD watches over the city. The civic anxiety that says "if I don't carry it all, it collapses" is unbelief in respectable clothes. Lay it down. Let Him give His beloved sleep tonight, and rise tomorrow ready to stand your post again.
