
## 1st Watch — Husband's Post

**Joshua 22 — The Altar of Witness**
*Monday, March 30, 2026 | H.A.P.P.Y. — P: Prioritize Her*

### Scripture
Then Joshua called the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half-tribe of Manasseh, and said to them: "You have kept all that Moses the servant of the LORD commanded you, and have obeyed my voice in all that I commanded you. You have not left your brethren these many days, but have kept the charge of the commandment of the LORD your God. Return and go to your tents, to the land of your possession. But take careful heed to do the commandment and the law — to love the LORD your God, to walk in all His ways, to keep His commandments, to hold fast to Him, and to serve Him with all your heart and with all your soul."

So Joshua blessed them and sent them away.

When they came to the Jordan, the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh built there a great altar. And when the children of Israel heard of it, the whole congregation gathered to go up to war against them.

But the eastern tribes answered: "The Mighty One, God the LORD! He knows — if this is rebellion, do not save us. But this is why we built it: in time to come your children might say to our children, 'What have you to do with the LORD God of Israel?' Therefore we said, 'Let us build an altar — not for burnt offering or sacrifice, but that it may be a witness between us and you and our generations after us, that we serve the LORD.'"

And Phinehas the priest said, "This day we perceive that the LORD is among us." And the children of Reuben and Gad called the altar Ed — "For it is a witness between us that the LORD is God."

### Context
The Promised Land is conquered and divided. The two and a half tribes had kept their word — years of battle alongside their brothers, then released by Joshua with blessing and plunder. But on the journey home they stopped at the Jordan and built a great altar. The western tribes nearly went to war over it. But there was no sin — the altar was a witness. Not for sacrifice but for declaration: We are still Israel. Don't let this river become a wall that cuts our children off from God. War was averted. The altar stands as testimony: the LORD is God.

### H.A.P.P.Y. Husband — P: Prioritize Her
The two and a half tribes were going home. Battle-scarred, exhausted, dreaming of family — they could have just crossed the river and disappeared. But they stopped. They built something permanent. A visible declaration for the next generation.

Husband — that is what it means to prioritize your wife. Not just a few hours on the weekend. Building something that outlasts the moment. Showing your children — by what you do, not what you say — that this woman is the most important person in your earthly life.

Distance creates drift. The Jordan could quietly become a wall. What rivers are you letting build in your marriage? Things don't slow down. Build the altar now. Put the phone down at dinner. Ask how she's really doing and actually listen. Send the text: "Thinking of you."

Your marriage is a testimony. The watching world needs to see a man who chose his wife on purpose, every day. The LORD is God. Let your marriage declare it.

### Application
Name one "Jordan" — one quiet wall of distance in your marriage. Then do one concrete thing today to build the altar: a real conversation, an act of service, a moment of focused, phone-free presence. Build now.

### Prayer
Father, You know every promise I made at that altar — to You and to my wife. Like the eastern tribes, I've sometimes let the distance grow without noticing. Today I come back to the riverbank. Let my marriage be a witness — to my children, to those watching — that covenant love is real and that You are God. Show me where I've let distance creep in, and give me the will to close the gap today. In Jesus' name. Amen.

Then the anger of the Lord will be kindled against you, and you shall perish quickly from off the good land that he has given to you."
――――――――――――――――――――

🧭 CONTEXT
Joshua, now old and nearing death, gathers Israel's leaders for his farewell address. This is the final charge from the man who led them into the Promised Land, a legacy transfer from one generation to the next. His words carry the weight of a lifetime of faithful obedience and victory through God's faithfulness.
――――――――――――――――――――

👨‍👧‍👦 F.U.L.F.I.L.L.E.D. FATHER — F: Faithful

Joshua stands at the threshold every father must face: the moment when we can no longer lead from the front, when our children and grandchildren must carry the mission forward without us. This passage is a masterclass in legacy transfer, and it reveals what truly matters when we hand off the baton.

Notice what Joshua doesn't say. He doesn't recount his greatest military victories. He doesn't remind them of his courage at Jericho or his wisdom in dividing the land. Instead, he points relentlessly to God's faithfulness: "Not one word has failed of all the good things that the Lord your God promised concerning you."

This is the mark of a faithful father. We measure success not by our accomplishments, but by whether we've taught our children to trust God more than they trust us. Joshua's legacy isn't his battlefield prowess—it's his single-minded devotion to the Word of God and his unshakeable confidence in God's promises.

He also doesn't sugarcoat the future. Faithful fathers tell hard truths. Joshua warns them that compromise with the surrounding nations will become "a snare and a trap for you, a whip on your sides and thorns in your eyes." He knows his sons and daughters will face temptations he cannot shield them from, so he arms them with clear warnings and the knowledge that God's blessings and curses are both real.

What are you transferring to your children? Are you building your own legacy or pointing them to God's faithfulness? Are you teaching them to cling to the Lord when cultural currents pull them away? Joshua's final charge reminds us: our job isn't to be their hero forever—it's to introduce them to the Hero who never fails, then step aside and let them walk with Him.

The greatest gift a father can give is not his strength, but his testimony of God's strength. Not his wisdom, but his dependence on God's Word. Not his victories, but his confidence in God's promises.
――――――――――――――――――――

⛏️ APPLICATION
• Identify one specific way you've seen God's faithfulness in your family's story—share it with your kids this week
• Audit your conversations with your children: Are you talking more about your achievements or God's faithfulness?
• Write down one hard truth your kids need to hear before they face it in the world—then speak it with love and clarity
• Evaluate your legacy: If your kids remember only one thing about your faith, what do you want it to be?
