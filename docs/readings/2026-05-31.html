The words of King Lemuel, the utterance which his mother taught him:

“Many daughters have done well, But you excel them all.”
Charm is deceitful and beauty is passing, But a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be praised.

🧭 Context Summary

King Lemuel receives maternal instruction on the duties of civil authority and the dangers of indulgence. The passage contrasts the heavy burden of kingship with the urgent call to defend the voiceless and uphold justice. It establishes that true power is exercised not through self-aggrandizement but through righteous judgment and restraint. The text serves as a sobering reminder that earthly authority is accountable to the divine King.

🗺️ Briefing Summary

This passage chronicles the rapid decay of Israel’s northern kingdom through a succession of violent usurpers who refused to repent of their idolatry. While Judah’s King Jotham maintained a measure of righteousness, King Ahaz descended into deep apostasy, even sacrificing his own children to false gods. The text highlights the severe consequences of abandoning the covenant, showing how spiritual compromise leads to national vulnerability and divine judgment.

So Azariah the priest went in after him, and with him were eighty priests of the LORD—valiant men.
And they withstood King Uzziah, and said to him, “It is not for you, Uzziah, to burn incense to the LORD, but for the priests, the sons of Aaron, who are consecrated to burn incense. Get out of the sanctuary, for you have trespassed! You shall have no honor from the LORD God.”

King Uzziah was a leper until the day of his death. He dwelt in an isolated house, because he was a leper; for he was cut off from the house of the LORD. Then Jotham his son was over the king’s house, judging the people of the land.

🗺️ Field Notes

Uzziah’s story is a stark warning for any leader who confuses temporal success with spiritual fidelity. His strength in battle and wisdom in agriculture were gifts from God, yet they became the very foundation of his pride. When he overstepped the priestly boundary, God’s judgment was immediate and total, stripping him of his honor and his position. This passage teaches us that no amount of earthly victory excuses disobedience to God’s ordained order.

🦅 This Day in American History — May 31

On May 31, 1790, President George Washington signed the nation's first Copyright Act, securing to authors the fruit of their minds for the express end of the encouragement of learning. On the same day in 1889, the South Fork Dam gave way above Johnstown, Pennsylvania, and a thirty-foot wall of water swept more than two thousand souls to their deaths in minutes, drawing Clara Barton and her young Red Cross to their first great work of mercy. The law that nurtured honest learning and the catastrophe that called forth selfless aid both summon a Steadfast citizen to labor faithfully in calm and to stand firm in disaster, trusting his life and his works to the sovereign God who orders all our days.
