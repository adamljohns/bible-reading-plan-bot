📖 **Scripture: Proverbs 6**

My son, if thou be surety for thy friend, if thou hast stricken thy hand with a stranger, thou art snared with the words of thy mouth, thou art taken with the words of thy mouth. Do this now, my son, and deliver thyself, when thou art come into the hand of thy friend; go, humble thyself, and make sure thy friend. Give not sleep to thine eyes, nor slumber to thine eyelids. Deliver thyself as a roe from the hand of the hunter, and as a bird from the hand of the fowler.

Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise: which having no guide, overseer, or ruler, provideth her meat in the summer, and gathereth her food in the harvest. How long wilt thou sleep, O sluggard? when wilt thou arise out of thy sleep? Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep: so shall thy poverty come as one that travelleth, and thy want as an armed man.

A naughty person, a wicked man, walketh with a froward mouth. He winketh with his eyes, he speaketh with his feet, he teacheth with his fingers; frowardness is in his heart, he deviseth mischief continually; he soweth discord. Therefore shall his calamity come suddenly; suddenly shall he be broken without remedy.

These six things doth the LORD hate: yea, seven are an abomination unto him: a proud look, a lying tongue, and hands that shed innocent blood, an heart that deviseth wicked imaginations, feet that be swift in running to mischief, a false witness that speaketh lies, and he that soweth discord among brethren.

My son, keep thy father's commandment, and forsake not the law of thy mother: bind them continually upon thine heart, and tie them about thy neck. When thou goest, it shall lead thee; when thou sleepest, it shall keep thee; and when thou awakest, it shall talk with thee. For the commandment is a lamp; and the law is light; and reproofs of instruction are the way of life.

---

🧭 **Context**

Proverbs 6 is a father's field manual — three urgent dispatches before the great moral battle of chapter 7. Solomon moves from financial foolishness (don't pledge what you can't back), to the folly of laziness (the ant shames the sluggard), to the seven things God calls an abomination — and then ties them all together with a single command: *keep your father's commandment.* The binding thread is *character.* The financially reckless man, the sleeping sluggard, the discord-sower — they all share the same root disease: they have not bound wisdom to their hearts. Solomon's prescription is not a list of rules but a relationship with instruction itself. The word *lamp* here is *ner* — the small clay lamp that keeps burning through the night. The father's commandment is not a cage; it is the light that keeps you from walking off a cliff in the dark.

---

👨‍👧‍👦 **F.U.L.F.I.L.L.E.D. Father — Raising Children Who Keep Their Word**

**F — Faithful to Commitments**
"If thou be surety for thy friend, thou art snared with the words of thy mouth." Solomon opens with a financial warning, but the fatherhood lesson runs deeper: *your word is your bond.* When you model a man who keeps his commitments — financially, relationally, spiritually — your sons and daughters learn that character is not situational. They are watching whether you do what you said you would do. That is faithfulness in its plainest form.

**U — Unashamed of Hard Work**
"Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise." The ant has no manager, no overseer, no prodding — she just works. A father who models diligence, who rises early and works faithfully, preaches the loudest sermon on industry your children will ever hear. Don't just tell them to work hard; let them watch you do it. A lazy father produces confused children. An industrious father produces confident ones.

**L — Leading by Example in What God Hates**
Solomon lists seven abominations — pride, lying, innocent bloodshed, wicked schemes, swiftness to mischief, false witness, and discord-sowing. Read that list again. Most of them can happen inside a home. A father who gossips about neighbors teaches his children to sow discord. A father who stretches the truth teaches his children to be false witnesses. Your home is the first courtroom where your children learn what God hates — and they learn it from watching you.

**F — Fixing Commandment to the Heart**
"Bind them continually upon thine heart, and tie them about thy neck." This is not about rigid rule-keeping — it is about *internalized* wisdom. A father's job is not to create compliant children but children who have made their father's instruction their own. Speak it at the table. Speak it on the road. Return to it when they fail. The goal is that when they are alone — no parent in sight — the lamp is still burning inside them.

**I — Instruction as a Living Light**
"The commandment is a lamp; and the law is light." This is why devotion matters. This is why you are reading Proverbs with your children and not just sending them to Sunday school. You are the lamplighter. When instruction becomes a lamp in a child's heart, they can navigate darkness you will never see. Long after you are gone, the light you lit will still be burning in them.

**L — Launching Them Into Integrity**
"When thou goest, it shall lead thee." The goal of fatherhood is launch. You are not raising children to stay dependent on you — you are raising men and women who carry the lamp into every room they enter. The day will come when your sons and daughters walk into situations you cannot reach. The question is: did you bind enough light to their hearts to guide them when you cannot?

**L — Loving Correction as a Gift**
"Reproofs of instruction are the way of life." Reproof — correction with purpose — is one of the most loving things a father does. A child who is never corrected is a child who is not prepared for the world's hardness. When you correct your children, do it with purpose: not punishment for its own sake, but instruction sharpening them for life. They will thank you later.

**E — Embracing the Long Game**
Proverbs 6 is not a quick fix. The father who plants these seeds — diligence, integrity, bound-up wisdom — may not see the harvest for decades. That is the nature of fatherhood. Plant in faith. Water with consistency. Trust God for the growth. The commandment you sow into a child's heart today will talk to them in the night when they are forty years old and facing a decision only God can see.

**D — Dependent on God's Word, Not Just Your Own**
Solomon never says "do what I say because I said so." He points always back to the LORD. "These six things doth the LORD hate." It is the Father above who defines what matters. Our job as earthly fathers is to be conduits of His wisdom, not manufacturers of our own. Humility in fatherhood means admitting you are also under the Father's instruction — and letting your children see that.

⛏️ **Application**

Pick one item from Solomon's list of seven abominations and ask yourself honestly: is there any trace of this in your home this week? Not in your children — in *you.* Pride? Discord? A stretched truth? A quick-running-to-mischief moment? Own it before God, then own it before your household. A father who confesses and corrects in front of his children teaches them one of the most powerful lessons: that the lamp never goes out, even when we stumble.
