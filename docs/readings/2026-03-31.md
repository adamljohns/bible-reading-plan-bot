🧭 **Context**

Judges 1 opens the book with a stark contrast: Judah's faithful obedience leads to decisive victory, while tribe after tribe settles for compromise. Joshua's generation has passed, and Israel faces the ongoing work of possessing their inheritance. The Lord gives clear victories to those who trust Him—Judah defeats 10,000 enemies, Caleb's family conquers Hebron, and Othniel takes Debir with courage and receives blessing. Yet the chapter's second half chronicles failure after failure: Manasseh, Ephraim, Zebulun, Asher, Naphtali, and Dan all fail to drive out the inhabitants. They choose coexistence, forced labor, and compromise instead of complete obedience. This pattern sets the stage for Judges' tragic cycle of apostasy, oppression, and deliverance.

💍 **H.H.A.A.P.P.Y. Husband — Abiding**

Maria, as your husband, I'm called to abide in Christ so that our home abides in His presence. Judges 1 warns me what happens when we settle for "good enough" instead of God's best. Judah inquired of the Lord and moved forward in obedience—they experienced victory because they stayed connected to God's voice. But tribe after tribe stopped short. They conquered territory but left enemies in the land. They chose convenience over complete surrender.

**Abiding** means I stay rooted in Jesus daily—not just for personal strength, but so our marriage and family remain grounded in Him. When I drift from prayer, Scripture, and worship, compromise creeps in. The enemy doesn't need to defeat me; he just needs me to tolerate his presence. Unchecked attitudes, unresolved tensions, spiritual laziness—these are the "Canaanites" I must drive out through abiding.

Jesus said, "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in me" (John 15:4). I can't lead you, love you, or protect you in my own strength. I need to stay connected to the Source. When I abide, I bear fruit—patience when you're exhausted, kindness when stress rises, faithfulness when life feels overwhelming.

I'm recommitting today: I will not settle for spiritual compromise. I will inquire of the Lord, press into His presence, and let His Word shape me. Because you and our children deserve a husband and father who abides in Christ—not one who tolerates the enemy's influence. I love you, Maria.

⛏️ **Application**

Identify one area where you've settled for compromise instead of complete obedience. Is it a relationship you tolerate that undermines your faith? A habit you excuse rather than conquer? A calling you've postponed because the battle looks hard? Like Judah, inquire of the Lord—ask Him what needs to be driven out. Then move forward in faith, trusting that "the Lord was with Judah" applies to you too (v. 19). Don't stop halfway. Press through to victory, knowing God has already given you the land.
