Morning Wisdom

Wake, brother, and let the Word of the LORD sharpen your spirit for the day’s duties.

📖 Proverbs 23

Do not grumble when you eat the meat of men who offer it, nor desire their delicacies. For as he thinks in his heart, so is he. Eat and drink, he says to you, but his heart is not with you. The drink you take is a serpent, a viper that kills. Do not toil to acquire wealth; be discerning enough to let it go. When your eyes lighten on it, it is gone, for suddenly it takes wings, flying like an eagle toward heaven. Do not eat the food of a stingy man, nor desire his delicacies. For he is like a man inwardly counting the cost. Eat and drink, he says, but his heart is with you. You have eaten a little, but you vomit up your pleasant words. Do not speak in the hearing of a fool, for he will despise the wisdom of your words. Do not move the ancient landmarks that your fathers have set. For their Redeemer is mighty; He will plead your case against them. Say to yourself, "Why am I in pain? Why is my heart bitter? Why do I have wounds without cause? Why are my eyes dull? Why do my limbs feel heavy? I have paused to seek wine. Do not look at wine when it is red, when it sparkles in the cup and goes down smoothly. In the end it bites like a serpent and stings like a viper. Your eyes will see strange things, and your heart will utter perverse things. You will be like one who lies down in the midst of the sea, or like one who lies on the top of a mast. "They struck me, but I am not hurt; they beat me, but I did not feel it. When shall I awake? I must seek another drink."

🧭 Context Summary

Solomon addresses the young man here, warning him against the seductive danger of gluttony and the intoxicating allure of wine. He exposes the hypocrisy of those who offer hospitality while harboring hidden agendas, urging discernment in all social interactions. The passage culminates in a vivid description of the physical and spiritual devastation caused by drunkenness, serving as a stark deterrent to those who would seek escape in excess.

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Flee Temptation

Brother, the call to flee is not a sign of weakness, but of spiritual strength and discipline. You are not called to negotiate with sin, but to resist it with the full armor of God. The enemy seeks to dull your senses and cloud your judgment, so you must remain vigilant and sober-minded. Your household looks to you for protection, not from external threats alone, but from the internal corruption that leads to spiritual decay.

Do not think that you can handle temptation without consequence. The wine that seems to offer comfort ultimately delivers chains and sorrow. As a father and a husband, your clarity of mind is a sacred trust. You must guard your heart against the slow creep of compromise, recognizing that sin always begins with a desire that is not aligned with the will of the LORD.

When the world offers you a way to numb your pain or escape your responsibilities, remember that such relief is fleeting and destructive. True strength is found in self-control, a fruit of the Spirit that demonstrates your submission to God’s authority. Let your life be a testimony of sobriety and resolve, showing your family that you serve a Master who demands holiness in all things.

⛏️ Personal Application
  • Examine your daily habits for any compromise with alcohol or excess that dulls your spiritual sensitivity.
  • Set firm boundaries around social gatherings to ensure you never place yourself in a position of temptation.
  • Prioritize your role as the spiritual head of your home by maintaining clarity and strength for family leadership.
  • Confess any moments of weakness to God immediately, relying on His grace to renew your resolve.
🙏 Prayer

Forgive me for the times I have sought comfort in ways that dishonor You and weaken my witness. By the power of Your Holy Spirit, give me the courage to flee from temptation and the discipline to stand firm in righteousness. 📖 Acts 10:34-48

Then Peter opened his mouth and said: Of a truth I perceive that God shows no partiality. But in every nation whoever fears Him and works righteousness is accepted by Him. The word which God sent to the children of Israel, preaching peace through Jesus Christ—He is Lord of all—that word you know, which was published throughout all Judea and began from Galilee after the baptism which John preached: how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with Him. And we are witnesses of all things which He did both in the land of the Jews and in Jerusalem, whom they killed by hanging on a tree. Him God raised up on the third day, and showed Him openly, not to all the people, but to witnesses chosen beforehand by God, even to us who ate and drank with Him after He arose from the dead. While Peter was still speaking these words, the Holy Spirit fell upon all those who heard the word. And those of the circumcision who believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because the gift of the Holy Spirit had been poured out on the Gentiles also. And he commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then they asked him to stay a few days.

🗺️ Briefing Summary

Peter declares the gospel’s universal scope, dismantling any notion that God favors one lineage over another. The narrative confirms that faith in Christ is the sole requirement for salvation, not ethnic pedigree or ritual purity. The sudden outpouring of the Holy Spirit upon the Gentiles serves as undeniable evidence of God’s sovereign choice. This event establishes the foundation for the church as a body united by grace, not by human tradition.

Brother, stand firm in the truth that God’s sovereign grace extends beyond our familiar circles to reclaim a people for Himself.

📖 Acts 11:1-18

The apostles and the brothers who were in Judea heard that the Gentiles also had received the word of God. So when Peter went up to Jerusalem, the circumcised believers criticized him, saying, “You went to uncircumcised men and ate with them.”

Peter began and explained it to them in order: “I was in the city of Joppa praying, and in a trance I saw a vision. Something like a large sheet was let down by its four corners from the sky, and it came down to me. I looked into it and saw four-footed animals of the earth, wild beasts, creeping things, and birds of the air. I heard a voice saying to me, ‘Get up, Peter; kill and eat.’ But I said, ‘By no means, Lord, for I have never eaten anything common or unclean.’ But the voice answered a second time from heaven, ‘What God has cleansed, do not call common.’ This happened three times, and the object was taken up into heaven.

“And behold, at that very moment three men arrived at the house in which we were, sent to me from Caesarea. And the Spirit told me to go with them without hesitating. These six brothers also accompanied me, and we entered the man’s house. And he told us how he had seen the angel stand and say to him, ‘Send to Joppa and bring Simon, whose surname is Peter, who will speak a word to you by which you and all your household will be saved.’ And as I began to speak, the Holy Spirit fell on them just as on us at the beginning. And I remembered the word of the Lord, how he said, ‘John baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit.’ If then God gave the same gift to them as he gave to us when we believed in the Lord Jesus Christ, who was I that I could stand in God’s way?”

When they heard these things they fell silent. And they glorified God, saying, “Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance that leads to life.”

🗺️ Field Notes

Peter confronts the Jerusalem church to demonstrate that God’s sovereign election knows no ethnic boundaries. The vision of the clean and unclean animals symbolizes the removal of the ceremonial barrier between Jews and Gentiles. The outpouring of the Holy Spirit upon Cornelius’s household serves as undeniable proof of God’s approval. The believers are forced to acknowledge that repentance unto life is granted even to those outside the covenant nation.

The scattered saints, driven by persecution, become the very vessels of grace, proving that the Gospel advances not by human strategy but by Divine providence.

📖 Acts 11:19-30

Now those who were scattered because of the persecution that arose over Stephen traveled as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to no one except Jews alone. But there were some of them, men of Cyprus and Cyrene, who, when they had come to Antioch, spoke to the Greeks also, preaching the Lord Jesus. And the hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number who believed turned to the Lord. Then news of these things reached the ears of the church in Jerusalem, and they sent out Barnabas to go as far as Antioch. So when he came and had seen the grace of God, he was glad, and exhorted them all, with purpose of heart, to cleave to the Lord. For he was a good man, full of the Holy Spirit and of faith. And a great many people were added to the Lord. Then Barnabas departed for Tarsus to seek Saul. When he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. So it was that for a whole year they assembled with the church and taught a great many people. And the disciples were first called Christians in Antioch. And in these days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch. Then one of them, named Agabus, stood up and predicted by the Spirit that there would be a great famine over all the region of the world. And this also happened in the days of Claudius Caesar. 🛰️ Situation Report

The scattered believers, fleeing the violence in Jerusalem, inadvertently plant the seeds of a Gentile church in Antioch, demonstrating that God’s mission is not hindered by human disorder. Barnabas, a man of encouragement, is dispatched to verify this work, recognizing the sovereign hand of God at work among those who were once outsiders. The church’s response to the predicted famine reveals a unified, practical faith that transcends geographical and cultural divisions.

🛡️ Reflection for a R.E.S.O.L.U.T.E. Citizen — Steadfast

Fredericksburg (local)

Just as the scattered believers found new ground in Antioch, you must be steadfast in your role as the spiritual head of your home, regardless of the external chaos. Your wife and children rely on your unwavering commitment to Christ, not for their salvation, which comes by grace alone, but for their nurture and protection in the truth. You do not waver when the culture shifts; you stand firm in the covenant responsibilities God has given you, providing a stable anchor for your family amidst the storm.

Virginia (state)
The early church in Antioch did not wait for a centralized command from Jerusalem to begin preaching to the Greeks; they acted in obedience to the Great Commission, recognizing that God’s sovereignty extends beyond their immediate comfort zone. As a citizen of this state, you are called to be a steady presence in your community, upholding biblical order and virtue without apology. You understand that true liberty is found under the rule of Christ, and you exercise your civic duties with a clear conscience, supporting structures that allow the Gospel to flourish and families to thrive in godliness.

United States (nation)
The relief sent to Judea by the Gentile believers in Antioch was not merely charity; it was a testament to the unity of the body of Christ across ethnic lines, yet it preserved the distinct roles and responsibilities within that body. The nation needs men who are steadfast in their loyalty to God’s law above political expediency. You do not chase the shifting winds of public opinion but stand firm on the Rock, reminding your neighbors that the true hope of the nation lies in its return to biblical morality and the acknowledgment of God’s sovereignty over all kingdoms.

🦅 This Day in American History — September 23

On September 23, 1779, with his ship aflame and sinking beneath him off the English coast, Captain John Paul Jones answered the call to surrender, by tradition, 'I have not yet begun to fight,' and fought on until the British Serapis struck her colors to him. On the same day in 1806, Meriwether Lewis and William Clark brought their Corps of Discovery home to St. Louis after two and a half years, having pressed across the unmapped continent to the Pacific and returned. The captain who would not quit a burning deck and the explorers who would not turn back from the wilderness both teach a Steadfast citizen to hold to his purpose to the very end, trusting the God who strengthens those who do not faint.

📖 Psalm 117

By the power of Your Holy Spirit, may you instill in them the reverence due to the King of Kings, that they too may learn to Laud Him with the sincerity of their hearts, knowing that their Father in heaven is as faithful as their earthly father strives to be.

Do not be weary in doing good, brother, for the faithfulness of the LORD endures forever.
