Christian Civic Centers

Not municipal buildings — owned institutions where the gospel shapes civic life: church, household, business, school, media, and the men who hold the walls. Built for Aaron Renn's Negative World, American Reformer's fortress network, and rooted localism on the ground.

The problem: cut-flower civilization

Will Herberg's metaphor (via Providence Forum) still bites: a culture cut from its Judeo-Christian roots looks fine for a season, then wilts. Negative World is not a culture-war fair fight or a neutral marketplace — it is insurgency conditions. The answer is not monkish exit alone nor frontal assault alone, but strategic fortresses that are antifragile, networked, and aimed at a renewed Positive World.

Shift loyaltiesPatriotism to regime ≠ loyalty to people and place. Redirect time, tithe, and talent toward local Christian projects first.
Owned spaceEconomic, cultural, and physical ground you control — so hostile platforms cannot erase your livelihood or meeting.
NetworkSingle fortresses fail. Alfred's burhs were roads between strong points — mutual aid, not Lone Ranger ego.
Rooted localismFaithfulness where your feet are. Councils, schools, commerce, and church on one map — your Fredericksburg, not abstraction.

City-as-household lens (MOOP)

Provision — jobs, housing, food systems under wisdom
Protection — law, safety, spiritual watch
Formation — schools, discipleship, media
Legacy — institutions that outlive you

Every civic center you list should score on all four — not activism theater.
