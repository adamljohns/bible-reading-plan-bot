He contrasts the eternal value of fearing the LORD with the fleeting nature of worldly cunning and pride. The passage emphasizes that true leadership and lasting honor are rooted in humility, righteousness, and a reverent dependence on God’s wisdom.

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Fears the LORD
Brother, you stand at the helm of your household and the battlefield of your daily duties, but the true source of your strength is not in your own skill alone. It is in the fear of the LORD, which Solomons identifies as the very beginning of wisdom. This is not a cowering terror, but a reverent awe that aligns your will with the will of the Creator. When you fear God, you acknowledge His sovereignty over your career, your family, and your safety. You recognize that without His knowledge, your understanding is blind and your efforts are vanity.

The world tells you to trust your own heart, to rely on your instincts and your past victories. But the fool trusts himself, and the fool falls. You, brother, walk in wisdom by rejecting the pride of self-sufficiency. You serve your King first, and in doing so, you gain the respect of men and the favor of God. Honor does not come from seeking it, but from pursuing righteousness and humility. Let the fear of the LORD be the compass that guides your decisions, for it is the instruction that leads to true wisdom.

Your duty as a father and a husband is to model this reverence for your children and your wife. You do not rule by force, but by the example of a man who submits to the higher authority of God. When you face the crookedness of this world, do not become twisted by it. Stand upright, loving what is right and hating what is evil. Your integrity is your armor, and your reverence for God is the hinge upon which your life turns.

🦅 This Day in American History — September 22
On September 22, 1776, the young schoolmaster Captain Nathan Hale was hanged by the British as a spy in New York, meeting his death with a composure long remembered as the mark of a patriot who counted his life well spent for his country. On the same day in 1862, President Abraham Lincoln issued the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation, binding himself and the nation to set free the enslaved within the rebellious states. The soldier obedient unto death and the president heeding the demand of conscience alike call an Obedient citizen to submit himself to the higher law, doing the right that is set before him whatever it costs, in the fear of God.
