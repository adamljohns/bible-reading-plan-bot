🌅 0600 Morning Wisdom

We sit again at the table of Solomon — and he doesn't flinch. He aims at every appetite of a man: food, money, words, sons, women, and the cup.

📖 Scripture — Proverbs 23

When you sit down to dine with a ruler,
study carefully what is set before you —
put a knife to your own throat, brother,
if you are a man given to appetite.
Don't lust after his fine food;
it is deceitful bread.

Don't wear yourself out chasing riches;
have the discernment to drop the race.
You raise your eyes to wealth, and it is gone —
it sprouts wings like an eagle and flies into the sky.

Don't share the bread of a stingy man,
and don't crave his delicacies.
"Eat, drink," he says with his mouth —
but his heart is not with you.
The morsel you have swallowed you'll vomit back up,
and the courteous words you spent on him are wasted.

Don't speak in the ears of a fool —
he will despise the good sense of your words.

Don't move the ancient boundary stones
or push into the fields of the fatherless;
their Defender is mighty,
and He Himself will plead their case against you.

Bring your heart under instruction
and your ears under the words of knowledge.

Don't withhold correction from a child;
if you strike him with the rod, he will not die —
you strike him with the rod
and deliver his soul from Sheol.

My son, if your heart grows wise,
my own heart will leap —
yes, my deepest being will sing
when your lips speak what is right.

Do not let your heart envy sinners;
rather, walk all day long in the fear of the LORD.
There is a future ahead —
a hope that will not be cut off.

Listen, my son, and be wise;
direct your heart on the right road.
Do not run with heavy drinkers
or those who gorge themselves on meat,
for the drunk and the glutton sink into poverty,
and drowsiness clothes a man in rags.

Listen to the father who gave you life;
do not despise your mother when she is old.
Buy truth, and never sell it —
buy wisdom, discipline, and understanding.
The father of the righteous greatly rejoices;
the man who fathers a wise son finds joy in him.
Let your father and mother be glad;
let her who bore you rejoice.

My son, give Me your heart,
and let your eyes take pleasure in My ways.
The prostitute is a deep pit;
the seductive stranger is a narrow well;
she lies in wait like a robber
and multiplies the faithless among men.

Who has misery? Who has sorrow?
Who picks fights? Who has bruises without cause?
Who has bloodshot eyes?
Those who linger over wine,
those who go searching out the mixed cup.
Do not even stare at the wine when it is red,
when it sparkles in the cup
and slides down smoothly —
in the end it bites like a serpent
and poisons like an adder.

Your eyes will see strange things,
and your heart will speak perverse things.
You will be like a man lying down in the heart of the sea,
or asleep on top of a ship's mast.
"They struck me, and I felt no pain;
they beat me, and I did not know it.
When will I wake up?
I will look for another drink."

⸻

🧭 Context Summary
Solomon turns the lamp of wisdom into every dark corner of a man's life — the table, the wallet, the rod of discipline, the marriage bed, the wine cup. He doesn't moralize from a distance; he talks like a father at the dinner table to a son who is about to leave the house. Watch your appetite. Watch your money. Watch your words. Watch your sons. Watch your women. Watch your drink. The man who refuses to manage these things will be managed by them.

⸻

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Manages Faithfully

A REAL MAN who manages faithfully is not a tyrant and he is not a miser — he is a steward. Solomon paints stewardship that runs from the dinner table to the bank account to the rod of correction to the wine cup. The faithful manager understands that everything he touches is on loan from God: his appetite, his hours, his children, his wallet, his bed. He does not let any of them go wild, because anything ungoverned ends up governing him.

A passive man tells himself, "I work hard, I deserve this." And the deceitful bread, the slow-poured cup, and the second look at the woman slide under the gate while he isn't watching. A faithful manager keeps a knife at his own throat at the ruler's table. He doesn't run himself ragged chasing wealth. He doesn't crave a stingy man's favor or eat the bread of a deceitful host. He buys truth — and once he owns it, he never sells it.

Notice how Solomon handles the rod. The man who manages faithfully isn't afraid to correct his sons, because faithful management of a child sometimes costs the child a little pain now to save him from Sheol later. He handles the cup the same way: he refuses to stare into the red sparkle. He knows what bites at the end of the night.

Manage your house under God today, brother. The boundary stones do not move themselves.

⛏️ Personal Application — Manages Faithfully
• Audit one appetite — food, drink, screen, scroll — and put a knife to it today. Don't bargain.
• Open the spreadsheet. Open the bank app. Don't pretend the numbers don't exist; faithful management starts with knowing.
• Pick one son whose heart you've been "managing around" instead of managing — speak to him today, eye to eye, with both correction and tenderness.
• Buy truth before you buy convenience. If a conversation costs you comfort but earns you wisdom, pay full price.

🕖 0700 First Watch — The Husband's Post

Today's first watch carries us into Chronicles — Abijah's last stand against a rebel kingdom, and Asa's first reforms when he inherits the throne.

📖 Scripture — 2 Chronicles 13:7–22; 14:1–8

Around Rehoboam, while he was young and tender-hearted, worthless men gathered — rebels and idle fellows who flexed their numbers against him until he could not withstand them.

But now Abijah stood on a high place and called out to Jeroboam and all Israel:

"You think you can stand against the kingdom of the LORD held in the hand of the sons of David? You are a great multitude, and you have your golden calves that Jeroboam made you for gods. But haven't you driven out the priests of the LORD, the sons of Aaron and the Levites, and made priests for yourselves like the nations? Anyone who came with a bull and seven rams could be a priest of these no-gods.

As for us — the LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him. We have priests serving the LORD, the sons of Aaron, and the Levites at their work. They burn burnt offerings to the LORD every morning and every evening; they offer sweet incense; they set the bread of the Presence in order on the pure table; the golden lampstand and its lamps burn every evening — for we keep the charge of the LORD our God. But you — you have forsaken Him.

And look: God Himself is at our head, and His priests with the trumpets of alarm stand ready to sound against you. Sons of Israel, do not fight against the LORD, the God of your fathers — you will not prosper."

While Abijah spoke, Jeroboam had quietly sent an ambush around behind. Judah turned and saw the battle was in front of them and behind them. They cried out to the LORD, and the priests sounded the trumpets, and the men of Judah raised a shout. As they shouted, God struck Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah. Israel fled before Judah, and God delivered them into Judah's hand. Five hundred thousand chosen men of Israel fell that day. Judah prevailed because they relied on the LORD, the God of their fathers.

Abijah pursued Jeroboam and took cities from him: Bethel and Jeshanah and Ephron, with their villages. Jeroboam did not recover his strength again in the days of Abijah. The LORD struck him, and he died.

Abijah grew strong; he took fourteen wives and fathered twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters. The rest of his acts, his ways and his sayings, are written in the commentary of the prophet Iddo.

Abijah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the City of David. His son Asa reigned in his place. In Asa's days, the land had rest for ten years. Asa did what was good and right in the eyes of the LORD his God. He removed the foreign altars and the high places, broke the standing stones, and cut down the Asherah poles. He commanded Judah to seek the LORD, the God of their fathers, and to keep His law and commandment. He swept the high places and the sun-pillars out of every city in Judah, and the kingdom rested under him. Because the land had peace and he had no war in those years, he built fortified cities in Judah — for the LORD had given him rest.

He said to Judah: "Let us build these cities, surround them with walls, towers, gates, and bars; the land is still before us because we have sought the LORD our God. We have sought Him, and He has given us rest on every side." So they built and prospered.

Asa had an army of three hundred thousand men from Judah carrying shields and spears, and two hundred eighty thousand from Benjamin carrying bucklers and drawing the bow — all mighty men of valor.

⸻

🗺️ Briefing Summary
Abijah, the son of Rehoboam, is no shining king — but he says one true sentence loud enough to bend a battle: "The LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him." That confession turns the trap of an ambush into a routing of Israel. Then his son Asa takes the throne and starts where any faithful husband should start: he removes idols, gathers his people back under the law, and uses the season of rest God gives him to build walls — not just for the kingdom, but for his sons.

⸻

❤️ Reflection for Your Wife — H.A.P.P.Y. Husband — Protecting

As a HAPPY husband called to protect, this passage hits you square in the chest. Asa does not protect Judah primarily by stockpiling weapons — although he does that in season. He protects Judah first by removing what God hates from the land. Foreign altars come down. Standing stones are broken. Asherah poles are chopped to splinters. The sun-pillars in every city of Judah are torn out by their roots.

Brother, that is the work of a real protector at home. The man who guards his wife is not first the one who locks the front door — he is the one who removes the idols already inside the house. Foreign altars in your home look like the slow normalization of bitter speech, the carefully framed pictures of women on a screen, the spending pattern that exalts comfort over covenant, the silence in the evening that you've learned to call "tired." Asa did not say, "We'll deal with the high places later." He removed them — first.

And then, only then, did Asa build walls. Because peace under God comes before fortification — not the other way around. A man who builds walls around an idolatrous house has only fortified the wrong thing. But a man who cleans his house first, then strengthens it, makes a real refuge for his wife.

Your wife is not asking you to be ferocious; she is asking you to be reliable. She needs to know the same God who held Judah's line through Abijah's confession still holds the perimeter of your home. She needs to know that when you say, "The LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him," you mean it on a Tuesday morning, not just in church.

⛏️ Personal Application — Protecting
• Walk your house — physically — and identify one "high place" that quietly sits there. Take it down today. Don't argue with yourself.
• Speak the confession of Abijah out loud, plainly, to your wife: "The LORD is our God, and we have not forsaken Him in this house."
• Then build one wall — pick a real, practical safeguard (a budget rule, a screen rule, a bedtime rhythm, a Sunday liturgy) and put it in place this weekend.
• Make sure your wife knows where the trumpet is. If trouble surrounds the house, she should know you will sound the alarm, not freeze.

🕚 1100 Second Watch — The Father's Charge

We stay with Asa through his greatest moment of faith — and then sit under the voice of a prophet who calls a whole nation back to covenant.

📖 Scripture — 2 Chronicles 14:9–15; 15:1–19

Then Zerah the Ethiopian came out against Judah with an army of a million men and three hundred chariots, and pressed up to Mareshah. Asa marched out to meet him, and they drew battle lines in the valley of Zephathah at Mareshah.

Asa called upon the LORD his God and said:
"LORD, there is no one like You — between the mighty and him who has no strength — to help. Help us, O LORD our God, for we rely on You, and in Your name we have come against this multitude. O LORD, You are our God. Let no mere man prevail against You."

The LORD struck the Ethiopians before Asa and before Judah, and the Ethiopians fled. Asa and the people with him pursued them as far as Gerar. The Ethiopians fell until there was none left alive, for they were shattered before the LORD and before His army. Judah carried away a vast amount of plunder. They struck all the cities around Gerar — for the fear of the LORD had fallen upon those cities — and plundered them, since there was great spoil. They struck down the tents of those who had cattle, took many sheep and camels, and returned to Jerusalem.

Then the Spirit of God came on Azariah son of Oded. He went out to meet Asa and said:

"Hear me, Asa, and all Judah and Benjamin. The LORD is with you while you are with Him. If you seek Him, He will be found by you; but if you forsake Him, He will forsake you.

For a long time Israel was without the true God, without a teaching priest, and without law. But when in their distress they turned to the LORD, the God of Israel, and sought Him, He was found by them. In those days there was no peace for the one going out and none for the one coming in; great troubles were on all the inhabitants of the lands. Nation crashed against nation and city against city, because God troubled them with every kind of distress.

But you — be strong. Do not let your hands hang down, for your work will be rewarded."

When Asa heard these words — the prophecy of Azariah son of Oded the prophet — he took courage. He put away the detestable idols out of all the land of Judah and Benjamin and from the cities he had taken in the hill country of Ephraim, and he repaired the altar of the LORD that was in front of the porch of the LORD's house.

He gathered all Judah and Benjamin, along with the foreigners from Ephraim, Manasseh, and Simeon who had come over to him out of Israel in great numbers when they saw that the LORD his God was with him. They gathered to Jerusalem in the third month of the fifteenth year of Asa's reign. They sacrificed to the LORD that day, from the spoil they had brought, seven hundred head of cattle and seven thousand sheep.

They entered into a covenant to seek the LORD, the God of their fathers, with all their heart and all their soul — and to put to death whoever would not seek the LORD, the God of Israel, whether small or great, whether man or woman. They swore to the LORD with a loud voice, with shouting, with trumpets, and with horns. All Judah rejoiced over the oath, for they had sworn with their whole heart and sought Him with their whole desire — and He was found by them. The LORD gave them rest all around.

King Asa also removed his grandmother Maacah from being queen mother, because she had made a horrid image for Asherah. Asa cut down her image, ground it to dust, and burned it at the brook Kidron. But the high places out in Israel were not removed; nevertheless, the heart of Asa was wholly true to the LORD all his days. He brought into the house of God the dedicated things of his father and his own dedicated things — silver, gold, and vessels. And there was no more war until the thirty-fifth year of Asa's reign.

⸻

🗺️ Field Notes
This passage is a father's heritage moment. Asa stares down a million-man army with a sentence: "Help us, O LORD our God, for we rely on You." God strikes the army, and the chase continues until the fear of the LORD has fallen on entire cities. Then a prophet rises up out of nowhere and lays a charge on Asa's whole generation: "Be strong. Do not let your hands hang down." Asa hears it, takes courage, and leads the largest covenant renewal his nation has seen since the days of Joshua. A faithful father is not merely brave in battle — he leads his household into a sworn covenant with the LORD, even when it costs him his grandmother's throne.

⸻

👨‍👧 Reflection for Your Children — F.U.L.F.I.L.L.E.D. Father — Leading

As a FULFILLED father called to lead, you are looking at the gold standard in Asa. He led his kingdom three times in one day: he led his men into battle, he led them to call on the LORD before they swung a sword, and he led them in covenant after the battle was over. Gideon, Boaz, and Shiloh are watching you do all three of those things in a smaller scale — every week.

Notice the order. Asa does not say to Judah, "Be brave." He says to the LORD, "We rely on You." A father who leads from his own strength teaches his children to lean on their own — and they will collapse the first time they meet a million-man army. A father who leads by calling on God in front of his children teaches them where strength actually comes from. Gideon needs that lesson written into his bones — purpose without prayer is just ambition. Boaz needs it because strength without humility curdles into self-trust. Shiloh needs it because she is growing up in a world that wants her to live by feelings, not by faith.

Then notice Azariah's word: "Be strong. Do not let your hands hang down." Brother, your hands hang down when you let your children disciple themselves through screens and friends. Your hands hang down when you skip the evening reading because everyone is "tired." Your hands hang down when you stop correcting because correction makes the room uncomfortable. A leading father lifts his hands again. He does not shout — he gathers. He does not shame — he covenants.

And note the courage Asa shows with his own grandmother. He does not say, "She's family — I'll let it slide." He cuts down her image and grinds it to powder. Leading fatherhood loves the people in your home enough to break the idols even the family elders have set up. Sometimes the hardest idol in your house came from someone you love.

⛏️ Personal Application — Leading
• Pray out loud over one child today before you correct, instruct, or send him into something hard. Let them hear Asa's prayer in your own voice.
• Pick one "Asherah" your household has tolerated because the source was family — and break it cleanly today.
• Lift your hands: re-start one evening practice you have let drop (Word, prayer, songs, family table, ordered bedtime).
• Renew a small covenant out loud — even one sentence: "In this house we serve the LORD."

🕒 1500 Third Watch — The Citizen's Stand

The third watch is the long view today — Israel's revolving-door of kings under judgment, Asa's tragic late-life turn, and the steady rise of Jehoshaphat. Three lessons in one watch about what a citizen looks like when his nation forgets the LORD.

📖 Scripture — 1 Kings 16:1–24 & 2 Chronicles 16 & 2 Chronicles 17

The word of the LORD came to Jehu son of Hanani against Baasha, king of Israel:
"I lifted you out of the dust and made you ruler over My people Israel. But you walked in the way of Jeroboam and led My people Israel into sin to provoke Me with their sins. Therefore I will sweep away Baasha and his house, and I will make your house like the house of Jeroboam."

When Baasha died, his son Elah reigned in Tirzah for two years. Elah's servant Zimri, captain of half his chariots, conspired against him. While Elah was drinking himself drunk in the house of Arza, Zimri struck him down and made himself king. He destroyed the entire house of Baasha, just as the LORD's word through Jehu had spoken.

Zimri reigned seven days in Tirzah. When Israel heard he had murdered the king, the army made Omri, captain of the army, king over Israel in the camp. Omri marched up from Gibbethon and besieged Tirzah. When Zimri saw the city was taken, he went into the citadel of the king's house, burned it over himself, and died — for his sins which he had committed, walking in the way of Jeroboam.

Israel was then split: half followed Tibni son of Ginath, and half followed Omri. But Omri prevailed, and Tibni died. In the thirty-first year of Asa king of Judah, Omri began to reign over Israel for twelve years; he reigned six of them in Tirzah. Then he bought the hill of Samaria from Shemer for two talents of silver and built a city there, naming it Samaria after Shemer the previous owner of the hill.

⸻

Meanwhile in Judah, in the thirty-sixth year of his reign, Asa faced a different kind of test. Baasha king of Israel came up against Judah and started to build Ramah, blocking traffic in and out of Asa's kingdom. Asa did not seek the LORD this time. Instead, he stripped silver and gold out of the treasuries of the LORD's house and of his own palace and sent them as a bribe to Ben-hadad king of Aram in Damascus, saying:

"Let there be a treaty between me and you, as there was between my father and your father. I'm sending you silver and gold — break your treaty with Baasha so he will withdraw from me."

Ben-hadad took the silver and broke the treaty. He sent the captains of his armies against the cities of Israel, struck Ijon and Dan and Abel-maim and the storehouse cities of Naphtali. When Baasha heard, he stopped building Ramah. Asa took all Judah, and they carried away the stones and the timber of Ramah and built up Geba and Mizpah with them.

Then Hanani the seer came to Asa king of Judah and said:
"Because you relied on the king of Aram and not on the LORD your God, the army of the king of Aram has escaped your hand. Were not the Ethiopians and the Lubim a huge army with very many chariots and horsemen? Yet because you relied on the LORD, He delivered them into your hand. The eyes of the LORD range to and fro throughout the whole earth, to strengthen the heart of those who are wholly His. In this you have acted foolishly. From now on you will have wars."

Asa was angry with the seer and put him in prison for this. At the same time, Asa oppressed some of the people.

In the thirty-ninth year of his reign, Asa was struck with a disease in his feet — and his disease became severe. Yet even in his disease he did not seek the LORD, but only the physicians. Asa slept with his fathers in the forty-first year of his reign. They buried him in a tomb he had cut for himself in the City of David and laid him on a bier filled with spices and ointments prepared by the perfumers' art. They made a very great burning for him.

⸻

His son Jehoshaphat reigned in his place and strengthened himself against Israel. He stationed troops in all the fortified cities of Judah and set garrisons in the land of Judah and in the cities of Ephraim that his father Asa had captured.

The LORD was with Jehoshaphat because he walked in the earlier ways of his father David and did not seek the Baals. He sought the God of his father and walked in His commandments, not in the practices of Israel. Therefore the LORD established the kingdom in his hand. All Judah brought tribute to Jehoshaphat, and he had great riches and honor. His heart took courage in the ways of the LORD, and he again removed the high places and the Asherah poles out of Judah.

In the third year of his reign he sent his officials — Ben-hail, Obadiah, Zechariah, Nethanel, and Micaiah — to teach in the cities of Judah, along with the Levites Shemaiah, Nethaniah, Zebadiah, Asahel, Shemiramoth, Jehonathan, Adonijah, Tobijah, and Tobadonijah; and with them the priests Elishama and Jehoram. They taught throughout Judah, carrying the Book of the Law of the LORD with them, and they went through all the cities of Judah and taught the people.

The fear of the LORD fell on all the kingdoms of the lands around Judah, so that they did not make war against Jehoshaphat. Some Philistines brought tribute and silver; the Arabians brought him flocks — seven thousand seven hundred rams and seven thousand seven hundred male goats. Jehoshaphat grew greater and greater. He built fortresses and store cities in Judah and had much work in the cities. He kept seasoned warriors in Jerusalem and in the fortified cities throughout all Judah.

⸻

🗺️ Situation Report
Three kingdoms, three lessons. In Israel, four kings in a generation — Baasha, Elah, Zimri, Omri — and three of them die violently because they walked in the sin of Jeroboam. Down in Judah, the once-great Asa squanders his legacy at the end of his life: he bribes a pagan king instead of seeking the LORD, imprisons a prophet for telling him the truth, and dies refusing the help of God even on his deathbed. Then Jehoshaphat rises — and instead of inheriting his father's last decade of foolishness, he goes back to the earlier ways of David, sends teachers throughout the land, and the fear of the LORD goes out across the region. A nation lives or dies on whether its citizens — and especially its leaders — will steadily walk with God year after year.

⸻

🛡️ Reflection for a R.E.S.O.L.U.T.E. Citizen — Steadfast

Fredericksburg (local)
As a RESOLUTE citizen called to be steadfast, this passage is a hard mirror. The kings of Israel run through their thrones like a man burning through credit cards — each one starts strong, claims to know what he's doing, and ends as a cautionary tale. Steadfastness is the opposite of that. A steadfast Fredericksburg citizen shows up year after year at the same church, the same job, the same family table, the same voting booth, the same neighbor's porch — because faithfulness in your post is what actually changes a city. Be the man on your block who didn't drift.

Virginia (state)
Across Virginia, steadfastness looks like Jehoshaphat. He did not try to build a national following on a hot take — he sent teachers into every city of Judah with the Book of the Law of the LORD. Brother, in a state that careens between cultural fashions, the steadfast citizen is the one who keeps quietly investing in catechesis: at home, at church, in the homeschool group, in the men's huddle, on the long phone call with a younger man who's wobbling. You are not called to win the news cycle. You are called to teach the Book.

United States (nation)
And then there's Asa. He is the warning at the national level. A man who spent forty years walking with the LORD — strong, brave, reforming — turned at the end of his life and trusted Damascus over Jerusalem. He bribed a pagan king with the silver of God's own house. He imprisoned the prophet who told him the truth. He oppressed his own people. And when his feet rotted under him, he still would not seek the LORD. For our nation, the lesson is bare: you can spend decades blessed by God and then squander it in one foolish treaty. Steadfastness for an American citizen means refusing to outsource our trust to silver, to Damascus, to chariots, or to clever men. The eyes of the LORD still range to and fro throughout the whole earth to strengthen the heart of those who are wholly His. He has not become smaller because our nation has.

• Look at one place in your life where you have been tempted to bribe Damascus — pay a worldly source to "fix" something only the LORD can really hold. Repent of it cleanly and ask Him for help instead.

A Song of Ascents. Of Solomon.

Unless the LORD builds the house,

Unless the LORD watches over the city,

It is in vain for you to rise early,
to delay your rest,
to eat the bread of anxious toil —

Like arrows in the hand of a warrior

🌾 Reflection for a Man at Home and in Community

As a HAPPY husband, Honest tonight means you stop pretending that the security of your home rests on your hustle. Maria is not safe because you stay up late or because you wake up early — she is safe because the LORD watches over the house. Honest means you say out loud, "Lord, You built this — and unless You keep it, my arms are not enough." That confession is rest to a wife.

As a FULFILLED father, Faithful tonight means you put your arrows into the quiver of the LORD before you carve any plan for their lives. Gideon, Boaz, and Shiloh are not your projects — they are heritage from the LORD. Tonight, that means you stop trying to "fix" them and start praying over them. The arrows will fly straight when the warrior — God Himself — pulls the string.

As a RESOLUTE citizen, Steadfast tonight means you refuse the modern lie that the city is held together by the watchmen. Watchmen matter; you serve as one in your sphere. But the LORD watches over the city. The civic anxiety that says "if I don't carry it all, it collapses" is unbelief in respectable clothes. Lay it down. Let Him give His beloved sleep tonight, and rise tomorrow ready to stand your post again.
