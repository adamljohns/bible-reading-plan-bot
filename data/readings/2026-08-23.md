🌅 0600 Morning Wisdom
Proverbs presses in close this morning, brother, exposing the appetites — for wealth, for wine, for ease — that quietly enslave the man who will not govern himself under God.
📖 Scripture — Proverbs 23
When you sit down to eat with a ruler, Consider carefully what is before you;
And put a knife to your throat If you are a man given to appetite.
Do not desire his delicacies, For they are deceptive food.
Do not overwork to be rich; Because of your own understanding, cease!
Will you set your eyes on that which is not? For riches certainly make themselves wings; They fly away like an eagle toward heaven.
Do not eat the bread of a miser, Nor desire his delicacies;
For as he thinks in his heart, so is he. “Eat and drink!” he says to you, But his heart is not with you.
The morsel you have eaten, you will vomit up, And waste your pleasant words.
Do not speak in the hearing of a fool, For he will despise the wisdom of your words.
Do not remove the ancient landmark, Nor enter the fields of the fatherless;
For their Redeemer is mighty; He will plead their cause against you.
Apply your heart to instruction, And your ears to words of knowledge.
Do not withhold correction from a child, For if you beat him with a rod, he will not die.
You shall beat him with a rod, And deliver his soul from hell.
My son, if your heart is wise, My heart will rejoice—indeed, I myself;
Yes, my inmost being will rejoice When your lips speak right things.
Do not let your heart envy sinners, But be zealous for the fear of the LORD all the day;
For surely there is a hereafter, And your hope will not be cut off.
Hear, my son, and be wise; And guide your heart in the way.
Do not mix with winebibbers, Or with gluttonous eaters of meat;
For the drunkard and the glutton will come to poverty, And drowsiness will clothe a man with rags.
Listen to your father who begot you, And do not despise your mother when she is old.
Buy the truth, and do not sell it, Also wisdom and instruction and understanding.
The father of the righteous will greatly rejoice, And he who begets a wise child will delight in him.
Let your father and your mother be glad, And let her who bore you rejoice.
My son, give me your heart, And let your eyes observe my ways.
For a harlot is a deep pit, And a seductress is a narrow well.
She also lies in wait as for a victim, And increases the unfaithful among men.
Who has woe? Who has sorrow? Who has contentions? Who has complaints? Who has wounds without cause? Who has redness of eyes?
Those who linger long at the wine, Those who go in search of mixed wine.
Do not look on the wine when it is red, When it sparkles in the cup, When it swirls around smoothly;
At the last it bites like a serpent, And stings like a viper.
Your eyes will see strange things, And your heart will utter perverse things.
Yes, you will be like one who lies down in the midst of the sea, Or like one who lies at the top of the mast, saying:
“They have struck me, but I was not hurt; They have beaten me, but I did not feel it. When shall I awake, that I may seek another drink?”

⸻
🧭 Context Summary
Solomon, the wisest man to ever live, warns the young man against the seductive and ultimately deceptive nature of worldly wealth and the company of the miserly. He instructs the reader to guard his heart against the envy of the rich and the false hospitality of the stingy. The passage emphasizes that God is the ultimate Defender of the poor and the rightful Owner of all property, calling His people to trust in His provision rather than the fleeting security of mammon.

Your strength lies in your detachment from the world’s economy and your attachment to the King.

The cry of the beggar echoes through the corridors of grace, calling the King to stop and restore sight to the blind.

📖 Scripture — Luke 18:35-43 & Mark 10:46-52

Then it happened, as He was coming near Jericho, that a certain blind man sat by the road begging.
And hearing a multitude passing by, he asked what it meant.
So they told him that Jesus of Nazareth was passing by.
And he cried out, saying, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”
Then those who went before warned him that he should be quiet; but he cried out all the more, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”
So Jesus stood still and commanded him to be brought to Him. And when he had come near, He asked him,
saying, “What do you want Me to do for you?” He said, “Lord, that I may receive my sight.”
Then Jesus said to him, “Receive your sight; your faith has made you well.”
And immediately he received his sight, and followed Him, glorifying God. And all the people, when they saw it, gave praise to God.
Now they came to Jericho. As He went out of Jericho with His disciples and a great multitude, blind Bartimaeus, the son of Timaeus, sat by the road begging.
And when he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out and say, “Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!”
Then many warned him to be quiet; but he cried out all the more, “Son of David, have mercy on me!”
So Jesus stood still and commanded him to be called. Then they called the blind man, saying to him, “Be of good cheer. Rise, He is calling you.”
And throwing aside his garment, he rose and came to Jesus.
So Jesus answered and said to him, “What do you want Me to do for you?” The blind man said to Him, “Rabboni, that I may receive my sight.”
Then Jesus said to him, “Go your way; your faith has made you well.” And immediately he received his sight and followed Jesus on the road.

⸻

🗺️ Briefing Summary

These parallel accounts record the moment Jesus encounters Bartimaeus, a beggar who recognizes the Messiah’s identity when the crowds do not. The blind man persists in his petition despite the pressure to remain silent, demonstrating a faith that refuses to let go until he receives mercy. Jesus stops His progress to address the spiritual and physical need before Him, affirming that salvation comes through faith alone. The restoration of Bartimaeus serves as a public testament to God’s power and a call for the onlookers to glorify the Father.

⸻

Your provision is an act of worship, a tangible declaration that you trust God to supply all your needs so that you may supply hers. Like the blind man who trusted Jesus enough to leave his stool, you must trust God enough to take responsibility for the spiritual welfare of your family. This is not a burden of guilt, but a privilege of grace, for you are appointed to reflect Christ’s love to Maria in a way that is unique and irrevocable.

Your provision is the steady hand that guides her, the strong arm that protects her, and the voice that speaks life into her spirit. By providing spiritual leadership, you create a space where she can flourish in her God-given role, secure in the knowledge that her husband is watching over her soul. This is the glory of biblical manhood: to be a man of God who provides for the flock entrusted to his care.

And he sought to see who Jesus was, but could not because of the crowd, for he was of short stature.
So he ran ahead and climbed up into a sycamore tree to see Him, for He was going to pass that way.

Then came the first, saying, ‘Master, your mina has earned ten minas.’
And he said to him, ‘Well done, good servant; because you were faithful in a very little, have authority over ten cities.’
And the second came, saying, ‘Master, your mina has earned five minas.’
Likewise he said to him, ‘You also be over five cities.’
“Then another came, saying, ‘Master, here is your mina, which I have kept put away in a handkerchief.
For I feared you, because you are an austere man. You collect what you did not deposit, and reap what you did not sow.’

(But they said to him, ‘Master, he has ten minas.’)

🗺️ Field Notes

In this passage, Christ confronts the political expectations of His people by revealing that His kingdom is not of this world, yet it demands absolute loyalty. Zacchaeus illustrates the transformative power of grace, where a life of greed is replaced by generous stewardship upon encountering the Son of Man. The parable of the minas serves as a sobering warning that God’s sovereignty is absolute, and His servants will be held strictly accountable for their stewardship.

The King has come to His own house, and the faithful citizen stands ready to receive Him with open arms and a prepared heart.

When He had said this, He went on ahead, going up to Jerusalem.
And it came to pass, when He drew near to Bethphage and Bethany, at the mountain called Olivet, that He sent two of His disciples,
saying, “Go into the village opposite you, where as you enter you will find a colt tied, on which no one has ever sat. Loose it and bring it here.

Then they brought him to Jesus. And they threw their own clothes on the colt, and they set Jesus on him.

saying, “If you had known, even you, especially in this your day, the things that make for your peace! But now they are hidden from your eyes.

🛰️ Situation Report
Jesus enters Jerusalem not as a conquering general with swords, but as a humble King on a colt, fulfilling the prophecy of Zechariah and revealing the nature of His kingdom. The disciples rejoice in His presence, acknowledging Him as the Messiah, while the religious leaders remain blind to the significance of the moment. Jesus weeps over the city, knowing that their rejection of Him will lead to their destruction, yet He offers them a chance to find peace. This passage reveals the tension between the humble arrival of the King and the hardened hearts of those who should have welcomed Him.
