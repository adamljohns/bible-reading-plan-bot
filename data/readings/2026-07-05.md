🦅 This Day in American History — July 5
On July 5, 1852, Frederick Douglass delivered his famous address "What to the Slave is the Fourth of July?" in Rochester, New York. In this powerful sermon, Douglass, a man of deep Christian faith and moral courage, exposed the hypocrisy of celebrating freedom while enslaving his fellow man, calling his audience to repentance and true justice under God.

In 1957, President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed the Civil Rights Act of 1957 into law, the first federal civil rights legislation since Reconstruction. Though limited in scope, this act was a providential step in the long journey toward justice, affirming the principle that all citizens are equal before the law, a truth grounded in the imago Dei.
