🌅 0600 Morning Wisdom

The Lord calls His men to stand firm in truth, refusing to be swayed by the chaotic winds of folly.

📖 Scripture — Proverbs 26

As snow in summer and rain in harvest, So honor is not fitting for a fool. Like a flitting sparrow, like a flying swallow, So a curse without cause shall not alight. A whip for the horse, A bridle for the donkey, And a rod for the fool’s back. Do not answer a fool according to his folly, Lest you also be like him.

Answer a fool according to his folly, Lest he be wise in his own eyes. He who sends a message by the hand of a fool Cuts off his own feet and drinks violence. Like the legs of the lame that hang limp Is a proverb in the mouth of fools. Like one who binds a stone in a sling Is he who gives honor to a fool.

Like a thorn that goes into the hand of a drunkard Is a proverb in the mouth of fools. The great God who formed everything Gives the fool his hire and the transgressor his wages. As a dog returns to his own vomit, So a fool repeats his folly. Do you see a man wise in his own eyes? There is more hope for a fool than for him.

The lazy man says, “There is a lion in the road! A fierce lion is in the streets!” As a door turns on its hinges, So does the lazy man on his bed. The lazy man buries his hand in the bowl; It wearies him to bring it back to his mouth. The lazy man is wiser in his own eyes Than seven men who can answer sensibly.

He who passes by and meddles in a quarrel not his own Is like one who takes a dog by the ears. Like a madman who throws firebrands, arrows, and death, Is the man who deceives his neighbor, And says, “I was only joking!” Where there is no wood, the fire goes out; And where there is no talebearer, strife ceases.

As charcoal is to burning coals, and wood to fire, So is a contentious man to kindle strife. The words of a talebearer are like tasty trifles, And they go down into the inmost body. Fervent lips with a wicked heart Are like earthenware covered with silver dross. He who hates, disguises it with his lips, And lays up deceit within himself;

When he speaks kindly, do not believe him, For there are seven abominations in his heart; Though his hatred is covered by deceit, His wickedness will be revealed before the assembly. Whoever digs a pit will fall into it, And he who rolls a stone will have it roll back on him. A lying tongue hates those who are crushed by it, And a flattering mouth works ruin.
⸻

🧭 Context Summary
Solomon addresses the fool not with hope of conversion, but with warnings of futility and danger. The fool is characterized by a refusal to learn, a love of entertainment over instruction, and a chaotic influence on those around him. This section serves as a practical guide for the wise man to maintain his integrity and safety in the presence of persistent folly. The text emphasizes that engaging with such individuals on their own terms only validates their foolishness and endangers the wise.

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Rejects Folly

Brother, you have spent years in combat zones where the enemy’s deception could cost you your life or your brother’s life. In the same way, the fool in Proverbs 26 is a spiritual ambush, a chaotic force that seeks to drag the wise down into the mire of absurdity. The text uses stark imagery: honoring a fool is as unnatural as snow in summer, and engaging with him is like tying a stone in a sling, guaranteeing that your own strength will be turned against you. You must recognize that the fool is not merely mistaken; he is structurally opposed to wisdom, much like a drunkard cannot appreciate the value of a clear path.

Your duty as a head and a protector is to discern the difference between a teachable heart and a hardened fool. There is a time for correction, but there is also a time for withdrawal. To pour out wisdom into the void of a fool’s heart is not an act of love, but of folly. The fool will take your words, twist them, and use them to mock or to cause harm, just as a passerby might use a thornbush to wound the one holding it. You must guard your resources, your time, and your influence with the same vigilance you once guarded your perimeter.

Do not be fooled by the appearance of engagement. The fool thrives on reaction and validation. By refusing to honor him, by refusing to pay his wages, and by refusing to let him carry your message, you deny him the power he seeks. This is not cruelty, brother; it is the preservation of order and truth. Let the fool be as he is, but let you remain steadfast in the wisdom that comes from the fear of the LORD. Stand firm, keep your distance, and let your silence speak louder than his noise.

⸻

⛏️ Personal Application — Rejects Folly

• Identify one relationship or situation in your home or church where you have been enabling folly by engaging it; withdraw your validation and presence from that dynamic immediately.
• Audit your daily interactions to ensure you are not "paying the wages" of fools by spending excessive time, money, or energy on those who reject wisdom.
• Practice disciplined silence when confronted with provocation, recognizing that your response is ammunition that the fool will use against you.

🦅 This Day in American History — September 26
On September 26, 1774, John Chapman, the man known as Johnny Appleseed, was born in Massachusetts, and he would walk the frontier for decades planting orchards whose fruit he would never gather for himself. On the same day in 1789, the Senate gave its unanimous voice to John Jay as the first Chief Justice of the United States, and Washington sealed his commission, setting a faithful man at the head of the new courts. The nurseryman scattering seed for those who came after and the founding of a steady bench of justice both teach a Steadfast citizen to do quiet, lasting good in his appointed place, content to serve the purposes of God beyond his own lifetime.
