📅 Tuesday, February 3, 2026 — 0600
🌅 Morning Wisdom — We enter the heart of a father’s counsel—trust, fear God, embrace discipline,
and find wisdom’s tree of life.
📖 Scripture — Proverbs 3:1–18
My son, do not forget my teaching; let your heart keep my commands, for length of days and years of life
and peace they will add to you.
Let covenant-love and faithfulness not leave you; bind them around your neck, write them on the tablet of
your heart. So you will find favor and good insight in the sight of God and man.
Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways know
Him, and He will make your paths straight.
Do not be wise in your own eyes; fear the LORD and turn away from evil. It will bring healing to your
flesh and refreshment to your bones.
Honor the LORD with your wealth and with the firstfruits of all your produce; then your barns will be
filled with plenty and your vats will overflow with new wine.
My son, do not despise the LORD’s discipline or be weary of His reproof, for whom the LORD loves He
reproves, as a father the son in whom he delights.
Blessed is the one who finds wisdom, the man who gets understanding; for she is more profitable than
silver and yields better return than fine gold. She is more precious than jewels, and nothing you desire
compares with her.
Long life is in her right hand; in her left are riches and honor. Her ways are pleasantness, and all her paths
are peace. She is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her; those who hold her fast are called blessed.
⸻
🧭 Context Summary
A father ties trust, humility, generosity, and teachability to the straight path. Discipline is love, wisdom is
treasure, and her fruit is life and peace.
⸻
🛡 Reflection for a REAL MAN — Accepts Responsibility
A REAL MAN who Accepts Responsibility receives orders from God, not his gut. He trusts the LORD,
rejects the illusion of self-rule, tithes first, and welcomes correction. Passive men avoid discipline; proud
men lean on their own insight. Responsible men fear God, act on His words, and let wisdom’s “tree of
life” shade their household.
⛏ Personal Application — Accepts Responsibility
• Take one decision off “feelings” and place it under a clear command from this passage.
• Give first, not last—honor the LORD tangibly today.
• Ask for correction once—and thank the giver.
• Write Proverbs 3:5–6 on a card and obey it in one concrete choice.
⸻
🙏 Prayer
Father, You delight to teach sons—set my steps under Your commands.
By the Holy Spirit, bend my heart to trust, fear You, give first, and welcome reproof.
In the name of Jesus Christ, Wisdom from God, make my path straight. Amen.
⚓ Helm Command: Hands off the helm of self-reliance—follow the marked channel and make way.

praise Him—my father’s God, and I will exalt Him.
The LORD is a warrior; the LORD is His name.

floods covered them; they went down like a stone.
Your right hand, O LORD, magnificent in power, shatters the enemy. In the greatness of Your majesty
You overthrow Your adversaries; You send out Your fury—it consumes them like stubble.
At the blast of Your nostrils the waters piled up; the floods stood up like a heap…
The enemy said, ‘I will pursue, I will overtake…’ but You blew with Your wind; the sea covered them;
they sank like lead in the mighty waters.
Who is like You, O LORD, among the gods? Majestic in holiness, awesome in praises, working wonders.
You stretched out Your right hand; the earth swallowed them.
In steadfast love You have led the people You redeemed; in Your strength You will guide them to Your
holy dwelling.

You will bring them in and plant them on the mountain of Your inheritance—the place, O LORD, which
You have made for Your dwelling, the sanctuary, O Lord, which Your hands have established.

Then Miriam the prophetess, Aaron’s sister, took a tambourine, and all the women went out after her with

they found no water. They came to Marah, but the water was bitter; therefore it was called Marah. The

🛡 1500 — Third Watch — Citizen’s Stand

📖 Scripture — Exodus 16
They set out from Elim, and all the congregation of Israel came to the wilderness of Sin, between Elim
and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after they departed from Egypt. The whole
congregation grumbled against Moses and Aaron, “Would that we had died by the LORD’s hand in Egypt,
when we sat by the meat pots and ate bread to the full; you have brought us into this wilderness to kill this
whole assembly with hunger.”
Then the LORD said to Moses, “Behold, I am raining bread from heaven for you, and the people shall go
out and gather a day’s portion every day, that I may test them whether they will walk in My law or not.
On the sixth day they shall prepare what they bring in, and it will be twice as much.”
In the evening quail came up and covered the camp; in the morning there was a fine flake-like thing—fine
as frost—on the ground. Moses said, “It is manna,” for they did not know what it was. Moses told them,
“This is the bread the LORD has given you to eat. Gather of it, each one as much as he can eat, an omer a
head, each according to the number in his tent.” Some gathered much and some little, yet whoever
gathered much had nothing left over, and whoever gathered little had no lack; each gathered as much as
he could eat.
Moses said, “Let no one leave any of it till the morning.” But they did not listen; some left part of it until
morning, and it bred worms and stank.
On the sixth day they gathered twice as much—two omers each—and all the leaders of the congregation
came and told Moses. He said, “This is what the LORD has commanded: ‘Tomorrow is a day of solemn
rest, a holy Sabbath to the LORD; bake what you will bake and boil what you will boil, and all that is left
over lay up to be kept till the morning.’ ” They laid it up till the morning, as Moses commanded, and it did
not stink, and there were no worms in it.
Some went out on the seventh day to gather, but they found none. The LORD said to Moses, “How long
will you refuse to keep My commandments and My laws?” So the people rested on the seventh day.
The house of Israel called its name manna; it was like coriander seed, white, and the taste of it was like
wafers with honey. An omer of it was kept before the LORD throughout their generations.

Grumbling fractures a nation; disciplined gathering and resting stabilize it.

• Work your “omer”: gather what is yours today—no hoarding, no slacking.
• Keep Sabbath logic: plan toward true rest and worship this week.
• Kill nostalgia for “Egypt”—replace it with testimony of God’s provision.
