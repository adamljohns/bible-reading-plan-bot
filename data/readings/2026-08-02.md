📖 Scripture — Esther 9-10

The Jews gathered together in their cities throughout all the provinces of King Ahasuerus to lay hand on those who sought their harm, and no one could stand against them because the fear of them fell on all the peoples. The remaining Jews also gathered together to protect their lives and rested from their enemies, keeping the fourteenth day of the month Adar as a day of feasting and gladness. Mordecai recorded these events and sent letters to all the Jews near and far, under the hand of Ahasuerus, establishing the days of Purim so that they might be kept as days of feasting and gladness. Mordecai was great in the house of King Ahasuerus, and his fame spread throughout all the provinces, for the man Mordecai became increasingly powerful.

⸻

🧭 Context Summary

The narrative of Esther concludes with the decisive deliverance of the Jewish people from their enemies. Mordecai, now elevated to high office, institutes the festival of Purim to ensure that this deliverance is remembered and celebrated by future generations. This chapter marks the transition from the threat of annihilation to the establishment of peace and Jewish autonomy under the Persian crown.

Trust in His providence, even when the path is unclear, for He is faithful to His promises.

🕖 0700 First Watch — The Husband's Post

The Shepherd has come to judge the nations, calling His people to repentance and their King to reign.

📖 Scripture — Zechariah 11-12

Thus says the LORD my God: Shepherd the flock appointed for slaughter. Those who buy them kill them and go unpunished, and those who sell them say, Blessed be the LORD, for I have grown rich. So I will not pity the flock. For I will no longer pity the inhabitants of the land, declares the LORD, and behold, I will give everyone into the hand of his neighbor and into the hand of his king, and they shall strike the land, and I will deliver them out of the hand of any who would strike them down.

So I shepherded the flock of slaughter. I took two staffs. I called the one Favor and the other Union. And I shepherded the flock. In one month I destroyed the three shepherds. But I grew weary of them, and also their soul loathed me. Then I said, I will not shepherd you. What is to die, let it die, and what is to be destroyed, let it be destroyed, and let those who are left eat one another’s flesh.

Then I took my staff, called Union, and broke it, that I might break the brotherhood of Judah and Israel. And the LORD said to me, Take again the equipment of a foolish shepherd. For behold, I am raising up in the land a shepherd who shall not care for those being destroyed, nor seek the young, nor heal the injured, nor feed the healthy, but shall eat the fat of the fattest and tear off their hooves.

Woe to my worthless shepherd, who leaves the flock! May the sword strike his arm and his right eye! May his arm be withered, his right eye utterly blinded!

On that day a lamentation will be like a loud lamentation in Har-Magedon. The land will mourn, family by family: the family of the house of David apart, and their wives apart; the family of the house of Nathan apart, and their wives apart; the family of the house of Levi apart, and their wives apart; the family of the Shimeites apart, and their wives apart; all the families that remain, apart, each by itself, and their wives apart.

On that day there shall be a fountain opened to the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and for uncleanness. And on that day, declares the LORD of hosts, I will cut off the names of the idols from the land, so that they shall be remembered no more. And I will remove from the land both the prophets and the spirit of uncleanness. And if anyone still prophesies, his father and his mother who bore him will say to him, You shall not live, for you speak lies in the name of the LORD. And his father and his mother who bore him will pierce him through when he prophesies. On that day every prophet will be ashamed of his vision when he prophesies. He will not put on a robe of sackcloth to deceive. But he will say, I am no prophet, I am a man who works the soil, and my master taught me to work the soil from my youth. And on that day the LORD will say to the shepherds, Woe to the worthless shepherd who leaves the flock! May the sword strike his arm and his right eye! May his arm be completely withered, his right eye utterly blinded!

🗺️ Briefing Summary
Zechariah receives a sobering command to act out the coming rejection of Israel by their own leaders, symbolizing the breaking of national unity through the shattering of his staffs. This act of symbolic judgment reveals God’s sovereignty in allowing wickedness to run its course before He intervenes with severe discipline. The passage then shifts to a glorious promise of spiritual cleansing and the ultimate triumph of the Messiah, who opens a fountain for sin and removes the very presence of idolatry and false prophecy.

The prophet confronts the people with a stark indictment: they have grown weary of God's covenant blessings and have begun to offer Him their leftovers. The Lord reminds them that their attitude toward Him is directly tied to their attitude toward His law and their fellow man.

🦅 This Day in American History — August 2
On August 2, 1776, the members of the Continental Congress put their names to the engrossed Declaration of Independence, John Hancock signing first with a bold hand and the rest pledging their lives, their fortunes, and their sacred honor to a cause they could not win without Providence. On the same day in 1909, the United States Mint released the first Lincoln cent, setting the face of a fallen president into the common coin so that the humblest pocket might carry the memory of a man who held the Union together. An Obedient citizen submits himself as those signers did to a higher law than his own comfort, binding his name and his labor to the truth and trusting God with the cost.
