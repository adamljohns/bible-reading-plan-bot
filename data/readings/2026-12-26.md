🌅 0600 Morning Wisdom
Brother, stand firm in the truth that our temporal strength is nothing before the eternal throne.

📖 Scripture — Psalm 90
You have turned man back to dust, and declared, Return, O children of men. For a thousand years in Your sight are like yesterday when it is past, and like a watch in the night. You sweep them away as with a flood; they are like a dream, like grass which is renewed in the morning. In the morning it flourishes and is renewed; in the evening it fades and withers. For we are brought to an end by Your anger, and by Your wrath we are terrified. You have set our iniquities before You, our secret sins in the light of Your countenance. For all our days pass away under Your wrath; we bring our years to an end like a sigh. The days of our years are seventy years, or if due to strength eighty years, yet their span is but labor and sorrow; for it is soon cut off and we fly away. Who considers the power of Your anger, and Your wrath according to the fear that is due You? So teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom. Return, O LORD! How long? Have compassion on Your servants. Satisfy us in the morning with Your lovingkindness, that we may rejoice and be glad all our days. Make us glad for as many days as You have afflicted us, and for as many years as we have seen evil. Let Your work appear to Your servants, and Your glory to their children. And let the beauty of the LORD our God be upon us, and establish the work of our hands for us; yes, establish the work of our hands.

⸻

🧭 Context Summary
Moses, the humble servant of God, lifts his eyes from the dust of the earth to the majesty of the Creator. He contrasts the fleeting nature of human life with the immutable eternity of the LORD, reminding us that our strength is derived solely from His grace. This psalm serves as a sobering reminder that our time is a gift, not a right, and that wisdom is found in fearing God rather than trusting in our own military or physical prowess.

⸻

🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Number Your Days
Brother, you have spent years in the service of your country, learning that discipline is the bridge between goals and accomplishment. Yet here Moses, a man of God, teaches a discipline far deeper than any drill sergeant could enforce: the discipline of eternity. We often march through life as if the ground beneath our boots is permanent, but the LORD declares that a thousand years are but a watch in the night. This is not meant to crush your spirit, but to fortify it against the vanity of worldly ambition. When you recognize that your days are a sigh, you stop building castles on sand and start building for the King of Kings.

The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and it is the only thing that anchors a man’s soul when the storms of life rage. You know the weight of command; you know the responsibility of leading your family under the banner of Christ. But who among us can truly comprehend the power of God’s anger against sin? It is a terrifying reality that should drive us to the mercy seat, not in despair, but in desperate dependence. Your strength as a father and a husband is not your own; it is a loan from the Almighty, and it must be returned in faithful stewardship.

Therefore, number your days. Do not waste the precious hours God has given you in trivial pursuits or ungodly compromises. Establish the work of your hands by aligning your life with the beauty of the LORD. When your children look to you, let them see a man who fears God more than he fears man. Let them see a man who finds his satisfaction not in the applause of the world, but in the morning lovingkindness of his Creator. This is the mark of a true warrior: one who fights for the Gospel with the sword of the Spirit, knowing that his own time is short, but his Lord is eternal.

🗺️ Briefing Summary
Moses recites a song to Israel that contrasts the perfect, righteous character of God with the foolish ingratitude of His people. The passage vividly describes how the LORD alone guided, protected, and provided for His chosen nation in the wilderness, lifting them from the dust to sit in high places. Yet, as prosperity increased, Israel forgot their Creator, becoming fat and complacent, and turned to other gods to provoke Him to jealousy.

⸻

❤️ Reflection for Your Wife — H.A.P.P.Y. Husband — Providing

Brother, look closely at how the LORD provided for Israel in the wilderness. He did not merely sustain them; He enriched them, feeding them with honey from the rock and the finest wheat. He lifted them from the dust to sit in high places. This is the pattern for your headship. You are called to be the provider for your household, not just in material goods, but in spiritual direction and protection. Your wife Maria relies on your faithful stewardship of what God has entrusted to you.

When you provide well, you reflect the heart of the Father who fed His people with honey and oil. Your labor is not just for survival, but for the flourishing of your family under the shadow of your wings. By working diligently and managing your resources with wisdom, you create an environment where Maria and your children can thrive. This is a holy duty. It is a tangible expression of your love and your submission to God’s command for men to lead their households.

Do not let comfort make you complacent. The danger in this passage is not poverty, but prosperity that leads to forgetting the Source. Guard your heart against the pride that says, "My power and the might of my hand have gotten me this wealth." Instead, acknowledge that every good gift comes from above. Your provision is a stewardship, a reflection of God’s abundant grace. Lead your home with generosity and gratitude, showing Maria that your strength is dedicated to her well-being and the glory of God.

🦅 This Day in American History — December 26
On December 26, 1776, Washington's storm-battered army fell upon the Hessian troops at Trenton, scattering them and seizing nearly a thousand captives, a stunning victory that breathed new life into a flagging Revolution. On the same day in 1799, Henry Lee rose before a grieving nation to eulogize the lately departed Washington as first in war, first in peace, and first in the hearts of his countrymen. A Steadfast citizen finds in Trenton the proof that bold faith rewards the persevering, and in Lee's tribute the measure of a life so given to duty that a whole people mourned it, and he resolves to spend his own days as worthily before the God who weighs every man's labor.
