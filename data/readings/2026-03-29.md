🧭 Context Summary
Solomon concludes his collection of proverbs by contrasting the chaotic rise of the wicked with the stable, enduring justice of the righteous ruler who fears God. He warns that human authority, whether in kings or fathers, is accountable to the Supreme Judge, who sees the heart of the poor and the oppressor alike. The passage emphasizes that true stability in any realm of leadership comes not from power or wealth, but from a steadfast reliance on God’s law and a rejection of self-exaltation. It calls the believer to a life of humility, correction, and awe before the Lord, recognizing that all earthly structures must submit to His eternal throne.

Joshua 20 establishes a legal framework where the blood of the innocent is protected from reckless vengeance, ensuring that intent matters to the Judge of all the earth. The provision of these cities was a tangible grace, showing that even in a land of judgment, there is a way of escape for the repentant and the innocent.
