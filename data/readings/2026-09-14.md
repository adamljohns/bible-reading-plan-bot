📖 Scripture — Proverbs 14
The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and knowledge of the Holy One is understanding. Fools despise their father’s instruction, but whoever heeds correction shows honor. The wise fear the LORD and shun evil, but a fool is hotheaded and recklessly bold. The simple believe anything, but the prudent give thought to their steps. A fool’s mouth is a rod for his own back, but the lips of the wise preserve them. The wise in heart accept commands, but a chattering fool comes to ruin. A wise man has great power, and knowledge brings strength. For by wise guidance you can wage your war, and in abundance of counselors there is victory. 🧭 Context Summary
Solomon contrasts the eternal stability of the fear of the LORD with the chaotic destruction of foolishness. He illustrates how a man’s heart orientation determines his conduct, his speech, and his ultimate security. The passage emphasizes that true strength and victory in life’s battles are not products of human might alone, but are granted by the sovereign will of God.

🗺️ Briefing Summary

Peter addresses the crowd with bold clarity, establishing that the events unfolding are the fulfillment of Joel’s prophecy regarding the outpouring of the Spirit. He confronts the men of Israel with the hard truth of their role in the crucifixion, yet immediately pivots to the glorious reality that God’s sovereign plan brought redemption through this very act. By citing David’s psalm, Peter proves that the Messiah was not abandoned to death but was exalted to the right hand of the Father. The passage concludes with a thunderous declaration that the crucified Jesus is now the reigning Lord and Christ, demanding a response of faith from all who hear.

🦅 This Day in American History — September 14

On September 14, 1814, Francis Scott Key witnessed the bombardment of Fort McHenry during the War of 1812. As the British cannons fired through the night, Key wondered if the American flag would still be flying at dawn. When he saw the "star-spangled banner" waving in the morning light, it confirmed that the fort had held its ground. This moment of steadfast defense inspired the lyrics to "The Star-Spangled Banner," reminding us that national survival often depends on those who endure the night so that the morning may bring victory.

On September 14, 1901, President William McKinley died from wounds inflicted by an assassin, leading to the presidency of Theodore Roosevelt. While the tragedy of assassination is a dark reminder of human sinfulness, Roosevelt’s subsequent rise marked a period of vigorous stewardship and moral clarity. He championed the "square deal" and conservation, reflecting a belief that men in authority have a God-given duty to protect the weak and preserve creation. His life serves as a sobering reminder that leadership is a sacred trust, and that men must be prepared to bear the weight of responsibility with courage and integrity.
