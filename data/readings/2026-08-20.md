🗺️ Briefing Summary

Jesus addresses the multitudes with a startling demand for absolute priority, establishing that love for Him must exceed all natural affections. He uses the metaphors of building a tower and going to war to illustrate the necessity of counting the cost before committing to the Christian life. The passage concludes with a warning that disciples who fail to endure or remain faithful lose their spiritual utility and value. This is not a call to emotional indifference, but a declaration that Christ must hold the supreme place in the hierarchy of a man’s devotion.

The parable reveals the heart of the King toward the prodigal who returns to his post and the resentful servant who remains in the field but loses his joy. The older brother’s anger exposes a heart that trusts in its own performance rather than the Father’s goodness.

🦅 This Day in American History — August 20
On August 20, 1794, General Anthony Wayne led his well-drilled Legion to a decisive victory at Fallen Timbers, ending years of bloody warfare on the frontier and opening the Ohio country to settlement under American law. On the same day in 1833, Benjamin Harrison was born in North Bend, Ohio, an heir of a founding family who would himself rise to the presidency. A Loyal citizen gives his steady devotion to his country as Wayne gave his discipline in the field and as Harrison carried forward his family's service, holding fast to the cause and the heritage God has placed in his keeping rather than abandoning his post when ease beckons.
