🌅 0600 Morning Wisdom
The Lord calls His sons to seek understanding as diligently as a soldier seeks the map before entering hostile territory.

📖 Scripture — Proverbs 2
Listen, my son, to my wisdom; turn your ear to my words of insight.
For I will guide you in the good and lead you along straight paths.
When wisdom enters your heart, and knowledge is pleasant to your soul,
discretion will guard you, and understanding will watch over you,
to save you from the way of the evil man, from the man whose words are perverse,
who forsakes the paths of uprightness to walk in ways of darkness,
who rejoices in doing evil and delights in the perverseness of the wicked,
whose ways are crooked and who is devious in his paths.
You will walk in your way securely, and your foot will not stumble.
You will guard your way and find rest for your soul.
Do not be wise in your own eyes; fear the LORD and shun evil.
It will be health to your flesh and refreshment to your bones.
For the LORD gives wisdom; from His mouth come knowledge and understanding.
He holds success in store for the upright, He is a shield to those who walk blamelessly,
for He guards the course of the just, and He protects the way of His saints.
Then you will discern what is right and just and fair, every good path.
For wisdom will enter your heart, and knowledge will be pleasant to your soul;
discretion will watch over you, understanding will guard you.
You will be delivered from the evil woman, from the flattery of the wayward wife.
For her house leads down to death, and her paths to the spirits of the dead.
None who go to her return, nor do they attain to the paths of life.
So you will walk in the way of the good and keep to the paths of the righteous.
For the upright will dwell in the land, and the blameless will remain in it;
but the wicked will be cut off from the land, and the treacherous will be rooted out of it.

⸻
🧭 Context Summary
Solomon addresses his son with the urgency of a commander briefing his troops, urging him to pursue divine insight with total commitment. This passage establishes that true wisdom is not merely intellectual but a spiritual discipline that requires the fear of the LORD. By seeking God’s direction, the son receives protection from moral corruption and the deceptive allure of sin. The text promises that obedience to this divine instruction results in security, health, and a steadfast path through a fallen world.

⸻
🛡️ Reflection for a REAL MAN — Fear the LORD
Brother, you know the value of a clear chain of command. In the military, ambiguity gets men killed. In the spiritual life, self-reliance is equally fatal. Solomon warns against being "wise in your own eyes." This is the pride of the independent operator, the man who trusts his own judgment over the revealed will of God. As a father and a husband, you are called to lead your household not by your own intuition, but by the fear of the LORD. This fear is not a cowering terror, but a reverent awe that recognizes God’s absolute sovereignty and your total dependence on Him.

When you fear the LORD, you acknowledge that He is the source of all true strength and strategy. He holds success in store for the upright. This is not a promise of material wealth, but of spiritual stability and divine favor. You are to be a shield to those who walk blamelessly. Your wife and children look to you for protection, not just from physical harm, but from the spiritual decay that surrounds our culture. You provide this protection by anchoring your home in the fear of the LORD, shunning evil, and pursuing righteousness with relentless discipline.

Brother, the world offers you a path of ease and self-gratification. The enemy offers you a way that seems clever but leads to death. Do not be deceived. Walk in the way of the good. Let your wisdom be rooted in the fear of the LORD, for He gives understanding and guards your course. Stand firm in this truth, and your foot will not stumble.

📖 Scripture — Judges 6:25-40

Now it came to pass the same night that the LORD said to him, “Take your father’s young bull, the second bull of seven years old, and tear down the altar of Baal that your father has, and cut down the wooden image that is beside it;
and build an altar to the LORD your God on top of this rock in the proper arrangement, and take the second bull and offer a burnt sacrifice with the wood of the image which you shall cut down.”
So Gideon took ten men from among his servants and did as the LORD had said to him. But because he feared his father’s household and the men of the city too much to do it by day, he did it by night.
And when the men of the city arose early in the morning, there was the altar of Baal, torn down; and the wooden image that was beside it was cut down, and the second bull was being offered on the altar which had been built.
So they said to one another, “Who has done this thing?” And when they had inquired and asked, they said, “Gideon the son of Joash has done this thing.”
Then the men of the city said to Joash, “Bring out your son, that he may die, because he has torn down the altar of Baal, and because he has cut down the wooden image that was beside it.”
But Joash said to all who stood against him, “Would you plead for Baal? Would you save him? Let the one who would plead for him be put to death by morning! If he is a god, let him plead for himself, because his altar has been torn down!”
Therefore on that day he called him Jerubbaal, saying, “Let Baal plead against him, because he has torn down his altar.”
Then all the Midianites and Amalekites, the people of the East, gathered together; and they crossed over and encamped in the Valley of Jezreel.
But the Spirit of the LORD came upon Gideon; then he blew the trumpet, and the Abiezrites gathered behind him.
And he sent messengers throughout all Manasseh, who also gathered behind him. He also sent messengers to Asher, Zebulun, and Naphtali; and they came up to meet them.
So Gideon said to God, “If You will save Israel by my hand as You have said—
look, I shall put a fleece of wool on the threshing floor; if there is dew on the fleece only, and it is dry on all the ground, then I shall know that You will save Israel by my hand, as You have said.”
And it was so. When he rose early the next morning and squeezed the fleece together, he wrung the dew out of the fleece, a bowlful of water.
Then Gideon said to God, “Do not be angry with me, but let me speak just once more: Let me test, I pray, just once more with the fleece; let it now be dry only on the fleece, but on all the ground let there be dew.”
And God did so that night. It was dry on the fleece only, but there was dew on all the ground.

🗺️ Briefing Summary
The narrative shifts from your initial fear to your decisive action in tearing down the altar of Baal, establishing the priority of covenant loyalty over familial pressure. This act of spiritual demolition is followed by the Lord’s call to lead your household, a call you meet with hesitation and a request for confirmation through the fleece. The passage illustrates the tension between the believer’s duty to uproot idolatry in his own household and the human tendency to seek tangible proof of God’s presence before moving forward.

❤️ Reflection for Your Wife — H.A.P.P.Y. Husband — Protecting

Brother, look to Gideon as a type of the husband who must protect the spiritual integrity of his home. The first command given to him was to tear down the altar of Baal that his father had built. This was not merely a religious ritual; it was a protective act. Gideon had to shield his own household from the creeping influence of idolatry, even when it meant standing against his father and the entire town. You see, a husband’s protection is not just physical; it is primarily spiritual. He must identify the "altars" in his home—the compromises, the idols of comfort, the secret sins—and tear them down before they take root in the hearts of your wife and children.

When your son did this by night, he was acting in the fear of the LORD, though he was afraid of men. He understood that the safety of his family depended on their exclusive allegiance to the LORD. As a father and a husband, you are the gatekeeper of your home’s spiritual climate. You must be willing to endure the ridicule of the world and the pressure of your own flesh to maintain the purity of worship in your house. The altar you build to the LORD must be higher than the altars the world tries to impose on your family.

This protection requires courage, brother. Gideon was called a "mighty man of valor" not because he was already strong, but because he was willing to be used by the LORD. Your valor is shown in your willingness to confront the spiritual enemies of your wife’s peace and your children’s souls. You protect your wife by leading her in the truth, by ensuring that no other master holds sway over your home. You protect your children by teaching them that the LORD alone is worthy of their fear and trust.

Then his companion answered and said, “This is nothing else but the sword of Gideon the son of Joash, a man of Israel! Into his hand God has delivered Midian and the whole camp.”

This is the heritage you leave them: a life anchored in the sovereignty of a God who fights for His people.
