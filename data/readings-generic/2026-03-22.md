🧭 Context Summary
The wise instructor urges the student to internalize divine instruction so that the LORD becomes his living trust rather than his own cunning. This passage contrasts the security found in God’s justice for the poor with the self-destructive habits of anger and sloth. It concludes by affirming that diligence and skill, when grounded in wisdom, lead to honor, whereas laziness invites ruin. The text calls the reader to respect established boundaries and to pursue excellence as a witness to the glory of God.

The LORD calls you to wakefulness, to a vigilant spirit that recognizes the reality of spiritual warfare. Finally, consider the promise that the skillful man stands before kings. This is not a promise of worldly ambition for you, brother, but a testament to the value of faithfulness. When you apply yourself to your duties with excellence, you reflect the glory of the Creator. Your work in your trade, your leadership in your church, and your care for your household are all arenas where you demonstrate the certainty of the LORD’s word. Stand firm in your calling, knowing that the LORD honors those who honor Him through obedient action.

🦅 This Day in American History — March 22
On March 22, 1765, the British Parliament passed the Stamp Act, the first direct tax laid upon the colonies, which kindled the cry of no taxation without representation and steeled a people's resolve. On the same day in 1622, a sudden Powhatan assault swept the Virginia settlements and killed nearly a quarter of the Jamestown colonists, yet the battered colony held on and survived. An Enduring citizen takes both to heart, for a hard tax forged American resolve and a brutal morning could not extinguish a planted colony, and he learns to outlast every trial by the sustaining hand of God.
